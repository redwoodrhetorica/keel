# Track D: Boolean Operations and Tolerant Modeling

Literature review supporting the design of Keel, an open-source Rust B-rep solid modeling kernel with Parasolid-class ambitions. Scope: classic B-rep boolean pipelines, tolerant modeling, formal foundations for imprecise geometry, the 2016-2023 mesh-boolean renaissance, degeneracy handling, and the documented behavior of open-source boolean implementations. Style note: this file deliberately uses no em-dashes.

## 1. Executive Summary

Robust boolean operations are the single hardest deliverable for a B-rep kernel, and the literature is unambiguous about why: the robustness problem is rooted in the impossibility of making consistent incidence and non-incidence decisions when geometry is computed in finite precision. Hoffmann's framing remains the canonical statement of the problem. Two broad strategies have emerged. The first, embodied by the entire commercial B-rep lineage (Parasolid, ACIS), is tolerant modeling: accept that surface-surface intersections cannot be represented exactly, attach per-entity tolerances to vertices and edges, and build a topology that is consistent with respect to those tolerances even when the underlying geometry has gaps. The second, embodied by the mesh-arrangement renaissance of 2016 to 2023, is exactness: represent or reason about geometry so that every predicate is decided correctly, eliminating the inconsistency at its source. The mesh community achieved a decisive practical win here through exact and indirect predicates plus arrangement-then-classify pipelines, producing the first boolean algorithms with genuine robustness guarantees that also run at interactive rates.

The central design tension for Keel is that the exactness strategy that won for meshes does not transfer cleanly to NURBS-bounded solids. Surface-surface intersection of two degree-(3,3) NURBS patches is a transcendental algebraic problem with no exact rational closed form, so the intersection curve cannot be represented exactly in the way a triangle-triangle intersection point can. This is the fundamental reason tolerant modeling exists: it is not a workaround for sloppy engineering, it is a response to a genuine mathematical obstruction. Keel therefore cannot copy Cherchi or Lalish wholesale. It must adopt the architectural lessons (arrangement-then-classify, winding-number classification, symbolic perturbation for degeneracy, exact predicates wherever the geometry is linear or rational) while building tolerant-modeling machinery from day one for the irreducibly inexact NURBS intersection curves. The recommendation is a hybrid: exact predicates and exact arithmetic on the combinatorial and linear/rational layers, per-entity tolerances and gap-tolerant stitching on the NURBS surface-intersection layer, and a winding-number-based classification stage that does not depend on watertight intermediate geometry.

## 2. Annotated Key References

### Classic B-rep foundations

**Requicha, A. A. G. (1980). "Representations for Rigid Solids: Theory, Methods, and Systems." ACM Computing Surveys 12(4), 437-464.** https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/356827.356833 (mirror: https://lvelho.impa.br/i3d14/modtec/p437-requicha.pdf). The foundational taxonomy of solid representations and the source of the r-set (regularized set) formalism and regularized boolean operations. Requicha established that solids must be modeled as regular closed sets and that boolean results must be regularized to remove dangling lower-dimensional artifacts. This is the conceptual bedrock: a boolean is a regularized set operation whose result must again be a valid solid. Point Membership Classification (PMC), the operation of deciding whether a point is in, on, or out of a solid, originates in this Rochester PADL lineage and remains the primitive that drives boundary classification.

**Requicha, A. A. G. and Voelcker, H. B. "Boolean operations in solid modeling: Boundary evaluation and merging algorithms." Proc. IEEE 73(1), 1985.** https://scispace.com/pdf/boolean-operations-in-solid-modeling-boundary-evaluation-and-9zz6lpq7h1.pdf. Defines the boundary-evaluation pipeline that every B-rep boolean still follows in some form: intersect the boundaries of the two operands, classify the resulting boundary pieces against the other solid using set-membership classification and neighborhood (local topological) information, then assemble the surviving pieces. The imprint/classify/stitch architecture descends directly from this. Takeaway: the merging algorithm and neighborhood manipulation at on-boundary points are where degeneracy lives, and this is exactly where tolerant modeling will be forced to make decisions.

**Hoffmann, C. M. "Geometric and Solid Modeling: An Introduction" (1989), and "Robustness in Geometric Computations." J. Computing and Information Science in Engineering 1(2), 143-156 (2001).** Scholar record: https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?title=Robustness+in+geometric+computations. Hoffmann is the authoritative voice on B-rep robustness. His central thesis: geometric software is fragile because it cannot make unambiguous incidence/non-incidence decisions, and this fundamentally impairs the reliable layering of geometric algorithms. He catalogs the explosion of special and singular cases and frames the two responses (exact computation vs. tolerance-based consistency) that still define the field. Required reading for the failure-mode mindset Keel must design against.

### Tolerant modeling

**Jackson, D. J. "Boundary representation modelling with local tolerances." Proc. 3rd ACM Symposium on Solid Modeling and Applications (1995), 247-253.** https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/218013.218067 (PDF: https://ftp.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf). The seminal academic statement of per-entity local tolerances, the model that Parasolid and ACIS productized as "tolerant edges" and "tolerant vertices." The idea: when two surfaces do not intersect cleanly (they nearly miss or nearly coincide), do not force an exact intersection curve. Instead create a tolerant edge whose geometry is approximate and whose tolerance value bounds the gap between the true surface intersection and the stored curve. Topology becomes consistent at the scale of the tolerance, not at machine epsilon. Takeaway: this is the day-one machinery Keel needs. Tolerances are attached to entities, propagate through operations, and bound the geometric uncertainty of contact curves and points.

### Formal foundations for imprecise geometry

**Qi, J. and Shapiro, V. "Epsilon-solidity in geometric data translation" / "Geometric Interoperability With Epsilon Solidity." J. Computing and Information Science in Engineering 6(3), 213-222 (2006).** https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Geometric-Interoperability-With-Epsilon-Solidity-Qi-Shapiro/8579b8ba1032def1602fccfe06510cd317493b79. **Qi, J. and Shapiro, V. "Epsilon-topological formulation of tolerant solid modeling." Computer-Aided Design 38(4), 367-377 (2006).** https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cad.2005.10.010. The most serious attempt to give tolerant modeling a formal foundation. Replaces point-set topology's infinitesimal neighborhoods with finite epsilon-neighborhoods, defines epsilon-regularity and epsilon-solidity that subsume the classical exact notions as the epsilon-to-zero limit, and shows that the usual B-rep validity checks are neither necessary nor sufficient for maintaining epsilon-solidity under numerical inaccuracy. Crucial takeaway for Keel: this gives a principled definition of "valid enough" that healing-free pipelines can target, and it justifies tolerant booleans as something more than engineering pragmatism.

**Guibas, L., Salesin, D., Stolfi, J. "Epsilon Geometry: Building Robust Algorithms from Imprecise Computations." Proc. 5th Annual Symposium on Computational Geometry (1989).** https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/73833.73857 (PDF: https://members.loria.fr/Sylvain.Lazard/BellairsWorkshops/Bellairs-2009/background/epsilon-geometry-guibas-salesin-stolfi.pdf). The ancestor of the epsilon-solidity line. Introduces epsilon-predicates that return not just true/false but a bound on the perturbation needed to make the answer certainly true. Establishes the idea that a robust algorithm can be built from predicates that honestly report their own uncertainty. Relevant to Keel as the conceptual model for how NURBS-intersection predicates should report tolerance alongside their boolean answer.

### The mesh-arrangement renaissance

**Zhou, Q., Grinspun, E., Zorin, D., Jacobson, A. "Mesh Arrangements for Solid Geometry." ACM TOG 35(4), SIGGRAPH 2016.** https://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/. The paper that reframed mesh booleans. Rather than pairwise-intersecting and stitching (fragile), it resolves all self-intersections into a single arrangement, then assigns each input mesh a winding number to every cell of the arrangement, producing a per-cell winding-number vector. Any boolean (union, intersection, difference, and arbitrary set expressions including n-ary minkowski-style operations) is then a predicate on that vector: extract the faces between a cell that is "in" and a cell that is "out" under the chosen predicate. Uses exact predicates to make the arrangement robust. Output is guaranteed manifold regardless of input quality. This architecture (arrange once, classify by winding number, extract) is the single most important transferable idea in this review.

**Attene, M. "Indirect predicates for geometric constructions." Computer-Aided Design 126 (2020).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09772, https://github.com/MarcoAttene/Indirect_Predicates. The robustness enabler behind the fast mesh booleans. An indirect predicate evaluates a geometric test on a constructed point (such as the intersection of two lines or three planes) without ever explicitly rounding that point to a floating-point coordinate. It tracks the construction symbolically through the predicate, using floating-point filters plus expansion arithmetic so the common case runs at near-float speed and the exact path runs only when the filter is inconclusive. Result: exact predicates on constructed geometry at near-float cost and memory. Scope caveat from the paper itself: it covers linear elements (lines, planes) only. Extending indirect predicates to curved (NURBS) intersection points is an open problem and likely intractable in the rational sense.

**Cherchi, G., Livesu, M., Scateni, R., Attene, M. "Fast and Robust Mesh Arrangements using Floating-point Arithmetic." ACM TOG 39(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2020.** https://www.gianmarcocherchi.com/pdf/mesh_arrangement.pdf, https://github.com/gcherchi/FastAndRobustMeshArrangements. Combines the arrangement-then-classify architecture with Attene's indirect predicates to get a mesh-arrangement pipeline that is provably correct yet nearly as fast as a naive float implementation. The key engineering insight: keep coordinates implicit (as constructions) rather than rounding them, so you never introduce the inconsistencies that float rounding creates. Takeaway: exactness need not be slow if you filter aggressively and only fall back to exact arithmetic at genuine ambiguities.

**Cherchi, G., Pellacini, F., Attene, M., Livesu, M. "Interactive and Robust Mesh Booleans." ACM TOG 41(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2022.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14151, https://github.com/gcherchi/InteractiveAndRobustMeshBooleans. The first boolean algorithm with robustness guarantees that runs at interactive frame rates (meshes up to ~200K triangles). Demonstrates that the exact-predicate arrangement approach is production-viable for interactive modeling, closing the historical performance gap that made exact methods (CGAL Nef) unusable interactively. Takeaway: robustness and interactivity are no longer mutually exclusive for linear geometry.

**Trettner, P., Nehring-Wirxel, J., Kobbelt, L. "EMBER: Exact Mesh Booleans via Efficient and Robust Local Arrangements." ACM TOG 41(4), SIGGRAPH 2022.** https://www.graphics.rwth-aachen.de/media/papers/339/ember_exact_mesh_booleans_via_efficient_and_robust_local_arrangements.pdf, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3528223.3530181. Uses homogeneous integer coordinates to represent points exactly and computes booleans through localized (not global) arrangements, with aggressive parallelization. The fastest exact mesh boolean to date, scaling to millions of elements. The "local arrangement" idea (only resolve the arrangement near actual intersections rather than globally) is directly relevant to Keel: B-rep booleans should imprint only along intersection curves, never globally re-mesh.

**Lalish, E. "Manifold" geometry library (2022-2024).** https://github.com/elalish/manifold, https://elalish.blogspot.com/2022/02/introducing-my-manifold-library.html. A production-grade, parallel, guaranteed-manifold mesh boolean library now used by OpenSCAD and others. Design philosophy explicitly rejects the "general position" assumption common in academic work, observing that human-designed CAD geometry is full of exact coincidences (a hole exactly matching a cube face). Achieves manifoldness for any input with no per-case handling by avoiding Euclidean real-number assumptions and using intuitive symbolic perturbation to resolve exactly-equal coordinates in geometrically sensible directions, plus a degenerate-triangle removal pass that paradoxically speeds the whole pipeline by preventing triangle proliferation. Takeaway: Lalish's coincidence-first mindset (coincidences are the common case, not the exception) is the correct mental model for CAD booleans and a direct rebuke to general-position thinking.

### Degeneracy handling

**Edelsbrunner, H. and Mucke, E. P. "Simulation of Simplicity: a technique to cope with degenerate cases in geometric algorithms." ACM TOG 9(1), 1990.** https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/77635.77639, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9410209. The standard technique for eliminating degenerate cases. Adds a symbolic infinitesimal perturbation to every input coordinate, parameterized so that no determinant predicate can ever evaluate to exactly zero, which removes all degenerate (collinear, coplanar, coincident) cases from the algorithm's logic without ever perturbing the actual data. The programmer writes only the general-position code path. Takeaway: SoS, or a domain-specific symbolic perturbation in the Manifold style, is how Keel should avoid a combinatorial explosion of special-case code in its arrangement and classification stages.

### Open-source implementations and their failure modes

**Open CASCADE Technology (OCCT) BOPAlgo.** https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/occt-7.4.0/overview/html/occt_user_guides__boolean_operations.html. The reference open-source B-rep boolean on real NURBS geometry. Architecture: BOPAlgo_PaveFiller performs the intersection phase (pairwise sub-shape intersection, producing intersection curves stored as BOPDS_Curve), then BOPAlgo_Builder (the General Fuse) splits the argument shapes and recombines them per the operation. It exposes a Fuzzy mode (a user-supplied global tolerance added to entity tolerances, i.e. user-tunable tolerant modeling) and a Gluing mode for coincident faces. Documented failure surface: BOPAlgo_AlertIntersectionFailed and a family of warnings about failed pairwise intersections. Takeaway: OCCT is the cautionary tale. Its booleans are famous for fragility on near-tangent and near-coincident inputs, and the existence of Fuzzy/Glue modes is direct evidence that real NURBS booleans cannot escape tolerant modeling. Study its data structures (pave, paveblock, face-info) as a worked example of imprint-and-classify on curved geometry.

**CGAL 3D Nef Polyhedra (Nef_3) and 3D Selective Nef Complexes.** https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Nef_3/. Hachenberger, Kettner, Mehlhorn. Boolean operations on Nef polyhedra are fully general (handle unbounded, mixed-dimensional, and non-manifold cells) and fully exact, using an exact rational kernel (Gmpq/leda_rational) and per-vertex sphere maps. The acknowledged cost: exact rational arithmetic and the sphere-map data structure are slow and very memory-heavy, "unacceptably slow for interactive modeling." Takeaway: this is the proof that pure exact-rational B-rep is robust but not interactive, and the reason the 2020-2022 indirect-predicate work was such a breakthrough. Also the canonical reference for non-manifold and mixed-dimensional results, which arise from regularized booleans on tangent contacts.

**Rossignac, J. and O'Connor, M. "Selective Geometric Complexes (SGC)" (1989).** Context: http://solidmodeling.org/awards/bezier-award/jaroslaw-jarek-rossignac/. A dimension-independent representation for cellular pointsets with internal structure and incomplete boundaries, a precursor to non-manifold B-rep. Relevant because regularized booleans on solids in tangential or coincident contact naturally produce non-manifold and mixed-dimensional intermediate results, and a representation that can hold those without panic (rather than forcing premature regularization) makes the classification stage more robust.

## 3. State-of-the-Art Assessment

For linear/polyhedral geometry, the boolean robustness problem is essentially solved. The Cherchi/Attene/Livesu line (arrangement plus indirect predicates) and Lalish's Manifold deliver guaranteed-correct, guaranteed-manifold booleans at interactive-to-real-time speeds on million-element meshes. The winning recipe is now standard: (1) resolve intersections into an arrangement using exact or indirect predicates so no incidence decision is ever wrong, (2) classify cells by winding number, (3) extract by a per-operation predicate on the winding-number vector, (4) use symbolic perturbation to eliminate degenerate code paths. Exactness, not tolerance, is the linear-geometry answer, because triangle-triangle intersection points are rational and therefore exactly representable.

For curved (NURBS) B-rep geometry, the problem is not solved and arguably cannot be solved by exactness alone. The surface-surface intersection (SSI) of two rational or polynomial patches is a transcendental algebraic curve with no exact rational parameterization, so the intersection curve must be approximated, which reintroduces exactly the incidence-ambiguity that exact predicates removed for meshes. This is why every production NURBS kernel (Parasolid, ACIS, OCCT) is built on tolerant modeling rather than exactness. The state of the art for curved B-rep is therefore still the tolerant-modeling architecture of the 1990s (Jackson, productized by Parasolid/ACIS), now with a stronger formal underpinning (Qi/Shapiro epsilon-solidity) but without the clean robustness guarantees the mesh world enjoys. No published method gives provably correct booleans on general NURBS-bounded solids.

## 4. Design Impact for Keel

### Recommended boolean pipeline architecture

A four-stage pipeline that borrows the mesh-renaissance architecture but is tolerant where the geometry forces it:

1. **Broad phase.** Spatial-hierarchy (BVH/AABB tree) culling of face-face pairs that cannot intersect. Cheap, embarrassingly parallel, no robustness content.
2. **Imprint (intersection) phase, local not global.** For each candidate face pair, compute the surface-surface intersection. For linear/rational surface pairs (plane-plane, plane-cylinder, etc.) use exact or indirect predicates so the intersection geometry is exact. For NURBS-NURBS pairs, compute an approximate intersection curve and a rigorous error bound, and record that bound as the tolerance of the resulting tolerant edge. Imprint only along intersection curves; never globally re-tessellate (the EMBER "local arrangements" lesson).
3. **Classify by winding number.** Adopt the Zhou/Jacobson winding-number-vector model rather than neighborhood-walk classification. Compute, for each delimited region of the imprinted boundary, a winding number against each operand. This is more robust than ray-cast PMC because it degrades gracefully and does not require watertight intermediate geometry. Boolean operations (union, intersection, difference, and arbitrary set expressions) become predicates on the winding-number vector.
4. **Stitch with tolerance.** Assemble surviving faces into a valid tolerant B-rep. Stitching tolerantly covers the gaps left by approximate NURBS intersection curves, creating tolerant edges/vertices whose tolerance bounds the gap. Target epsilon-solidity (Qi/Shapiro) as the validity criterion rather than exact watertightness.

### ADOPT

- **Arrangement-then-classify with winding-number classification** (Zhou/Jacobson). The most robust and most composable classification strategy known; supports n-ary and arbitrary boolean expressions for free.
- **Per-entity local tolerances from day one** (Jackson, Parasolid/ACIS model). Tolerant edges and tolerant vertices with a stored tolerance value, plus tolerance propagation through every operation. This is not optional for NURBS booleans.
- **Exact / indirect predicates on the linear and rational sub-layer** (Attene, Shewchuk). Use them everywhere the geometry is linear or rational (combinatorial decisions, plane/line constructions, analytic-surface intersections). Rust crates such as `robust` (Shewchuk adaptive predicates) exist; budget for an indirect-predicate layer for constructed points.
- **Symbolic perturbation for degeneracy** (Simulation of Simplicity, or Manifold-style intuitive perturbation). Eliminate the special-case explosion in the arrangement/classification code.
- **Coincidence-first mindset** (Lalish). Treat exact coincidences (coplanar faces, shared edges, hole-exactly-fits-peg) as the common case to be handled by design, not as rare degeneracies bolted on later.
- **A fuzzy/global-tolerance mode** (OCCT Fuzzy mode). Let callers raise the working tolerance to bridge known-dirty input.

### AVOID

- **Pure exact-rational B-rep** (CGAL Nef style) as the primary representation. Robust but too slow and too memory-heavy for interactive modeling, and impossible for true NURBS intersection curves anyway.
- **Neighborhood-walk / ray-cast PMC as the sole classifier.** Fragile at on-boundary and tangential contacts; keep winding numbers as the primary classifier and use ray casting only as a corroborating signal.
- **Global re-tessellation or global arrangements** of the whole model for each boolean. Imprint locally along intersection curves only.
- **A single global epsilon.** Tolerances must be per-entity and must propagate; a single machine-epsilon comparison is the classic source of fragility Hoffmann warns about.
- **Deferring tolerant modeling to "version 2."** Retrofitting tolerances into a kernel that assumed exact intersections is a known dead end; the commercial kernels that tried it paid heavily.

### INVESTIGATE

- **Indirect predicates for analytic (non-NURBS) surface intersections.** How far can the exact-construction idea be pushed for plane/quadric/torus intersections before NURBS forces approximation? This determines how large the exact island can be inside the tolerant sea.
- **Epsilon-solidity as Keel's formal validity contract** (Qi/Shapiro). Can epsilon-solidity be made a checkable invariant that the boolean output is guaranteed to satisfy, giving Keel a defensible robustness claim that NURBS kernels usually lack?
- **Non-manifold / SGC-style intermediate representation** for the classification stage, so tangential and coincident contacts can be held faithfully before regularization rather than triggering failures.
- **Rigorous SSI error bounds.** The quality of the whole tolerant pipeline hinges on tight, certified bounds on the gap between the true NURBS intersection curve and its stored approximation. This is the highest-leverage research investment.
- **Interval / affine arithmetic for SSI predicates** as a tractable substitute for exactness on transcendental intersections, paired with epsilon-predicates (Guibas/Salesin/Stolfi) that report their own uncertainty.

### Position: tolerant-modeling machinery required on day one

Keel must ship, in its first topology and geometry layer, the following: a tolerance field on vertices and edges; a tolerant-edge geometry type whose curve is approximate and whose tolerance bounds the deviation from the true surface intersection; tolerance propagation rules through booleans; and a stitching stage that closes sub-tolerance gaps into tolerant entities. This is non-negotiable because the NURBS surface-surface intersection problem is mathematically inexact, and any kernel that assumes exact intersection curves will become unfixable. The exactness machinery (indirect/exact predicates, winding numbers, symbolic perturbation) is layered on top for everything that is genuinely linear or rational, where it pays for itself in robustness at near-zero performance cost.

## 5. Gaps and Open Problems

1. **No provably correct boolean exists for general NURBS-bounded solids.** The mesh community's exactness guarantees do not extend to transcendental intersection curves. Keel will be in the same boat as every commercial kernel: empirically robust, not provably robust, on full NURBS.
2. **Tolerance propagation under chained booleans is poorly characterized in the open literature.** Tolerances grow as operations compose, but there is no widely accepted theory bounding that growth or specifying when re-intersection/healing is required. Qi/Shapiro epsilon-solidity is the closest formal handle and is underexplored in practice.
3. **Indirect predicates stop at linear geometry.** Extending the exact-construction-without-rounding idea to even quadric or torus intersections, let alone NURBS, is open and possibly infeasible in exact rational arithmetic.
4. **Interactive robust NURBS booleans are unsolved.** The 2022 interactive results (Cherchi, EMBER, Manifold) are all mesh-based. Whether their architecture can be lifted onto tolerant NURBS B-rep while preserving interactivity is the central open engineering question for a kernel like Keel.
5. **Validation and a degeneracy test corpus.** The mesh world has Thingi10K as a robustness stress corpus; there is no comparable open NURBS-boolean stress suite. Building one (near-tangent, near-coincident, sliver, and self-intersecting NURBS cases) is prerequisite to any credible robustness claim and is itself a contribution Keel could make.
