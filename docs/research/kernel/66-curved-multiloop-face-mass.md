## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **A multiply-connected (holed) curved face integrates by the SAME boundary-flux loop the kernel already runs, with ZERO new integration theory: the per-face flux equals the line integral over the OUTER trim loop MINUS the line integral over each INNER (hole) trim loop, where "minus" is not a special rule but the automatic consequence of the standard B-rep loop-orientation convention (outer loop CCW about the face normal, every inner loop CW). Walk every loop with the region on the left and feed each (point, tangent, weight) to the same `node()` accumulator: the inner loops, being CW, contribute flux of the opposite sign, so the holes subtract themselves. The two fixes are therefore (i) a CYLINDER rectangle-witness, the exact dual of the landed cone/sphere witnesses, using the analytic full-band lateral area `2 pi r h` vs the tessellated face area to detect a region-mismatched partial-azimuth or holed band and route it off the iso-rectangle fast path; and (ii) iterate the boundary integrator over ALL of a face's loops (outer + inner), not just the outer one, trusting the stored CW orientation of inner loops to make the flux subtract. Once dossiers 64 (periodic split) and 65 (canonical seam identity) make the holed wall a PROPER multi-loop face with correctly oriented inner loops, the integrator reads V(A UNION B) and V(A - B) exactly, and the 2 percent mass==mesh gate, cross-checked against an INDEPENDENT inclusion-exclusion volume oracle, certifies the result. THAT independent oracle, not mass==mesh self-consistency, is what forbids a self-consistent WRONG and keeps DECLINE-never-WRONG intact through the guard removal.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. The divergence theorem reduces the volume of the solid to a sum of per-face surface-flux integrals; Green's theorem on the planar trim region reduces each per-face term to a line integral over that region's BOUNDARY (dossier 60). For a SIMPLY-connected face that boundary is one outer loop. For a MULTIPLY-connected (holed) face, Green's theorem still holds verbatim, but the boundary `partial D` now has several components: the outer curve traversed CCW and each hole curve traversed CW, oriented so the material is always on the left (verified, math references below). The line integral over the full boundary is the SUM over all loops, and because the inner loops run CW they carry the opposite sign, so `flux(face) = flux(outer loop) - sum_k flux(inner loop k)`. The kernel already stores inner loops in exactly this CW orientation (the universal B-rep convention, verified across OpenNURBS, ACIS, SMLib). So the entire fix on the inner-loop side is to STOP integrating only the outer loop and instead iterate every loop of the face through the existing `node()` accumulator: the subtraction is not coded, it EMERGES from orientation, exactly as it does for a planar holed face. The cylinder witness side is the literal transcription of the landed cone/sphere witness with the cone frustum area `pi (R + r) l` replaced by the cylinder band area `2 pi r h`. Nothing in the integrator's core changes.

## 1. The cylinder analog of the cone/sphere rectangle-witness

The cone and sphere already carry a rectangle-witness (massprops.rs ~877 and ~905): compute the analytic full-band lateral area, compare to the tessellated face area, and if they disagree the iso-rectangle is NOT the face, so route to the boundary integrator over the true loop. The cylinder was never given one. This section supplies it: the cylinder's full-band area is `2 pi r h`, exact and elementary, and the witness is the exact dual.

### 1.1 The cylinder lateral surface and its exact full-band area

> **Citation.** *Cylinder* and *Lateral surface*, Wikipedia (lateral area `2 pi r h`, volume `pi r^2 h`). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_surface . Pappus's centroid theorem (the area of a surface of revolution is `2 pi r_centroid * L`): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus%27s_centroid_theorem ; UF derivation: https://web.mae.ufl.edu/uhk/PAPPUS.pdf . *Surface of Revolution*, Wolfram MathWorld (cylinder / frustum / zone closed forms): https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SurfaceofRevolution.html .
> **Method / Content.** The right circular cylinder of radius `r`, axis `a`, height `h`, has the ruling parameterization `P(u, v) = C + r (cos u e1 + sin u e2) + v a`, with `u in [0, 2*pi)`, `v in [0, h]`. The surface element is `|P_u x P_v| = r` (constant, the cylinder has NO Jacobian singularity, unlike the cone apex or sphere pole, so it needs no anchor). Integrating the constant `r` over the full band gives the lateral area `integral_0^{2 pi} integral_0^h r dv du = 2 pi r h`, the Pappus result (centroid radius `r`, generator length `h`, distance travelled `2 pi r`). The enclosed-solid contribution of one cylinder-band face to the divergence-theorem volume is the band's signed flux; for a coaxial full band between `v0` and `v1`, that flux is the closed-form `pi r^2 (v1 - v0)` times the sense sign. This is the EXACT oracle, evaluable to 1e-9, far inside the 2 percent band.
> **Limitations.** `2 pi r h` is the FULL-azimuth band area. A partial-azimuth band (a cut that removed a wedge) has area `r * (u1 - u0) * h < 2 pi r h`; a HOLED band (the bore case) has area `2 pi r h - sum of bore areas`. Both are STRICTLY LESS than the full-band area, which is exactly why an over-read is detectable: the iso-rectangle path assumes the full `2 pi r h` and the true face is smaller.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the analytic number the cylinder witness compares against. The cylinder is the EASIEST of the three witnesses to land because its band area is a single product `2 pi r h` with no half-angle (cone) or zone-height (sphere) subtlety, and it has no Jacobian singularity, so there is no apex/pole anchor to compose with. **Verified** (`2 pi r h` is elementary; the constant Jacobian is standard).

### 1.2 The witness: analytic band area vs tessellated face area, and the route on disagreement

> **Citation.** Reconstruction from the landed cone/sphere rectangle-witnesses (massprops.rs ~877, ~905) plus the tessellation-area cross-check practice (dossier 63 adaptive curved tessellation; MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap, the chord-vs-arc area gap). Closed-form band areas: MathWorld *Surface of Revolution* (above).
> **Method / Content (claimed, the deliverable, dual of the cone/sphere witness).** Before taking the cylinder iso-rectangle fast path:
> 1. **Analytic full-band area `A_iso = 2 pi r h`** for the candidate iso-rectangle `(u: 0 to 2*pi) x (v: v0 to v1)`, `h = v1 - v0`. This is the area the fast path implicitly assumes.
> 2. **Tessellated face area `A_tess`** = sum of the face's own triangle areas (the mesh the `mass == mesh` gate already builds). This is the TRUE face area, independent of any pcurve, and it ALREADY accounts for both a partial-azimuth notch AND any inner hole loops, because the tessellation triangulates the actual trimmed region.
> 3. **Compare.** If `|A_iso - A_tess| / A_iso` is within the tessellation-coarseness tolerance (section 1.3), the iso-rectangle IS the face: take the fast path, `flux = pi r^2 (v1 - v0) * sense`. If they disagree (the band is notched or holed, so `A_tess < A_iso`), the iso-rectangle is NOT the face: route to the **boundary integrator over the true loop set** (the outer loop plus every inner loop, section 2).
> 4. **The cylinder witness is the exact dual of the landed cone/sphere witness**, with the cone frustum area `pi (R + r) l` or the sphere zone area `2 pi R h` replaced by `2 pi r h`. Same control flow, same routing, same DECLINE-never-WRONG posture.
> **Exact oracle.** The cyl/cyl probe wall: the kept lateral of `A` after `B` punches through is `2 pi r_A h_A` MINUS the two oval bore areas. The witness fires (`A_iso = 2 pi r_A h_A` exceeds `A_tess`), the boundary integrator walks the outer rim band plus the two inner oval loops, and the flux comes out V(A wall) minus the bore flux, the value the inclusion-exclusion oracle (section 3.3) predicts.
> **Limitations.** The tessellated area is itself chord-approximate (it under-reads a convex curved patch by the chord deficit), so the witness threshold must be set by the known chord error, not tighter, else a coarse tessellation triggers a false mismatch on a genuine full band (section 1.3). The witness DETECTS that the region is wrong; it does not by itself integrate the holed region, which is section 2.
> **Kernel relevance.** This closes the FIRST of the two cyl/cyl sub-defects: the cylinder, lacking a witness, would silently take the iso-rectangle path on a notched or holed band and over-read. With the witness it routes to the boundary integrator instead. **Verified** that area mismatch is a sound region witness (same argument as the landed cone/sphere case); the cylinder threshold and route are **claimed**, validated against the 17.09 / 12.566 probe.

### 1.3 Threshold vs cylinder tessellation coarseness

> **Citation.** Dossier 63 (adaptive curved tessellation, chord-tolerance to facet-area-error relation); MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap (do not mis-attribute a chord-vs-arc area gap to a geometry bug).
> **Method / Content (claimed).** A cylinder band tessellated to chord tolerance `eps_chord` is faceted into `N` azimuthal segments with `N ~ pi / sqrt(2 eps_chord / r)`; the inscribed-polygon lateral area under-reads the true `2 pi r h` by a relative deficit of order `(pi / N)^2 / 6 ~ eps_chord / (3 r)`, a known, bounded, sub-percent quantity for any reasonable faceting. Set the witness threshold to a few times that bound. A real bore or wedge removes a finite, percent-to-tens-of-percent fraction of the band (an oval bore from an interpenetrating cylinder of comparable radius removes a large, unmistakable area), FAR above the tessellation deficit. So the witness has a wide safe margin: it fires loudly on a real hole or notch and never on tessellation noise.
> **Limitations.** A sub-tessellation-scale sliver bore (a hole removing less area than the chord deficit) is below the witness floor; such a sliver is a dossier-64/65 sub-tolerance feature and is declined upstream (it never forms a proper inner loop), so the witness never needs to resolve it.
> **Kernel relevance.** Sets the cylinder witness tolerance honestly, tied to the faceting parameters, wide margin for real bores, sub-resolution slivers handled upstream by DECLINE. **Claimed**, tunable against dossier 63's faceting constants.

## 2. The inner-loop (hole) generalisation: outer-loop flux MINUS inner-loop flux

This is the core of the dossier and the second cyl/cyl sub-defect. A periodic lateral with K closed inner seam loops (bore holes) must integrate as the outer-loop flux MINUS each inner loop's flux. The key result is that this subtraction is NOT a special rule: it is the automatic consequence of the standard inner-loop orientation, so the fix is to iterate the existing accumulator over ALL loops.

### 2.1 Green's theorem on a multiply-connected region: the signed-boundary formula

> **Citation.** *Green's Theorem on General Regions*, Lumen Learning Calculus III. https://courses.lumenlearning.com/calculus3/chapter/greens-theorem-on-general-regions/ . *Green's theorem with multiple boundary components*, Math Insight (the slit / cancellation argument). https://mathinsight.org/greens_theorem_multiple_boundary_components . MIT 18.02SC multivariable notes (multiply-connected Green's theorem). https://www.ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/1bc33736db05b99f564ba15fe7174311_MIT18_02SC_MNotes_v5.pdf . *4.3 Green's Theorem*, Corral, Math LibreTexts. https://math.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Calculus/Vector_Calculus_(Corral)/04:_Line_and_Surface_Integrals/4.03:_Greens_Theorem .
> **Method / Content.** For a planar region `D` with an outer boundary and `K` holes, Green's theorem holds in its original form,
>
> ```
> integral_over_partial_D ( P dx + Q dy ) = double_integral_over_D ( Q_x - P_y ) dA
> ```
>
> where `partial D` is the FULL boundary: the outer curve `C_0` traversed CCW (positive orientation) plus every hole curve `C_1, ..., C_K` traversed CW (negative orientation), each oriented so the region `D` stays on the LEFT. Decomposed by loop, with all loops written CCW and the inner ones explicitly negated:
>
> ```
> double_integral_over_D ( Q_x - P_y ) dA
>   =  oint_{C_0, CCW} ( P dx + Q dy )  -  sum_{k=1..K} oint_{C_k, CCW} ( P dx + Q dy )
> ```
>
> The proof (Math Insight, MIT 18.02SC) cuts "slits" joining each hole boundary to the outer boundary, turning the holed region into a simply-connected one; the two sides of each slit are traversed in opposite directions and cancel, leaving exactly the outer loop CCW plus each inner loop CW. The minus sign on the inner loops is the difference between the hole's CW boundary traversal (the one that keeps `D` on the left) and the CCW traversal of the same closed curve.
> **Limitations.** This is the PLANAR statement, in the face's parameter `(u, v)` plane. The mass per-face integrand Keel evaluates is the Green-reduced form of the surface flux; the SAME signed-loop structure applies because the per-face reduction lands in the planar trim region (dossier 60). The formula assumes the loops are non-intersecting and properly nested (one outer, holes strictly inside, holes disjoint from each other), which is the dossier-64/65 precondition.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the exact theory for the inner-loop subtraction. The flux Keel needs for a holed face is `flux(C_0) - sum_k flux(C_k)`, and the minus is supplied by the inner loops' CW orientation. **Verified** (multiply-connected Green's theorem is textbook; the signed-loop decomposition is the standard slit argument).

### 2.2 The B-rep inner-loop orientation convention: why the flux subtracts automatically

> **Citation.** *B-rep Loop & Edge Directions*, McNeel OpenNURBS developer guide ("the active region of the face is to the left of the 2D curve", outer loops CCW, inner loops CW). https://developer.rhino3d.com/guides/opennurbs/brep-loop-edge-directions/ . SMLib topology manual (Loopuse orientation: outer CCW, inner CW about the face normal). https://docs.nvidia.com/smlib/manual/smlib/topology/index.html . ACIS topology chapter 6 (loop senses). Cross-confirmed in the B-rep loop-orientation web survey (outer loops CCW, inner loops CW, region on the left).
> **Method / Content.** Every production B-rep kernel stores face loops so the material is on the LEFT of the directed boundary in the face's parameter space: the outer loop runs CCW about the face normal, every inner (hole) loop runs CW. This is identical to the Green's-theorem orientation of section 2.1. Therefore, when the boundary-flux integrator walks each loop in its STORED direction and accumulates `node(point, tangent, weight)`, the inner loops, running CW, contribute `(point, tangent)` with the tangent reversed relative to a CCW traversal, which flips the sign of the line-integral contribution. The hole's flux SUBTRACTS itself with no special-casing: the integrator does not need to know which loop is outer and which is inner, it just walks them all in stored order.
> **Limitations.** This requires the inner loops to ACTUALLY be stored CW, which is the dossier-64 (periodic split) and dossier-65 (canonical seam identity) deliverable: the bore seam loops must be built as proper, correctly oriented inner loops of the wall face. If an inner loop is stored with the wrong sense (a dossier-65 orientation bug), the flux would ADD instead of subtract, an over-read; this is precisely the failure mode the mass==mesh gate plus the independent oracle (section 3) must catch. The convention is also parameter-space CCW/CW, which couples to the surface-normal sense (dossier 46); a periodic cylinder's seam slit must be handled so the `u`-wrap does not spuriously flip a loop's apparent winding.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is WHY the fix is just "iterate over all loops". The subtraction is structural, inherited from the universal loop-orientation convention Keel already follows. The integrator change is: replace "walk the outer loop" with "for each loop in face.loops: walk it through node()". **Verified** that outer-CCW / inner-CW is the universal convention; **claimed** that Keel's stored inner-loop sense matches it after dossiers 64/65 (to validate against the sense convention of dossier 46).

### 2.3 The multi-loop boundary-flux formula over a curved face, per surface type

> **Citation.** C. Gonzalez-Ochoa, S. McCammon, J. Peters, "Computing Moments of Objects Enclosed by Piecewise Polynomial Surfaces," *ACM TOG* 17(3):143-157, 1998 (divergence-to-boundary moment reduction; the trimmed-patch boundary is walked per loop). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2459122_Computing_Moments_of_Piecewise_Polynomial_Surfaces . S. Cattani, A. Paoluzzi, "Boundary integration over linear polyhedra," *Computer-Aided Design* 22(2):130-135, 1990, and "Symbolic analysis of linear polyhedra," *Engineering with Computers* 6:235-249, 1990 (signed boundary integration over oriented loops; the inner-loop sign emerges from edge orientation). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/001044859090007Y ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01200201 . J. Spainhour, K. Weiss, "Robust Containment Queries over Collections of Trimmed NURBS Surfaces via Generalized Winding Numbers," *ACM TOG*, 2025 (the boundary `partial S` "may contain multiple disconnected curves", iterated uniformly with orientation-dependent sign). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11435 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11435 .
> **Method / Content.** The per-face volume contribution for a curved face `S(u, v)` with vector field `F` (`div F = 1`) is `integral_over_trimregion F(S) . (S_u x S_v) du dv`. Green-reducing the inner `u`-slab to a boundary integral over the trim region `D` (which has outer loop `L_0` and inner loops `L_1..L_K`) gives the **multi-loop boundary-flux formula**:
>
> ```
> flux(face) = sum over all loops L_j of  oint_{L_j, stored orientation}  g(point(t), tangent(t)) dt
>            = flux(L_0)  -  sum_{k=1..K} flux(L_k)          [inner loops CW => negative]
> ```
>
> where `g(point, tangent)` is the slab/winding integrand the `node()` helper already evaluates, and the minus on the inner loops is the automatic consequence of their CW storage (section 2.2). Per surface type the integrand is the one `local_geometry(u, v)` already supplies:
> - **Cylinder** (`|P_u x P_v| = r`, constant): the slab integrand has the cylinder `local_geometry`; an inner oval bore loop is walked CW and subtracts its flux. The full-band fast path (`2 pi r h`, `pi r^2 h`) is the witness-gated special case (section 1).
> - **Cone** (`|P_u x P_v| = v cos alpha`, linear in `v`): same loop iteration; an inner loop on a cone lateral (a bore through a conical wall) subtracts via its CW orientation, composing with the apex anchor of dossier 60 if the outer region reaches the apex.
> - **Sphere** (`|P_u x P_v| = R^2 sin phi`): same; an inner loop on a spherical face (a bore through a spherical shell) subtracts via CW orientation, composing with the pole anchor of dossier 60 if the outer region reaches a pole.
> In all three the inner-loop handling is IDENTICAL: the same `node()` accumulator, the same generic boundary-arc arm (dossier 60 section 2) sampling each inner-loop arc's (point, tangent), the sign carried by orientation. Cattani-Paoluzzi is the canonical prior art that signed boundary integration over oriented loops yields the correct moment with holes handled purely by edge orientation; Spainhour-Weiss confirms the modern trimmed-NURBS form iterates all boundary curves uniformly with orientation-dependent sign.
> **Limitations.** The inner-loop arcs are the SSI seam curves (the bore rim where the other cylinder cut through), so they are integrated by the generic boundary-arc arm (dossier 60 section 2), which for a sharp rational conic uses adaptive GL8 panels and DECLINEs on the panel-depth cap. An inner loop whose arc cannot be certified to the band forces the whole face to DECLINE, preserving DECLINE-never-WRONG. Proper nesting (holes strictly inside the outer loop, holes mutually disjoint) is assumed; a degenerate nesting is an upstream dossier-64/65 DECLINE.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the second cyl/cyl fix in one formula: iterate the boundary integrator over ALL loops, inner loops subtract by orientation. The cylinder wall with two oval bores reads `flux(outer band) - flux(bore 1) - flux(bore 2)`, the correct under-count, instead of the outer-only over-count. **Verified** the multi-loop boundary-flux formula and the orientation-driven sign (Green / Cattani-Paoluzzi / Spainhour-Weiss); **claimed** the per-type wiring (iterate `face.loops` through the existing `node()`), to validate against the cyl/cyl probe and the existing curved-band regression.

## 3. Composition with dossiers 64/65, and the guard-removal soundness argument

Once the periodic split (dossier 64) and the canonical seam identity (dossier 65) make the holed wall a proper multi-loop face with correctly oriented inner loops, the mass integrator (sections 1 to 2) reads the right number and the mass==mesh gate certifies it. This section confirms the composition and, critically, states the INDEPENDENT oracle that prevents a self-consistent WRONG, so the guard removal is sound.

### 3.1 The composition: what 64/65 must deliver for the integrator to be correct

> **Citation.** Dossier 64 (periodic split: the seam slit and the `u`-wrap bookkeeping that lets a periodic cylinder carry a proper outer loop and inner loops); dossier 65 (canonical seam identity: the shared `EdgeId` and the oriented, deduplicated inner loop). Dossier 45 (genus-1 solid-of-revolution Euler sequence: the seam/slit and pole/apex topology bookkeeping). Dossier 46 (face orientation / sense convention: the per-face `orient = sense_sign * outer_sign` that supplies the flux sign).
> **Method / Content (claimed composition).** The integrator of sections 1 to 2 is correct ONLY if its inputs are correct:
> 1. **Dossier 64 (periodic split)** must produce a wall face whose OUTER loop is the closed lateral band (seam-slit handled so the `u`-range is a proper interval, not a spuriously open periodic edge) and whose INNER loops are the closed bore rims. Without this, the bores are not loops of the face and there is nothing to subtract.
> 2. **Dossier 65 (canonical seam identity)** must store each inner bore loop ONCE, oriented CW about the face normal (region on the left), with its arcs shared by `EdgeId` so the wall and the cap agree on the seam. A doubled or wrongly-oriented inner loop would make the flux ADD (over-read) or double-subtract (under-read).
> 3. **Dossier 46 (sense convention)** supplies the per-face sense sign multiplying the flux magnitude this dossier computes; the inner-loop CW orientation is RELATIVE to the face normal, so the sense and the loop orientation must be consistent (a flipped face normal flips both the outer and inner loop sense together, preserving the subtraction).
> With those three in place, the integrator iterates `face.loops`, the outer loop contributes the band flux, each inner loop subtracts its bore flux, and `flux(wall) = flux(band) - sum flux(bores)`.
> **Limitations.** Any defect in 64/65 (open periodic edge, doubled inner loop, wrong inner-loop sense) silently changes the integrated value; the integrator cannot detect it from the flux alone. This is exactly why the independent oracle of section 3.3 is mandatory: it is the cross-check that catches a 64/65 orientation defect that the integrator would otherwise propagate.
> **Kernel relevance.** Defines the precondition contract: the integrator is correct GIVEN 64/65 deliver proper, correctly-oriented multi-loop faces. The guard must NOT be removed until 64/65 land AND the independent oracle (3.3) confirms the composed result. **Claimed** composition, to validate end-to-end on the cyl/cyl probe.

### 3.2 The mass==mesh gate is necessary but not sufficient: the self-consistent-WRONG risk

> **Citation.** MEMORY fuzz-soak-completion-gate (validate() is necessary-not-sufficient; the THREE-BUCKET oracle PASS = strict mass==mesh==ref); MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap (a chord-approximated mesh can itself be biased). Dossier 37 (numerical substrate, the 2 percent curved band). Dossier 60 cross-cutting catalog (tessellation is oracle/witness, never the fallback answer).
> **Method / Content.** The 2 percent curved `mass == mesh` self-consistency gate compares the boundary-flux mass against the tessellated-mesh mass of the SAME face set. This catches a mass that disagrees with the mesh. But it is a SELF-consistency check: if BOTH the mass integrator and the tessellation make the SAME over-count (for instance, both integrate the outer loop without subtracting the bore, because the mesh was also built over the un-holed band), they AGREE on a wrong number and the gate passes. The op-volume-bound slack (a result is sanity-bounded by `V(A) + V(B)` for union, `V(A)` for difference) does NOT catch this either, because the over-read 17.09 = V(A)+V(B) and 12.566 = V(A) are EXACTLY at the bound, not beyond it. So mass==mesh plus the volume bound can let a self-consistent WRONG sit precisely at the slack edge. This is the specific danger the prompt flags and the reason the guard cannot be removed on the strength of mass==mesh alone.
> **Limitations.** In practice the tessellation of a properly holed face (dossier 63) DOES triangulate the actual trimmed region including the bores, so a correct mesh would NOT make the same over-count; the danger is the narrow window where the mesh is ALSO built over the un-holed region (a tessellation that ignores the inner loops). The defense is to ensure the tessellation and the integrator consume the SAME loop set (both see the inner loops) AND to add an independent oracle (3.3).
> **Kernel relevance.** Establishes that mass==mesh is necessary, not sufficient, for the guard removal. The sufficient condition is the independent oracle of 3.3. **Verified** that self-consistency gates cannot catch a co-directional bias (standard validation logic).

### 3.3 The independent oracle that forbids a self-consistent WRONG (the soundness deliverable)

> **Citation.** Inclusion-exclusion volume identity (`V(A UNION B) = V(A) + V(B) - V(A INTERSECT B)`; `V(A - B) = V(A) - V(A INTERSECT B)`), elementary measure theory. Pappus / closed-form quadric volumes (dossier 60 catalog; MathWorld *Surface of Revolution*). The cone/sphere rectangle-witness oracles already landed (massprops.rs ~877, ~905) as the template for an analytic cross-check independent of both mass and mesh.
> **Method / Content (claimed, the first-class deliverable).** The independent oracle is an ANALYTIC volume computed by a path that shares NO machinery with either the boundary-flux mass or the tessellated mesh, so a bug in one cannot bias the other into agreement:
> 1. **Inclusion-exclusion against the lens volume.** For the cyl/cyl probe, compute `V(A INTERSECT B)` (the shared lens) by an INDEPENDENT method, then `V(A UNION B) = V(A) + V(B) - V(lens)` and `V(A - B) = V(A) - V(lens)`. The two cylinder volumes `V(A) = pi r_A^2 h_A`, `V(B) = pi r_B^2 h_B` are exact closed forms. The lens volume of two interpenetrating right circular cylinders is the Steinmetz-solid integral (closed form for equal radii, a certified 1D quadrature for unequal radii), computed WITHOUT the boundary-flux integrator and WITHOUT the tessellation. If the boundary-flux mass equals this inclusion-exclusion value to the band, the holes were subtracted correctly; if it equals `V(A)+V(B)` or `V(A)`, the bore subtraction failed. The probe's 17.09 (vs the IE truth `< V(A)+V(B)`) and 12.566 (vs the IE truth `V(A) - V(lens) < V(A)`) are caught precisely because the IE oracle is BELOW the over-read.
> 2. **Per-face analytic band-minus-bore area cross-check.** The wall face's area must equal `2 pi r h` MINUS the closed-form bore areas (each oval bore is the intersection curve's enclosed area on the cylinder, a certified quadrature); compare to BOTH the integrated boundary length-consistent value and the tessellated area. Three-way agreement (analytic, mass-side, mesh-side) is the per-face certificate.
> 3. **Route on disagreement: DECLINE.** If the boundary-flux mass and the independent IE oracle disagree beyond the band, the face/op DECLINEs (it does not emit either number). This is the DECLINE-never-WRONG floor.
> **The guard-removal soundness argument, stated in two lines:** *The cyl/cyl decline guard may be removed ONLY when (a) the cylinder witness (section 1) plus the multi-loop inner-loop subtraction (section 2), fed by correctly-oriented inner loops from dossiers 64/65, make the boundary-flux mass equal the INDEPENDENT inclusion-exclusion oracle `V(A)+V(B)-V(lens)` (union) and `V(A)-V(lens)` (difference) to within the 2 percent band, AND (b) the tessellated mesh, built over the SAME multi-loop region, also equals that oracle. Because the independent oracle shares no code with either the mass integrator or the tessellator, a self-consistent WRONG (both over-counting in lockstep at the volume-bound edge) cannot satisfy it, so passing all three certifies the result and DECLINE-never-WRONG is preserved; any face whose three numbers do not agree DECLINEs rather than emit a wrong mass.*
> **Limitations.** The lens volume `V(A INTERSECT B)` is itself a computed quantity; for unequal-radius cylinders it is a certified 1D quadrature, not a closed form, so the oracle carries its own achieved-tolerance, which must be tighter than the 2 percent band (it is: a smooth 1D Steinmetz-type integral converges to machine precision with modest GL order). For the equal-radius case it is the exact Steinmetz closed form `(16/3) r^3` (bicylinder), an exact 1e-9 oracle.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the load-bearing soundness deliverable. The independent IE/Steinmetz oracle is what makes the guard removal SOUND: it is the third, code-independent number that mass==mesh self-consistency cannot fake. **Verified** the inclusion-exclusion identity and the Steinmetz closed form; **claimed** the three-way certificate and the DECLINE-on-disagreement route, to validate against the 17.09 / 12.566 probe before the guard is removed.

## Synthesis: ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE

**ADOPT.**
1. **The cylinder rectangle-witness** (section 1) as the exact dual of the landed cone/sphere witnesses: analytic full-band area `2 pi r h` vs tessellated face area, threshold tied to tessellation coarseness (dossier 63), and on mismatch route off the iso-rectangle fast path to the boundary integrator over the true loop set. The cylinder is the easiest witness (single product, no Jacobian singularity, no anchor).
2. **The multi-loop inner-loop subtraction** (section 2): iterate the boundary integrator over ALL of a face's loops (outer + inner), not just the outer, feeding each loop's (point, tangent, weight) to the existing `node()` accumulator. The inner loops, stored CW per the universal B-rep convention, subtract their flux automatically. The formula is `flux(face) = flux(outer loop) - sum_k flux(inner loop k)`, the multiply-connected Green's theorem, with the minus supplied by orientation, not by special-casing.
3. **The independent inclusion-exclusion / Steinmetz oracle** (section 3.3) as the guard-removal soundness condition: a third, code-independent volume (`V(A)+V(B)-V(lens)` for union, `V(A)-V(lens)` for difference, lens by the certified Steinmetz integral) that mass==mesh self-consistency cannot fake. Remove the cyl/cyl guard only when mass, mesh, and this oracle all agree to the 2 percent band; DECLINE on any disagreement.
4. **The exact closed forms (cylinder band `2 pi r h` / `pi r^2 h`, Steinmetz bicylinder `(16/3) r^3`) as the per-fix oracle**, with the tessellation as the mass==mesh cross-check and the witness input, never as the fallback answer.

**AVOID.**
- **Integrating only the outer loop of a holed face.** That is the cyl/cyl over-read (17.09 / 12.566, bores not subtracted). Always iterate every loop.
- **Special-casing the inner-loop sign in code.** The subtraction must come from the stored CW orientation, not from a hand-coded sign, so it stays consistent with the tessellator and the sense convention (dossier 46); a hand-coded sign would diverge from the loop orientation on a flipped-normal face.
- **Letting the cylinder iso-rectangle fast path run without the witness.** That is the region-mismatch silent over-count, the dual of the pre-fix cone bug.
- **Removing the cyl/cyl guard on the strength of mass==mesh alone.** Self-consistency cannot catch a co-directional over-count at the volume-bound edge; require the independent inclusion-exclusion oracle first.
- **Using the tessellated holed-face area/volume as the mass answer** when the boundary-flux certificate or the IE oracle misses the band. Tessellation is chord-approximate (MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap); DECLINE instead.

**INVESTIGATE.**
- Whether the cylinder, cone, and sphere witnesses can share ONE "analytic-band-area vs tessellated-area" abstraction parameterized by `local_geometry`, reducing duplication across massprops.rs ~877 / ~905 / the new cylinder site.
- Whether the inner-loop iteration composes cleanly with the apex/pole anchors (a bore through a cone or sphere wall whose outer region also reaches the apex/pole), validated against a holed-cone and holed-sphere probe.
- The unequal-radius Steinmetz lens quadrature: the right GL order and achieved-tolerance so the independent oracle is always tighter than the 2 percent band without excess work.
- Whether a doubled or wrong-sense inner loop from a dossier-65 defect is reliably caught by the three-way (analytic / mass / mesh) certificate, or whether an additional inner-loop-orientation assertion is warranted at face-build time.

**Recommended FIRST MILESTONE (narrowest honest slice with an exact oracle):**
**The cylinder full-band rectangle-witness for a partial-azimuth cylinder band, validated against the exact band area `2 pi r h` and the partial-band volume `pi r^2 (v1 - v0) * (u1 - u0) / (2 pi)`.** This is the smallest, highest-certainty fix: it is the exact structural dual of the already-working cone/sphere witness, it has a closed-form 1e-9 oracle, it has no Jacobian singularity or anchor to compose with, and it lands the cylinder's missing region check (the first cyl/cyl sub-defect) before the harder multi-loop work. It proves the cylinder routing off the iso-rectangle path on a notched band, the precondition for the holed-band case.

**Follow-up ladder:**
1. The cylinder rectangle-witness for a partial-azimuth band (the first milestone), exact `2 pi r h` / partial-volume oracle.
2. The multi-loop inner-loop subtraction (section 2): iterate the boundary integrator over all loops, inner loops subtract by CW orientation, validated on a single-bore cylinder wall against `2 pi r h - bore area` and `pi r^2 h - bore flux`.
3. The two-bore cyl/cyl probe: the wall with two oval bores, validated against the inclusion-exclusion oracle (3.3), confirming `V(A - B) = V(A) - V(lens)` and `V(A UNION B) = V(A)+V(B)-V(lens)`.
4. The independent Steinmetz oracle wired as the guard-removal gate (3.3): three-way agreement (analytic / mass / mesh), DECLINE on disagreement.
5. Remove the cyl/cyl decline guard, with the three-way gate active, and confirm WRONG=0 on the cyl/cyl soak corpus.
6. Compose inner-loop subtraction with the apex/pole anchors (holed cone, holed sphere), validated against holed-quadric probes.

**Configuration to DECLINE forever (justified):**
**A holed curved face whose inner-loop arc cannot be certified to the 2 percent band after the adaptive-panel cap, OR whose multi-loop result disagrees with the independent inclusion-exclusion oracle beyond the band.** Justification: a bore rim that is a sharp rational conic approaching its asymptote, or a face whose three-way certificate (analytic / mass / mesh) does not agree, cannot be integrated to the band with bounded work or cannot be certified independent of self-consistency; emitting the under-resolved or unverified number would risk a WRONG mass inside the gate. Declining the face (and the boolean) is honest and rare (real CAD bores are well inside the asymptote, real holed faces certify three-way). It moves from permanent-decline to staged-recovery only if a closed-form bore boundary integral removes the quadrature uncertainty or the seam fit is re-certified tighter. Also DECLINE-forever (upstream, not the integrator's job): a sub-tessellation-scale sliver bore and a degenerate inner-loop nesting, both declined before the integrator runs (dossiers 64/65).
