# 61. A provable disjointness certificate for the disjoint-union combine path: a sound non-intersection proof for analytic and NURBS B-rep solids

## Title and scope

This dossier is the implementation-grade literature foundation for a **provably sound disjointness certificate**: a decision procedure that, given two B-rep solids whose AABBs overlap, returns either "PROVEN DISJOINT" (their solid point sets do not touch, with a rigorous separation proof) or "UNKNOWN" (fall through to the full boolean). The single non-negotiable property is **soundness in one direction**: the certificate must NEVER report a gap that is not there. A false "disjoint" silently drops a real intersection and produces a wrong body, which violates Keel's DECLINE-never-WRONG contract at the worst possible place (a missed overlap is invisible to every downstream check). A false "unknown" is merely a missed optimization: the pair takes the slow path, which is always correct.

The motivation is concrete. LOG Add.250 added `combine_disjoint`: when two operands' AABBs are apart by a margin, the union skips imprint and SSI and imports both operands verbatim, correct by construction. That fires only on AABB-separated pairs. The large and currently mishandled class is **AABB-overlapping but solid-disjoint**: two bodies whose boxes interpenetrate but whose actual surfaces never meet (a bolt beside a bracket, two interlocking but non-touching brackets, a ring around a rod it does not contact). Today those hit the overlapping path, which is broken for curved operands (dossier 58 documents the missing analytic SSI rungs), so the boolean either keeps both operands whole by luck or, worse, the tilted-cylinder / cylinder class shows SSI can MISS a real intersection and emit a wrong body. Extending `combine_disjoint` to the AABB-overlap case is the prize, but it is **unsafe without a non-intersection PROOF**, because none of Keel's existing in-band checks catch a missed intersection:

- The op-volume bound does not catch it: two overlapping closed solids each integrate to their full `v_a` and `v_b`, and the union's upper bound is `v_a + v_b`, so a double-counted overlap reads as in-band.
- `mass == mesh` does not catch it: a verbatim disjoint-union tessellation produces `v_a + v_b` for both mass and mesh, identical and self-consistent, whether or not the truth is an overlap.
- Keel's `min_distance` is tessellation-based and only vertex-to-triangle, so it misses edge-edge and interior minima and is **not a sound lower bound**.
- `clashes()` relies on SSI completeness, which is exactly what is broken for curved pairs.

So the disjointness certificate must come from a **separate, sound lower-bound machine** that does not depend on SSI completeness, the volume oracle, or the tessellation. This dossier maps that machine: exact closed-form separation for the natural-quadric pairs that admit it, certified interval / affine lower bounds for NURBS via the convex-hull and exclusion-test substrate (dossiers 11 and 37), the conditions under which an empty SSI is itself a proof, and the composition with the tolerant-f64 model so a genuine sub-tolerance touch is never certified as a gap.

The dossier does **not** re-derive the exact-predicate cascade (dossier 11), the numerical substrate of interval and affine arithmetic (dossier 37), the SSI seam-extraction rungs (dossier 58), the Minkowski / medial machinery (dossier 10), the model-diff distance metrics (dossier 22), or the verification-oracle discipline (dossier 35). It treats those as clients and suppliers and builds the certificate on top.

Structure mirrors files 01 to 60: annotated source blocks (Citation / Method / Limitations / Kernel relevance), explicit verified-vs-claimed labels, patent flags, and a closing synthesis with ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE, a first milestone with an exact oracle, the follow-up ladder, and a DECLINE-forever set.

A note on the asymmetry, used throughout. A disjointness certificate is a **one-sided** decision. We need a rigorous **lower bound** `d_low` on the true minimum distance `d*` between the two solids, with the guarantee `d_low <= d*`. If `d_low > tol` (the tolerance envelope, section 3), the solids provably do not touch. We never need the converse (an upper bound or the true distance); failing to prove disjointness just means "fall through." This asymmetry is what makes the certificate cheap: every bounding step is allowed to be conservative in the safe direction, and conservatism only costs missed optimizations, never correctness.

## 1. The soundness contract and why a lower bound is the right object

### 1.1 Exact geometric computation as the contract, not the implementation

> **Citation.** Yap, C. K. (1997). Towards exact geometric computation. *Computational Geometry: Theory and Applications* 7(1-2), 3-23. Sharma, V., & Yap, C. K. (2017). Robust geometric computation. In *Handbook of Discrete and Computational Geometry* (3rd ed.), Ch. 45. CRC Press.
> **Method / Content.** EGC frames every robustness-critical decision as the **sign of an algebraic expression**, certified by a filter with an exact fallback. The disjointness certificate is exactly one such decision: the sign of `d* - tol`, or more precisely the sign of `d_low - tol` where `d_low` is a certified lower bound. The key EGC move that applies here: we do NOT need to compute `d*` (the true minimum distance, an algebraic number of high degree for curved faces, see dossier 11). We only need a **provable one-sided bound** whose sign relative to `tol` is decided in finite precision. This is strictly easier than the seam-extraction problem of dossier 58: a lower bound is a relaxation, and relaxations are cheap and sound by construction.
> **Verified vs claimed.** Verified: EGC is a correctness paradigm; the reduction of "do they touch" to a sign decision is standard. Claimed (Keel-specific): that a *conservative* lower bound, not an exact distance, suffices for the combine-disjoint extension, because the only consumer is the binary gate `d_low > tol`.
> **Limitations.** EGC says nothing about which bound to use; it only fixes the contract that the bound must be sound (never over-report `d_low`).
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the north star: the certificate is a **sound sign decision** `d_low - tol > 0`, computed by a cascade of relaxations (exact closed form where available, certified interval / affine where not), and it inherits Keel's existing predicate-cascade discipline (dossier 11). A certificate that returns UNKNOWN is always acceptable; a certificate that returns DISJOINT must be backed by `d_low`.

### 1.2 Why the existing min_distance is unsound and what "sound lower bound" means

A sound lower bound `d_low` on the distance between two solids `A` and `B` must satisfy `d_low <= min over (p in A, q in B) of |p - q|` for the **continuous** point sets, not the sampled mesh. Keel's tessellation vertex-to-triangle `min_distance` violates this in two ways, both of which can over-report (claim a gap that is not there):

1. **It samples the boundary.** The true minimum between two curved faces can occur at an interior parameter pair where neither face has a tessellation vertex; the chord-vs-arc gap (dossier 22, the tessellation-tolerance trap) means a tessellated edge sits *inside* the true convex surface, so a vertex-to-triangle distance can read positive while the smooth surfaces already touch.
2. **It misses edge-edge minima.** The global minimum distance between two non-convex bodies is frequently realized by a closest pair on two *edges* (skew edge-edge), not a vertex-face pair. A pure vertex-to-triangle scan can skip the closest configuration entirely and return a larger, wrong distance.

Either failure makes the tessellation distance an **upper-ish estimate of unknown sidedness**, useless as a disjointness proof. The certificate must instead bound the **continuous** geometry: analytic faces by their exact surfaces, NURBS faces by their control nets (section 2.2), and it must enumerate **all feature-pair types** (face-face, edge-edge, vertex-face, edge-face) so no minimum is skipped. The edge-edge and interior coverage is the load-bearing correction relative to today's code.

## 2. The lower-bound machines: a three-bucket distance certificate

The certificate is a hierarchy. Body-level AABB / OBB / BVH culls cheaply (and soundly) to a small set of feature pairs; each surviving feature pair is bounded by the tightest available sound machine for its type. The face-pair classification mirrors the SSI dispatch of dossier 58 but inverts the goal: dossier 58 wants the seam, this wants a proof there is none.

### 2.1 Convex broad phase: separating axis and GJK as sound, cheap culls

> **Citation.** Gilbert, E. G., Johnson, D. W., & Keerthi, S. S. (1988). A fast procedure for computing the distance between complex objects in three-dimensional space. *IEEE J. Robotics and Automation* 4(2), 193-203. Van den Bergen, G. (1999). A fast and robust GJK implementation for collision detection of convex objects. *J. Graphics Tools* 4(2), 7-25. http://www.dtecta.com/papers/jgt98convex.pdf . Separating Axis Theorem exposition: https://dyn4j.org/2010/01/sat/ .
> **Method / Content.** The **separating axis theorem** (verified): two convex sets are disjoint iff there exists an axis on which their projections do not overlap. Finding any one separating axis is a **sound disjointness certificate** for convex bodies, because the projection gap is a rigorous lower bound on the true gap (the axis-projected distance is `<= d*`, and if it is positive the bodies are apart). For polytopes the candidate axes are the face normals and edge-edge cross products; for the natural quadrics the axes include the surface axes. **GJK** computes the minimum distance between two convex sets by searching the Minkowski difference for the simplex nearest the origin; for non-convex bodies, GJK on the convex hulls returns the **hull-to-hull distance, which is a sound lower bound** on the true distance (the hulls contain the bodies, so the hull gap underestimates the body gap). GJK maintains a **monotone lower bound** during iteration (the support-plane bound `v . (support_A - support_B) / |v|`), so it can be stopped early the moment its lower bound exceeds `tol` and still emit a sound DISJOINT.
> **Verified vs claimed.** Verified: SAT separation is iff for convex sets; GJK hull distance lower-bounds the body distance; the GJK lower bound is monotone (van den Bergen). Claimed (Keel-specific): that hull-to-hull GJK is the right broad-phase cull for Keel's mostly-convex-per-face operands; needs the convex-hull builder to be exact-enough that the hull still contains the body (use control points / surface extents, not tessellation, section 3).
> **Limitations.** GJK is a *broad-phase* lower bound only: the hull gap can be far smaller than the true gap for non-convex bodies (two interlocking C-shapes have touching hulls but a large true gap), so a failed GJK proof means "go to narrow phase," not "they touch." GJK on curved surfaces needs support functions for the quadrics (cheap: the support of a sphere, cylinder, cone is closed form) and a conservative support for NURBS (the control-point convex hull support). Penetration depth from GJK is not needed and not used.
> **Patent.** GJK (1988) and SAT are decades old, textbook, no live patent. A 1990s polyhedral-collision patent family (e.g. EP0899692A2, and a Havok-acquired family) covered *specific acceleration structures*; the base GJK/SAT algorithms are unencumbered. Use the public van den Bergen / Bullet `btGjkPairDetector` lineage (zlib) or Parry's `sat`/`gjk` (Apache/MIT) as design references, not the patented acceleration wrappers. Post-2006 differentiable-collision and learned-collision patents exist; avoid those claim-reading methods entirely (they are not needed for a sound bound).
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the **broad phase of the certificate**. Per operand, build a per-face convex support (closed form for the quadrics, control-hull for NURBS). Run GJK / SAT on hull pairs surviving the BVH. A positive separating-axis gap `> tol` on the whole-body hulls (rare but cheap) proves disjointness outright. More commonly it culls the feature-pair set down to the few that are close, which then go to the exact / interval narrow phase below. The monotone GJK lower bound is the cheapest sound `d_low` Keel can compute and should be the first gate after the AABB margin.

### 2.2 NURBS narrow phase: the convex-hull property and Bezier-clipping subdivision as the sound interval bound

> **Citation.** Piegl, L., & Tiller, W. (1997). *The NURBS Book* (2nd ed.), strong convex-hull property. Sederberg, T. W., & Nishita, T. (1990). Curve intersection using Bezier clipping. *Computer-Aided Design* 22(9), 538-549. Sederberg, T. W. (1989). *A comparison of three curve intersection algorithms* (NASA TR). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19850020280 . Chen, X.-D., Yong, J.-H., Wang, G., Paul, J.-C., & Xu, G. (2008). Computing the minimum distance between a point and a NURBS curve. *Computer-Aided Design* 40(10-11), 1051-1054. http://www-sop.inria.fr/members/Gang.Xu/English/paper/CAD08_miniDistance.pdf .
> **Method / Content.** The **strong convex-hull property** (verified): a NURBS surface patch lies entirely within the convex hull of the control points whose basis functions are nonzero on that patch; over a knot span the curve point lies in the hull of the local `p+1` control points. This is the sound bound generator: the **distance between two control-point convex hulls is a rigorous lower bound** on the distance between the two NURBS patches, because each patch is contained in its hull. The bound is refined by **subdivision** (knot insertion / Bezier clipping): split each patch, recompute the now-tighter local hulls, and the hull-to-hull distance increases monotonically toward the true distance under refinement (the hull shrinks to the curve quadratically in the second difference of control points, verified Hausdorff bound). The disjointness loop: maintain a priority queue of patch-pairs ordered by hull-to-hull lower bound; pop the pair with the smallest lower bound; if that smallest lower bound already exceeds `tol`, **every** pair exceeds it and the patches are PROVEN DISJOINT; otherwise subdivide the popped pair and re-insert. **Chen et al.'s circular clipping** is the point-to-curve specialization: eliminate curve parts outside an elimination circle centered at the test point, the radius shrinks during subdivision, terminating in very few steps; it gives a certified point-to-NURBS bound usable for the vertex-face feature pair.
> **Verified vs claimed.** Verified: convex-hull containment, monotone refinement, the quadratic hull-shrink Hausdorff bound, circular clipping termination. Claimed (Keel-specific): that the patch-pair priority-queue terminates promptly for the AABB-overlap-but-disjoint class (it does when the true gap is comfortably above `tol`; near-tangent pairs do not terminate and must fall through, section 4).
> **Limitations.** The bound is only as sound as the control hull's containment, which holds exactly for the *mathematical* NURBS; rational weights must be handled (the rational patch lies in the hull of the *projective* control points, so use the homogeneous hull or the tighter rational-hull of Hu/Wang). Subdivision can be slow to terminate near tangency (the hulls overlap until very deep refinement), so the loop needs a **depth budget**: exceed it and return UNKNOWN (fall through), never DISJOINT.
> **Patent.** Bezier clipping (Sederberg-Nishita 1990) and the convex-hull property are old and unencumbered. Two 1990s parametric-evaluation patents surfaced in search (US5278948, US5317682) but cover hardware evaluation pipelines, not the convex-hull distance bound; avoid reading their claims, the bound itself predates and is independent.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is **the** sound NURBS lower-bound machine and the substrate the certificate stands on. It reuses dossier 37's interval / affine substrate and the bivariate-Bernstein control-net already in `ssi.rs`. Critically, it is **independent of SSI completeness**: it proves a gap by hull separation, never by finding-no-seam, so it sidesteps the tilted-cylinder missed-intersection failure mode (section 4). The same machinery answers all four feature-pair types: patch-patch (face-face), control-polygon edge to patch (edge-face), and point-to-patch via circular clipping (vertex-face).

### 2.3 Tighter interval enclosures: affine arithmetic and the exclusion-test family

> **Citation.** Comba, J. L. D., & Stolfi, J. (1993). Affine arithmetic and its applications to computer graphics. *Proc. SIBGRAPI*, 9-18. de Figueiredo, L. H., & Stolfi, J. (2004). Affine arithmetic: concepts and applications. *Numerical Algorithms* 37, 147-158. https://doi.org/10.1023/B:NUMA.0000049462.70970.b6 . Snyder, J. M. (1992). Interval analysis for computer graphics. *Computer Graphics (SIGGRAPH)* 26(2), 121-130. https://ohiostate.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/45/2017/09/interval-snyder.pdf .
> **Method / Content.** **Interval arithmetic** evaluates a function `f` over a box to produce an enclosure `[f_low, f_high]` that provably contains all values of `f` on the box (verified: the fundamental theorem of interval arithmetic). For the squared-distance function `g(u, v) = |S_A(u) - S_B(v)|^2` over the parameter box, an interval enclosure of `g` gives `g_low`, and `sqrt(g_low)` is a **sound lower bound** on the distance over that box. **Affine arithmetic** (Comba-Stolfi) improves interval arithmetic by tracking first-order correlations between sub-expressions via shared noise symbols, so the enclosures are dramatically tighter (no dependency-problem blow-up), which means the subdivision terminates far shallower and the certificate is cheaper for the same soundness. **Snyder's SOLVE / MINIMIZE** is the canonical interval-subdivision framework: an inclusion function plus a subdivision rule plus a pruning test (discard any box whose enclosure cannot contain the minimum). For disjointness, Snyder's MINIMIZE is run as a **branch-and-prune on a lower bound**: prune any box whose `g_low` already exceeds `tol^2` (it cannot witness a touch), and if every box is pruned, PROVEN DISJOINT.
> **Verified vs claimed.** Verified: interval and affine enclosures are sound; affine is tighter; Snyder's framework is a sound branch-and-prune. Claimed (Keel-specific): that affine arithmetic over the analytic surface formulas (sphere, cylinder, cone, torus) and the Bernstein NURBS gives a practical, shallow certificate; the tightness gain over plain intervals is well documented for these low-degree forms.
> **Limitations.** Affine arithmetic costs more per box (it carries noise terms) and needs careful handling of non-affine operations (multiplication, sqrt) where the linearization residual must be bounded soundly; the standard Comba-Stolfi rounding-safe implementation handles this. As with all subdivision, near-tangent configurations do not terminate; the depth budget plus UNKNOWN fallback is mandatory.
> **Kernel relevance.** Affine arithmetic is the **tightening layer** over section 2.2: where the raw control-hull distance is too loose to clear `tol` quickly, the affine enclosure of the squared-distance function over the parameter box clears it in fewer subdivisions. This is the same `keel-math` affine substrate dossier 37 specifies; the certificate is one of its first real consumers. Snyder's branch-and-prune is the loop skeleton.

### 2.4 Exclusion certificates: Krawczyk / interval Newton and Bernstein no-root tests

> **Citation.** Krawczyk, R. (1969). Newton-Algorithmen zur Bestimmung von Nullstellen mit Fehlerschranken. *Computing* 4, 187-201. Rump, S. M. (1983 / INTLAB). `verifynlss`. Kearfott, R. B. (1996). *Rigorous Global Search: Continuous Problems*. Kluwer. Sederberg, T. W. (1989), Bezier clipping as an intelligent interval-Newton (above). Mourrain, B., & Pavone, J.-P. (2009). Subdivision methods for solving polynomial equations. *J. Symbolic Computation* 44(3), 292-306.
> **Method / Content.** The disjointness question can be posed as a **root-exclusion** question: the two surfaces touch within `tol` iff the system `S_A(u) = S_B(v)` (or the relaxed `|S_A(u) - S_B(v)|^2 <= tol^2`) has a solution in the parameter domain. Proving NO solution is a sound disjointness certificate. Two certified exclusion tests apply. (1) The **Krawczyk operator** `K(X) = x0 - Y f(x0) + (I - Y f'(X))(X - x0)`: if `K(X) ∩ X = ∅`, then **`X` provably contains no root** of `f` (verified, Moore / Rump). Run over the parameter box, this excludes touching. (2) The **Bernstein / Bezier-clipping exclusion test**: a polynomial in Bernstein form has no root in a box if all its control coefficients share one sign (the convex-hull / variation-diminishing property), which is exactly the control-hull test of section 2.2 in algebraic dress; Bezier clipping behaves as an "intelligent interval-Newton" (Sederberg) that geometrically prunes root-free regions. The subdivision scheme of Mourrain-Pavone needs only the **exclusion** side for disjointness (it does not need the inclusion / one-root side at all), which is the cheap half.
> **Verified vs claimed.** Verified: `K(X) ∩ X = ∅` implies no root (the Krawczyk exclusion theorem); same-sign Bernstein coefficients imply no root. Claimed (Keel-specific): that for the natural-quadric and low-degree-NURBS systems the exclusion test clears the AABB-overlap-disjoint class without deep subdivision; supported by the certified-surface-approximation literature using the interval Krawczyk test (the 2026 arXiv certified-surface-approximation work uses exactly this operator).
> **Limitations.** Krawczyk needs an interval enclosure of the Jacobian `f'(X)` (cheap for quadrics, available from the Bernstein derivative net for NURBS) and a preconditioner `Y` (the inverse of the midpoint Jacobian); near a tangency the Jacobian is singular and the exclusion test weakens, so tangent pairs fall through. Exclusion proves "no transverse root in this box," but a **tangential touch** (the surfaces graze) is a degenerate root the exclusion test may not cleanly exclude; this is the same boundary as section 4 and must default to UNKNOWN.
> **Patent.** Krawczyk (1969), interval Newton, Bernstein exclusion: all old, unencumbered. No claim-reading risk.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the **algebraic alternative to the geometric hull bound**, and it is the bridge to section 4 (empty-SSI as proof). For Keel, the Bernstein exclusion test is essentially free: it is the same control-net sign test already implicit in the `ssi.rs` Bernstein tracer, run as a no-root certificate instead of a root-finder. Adopting it as a disjointness gate reuses existing code with inverted intent.

## 3. Exact closed-form separation for the natural quadrics (bucket a)

The cheapest sound bound is an exact one. For the natural-quadric pairs whose **infinite** surfaces have a closed-form minimum distance, Keel can certify disjointness with a single algebraic expression, no subdivision. The catch, central to soundness, is **finiteness**: Keel's faces are *trimmed* (a finite cylinder, a capped cone, a spherical cap), so an infinite-surface distance is only a valid certificate when combined with the trim, and the literature is explicit that the *finite* quadric distance often has **no** closed form.

> **Citation.** Eberly, D. *Intersection of Cylinders*, *Distance Between Two Line Segments*, *Distance Between Line Segments and a Cylinder*, Geometric Tools (public domain / Boost). https://geometrictools.com/Documentation/IntersectionOfCylinders.pdf . Lumelsky, V. J. (1985). On fast computation of distance between line segments. *Information Processing Letters* 21, 55-61. Miller, J. R., & Goldman, R. N. (1995). Geometric algorithms for detecting and calculating all conic sections in the intersection of any two natural quadric surfaces. *Graphical Models and Image Processing* 57(1).
> **Method / Content.** Exact, closed-form, sound separation distances that exist:
> - **Sphere-sphere**: `d* = |C_A - C_B| - r_A - r_B`. Exact, one expression. Disjoint iff `d* > tol`. This is the model case.
> - **Sphere-anything-with-a-closed-form-point-distance**: `d* = (point-to-surface distance from the sphere center) - r_sphere`. Point-to-plane (exact), point-to-infinite-cylinder (`|radial| - r_c`, exact), point-to-infinite-cone (closed form via the apex-angle projection, Eberly), point-to-sphere (exact). So sphere-vs-{plane, infinite cylinder, infinite cone} all have an exact sphere-center reduction.
> - **Infinite-cylinder vs infinite-cylinder**: the distance between the two **axes** is `|Δ - (W0 . Δ) W0|` for parallel axes, or the skew-line distance `|Δ . (W0 × W1)| / |W0 × W1|` for non-parallel axes (verified, Eberly); subtract `r_A + r_B`. Exact for the *infinite* cylinders.
> - **Plane-vs-anything**: signed distance from the surface's extremal point to the plane; exact for all quadrics (the extremal point in the plane-normal direction is closed form).
> - **Segment-segment and segment-to-infinite-cylinder**: exact (Lumelsky; Eberly), which bounds **edge-edge** feature pairs for linear and cylindrical edges.
>
> Exact closed forms that do **NOT** exist (verified, must fall to bucket b/c): the minimum distance between two **finite** (truncated) cylinders, between a finite cone and a finite cylinder, between two tori, and in general any pair where the closest points land on the *trim boundaries* (the cap circles, the apex truncation). The search-confirmed statement: "for general cylinder distance problems, a closed-form solution does not exist." The finite case reduces to a constrained minimization whose stationarity conditions are a polynomial system, which is bucket b (certified interval).
> **Verified vs claimed.** Verified: all the listed closed forms (standard, Eberly, Lumelsky); the non-existence of a general finite-cylinder closed form. Claimed (Keel-specific): that combining the infinite-surface closed form with a **sound trim test** (is the closed-form closest point actually inside the trimmed face? if not, the true closest point is on the trim boundary and the infinite-surface distance is a valid *lower bound* anyway, because the trimmed face is a subset of the infinite surface) yields a sound certificate even for finite faces. This subset argument is the key: **the infinite-surface distance always lower-bounds the finite-face distance**, because the finite face is contained in the infinite surface, so an infinite-surface gap `> tol` is a sound proof for the finite faces too. This makes the closed forms usable as conservative lower bounds without the finite closed form.
> **Limitations.** The infinite-surface lower bound can be loose (two short finite cylinders far apart along axes that nearly cross have a small infinite-axis distance but a large finite gap), so it culls but does not always prove; the residual goes to bucket b. Cone distance closed forms are delicate near the apex and for the back-nappe; use Eberly's apex-angle reject predicates carefully and default to bucket b on the degenerate ranges.
> **Patent.** Eberly's Geometric Tools is Boost-licensed, public; Lumelsky and Miller-Goldman are old. No risk.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is **bucket a**, the exact-oracle tier and the honest first milestone (section 7). Sphere-sphere and sphere-vs-{plane, cylinder, cone} and plane-vs-anything are one-expression sound certificates. The **subset / infinite-surface lower bound** is the trick that lets the closed forms serve as conservative bounds for trimmed faces without solving the finite case. Everything that the closed form cannot prove (loose bound, or a pair with no closed form: finite-cyl/cyl, cone/cyl, the entire torus row) falls to bucket b/c. This mirrors dossier 58's exact-rung / NURBS-fallback split, inverted.

## 4. Is empty-SSI ever a sound disjointness proof?

This is the sharpest sub-question, because it is where the current code is dangerous. Today `clashes()` and the boolean infer non-intersection from "`seam_curves` returned empty." That inference is **unsound** as written, and the tilted-cylinder / cylinder class proves it: SSI returns empty not because there is no intersection but because the rung MISSED it (the seam exists but the tracer failed to find it). Empty-SSI is a proof of non-intersection **only if the SSI procedure is certified complete on that input**. The literature gives exactly the certification that closes the gap.

> **Citation.** Krawczyk / interval Newton exclusion (section 2.4). Mourrain, B., & Pavone, J.-P. (2009), subdivision exclusion (above). Hu, C.-Y., Maekawa, T., Patrikalakis, N. M., & Ye, X. (1996). Robust interval algorithm for surface intersections. *Computer-Aided Design* 29(9), 617-627. Patrikalakis, N. M., & Maekawa, T. (2002). *Shape Interrogation for Computer Aided Design and Manufacturing*, Springer (the IPP / interval-projected-polyhedron solver). Aberth, O. (1994); Franek, P., & Ratschan, S. (2015). Effective topological degree computation based on interval arithmetic. *Mathematics of Computation* 84, 1265-1290. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.6331 .
> **Method / Content.** "Empty SSI" becomes a sound proof under one of these certifications, all of which are **exclusion certificates over the full parameter domain** rather than seam-tracers:
> - **Krawczyk / interval-Newton exclusion over the whole domain** (section 2.4): subdivide the `(u, v)` parameter box; on each sub-box run the Krawczyk test on the intersection system `S_A(u) - S_B(v) = 0`; if every sub-box is excluded (`K(X) ∩ X = ∅` everywhere, or pruned by the squared-distance enclosure exceeding `tol^2`), there is **provably no intersection point**. This is a *complete* negative certificate: it does not trace a curve, it proves the curve is empty. It is immune to the tilted-cylinder miss, because it never relies on a per-rung closed form; it relies only on the sound interval enclosure of the surfaces.
> - **Interval-projected-polyhedron (IPP) / robust interval SSI** (Hu-Maekawa-Patrikalakis): the production-grade version of the same idea, the standard "no roots in this box" SSI solver that returns either certified seam components or a certified-empty answer; its empty answer **is** a sound disjointness proof by construction (it exhausts the domain with rounding-safe interval enclosures).
> - **Topological degree** (Franek-Ratschan, Aberth): compute the topological degree of `S_A - S_B` over the boundary of the parameter domain; a well-defined degree computation can *certify a root exists*, and combined with exhaustive exclusion certifies the count, but for **disjointness the exclusion side alone suffices** (degree is the inclusion tool; we only need exclusion). Noted for completeness, not the shipping path.
> **The tilted-cylinder / cylinder class specifically.** The current rung misses the intersection because the closed-form branch field has a gap (a `theta`-range where the per-ruling quadratic is mishandled, dossier 58 section 2.3). The exclusion certificate does **not** use the per-ruling closed form: it subdivides the full `(u, v)` box and tests each sub-box for the system having no root via the interval enclosure of `|cyl_A(u) - cyl_B(v)|^2`. If the cylinders truly intersect, some sub-box will fail to be excluded (its enclosure brackets zero), the certificate returns UNKNOWN, and the pair falls through to the full boolean. The certificate **cannot** falsely certify disjoint here, because a real intersection point forces a non-excludable box. So the exclusion certificate is exactly the sound replacement for the unsound "rung returned empty" inference.
> **Verified vs claimed.** Verified: Krawczyk exclusion, IPP completeness, that an exhausted exclusion subdivision is a sound empty-intersection proof. Claimed (Keel-specific): that running the exclusion certificate over the AABB-overlap parameter region terminates promptly for the genuinely-disjoint class (it does when the true gap exceeds `tol`; tangent and sub-tolerance-touch pairs do not terminate and correctly return UNKNOWN).
> **Limitations.** The certificate's cost is bounded by subdivision depth; **tangential intersection** (the surfaces touch at a single curve or point with a shared tangent plane) is the worst case: the squared-distance enclosure brackets zero in a shrinking-but-nonempty region, so the certificate neither excludes nor cheaply confirms, and must hit the depth budget and return UNKNOWN. This is the right behavior (tangency is not disjointness), but it means tangent pairs never get the fast path. Near-tangent disjoint pairs are slow but still terminate. The Jacobian-singular degeneracy weakens Krawczyk specifically (use the squared-distance enclosure pruning as the primary test, Krawczyk as the sharpener).
> **Patent.** All exclusion / interval-SSI methods here are pre-2002, unencumbered.
> **Kernel relevance.** This answers the title question directly: **empty-SSI is a sound disjointness proof if and only if the SSI is replaced by, or backed by, a domain-exhaustive interval exclusion certificate** (Krawczyk / IPP / Bernstein no-root), which is independent of the per-rung closed forms and therefore immune to the tilted-cylinder miss. For Keel, the cheapest realization is the **Bernstein no-root test on the recovered bivariate control net** (the `ssi.rs` tracer already has this net; run its convex-hull sign test as an exclusion certificate before declaring empty). This is the single most important adoption in this dossier: it converts the current unsound `clashes()` shortcut into a sound one, at the cost of a domain-subdivision pass that returns UNKNOWN on tangency.

## 5. Composition with the tolerant-f64 model

The certificate must respect Keel's tolerant model: a vertex carries a tolerance, an edge a tolerance tube, and two features within their combined tolerance are *coincident* by the model, not disjoint. A sound disjointness proof must therefore certify a gap larger than the **tolerance envelope**, not merely a gap larger than zero.

> **Citation.** Jackson, D. J. (1995). Boundary representation modelling with local tolerances. *Proc. ACM Solid Modeling*, 247-254. Segal, M., & Sequin, C. H. (1988). Partitioning polyhedral objects into nonintersecting parts. *IEEE CG&A* 8(1). Keel internal: the tolerant-f64 vertex/edge model, dossiers 11 and 35.
> **Method / Content.** The composition rule is a single threshold shift, and it is sound because the lower bound is conservative. Let `tol_env(p, q)` be the combined tolerance of the two closest features (the sum or the max-with-margin of the two feature tolerances, per Keel's coincidence convention). The certificate returns PROVEN DISJOINT only when `d_low > tol_env + safety`, where `safety` absorbs the floating-point error in computing `d_low` itself (the interval / affine machinery already provides a rounding-safe `d_low`, so `safety` is the directed-rounding slack, not an ad-hoc fudge). Three soundness obligations:
> 1. **Never certify a sub-tolerance touch as a gap.** If `d* <= tol_env`, the model says the features are coincident; the certificate must NOT return DISJOINT. Because `d_low <= d*`, requiring `d_low > tol_env` guarantees `d* >= d_low > tol_env`, so a sub-tolerance touch (`d* <= tol_env`) can never pass the gate. Sound by the lower-bound property.
> 2. **Use the per-feature tolerance, not a global epsilon.** The envelope is the *local* tolerance of the two closest features (a loose imported vertex near a tight analytic face uses the loose vertex's tolerance), matching Keel's local-tolerance model (Jackson). A global epsilon would be unsound where a feature's local tolerance exceeds it.
> 3. **The lower bound must be on the tolerant point sets.** A tolerant edge is a *tube* of radius `tol_edge`; the certificate's surfaces should be **fattened by their tolerance** (Minkowski-inflated by the tolerance ball, dossier 10) before the distance bound, OR equivalently the threshold raised by `tol_env`. The threshold-raise is the cheap, exactly-equivalent realization (inflating a sphere by `t` and subtracting `t` from the gap are identical), so the certificate inflates nothing and instead tests `d_low > tol_env + safety`.
> **Verified vs claimed.** Verified: the lower-bound property makes the threshold-shift sound (the algebra in obligation 1). Claimed (Keel-specific): that the threshold-raise is exactly equivalent to tolerance-fattening for the feature types Keel uses (true for balls/tubes around analytic and NURBS faces; the inflated NURBS is not a NURBS but the *distance* identity still holds, so the threshold form is the right realization).
> **Limitations.** If the two operands' local tolerances are wildly mismatched, the envelope is dominated by the loose one, and the certificate is conservative there (it declines to prove disjointness for pairs separated by less than the loose tolerance); this is correct, not a defect. The `safety` slack must come from the rounding-safe arithmetic, not be guessed; using non-directed-rounded f64 for `d_low` would break soundness, so the certificate must use the interval/affine substrate (dossier 37) end to end.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the **glue** that makes the certificate safe inside Keel's tolerant world. The rule is one line: `PROVEN_DISJOINT iff d_low > tol_env(closest features) + rounding_slack`, with `d_low` from buckets a/b/c and `tol_env` from the local feature tolerances. The lower-bound property does all the soundness work; the tolerant model only raises the bar, never lowers it. A genuine sub-tolerance touch is, by construction, never certified as a gap.

## 6. Cross-cutting three-bucket catalog

Which operand / feature pairs admit which certificate. "Sound" is the invariant in every row; the buckets differ only in cost and tightness.

**Bucket a, exact closed-form sound separation (one expression, no subdivision):**
- Sphere-sphere; sphere vs plane; sphere vs infinite cylinder; sphere vs infinite cone (apex-angle reduction).
- Plane vs any quadric (extremal-point signed distance).
- Infinite-cylinder vs infinite-cylinder (axis skew-distance minus radii), parallel or skew.
- Linear edge vs linear edge; linear edge vs infinite cylinder (segment-segment, segment-cylinder, Lumelsky / Eberly), for edge-edge feature pairs.
- Realization note: for *trimmed* faces these serve as **conservative lower bounds** via the subset argument (finite face ⊂ infinite surface), so they always cull and often prove.

**Bucket b, certified interval / affine lower bound (subdivision, terminates above tolerance, UNKNOWN on tangency):**
- Finite-cylinder vs finite-cylinder, cone vs cylinder, cone vs cone (no closed form; constrained-minimization stationarity is a polynomial system, bound by Krawczyk / Bernstein exclusion).
- The entire **torus row** (torus-plane, torus-cylinder, torus-sphere, torus-cone, torus-torus): degree 4 to 16, no usable closed form, bounded by affine-arithmetic squared-distance enclosure plus subdivision.
- **NURBS vs anything**: control-hull distance refined by Bezier-clipping subdivision (section 2.2), tightened by affine arithmetic (section 2.3), excluded by Bernstein no-root (section 2.4). Point-to-NURBS via Chen circular clipping for vertex-face pairs.
- Any pair where bucket a's closed form is too loose to clear `tol`.

**Bucket c, cannot be certified cheaply, fall through to the full SSI / boolean path:**
- Tangent and near-tangent pairs (the squared-distance enclosure brackets zero in a non-vanishing region; subdivision hits the depth budget; certificate returns UNKNOWN, never DISJOINT). Sub-tolerance touches (correctly, these are coincident, not disjoint).
- Pairs where subdivision exceeds the depth budget for any reason (high curvature, near-degenerate trims, self-near-tangency). UNKNOWN is always sound; the cost is a missed fast path.
- Anything the certificate is not yet implemented for: default UNKNOWN, full boolean, correct but slow.

The buckets degrade gracefully: a always beats b beats c on cost, and every bucket is sound. The certificate tries a, falls to b, and any non-termination in b lands in c (fall through), which is exactly Keel's existing behavior, now reached only after a sound attempt to prove the gap.

## 7. Synthesis: ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE

**ADOPT.**
- The **one-sided lower-bound contract**: the certificate computes a rounding-safe `d_low <= d*` and returns DISJOINT only when `d_low > tol_env + slack`. Soundness is structural, from the lower-bound property, not from testing.
- **Bucket a exact closed forms** for sphere-sphere, sphere-vs-{plane, cylinder, cone}, plane-vs-quadric, infinite-cylinder pairs, segment-segment / segment-cylinder, used as conservative lower bounds for trimmed faces via the subset argument. This is the cheap, exact tier.
- The **GJK / SAT convex broad phase** on per-face convex supports (closed-form support for quadrics, control-hull support for NURBS), with the monotone GJK lower bound as the first narrow-phase gate. Public, unencumbered implementations only.
- The **NURBS control-hull distance bound refined by Bezier-clipping subdivision and tightened by affine arithmetic** as the universal bucket-b machine, reusing the `ssi.rs` bivariate-Bernstein net and the dossier-37 affine substrate.
- The **Bernstein no-root / Krawczyk exclusion certificate** as the *sound replacement* for the current unsound empty-SSI inference. This is the highest-leverage single adoption: it makes `clashes()` honest and is immune to the tilted-cylinder miss.
- The **tolerant-threshold composition**: test `d_low > tol_env + slack` with local per-feature tolerances, equivalent to tolerance-fattening, sound by the lower-bound property.

**AVOID.**
- The current tessellation-based vertex-to-triangle `min_distance` as a disjointness proof: it is unsound (misses edge-edge and interior minima, and the chord-vs-arc gap can over-report). Keep it for display / heuristics only.
- Inferring disjointness from "rung returned empty" without an exclusion certificate. This is the live bug; bucket-a/b exclusion replaces it.
- Adopting QI / full algebraic quadric-intersection machinery (dossier 58) for this purpose: it imports the algebraic-number wall for a problem that only needs a one-sided relaxation. Overkill and heavy.
- Post-2006 differentiable / learned collision-detection patent methods, and the patented polyhedral-collision acceleration wrappers (EP0899692A2 family, Havok-acquired family). The base GJK / SAT / Bezier-clipping / Krawczyk methods are all old and unencumbered; design around the wrappers, not the base algorithms.

**INVESTIGATE.**
- Whether affine arithmetic's tightness gain over plain interval arithmetic is worth its per-box cost for Keel's low-degree analytic forms, or whether plain rounding-safe intervals suffice for the quadric pairs and affine is reserved for high-degree torus / NURBS pairs.
- A **lazy / incremental certificate** that caches the BVH-survivor feature-pair set and the partial subdivision tree across repeated booleans on the same operands (interactive editing), so re-proving disjointness after a small move is cheap (conservative-advancement style, but for the static gap).
- Topological-degree (Franek-Ratschan) as a *positive* intersection certificate to pair with the exclusion certificate, giving a complete decide-or-UNKNOWN for non-tangent pairs (degree confirms a root exists, exclusion confirms none); deferred, since disjointness needs only exclusion.

**Recommended FIRST MILESTONE (narrowest honest slice with an exact oracle).**
Implement **bucket a for the sphere row and plane row, plus the Bernstein no-root exclusion gate as a sound `clashes()` backstop**, gated behind a feature flag, fired only on AABB-overlapping pairs:
1. Sphere-sphere, sphere-vs-plane, sphere-vs-infinite-cylinder, sphere-vs-infinite-cone, plane-vs-any-quadric: one-expression `d_low` (conservative lower bound for trimmed faces via subset). DISJOINT iff `d_low > tol_env + slack`.
2. For everything else, before any empty-SSI shortcut, run the **Bernstein no-root exclusion** over the recovered bivariate net; certify empty only if exclusion exhausts the domain, else fall through.
The exact oracle: a battery of *constructed* sphere/plane pairs with **known** signed gaps straddling `tol_env` (placed by exact rational construction), asserting the certificate's DISJOINT/UNKNOWN matches the constructed truth, and a battery of **known-touching** pairs (including the tilted-cylinder / cylinder intersecting case) asserting the certificate NEVER returns DISJOINT. The touching battery is the soundness oracle; zero false-disjoint is the gate.

**FOLLOW-UP LADDER.**
1. (above) Sphere/plane bucket-a exact, plus Bernstein exclusion backstop.
2. GJK / SAT convex broad phase on per-face supports (cheap whole-body cull).
3. Infinite-cylinder-pair and segment-segment / segment-cylinder closed forms (edge-edge feature coverage).
4. NURBS control-hull subdivision bound for face-face and point-to-NURBS (vertex-face), terminating above `tol`.
5. Affine-arithmetic tightening over the squared-distance function for the torus row and the loose-bucket-a residuals.
6. Krawczyk-sharpened exclusion for the finite-quadric and cone pairs where the hull bound is too loose.
7. Incremental / cached certificate for interactive re-boolean.

**DECLINE-forever set (return UNKNOWN, fall through, never certify):**
- Tangent and near-tangent pairs (shared tangent plane along the closest approach): the certificate cannot cheaply distinguish tangent-touch from tangent-near-miss; UNKNOWN is correct and permanent.
- Sub-tolerance touches: by the tolerant model these are coincident, not disjoint; never certified as a gap.
- Any pair exceeding the subdivision depth budget: UNKNOWN is always sound; chasing termination into deep subdivision is not worth it versus the full boolean.
- Self-intersecting or invalid operands: out of scope; the certificate assumes valid solids and declines otherwise.

The DECLINE-forever set is the honest boundary: the certificate's job is to *accelerate the provably-separated common case*, not to decide every pair. Tangency and sub-tolerance contact belong to the full boolean, which is correct (if slow) there. The certificate's contract is the project's: it may DECLINE (UNKNOWN) freely, but it must never be WRONG (false DISJOINT).
