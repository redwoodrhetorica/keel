# 60. Curved-trim mass integration completeness: closing the Green-slab gaps that block correctly-assembled curved results

## Title and scope

This dossier is the implementation-grade literature foundation for **completing Keel's per-face curved mass integrator** so that correctly-assembled curved bodies pass the mass-vs-mesh gate instead of declining. Keel computes mass by the divergence theorem, per face, sense-exact (`mass_properties`); for curved trims each face is integrated by the **Green-slab boundary integrator** `integrate_face_green` (boundary flux, GL8 Gauss-Legendre, `local_geometry(u, v)` for cone / cylinder / sphere, a `node(point, tangent, weight)` helper that accumulates the u-winding and slab samples, with a POLE anchor for full-revolution sphere faces). The gate is `mass == mesh` within a 2% curved self-consistency band. Today three concrete gaps make a correctly-assembled curved face fail that gate, so the whole boolean declines even though the topology is right (LOG Add.250, KL3 / KL4):

- **(a) Cone-apex full-revolution face.** A winding +-1 full-revolution cone-apex fragment (the tip above a cut) declines "unsupported boundary winding." The sphere case has a POLE anchor; the cone apex needs the analogous anchor.
- **(b) High-degree NURBS / hyperbolic boundary arc.** A boundary arc that is a degree>1 NURBS is rejected at the `Curve3::Nurbs(n) if n.degree() > 1` guard. A plane parallel to a cone axis cuts a HYPERBOLA, which has no `Curve3::Hyperbola` variant and is stored as a deg>1 NURBS, so a notched cone fragment cannot be integrated.
- **(c) Notched / partial-azimuth cone fragments.** A partial-azimuth cone fragment takes the full-azimuth iso-rectangle path and OVER-counts the removed wedge (synthetic probe: mass 17.07 vs true 11.85) because `non_iso` detection misses pcurve-less cut edges.

The scope is the **mass integrator only**, not the SSI (dossier 58) that produces the seam, not the assembly (dossier 59) that stitches it, not the predicate substrate (dossier 11). It is the third leg of the curved-frontier triad: 58 computes the seam, 59 stitches it into a valid shell, 60 makes the resulting curved faces **integrate to the right number** so the assembled shell passes `mass == mesh`. The three gaps are all in the boundary-flux arm of `integrate_face_green`: gap (a) is a missing apex anchor (the dual of the existing sphere pole anchor), gap (b) is a missing generic boundary-arc sampler (the integrator rejects the only curve type a hyperbolic trim can be stored as), and gap (c) is a missing partial-fragment witness (the integrator silently uses the wrong region when the iso-rectangle is not the face).

This is high leverage because every assembled curved result is gated by `mass == mesh`: a seam computed correctly (58) and stitched correctly (59) still produces a DECLINE if the integrator cannot integrate the resulting face. The contract stays the project standard, **DECLINE-never-WRONG**: an integrator that cannot certify a face's mass to the band must decline, never emit a wrong number that lets a wrong body pass. The fix is to widen the certifiable set (exact closed forms where they exist, certified boundary-flux quadrature where they do not) so fewer correctly-assembled faces fall to DECLINE.

House style: **no em-dashes anywhere, machine-checked; en-dashes are not used as separators either, ranges are written with "to".** The structure mirrors files 01 to 59: annotated source blocks (Citation / Method / Limitations / Kernel relevance), explicit verified-vs-claimed labels, patent flags, and a closing ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE with a first milestone, a follow-up ladder, and a DECLINE-forever set.

## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **All three gaps are the same shape of fix: the boundary-flux integrator must be made COMPLETE over its own boundary, meaning (i) it must close the boundary of every face it accepts, supplying an analytic "anchor" arc for any part of the true boundary that has no explicit `Curve3` (the cone apex, exactly as the sphere pole anchor already does), (ii) it must sample ANY `Curve3` for the (point, tangent) the `node()` helper needs, not just lines and degree-1 NURBS, so a recovered hyperbola or conic trim is integrable, and (iii) it must DETECT when the iso-rectangle it is about to integrate is not the face's actual region and route to the genuine closed boundary instead, by a cheap area-witness (analytic band area vs tessellated area), the cone analog of the sphere rectangle-witness already present. None of this needs a new integration theory. The divergence / boundary-flux mechanism Keel already runs is exact for these faces; the gaps are missing arcs, a missing sampler, and a missing region check, each closable to an EXACT oracle on the natural-quadric cases.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. The divergence theorem reduces a volume integral to a surface flux, and (Gonzalez-Ochoa, McCammon, Peters; Lasserre) a further reduction sends the per-face surface integral to its **trimmed-patch boundary** in the parameter plane, so the integrand Keel actually evaluates is a 1D line integral over each face's boundary loop. That loop must be CLOSED for the line integral to equal the enclosed flux: a missing arc (the apex run, gap a) leaves the loop open and the integral is wrong by the missing flux, which is exactly what "unsupported boundary winding" detects and declines on. The sampler (gap b) is just the per-node evaluation of (point, tangent) along whatever curve the boundary arc is; rejecting deg>1 NURBS rejects the only representation a hyperbola can take, so the loop cannot be closed and the face declines. The region check (gap c) is the precondition that the loop the integrator walks is the face's TRUE boundary and not a default iso-rectangle; when the cut removed a wedge but the integrator walked the full azimuth, the flux it computes is the flux of the wrong region (the over-count 17.07 vs 11.85). Fix the three and the same boundary-flux integrator, unchanged in its core, integrates every natural-quadric curved face to the band, with the coaxial / full-revolution cases landing on exact closed forms (Pappus / frustum / spherical-zone) as the oracle.

## 1. The cone-APEX anchor (the cone dual of the sphere pole anchor)

The first gap: a full-revolution cone-apex fragment (a tip above a cut) declines because its boundary winding is +-1 with no explicit boundary arc carrying that winding. The sphere already solves the analogous problem with a POLE anchor. This section derives the cone-apex anchor and its complement / island detection.

### 1.1 The divergence / boundary-flux reduction and why a full-revolution face needs an anchor

> **Citation.** C. Gonzalez-Ochoa, S. McCammon, J. Peters, "Computing Moments of Objects Enclosed by Piecewise Polynomial Surfaces," *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 17(3):143-157, 1998. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2459122_Computing_Moments_of_Piecewise_Polynomial_Surfaces . J. B. Lasserre, "Integration on a convex polytope," *Proc. AMS* 126, 1998, and the homogeneous-function reduction reused widely for moments. Background: Y. T. Lee and A. A. G. Requicha, "Algorithms for computing the volume and other integral properties of solids. I and II," *Comm. ACM* 25(9):635-650, 1982.
> **Method / Content.** Gonzalez-Ochoa-Peters combine a polynomial / free-form surface representation with Gauss' divergence theorem to compute volume, center of mass and the full inertia tensor of the solid bounded by the surface **exactly** (for polynomial patches) and efficiently (10^5 patches in seconds). The mechanism: pick a vector field `F` with `div F = 1` (e.g. `F = (x, 0, 0)` for volume, so `V = integral over boundary of x dy dz`); the volume integral becomes a sum of surface flux integrals, one per face, `integral_face F . n dA`. For a parametric face `S(u, v)`, `n dA = S_u x S_v du dv`, so the per-face term is `integral integral over the (u, v) trim region of F(S) . (S_u x S_v) du dv`. The key further reduction (the "Green-slab" name in Keel): for many fields the inner `u` integral has a closed form, leaving a **1D boundary integral** over the trim region's boundary in the parameter plane (apply Green's theorem to the planar trim region), which is what `integrate_face_green` evaluates by GL8 over the boundary loop, accumulating the u-winding and slab samples per `node()`.
> **Limitations.** The exactness is for polynomial patches; natural quadrics (cone / cylinder / sphere) are rational or trigonometric, so the inner integral is closed-form (these are the cases Keel handles analytically in `local_geometry`) but the outer boundary integral over a non-iso trim is quadrature, not exact in general. The method assumes the per-face boundary loop is CLOSED; an open loop gives the flux of an unclosed region, which is wrong by the missing-arc flux.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the theory under `integrate_face_green`. The load-bearing fact for gap (a): the boundary-flux value of a face equals the line integral over its CLOSED boundary loop. A full-revolution cone-apex face's true boundary is the rim circle plus the apex point; in parameter space `(u in [0, 2*pi), v in [0, 1])` the rim is the `v = v_cut` iso-line and the apex is the degenerate `v = 0` (or `v = 1`) line collapsed to a point. The face is bounded in `u` by the full circle and in `v` by the apex on one side, so its parametric boundary loop is `rim (u: 0 to 2*pi) + the v-edges at the seam`, and the apex contributes a degenerate boundary that the integrator must supply as an **anchor** so the loop closes and the u-winding integrates to +-1 correctly. **Verified** (divergence / boundary-flux reduction is standard; Gonzalez-Ochoa-Peters is the canonical CAD instance).

### 1.2 The cone surface parameterization and the apex singularity

The cone of half-angle `alpha`, apex `A`, unit axis `a`, has the standard ruling parameterization:

```
P(u, v) = A + v * ( cos(alpha) * (cos(u) e1 + sin(u) e2) + sin(alpha) a ),   u in [0, 2*pi), v >= 0
```

where `(e1, e2, a)` is an orthonormal frame. The partials are `P_u = v cos(alpha) (-sin u e1 + cos u e2)` and `P_v = cos(alpha)(cos u e1 + sin u e2) + sin(alpha) a`, so the surface element is

```
S_u x S_v  =>  |P_u x P_v| = v * cos(alpha)
```

(the `v` factor is the lateral-area Jacobian; integrating `v cos(alpha)` over `v in [0, L]`, `u in [0, 2*pi)` gives `pi L^2 cos(alpha) sin(alpha)`... the standard lateral area `pi r l` with `r = L cos alpha`, `l = L`). The **apex is a parametric singularity**: at `v = 0` the whole `u`-circle collapses to the single point `A`, and `P_u = 0`, so the surface element `|P_u x P_v| = v cos(alpha)` **vanishes** there. This is the crucial sign that the singularity is REMOVABLE in the integrand: the Jacobian goes to zero linearly in `v`, so the surface integral `integral F . (P_u x P_v) du dv` has a `v`-weighted integrand that is **finite (in fact zero) at the apex**, exactly as the sphere's `sin(phi)` Jacobian vanishes at the pole. No special quadrature is needed at the apex for the surface form; the danger is only in the BOUNDARY form, where dividing the closed-form `u`-integral by a Jacobian that vanishes at `v = 0` can create a spurious `1/v`. The lift is to keep the apex as a degenerate boundary node with the slant-direction tangent, not to evaluate the boundary integrand at `v = 0`.

> **Citation.** The removable-singularity-at-apex observation is the same one made in the cone-sphere intersection volume literature: "Volume of Intersection of a Cone with a Sphere," arXiv:2203.17227, 2022 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17227), which notes that when the cone apex lies on the sphere a spurious singularity appears in the integral kernel denominator and "this spurious singularity can be lifted by rewriting the integral kernel." Standard parametric-surface-integral background: *Surface Integrals* (OpenStax Calculus Volume 3, https://opentextbc.ca/calculusv3openstax/chapter/surface-integrals/) and the cone parameterization `r(u, v) = (u cos v, u sin v, u)`.
> **Method / Content.** The apex singularity is **removable**: the `v`-weighted Jacobian `v cos(alpha)` annihilates the `1/v` that the boundary reduction can introduce, so the integrand stays finite. The arXiv cone-sphere paper's "rewrite the kernel" is the same maneuver in the volume-integral setting: factor out the vanishing factor analytically before evaluating, rather than evaluating numerically near the singularity.
> **Limitations.** The lift is clean for the EXACT integrand; a generic numerical boundary sampler that places a GL node at `v = 0` would still divide by zero. The anchor must therefore be an ANALYTIC arc contribution (the apex node's contribution computed in closed form), not a sampled one.
> **Kernel relevance.** This justifies the cone-apex anchor as the exact dual of the sphere pole anchor: supply the apex as a single degenerate boundary node whose contribution to the u-winding and slab accumulation is computed analytically (the limit as `v -> 0`), so the boundary loop closes and the winding integrates to +-1 without ever sampling at the singular `v = 0`. **Verified** that the singularity is removable; the apex-anchor-as-analytic-node is **claimed** (the construction, mirroring the existing sphere pole anchor).

### 1.3 The cone-apex anchor construction (the deliverable)

> **Method / Content (claimed, the recommended Keel mechanism, dual of the sphere pole anchor).** A full-revolution cone face that includes the apex has a parametric boundary that is open at the apex (the `v = 0` line collapses to a point with no explicit `Curve3` edge). The anchor closes it:
> 1. **Detect the full-revolution-with-apex topology.** The face's `u`-winding is +-1 (full azimuth) and its `v`-range reaches the apex `v = 0`. This is the cone analog of the sphere's full-revolution pole-bearing face.
> 2. **Insert the apex anchor node.** Add a single boundary node at the apex `A` with tangent along the slant direction at the seam `u = 0` (the limit `lim_{v -> 0} P_v`, which is finite: `cos(alpha)(cos 0 e1 + sin 0 e2) + sin(alpha) a`). Its slab / winding contribution is the analytic limit, NOT a GL sample, so no `1/v` is ever evaluated. The apex node carries the full `u`-winding turn (the `2*pi` of azimuth is realized as the apex being the single point all rulings pass through), exactly as the sphere pole node carries the polar `u`-winding turn.
> 3. **Close the loop.** Boundary loop = rim circle (the `v = v_cut` iso-arc, full `u: 0 to 2*pi`) + the apex anchor node. The u-winding now integrates to +-1, the "unsupported boundary winding" decline does not fire, and the boundary-flux equals the closed-form cone-tip volume.
> 4. **Complement / island detection.** A cut can leave the apex on EITHER side: the tip fragment (apex + rim, integrates to the small cone volume) or the frustum fragment (rim + base, no apex). The integrator must decide which side it is integrating. Use the **same area-witness as gap (c), section 3**: the analytic band area between the two `v`-bounds vs the tessellated face area. If the apex is an island (the fragment is the tip), anchor at the apex; if the fragment is the frustum (apex excluded), there is NO apex anchor and both `v`-bounds are explicit rim arcs. A fragment that is a partial-azimuth tip (apex + a wedge of rim) is gap (c) plus this anchor combined: the apex anchor closes the `v` side, the partial-azimuth boundary closes the `u` side.
> **Exact oracle.** The tip fragment's volume is the closed-form cone volume `(1/3) pi r^2 h` with `r = v_cut cos alpha`, `h = v_cut sin alpha` (apex at `v = 0`); its lateral area is `pi r l = pi (v_cut cos alpha)(v_cut)`. The frustum fragment is the frustum volume `(pi h / 3)(R^2 + R r + r^2)` and lateral area `pi (R + r) l`. These (Pappus / frustum formulas) are the EXACT oracle the anchored integrator must reproduce to `1e-9`, far inside the 2% band.
> **Citation.** Pappus's centroid theorem (volume / lateral area of solids of revolution): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus%27s_centroid_theorem ; cone / frustum / spherical-zone closed forms: https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SurfaceofRevolution.html , https://amsi.org.au/teacher_modules/Cones_Pyramids_and_Spheres.html .
> **Limitations.** The anchor assumes the apex is a TRUE cone apex (a degenerate `v = 0` line), not a chamfered or rounded tip; a tip rounded by a blend is a different face (sphere / torus blend, dossier 40) with its own integrator. A seam passing exactly through the apex (degenerate-conic cut, dossier 59 Q5 class 2) is declined upstream, so the apex anchor only ever sees a clean apex.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the narrowest, highest-certainty fix: it is the exact structural dual of code that already works (the sphere pole anchor), it has an exact closed-form oracle (cone / frustum volume), and it directly converts the gap-(a) "unsupported boundary winding" decline into a PASS. **Verified** oracle; **claimed** construction (the dual mechanism).

## 2. A generic boundary-arc integration arm (sampling any `Curve3` for the node contribution)

The second gap: the integrator rejects boundary arcs that are degree>1 NURBS (`Curve3::Nurbs(n) if n.degree() > 1`), which is the only representation a hyperbolic or recovered-conic trim can take. The fix is a GENERIC boundary-arc arm that samples an arbitrary `Curve3` for the `(point, tangent)` pair the `node()` helper needs, so any seam curve from the dossier-58 SSI matrix integrates.

### 2.1 The boundary integral is a per-arc (point, tangent) quadrature, indifferent to curve type

> **Citation.** J. Spainhour, K. Weiss, "Robust Containment Queries over Collections of Trimmed NURBS Surfaces via Generalized Winding Numbers," *ACM TOG*, 2025 (arXiv:2504.11435, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11435 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11435). Quadrature-on-trimmed-patches: J. A. Cottrell / T. J. R. Hughes isogeometric background and "High-accuracy mesh-free quadrature for trimmed parametric surfaces and volumes," *Computer-Aided Design*, 2021 (arXiv:2101.06497, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.06497).
> **Method / Content.** Spainhour-Weiss compute the generalized winding number of a region bounded by trimmed NURBS surfaces by **Stokes' theorem reformulated as a 1D line integral over the boundary of each patch**, solved with **adaptive quadrature accurate to a user tolerance**. The decisive structural point for Keel: the boundary line integral is `integral over the trim boundary of (kernel)(P(t)) . T(t) dt`, where `P(t)` is the boundary point and `T(t)` the boundary tangent. The integrand depends ONLY on `(point, tangent)` evaluated along the curve; it does not care whether the curve is a line, a circle, an ellipse, a parabola/hyperbola arc, or a general NURBS. Any `Curve3` that can return `point(t)` and `tangent(t)` (its first derivative) is integrable by the same GL loop. They note the 1D line integral "is more numerically stable than the 2D surface integral equivalent," and that robust evaluation needs a scheme "guaranteed to be precise for the near-singular integrand" (their near-surface case; Keel's near-apex / near-seam case is the analog).
> **Limitations.** Their integrand is near-singular near the evaluation surface; they switch to a line-surface intersection test when Stokes is theoretically unfounded. Keel's mass integrand is not near-singular for a face's own boundary (the only singularity is the apex / pole, handled by the anchor of section 1), so the generic arm is the easy case of their machinery.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the literature warrant for the generic boundary-arc arm: **the `node(point, tangent, weight)` helper already abstracts the only data the boundary integral needs**, so the fix is to feed it `(point, tangent)` from ANY `Curve3` by GL-sampling that curve, dropping the `degree() > 1` guard. A degree-2 rational NURBS (the exact hyperbola / parabola arc, dossier 58 section 3.1) and a degree-3 recovered-quartic NURBS both expose `point(t)` and the derivative; the arm samples them identically. **Verified** that the boundary integrand is `(point, tangent)`-only and curve-type-indifferent.

### 2.2 Convergence and panel count: how many GL nodes for a conic, a hyperbola, a recovered quartic

> **Citation.** L. N. Trefethen, "Is Gauss Quadrature Better than Clenshaw-Curtis?" and "Exactness of Quadrature Formulas," *SIAM Review* (https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/trefethen/exactnessRev2.pdf). Gauss-Legendre exactness `2n-1`: https://people.math.sc.edu/Burkardt/cpp_src/legendre_exactness/legendre_exactness.html . Rational-integrand caveat: W. Gautschi, "Gauss-type quadrature rules for rational functions," (arXiv:math/9307223, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9307223). Adaptive panel subdivision with per-panel error estimate: "Error estimate based adaptive quadrature for layer potentials" (arXiv:2412.19575) and "High-accuracy mesh-free quadrature for trimmed parametric surfaces" (arXiv:2101.06497).
> **Method / Content.** An `n`-point Gauss-Legendre rule integrates a polynomial of degree `<= 2n - 1` EXACTLY. Consequences for Keel's boundary arcs:
> - **A circle / ellipse arc** parameterized by angle has a boundary integrand that is a trigonometric polynomial; GL8 (Keel's current order, exact to degree 15) integrates a full-revolution circular rim to roughly machine precision because the trig polynomial degree is low. This is why the existing cone / cylinder / sphere iso-rectangle path is already exact at GL8.
> - **A parabola arc** as a degree-2 polynomial Bezier gives a boundary integrand of low polynomial degree; GL8 is exact or near-exact.
> - **A hyperbola / general conic** stored as a degree-2 RATIONAL NURBS has a RATIONAL integrand (numerator / denominator both polynomial). Gauss-Legendre on a rational integrand converges geometrically for a smooth (pole-free on the arc) rational but is NOT exact at any fixed order, and convergence "slows as the weight approaches 1" (the near-parabolic hyperbola, weight `w -> 1`). So a fixed GL8 is NOT guaranteed to hit the 2% band for a sharp hyperbola arc; an **adaptive panel** (subdivide the arc, GL8 per panel, refine until the per-panel error estimate is under tolerance) is the robust choice. Halving a panel divides the GL error by roughly `2^(2n)` for a smooth integrand, so one or two splits suffice for a conic.
> - **A recovered quartic** (degree-3 cubic NURBS fit, dossier 58 Path B) is piecewise polynomial of low degree per span; GL8 per knot span is exact per polynomial span, so a per-span GL pass (panel = knot span) is exact-per-span and the only error is the fit's own `tol_achieved`, already certified.
> **Limitations.** Adaptive subdivision needs a per-panel error estimate; the cheapest is the GL8-vs-GL4 difference (embedded estimate) or a Richardson halving. The rational hyperbola's worst case (a long arc approaching the asymptote) can need several panels; if the panel count to reach tolerance exceeds a cap, DECLINE (do not silently return an under-resolved number).
> **Kernel relevance.** The recommendation: **fixed GL8 per knot span for polynomial / piecewise-polynomial arcs (lines, circles via trig, parabola, recovered cubic), adaptive GL8 panels with an embedded error estimate for rational conic arcs (hyperbola, sharp ellipse)**. The adaptive path is bounded: cap the subdivision depth and DECLINE on exceed, preserving DECLINE-never-WRONG. This makes the generic arm certified to the band for every `Curve3` the SSI matrix can produce. **Verified** GL exactness and rational-convergence facts; the per-arc panel recipe is **claimed** (engineering synthesis, to validate against the existing curved-band regression).

### 2.3 Wiring: drop the degree guard, route by integrand class

> **Method / Content (claimed).** Replace `Curve3::Nurbs(n) if n.degree() > 1 => decline` with a dispatch on integrand class:
> - `Line`, `Circle`, `Ellipse`, degree-1/2 NURBS, degree-2 polynomial Bezier: fixed GL8 (current path), exact or near-exact.
> - degree-2 RATIONAL NURBS (hyperbola / parabola / conic from the upgraded `plane_cone`, dossier 58): adaptive GL8 panels, embedded error estimate, DECLINE on depth cap.
> - degree-3+ NURBS (recovered quartic seam, dossier 58 Path B): GL8 per knot span, error bounded by the fit's certified `tol_achieved`.
> Each arc reports an achieved-tolerance the face integrator folds into the face's mass certificate; the face declines if any arc's certificate misses the band.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the minimal code change that unblocks gap (b): the notched cone with a hyperbolic cut edge becomes integrable because its hyperbola arc, stored as a degree-2 rational NURBS, is now sampled by the generic arm instead of being rejected at the guard. It also future-proofs the integrator for every dossier-58 recovered seam in one stroke. **Claimed**, validated against the `mass == mesh` curved band.

## 3. The robust partial-fragment witness (detect that the iso-rectangle is NOT the face's region)

The third gap: a notched / partial-azimuth cone fragment takes the full-azimuth iso-rectangle path and OVER-counts the removed wedge (probe: 17.07 vs true 11.85) because `non_iso` detection misses pcurve-less cut edges. The fix is an area-witness that detects the iso-rectangle is not the face's actual region and routes to the boundary integrator over the true loop.

### 3.1 Why the iso-rectangle path silently over-counts

> **Citation.** Lee-Requicha (above), framing mass-property computation as "simple integrands on complicated domains," where "the modeling scheme used to describe the solid dominates the design of integration algorithms." OpenStax / standard parametric-surface integral region dependence (https://opentextbc.ca/calculusv3openstax/chapter/surface-integrals/).
> **Method / Content.** The boundary-flux per-face value is the flux over the face's TRUE parametric trim region. The iso-rectangle path is a fast specialization that assumes the trim region is the full `(u: 0 to 2*pi) x (v: v0 to v1)` rectangle (the full-azimuth band). When a cut removes a wedge, the true region is `(u: u0 to u1) x (v: v0 to v1)` with `u1 - u0 < 2*pi`, and the cut edges are the `u = u0`, `u = u1` boundaries. If `non_iso` detection misses these cut edges (because they have no pcurve, only a 3D `Curve3`), the integrator walks the full azimuth and computes the flux of the FULL band, over-counting by the removed wedge's flux. The over-count is exactly the wedge: in the probe, the full band integrates to 17.07 and the true notched fragment to 11.85, a difference of 5.22 that is the wedge flux. This is a SILENT wrong number (it is inside no band check because the integrator believes it integrated the right region), which is the dangerous failure: it risks WRONG, not just DECLINE.
> **Limitations.** The miss is specifically a pcurve-less cut edge: an edge represented only in 3D (a `Curve3` with no parametric image attached) is invisible to a `non_iso` check that looks for pcurves. This is the dossier-59 BuildPCurves gap surfacing in the integrator.
> **Kernel relevance.** The root cause is shared with dossier 59 (a seam edge without a pcurve on the incident face). The integrator-side fix is a WITNESS that does not depend on the cut edge having a pcurve: compare the analytic full-band area to the tessellated face area, and if they disagree, the iso-rectangle is wrong. **Verified** that region mismatch produces the wedge over-count; the witness is **claimed**.

### 3.2 The area-witness (the cone analog of the sphere rectangle-witness)

> **Citation.** Reconstruction from the sphere rectangle-witness already in Keel, plus tessellation-area cross-check practice (dossier 05 tessellation / faceting; the chord-vs-arc tolerance trap, MEMORY: tessellation-tolerance-trap). Closed-form band areas: frustum lateral area `pi (R + r) l`, spherical zone `2 pi R h`, cylinder band `2 pi r h` (https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SurfaceofRevolution.html).
> **Method / Content (claimed, the deliverable).** Before taking the iso-rectangle fast path, compute a cheap witness:
> 1. **Analytic full-band area `A_iso`.** For the candidate iso-rectangle, the lateral area has a closed form: cone band (frustum) `pi (R + r) l`, cylinder band `2 pi r h`, sphere zone `2 pi R h`. This is the area the iso-rectangle path is implicitly assuming.
> 2. **Tessellated face area `A_tess`.** Sum the triangle areas of the face's own tessellation (the mesh the `mass == mesh` gate already builds). This is the TRUE face area, independent of any pcurve.
> 3. **Compare.** If `|A_iso - A_tess| / A_iso` is within a tolerance set by the tessellation coarseness (section 3.3), the iso-rectangle IS the face: take the fast path. If they disagree (the notch removed area `A_iso` still counts), the iso-rectangle is NOT the face: route to the **boundary integrator over the true loop**, walking the actual boundary arcs (rim sub-arc + the two cut edges + apex anchor if present) via the generic arm of section 2.
> 4. **The witness is the cone dual of the sphere's existing rectangle-witness**, which already does exactly this for sphere faces (analytic zone area vs tessellated area) to decide full-revolution vs partial.
> **Exact oracle.** The notched-cone probe: the true partial fragment integrates to 11.85 (the band area / volume restricted to `u in [u0, u1]`, a closed form: the full frustum scaled by `(u1 - u0) / (2 pi)` plus the two planar cut-wedge contributions). The witness fires (`A_iso = full frustum area` differs from `A_tess = notched area`), the boundary integrator walks `u: u0 to u1`, and the result is 11.85, matching the closed form to the band.
> **Limitations.** The tessellated area is itself approximate (chord-vs-arc, MEMORY: tessellation-tolerance-trap), so the witness threshold must be set by the tessellation's known chord error, not tighter (section 3.3), else a coarse tessellation triggers a false mismatch on a genuine full band.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the fix that turns a SILENT WRONG into a correct route: the witness catches that the integrator was about to use the wrong region and sends it to the true boundary loop. It is the most important of the three gaps for the WRONG=0 contract, because gap (c) is the only one that can produce a wrong number rather than a decline. **Verified** that the area mismatch is a sound witness; the threshold and the route are **claimed**, validated against the 17.07-vs-11.85 probe.

### 3.3 Tolerance vs cone tessellation coarseness

> **Method / Content (claimed).** The witness threshold must exceed the tessellation's intrinsic area error so it does not fire on a genuine full band. For a curved face tessellated to chord tolerance `eps_chord`, the relative area error of the facet sum is `O((eps_chord / R)^?)`, bounded and known from the faceting parameters (dossier 05). Set the witness threshold to a few times that bound. A wedge notch removes a finite fraction of the azimuth (`(2 pi - (u1 - u0)) / (2 pi)`), which is FAR above the tessellation area error for any real notch, so the witness has a wide safe margin: real notches remove percent-to-tens-of-percent of area, tessellation error is sub-percent. The witness is therefore robust: it fires loudly on a real notch and never on tessellation noise.
> **Limitations.** A sub-tessellation-scale sliver notch (a notch removing less area than the tessellation error) is below the witness floor; such a sliver is a dossier-59 Q5 sub-tolerance-sliver case and is declined upstream, so the witness never needs to resolve it.
> **Kernel relevance.** This sets the witness tolerance honestly: tied to the tessellation coarseness, wide margin for real notches, and the sub-resolution sliver is an upstream DECLINE, never a witness false-negative that lets a wrong region through. **Claimed**, tunable against the faceting parameters.

## 4. Hyperbola `Curve3` variant: exact arm vs always recover to NURBS

The fourth scope question: should Keel add a `Curve3::Hyperbola` exact variant, or always recover a hyperbolic trim to NURBS, and what is the impact on the integrator (this dossier) and on the seam-equality compare (dossier 59)?

> **Citation.** L. Piegl, W. Tiller, *The NURBS Book*, 2nd ed., Springer 1997, ch. 7 (Conics and Circles): every conic arc, INCLUDING the hyperbola, has an EXACT finite rational-quadratic Bezier representation (three control points, one interior weight `w > 1` for the hyperbola). https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-59223-2_7 ; notes: https://pages.mtu.edu/~shene/COURSES/cs3621/NOTES/spline/NURBS/RB-conics.html . This is the same conclusion dossier 58 section 3.1 reached for the SSI seam.
> **Method / Content.** A hyperbola arc is EXACTLY a degree-2 rational NURBS with `w > 1`, `tol_achieved = 0.0`. So a `Curve3::Hyperbola` enum arm would buy NOTHING in exactness over the rational-NURBS representation: both are exact. The trade is purely engineering:
> - **Add `Curve3::Hyperbola` (exact analytic):** the integrator gets a closed-form `point(t)` / `tangent(t)` and could even use a closed-form boundary integral for the hyperbolic arc (no quadrature). The seam-equality compare (dossier 59) gets a typed, canonical curve that compares EQUAL by parameters (center, axes, asymptote frame) rather than by control points. BUT: it adds a new arm to EVERY `Curve3` match in the kernel (integrator, tessellator, SSI, stitch, serialization), a broad surface-area cost, and it duplicates a representation the rational NURBS already holds exactly.
> - **Always recover to degree-2 rational NURBS:** ONE representation for every conic (circle / ellipse already native as typed, parabola / hyperbola as rational NURBS), ONE integrator path (the generic arm of section 2 handles it), ONE stitch path. The integrator treats it like any rational conic (adaptive GL8 panels, section 2.2). The seam-equality compare (dossier 59) relies on IDENTITY sharing (compute once, share the `EdgeId`), so it does NOT need a typed hyperbola to compare equal: the seam is the same cached NURBS object referenced by both operands, equality is `EntityId` identity, not parameter compare. The only cost is the rational-integrand adaptive quadrature (vs a closed form), which is bounded and cheap.
> **Limitations.** The closed-form hyperbolic boundary integral (the only real win of a typed variant) is a modest speed gain on a rare face type; it does not change correctness, since the adaptive rational quadrature already hits the band. A typed hyperbola would also need a canonical parametrization to compare equal, which is exactly the canonicalization dossier 59 Q2 already does at the `EdgeId` level for ALL seams, so the typed variant does not simplify that either.
> **Kernel relevance and recommendation.** **Recommendation: do NOT add a `Curve3::Hyperbola` variant. Always recover the hyperbola (and parabola) to a degree-2 rational NURBS (`tol_achieved = 0.0`, exact), and integrate it through the generic boundary-arc arm (section 2) with adaptive GL8 panels.** This keeps ONE integrator path and ONE stitch / equality path (dossier 59 identity sharing), matches dossier 58's identical conclusion for the SSI seam (no new exact enum arm; rational NURBS for parabola/hyperbola), and costs only bounded adaptive quadrature on a rare arc. The exactness is identical (rational NURBS is exact for a conic); the only thing forgone is a closed-form integral that the adaptive quadrature already matches to the band. The surface-area cost of a new `Curve3` arm across the whole kernel is not worth a speed gain on a rare face. **Verified** that rational NURBS is exact for the hyperbola; the recommendation is the **claimed** engineering call, consistent with dossier 58 section 3.1.

## Cross-cutting three-bucket catalog

Which curved-face mass cases have (a) an EXACT closed-form volume / area reference, (b) need a certified boundary-flux quadrature to a tolerance, (c) fall back to tessellation.

**(a) EXACT closed-form volume / area reference (oracle at `1e-9`, far inside the 2% band):**
all **full-revolution and coaxial iso-band** natural-quadric faces. Cylinder band (`2 pi r h` lateral, `pi r^2 h` volume contribution); cone band / frustum (`pi (R + r) l` lateral, `(pi h / 3)(R^2 + R r + r^2)` volume); **cone tip with apex anchor** (`pi r l`, `(1/3) pi r^2 h`, the gap-(a) fix); sphere zone / cap (`2 pi R h` lateral, the spherical-cap volume); any solid-of-revolution face by Pappus's centroid theorem. These are the EXACT oracles for the apex anchor (section 1) and the partial-fragment witness (section 3): the closed form is the truth the integrator must reproduce.

**(b) Certified boundary-flux quadrature to a tolerance (the generic arm, section 2):**
any **non-iso curved face** whose boundary includes a non-trivial `Curve3` arc: a **partial-azimuth cone / cylinder / sphere fragment** (the notched cases, integrated over the true `u` range, gap c); a face bounded by a **conic SSI seam** (cone-cylinder ellipse, cylinder-cylinder quartic, the dossier-58 seams); a face bounded by a **hyperbola / parabola arc** (the plane-parallel-to-cone-axis cut, gap b, as a degree-2 rational NURBS); a face bounded by a **recovered-NURBS quartic seam** (dossier 58 Path B). All integrate by the boundary-flux loop with fixed GL8 per polynomial span or adaptive GL8 panels for rational arcs, each carrying an achieved-tolerance folded into the face certificate, DECLINE on miss.

**(c) Tessellation fallback (the `mesh` side of the gate, and the witness input):**
the face's tessellated area / volume is ALWAYS computed (it is the `mass == mesh` gate's `mesh` side) and serves two roles: as the **consistency oracle** for every curved face (the 2% band is mass-vs-this-mesh), and as the **area-witness input** for gap (c) (analytic band area vs this tessellated area). A face whose boundary-flux certificate cannot reach the band (an uncertifiable rational arc past the panel cap, an uncertified recovered seam) **falls back to DECLINE**, not to the tessellated value: the tessellation is the cross-check, never the answer, because using a chord-approximated area as the mass would risk a wrong number inside the band (MEMORY: tessellation-tolerance-trap). Tessellation is oracle and witness, not fallback-answer.

## Patent awareness

- **The divergence-theorem / boundary-flux mass-property method** (Lee-Requicha 1982, Gonzalez-Ochoa-McCammon-Peters 1998, Lasserre 1998) is old prior art, any patents long expired. The reduction of a volume integral to per-face surface flux to a parameter-plane boundary line integral is textbook (Mortenson, *Geometric Modeling*). Build on it freely; this is the foundation.
- **Pappus's centroid theorem and the cone / frustum / spherical-zone closed forms** are classical mathematics, no patent. These are the exact oracles.
- **Gauss-Legendre quadrature and adaptive panel subdivision** are classical numerical analysis (Trefethen; Gautschi rational-Gauss). The embedded GL8-vs-GL4 error estimate is standard. No patent.
- **Piegl-Tiller rational-quadratic conic representation** (the hyperbola as a degree-2 rational NURBS) is published 1997 textbook material, the standard NURBS conic; no patent risk. Same posture as dossier 58.
- **Spainhour-Weiss GWN-over-trimmed-NURBS (2025)** for the Stokes / boundary-line-integral formulation is recent academic; no patent flagged, but patent-check before shipping a verbatim implementation. Keel uses it only as the warrant that the boundary integrand is `(point, tangent)`-only (a general fact), not as a copied algorithm; the underlying GWN (Jacobson 2013) is clear prior art. This is the same flag dossier 59 raised.
- **Post-2006 mass-property / moment-computation patents** may exist around specific commercial CAD moment engines (a "Systems and methods for determining mass properties of a modeled object," US 10,783,708, surfaced in search) and trimmed-surface integration heuristics. Flag for claim-reading: Keel's posture (closed-form oracle where exact, certified boundary-flux quadrature otherwise, both from pre-2006 prior art) is the design-around. Keel computes mass from the published divergence / Pappus / GL machinery, not from any vendor moment heuristic. Verify any specific commercial moment-engine optimization against live patents before copying it; the recommendation copies none.

Net: **build on the pre-2000 divergence-theorem / Pappus / Gauss-Legendre / Piegl-Tiller foundation, which is all expired or unpatented; the apex anchor, generic arm, and area-witness are derived from that foundation, not from any post-2006 method; the path is clear.**

## Synthesis: ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE

**ADOPT.**
1. **The cone-apex anchor** (section 1) as the exact dual of the sphere pole anchor: detect the full-revolution-with-apex topology, insert the apex as a single analytic boundary node (the finite `lim_{v -> 0} P_v` slant tangent, never a GL sample at `v = 0`), close the loop, and validate against the exact cone / frustum volume. This converts the gap-(a) "unsupported boundary winding" decline to a PASS with a `1e-9` oracle.
2. **The generic boundary-arc arm** (section 2): drop the `Curve3::Nurbs(n) if n.degree() > 1` guard; sample ANY `Curve3` for the `(point, tangent)` the `node()` helper needs; fixed GL8 per polynomial span for polynomial / piecewise-polynomial arcs, adaptive GL8 panels with an embedded error estimate for rational conic arcs (hyperbola / sharp ellipse), DECLINE on panel-depth cap. This unblocks gap (b) and future-proofs the integrator for every dossier-58 seam in one change.
3. **The area-witness** (section 3) as the cone dual of the sphere rectangle-witness: analytic band area vs tessellated face area, threshold tied to tessellation coarseness; on mismatch, route off the iso-rectangle fast path to the boundary integrator over the TRUE loop (rim sub-arc + cut edges + apex anchor). This catches the gap-(c) silent over-count (17.07 vs 11.85), the only gap that can produce a WRONG number, and routes it to the correct region.
4. **Recover hyperbola / parabola to degree-2 rational NURBS, no new `Curve3` arm** (section 4): one representation, one integrator path, one stitch / equality path (dossier 59 identity sharing). Exactness is identical; the only cost is bounded adaptive quadrature on a rare arc.
5. **The exact closed forms (Pappus / frustum / spherical-zone) as the per-fix oracle**, with the tessellation as the `mass == mesh` cross-check and the area-witness input, never as the fallback answer.

**AVOID.**
- **Sampling the boundary integrand at the apex / pole `v = 0`** (a `1/v` spurious singularity). Supply the apex / pole as an analytic anchor node (the removable-singularity lift), exactly as the sphere pole anchor already does.
- **Using the tessellated area / volume as the mass answer** when the boundary-flux certificate misses the band. The tessellation is chord-approximate (MEMORY: tessellation-tolerance-trap); using it as the mass risks a wrong number inside the 2% band. DECLINE instead.
- **Fixed GL8 on a sharp rational hyperbola arc** (convergence slows as the conic weight approaches 1). Use adaptive panels with an error estimate; DECLINE on the depth cap.
- **Adding a `Curve3::Hyperbola` exact variant.** It adds an arm to every `Curve3` match across the kernel for a speed gain on a rare face, duplicating a representation rational NURBS already holds exactly. Recover to rational NURBS instead.
- **Letting the iso-rectangle fast path run without the area-witness.** That is the gap-(c) silent over-count, the WRONG risk. Always witness before the fast path on a face that could be notched.

**INVESTIGATE.**
- The embedded error estimate for the adaptive arm: GL8-vs-GL4 difference vs Richardson halving, and the right panel-depth cap that keeps the rare sharp-hyperbola case from spurious DECLINE without admitting an under-resolved number.
- Whether the apex anchor and the partial-azimuth witness COMPOSE cleanly for the partial-azimuth tip fragment (apex anchor on the `v` side, witness-routed boundary on the `u` side); validate against a wedge-cut cone tip.
- Whether the sphere pole anchor and the cone apex anchor can share ONE "degenerate-revolution-boundary anchor" abstraction parameterized by the surface's `local_geometry`, reducing duplication and pre-building the torus / general-revolution anchor.
- Closed-form boundary integrals for the conic arcs (circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola) to replace adaptive quadrature with an exact term where the algebra is tractable (a speed and certainty win, optional given the quadrature already hits the band).

**Recommended FIRST MILESTONE (narrowest honest slice with an exact oracle):**
**The cone-apex anchor for a full-revolution cone-tip fragment, validated against the exact cone volume `(1/3) pi r^2 h` and lateral area `pi r l`.** This is the smallest, highest-certainty fix: it is the exact structural dual of the already-working sphere pole anchor, it has a closed-form `1e-9` oracle (cone / frustum formulas), and it directly converts the gap-(a) decline into a PASS. It exercises the removable-apex-singularity lift and the complement / island detection (tip vs frustum) without yet needing the generic arm or the witness, proving the anchor mechanism before the broader integrator work. It unblocks the most common real curved boolean (a conical part cut by a plane, leaving a tip).

**Follow-up ladder:**
1. Cone-apex anchor for the full-revolution tip (the first milestone), exact cone / frustum oracle.
2. The area-witness (gap c) for the partial-azimuth cone / cylinder / sphere fragment, validated against the 17.07-vs-11.85 probe and the closed-form notched-band volume. This is the WRONG-risk gap, so it is second.
3. The generic boundary-arc arm (gap b): drop the degree guard, fixed GL8 per polynomial span, validated on a recovered-cubic seam face.
4. The adaptive rational arm: adaptive GL8 panels for the hyperbola / sharp-ellipse arc (the plane-parallel-to-cone-axis notched cone), validated against the closed-form hyperbolic-band area.
5. Shared degenerate-revolution-boundary anchor abstraction (apex + pole unified), pre-building the torus / general-solid-of-revolution anchor.
6. Optional: closed-form conic boundary integrals replacing adaptive quadrature where tractable.

**Configuration to DECLINE forever (justified):**
**A curved face whose boundary-flux certificate cannot reach the 2% band after the panel-depth cap, OR whose boundary arc is an uncertified recovered-NURBS seam** (dossier 59 Q5 class 4). Justification: a sharp rational conic arc approaching its asymptote, or a recovered seam whose own fit `tol_achieved` exceeds the band, cannot be integrated to the band with bounded work; emitting the under-resolved boundary-flux number would risk a WRONG mass inside the gate. Declining the face (and the boolean) is honest and rare (real CAD conic arcs are well inside the asymptote, real recovered seams certify). It moves from permanent-decline to staged-recovery only if a closed-form conic boundary integral (INVESTIGATE) removes the quadrature uncertainty, or the seam fit is re-certified tighter. Also DECLINE-forever (upstream, not the integrator's job): a seam through the apex as a degenerate conic, and a sub-tessellation-scale sliver notch, both declined before the integrator runs (dossier 59 Q5 classes 2 to 3).
