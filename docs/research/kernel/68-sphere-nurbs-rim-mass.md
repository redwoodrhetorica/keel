THE GAP this dossier closes (confirmed, Add.283). A sphere face is ONE periodic `(u, v)` domain: `u in [0, 2*pi)` seam-identified, `v in [-pi/2, +pi/2]` poles. When a sphere is wrapped by a non-coaxial `{cyl, cone}`, the two encircling NURBS intersection loops split the sphere into a **BAND** between the rims (a winding-2 region that wraps the full u-period, no pole) plus two **CAPS** (each pole-containing). Two stacked sub-defects, the sphere analogs of the cyl/cyl pair in dossier 66:

1. **the band wraps the full u-period and the anchor is wrong.** The encircling-band region has no pole inside, so the sphere pole anchor (built for a full-revolution pole-bearing face, dossier 60) must NOT fire for the band, and the band's u-winding is the 2-wrap case (the dossier-66 barrel analog) requiring a per-fin winding handling, not a pole closure; and

## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **A spherical face bounded by arbitrary closed NURBS loops integrates by the SAME boundary-flux loop the kernel already runs, with ZERO new integration theory: the per-face flux equals the line integral over each trim loop in its stored orientation, walked through the existing `node()` accumulator that samples the NURBS rim (point, tangent, weight) via the `FinCurve::Nurbs` arm. The CAP-vs-BAND distinction is purely about the ANCHOR. A CAP contains a pole, so its boundary in parameter space is open at the pole (the `v = +-pi/2` line collapses to a point with no explicit rim arc); the sphere POLE anchor (dossier 60) closes it, exactly as today, and the rim is whatever NURBS loop bounds the cap on the open side. A BAND contains NO pole; both its v-extremes are explicit NURBS rims, so there is NO pole anchor, the loop is already closed by the two rims, and the only subtlety is the full-u-period winding: a genuinely encircling band wraps u twice (the dossier-66 barrel case, |net| >= 2) and needs the per-fin winding handling, NOT a pole closure. Firing a pole anchor on a band (or omitting it on a cap) is the precise failure mode. The cap-vs-band-vs-anchor decision is made by the SAME area-witness that already routes the cone/sphere iso-rectangle: compute the analytic spherical-zone area `2 pi R h` (band) or spherical-cap area `2 pi R h` (cap, h measured from the pole) for the candidate iso-zone, compare to the tessellated face area; if they disagree the iso-zone is NOT the face (the rim is a non-circular NURBS loop, or the cap is holed) and the integrator routes to the boundary-flux loop over the TRUE NURBS rims plus the pole anchor only when a pole is actually enclosed. A multi-hole cap subtracts each inner NURBS bore loop by its stored CW orientation, identical to dossier 66. The guard is removed ONLY when the boundary-flux mass equals the INDEPENDENT analytic oracle (spherical cap/zone closed forms assembled by inclusion-exclusion, cross-checked by the tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume`) AND the tessellated mesh agrees, all to the 2 percent band; DECLINE on any disagreement. That independent oracle, not mass==mesh self-consistency, is what forbids a self-consistent WRONG and keeps DECLINE-never-WRONG intact.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. The divergence theorem reduces the solid's volume to a sum of per-face surface-flux integrals; the Green-slab reduction sends each per-face spherical term to a 1D line integral over the face's trim boundary in the `(u, v)` plane (dossier 60). For a sphere, the surface element is `|S_u x S_v| = R^2 cos(v)` (with `v` the latitude in `[-pi/2, +pi/2]`), vanishing at the poles `v = +-pi/2`, exactly the removable Jacobian singularity the pole anchor lifts. The line integral equals the enclosed flux ONLY when the boundary loop is CLOSED: a band's loop is closed by its two explicit NURBS rims and needs no anchor; a cap's loop is open at the enclosed pole and is closed by the pole anchor (the dual of the cone-apex anchor). The cap-vs-band question is therefore the anchor question, and it is answered by detecting pole enclosure, which the area-witness already does (a full-zone cap touching a pole has the cap area, an encircling band has the zone area, a notched or NURBS-rimmed region disagrees with both). The NURBS rim itself is no new theory: the `FinCurve::Nurbs` arm (Add.271) already samples any `Curve3` for the `(point, tangent)` the `node()` helper needs, so a circle rim and an arbitrary NURBS rim are walked by the identical GL loop, the circle being the trivial case. Inner bore loops subtract by their CW storage (dossier 66). Fix the anchor routing and wire the witness, and the same integrator reads the band, the cap, and the holed cap to the band, with the coaxial circle-rim case landing on the exact spherical-zone / cap closed forms as the oracle.

## 1. The boundary-flux integral over a NURBS-bounded spherical region (cap vs band, the anchor term)

The first scope question: state the exact Green / divergence surface integral for the enclosed volume contributed by a spherical face bounded by arbitrary closed NURBS loops, including the anchor term, and the cap-vs-band distinction that decides which anchor keeps the flux exact.

### 1.1 The sphere parameterization, the pole Jacobian singularity, and the divergence reduction

> **Citation.** C. Gonzalez-Ochoa, S. McCammon, J. Peters, "Computing Moments of Objects Enclosed by Piecewise Polynomial Surfaces," *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 17(3):143-157, 1998 (divergence theorem reduces volume / moments to a sum of per-face surface-flux integrals, exact for polynomial patches, efficient for 10^5 patches). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2459122_Computing_Moments_of_Piecewise_Polynomial_Surfaces ; Purdue tech report: https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cstech/1348/ . Y. T. Lee, A. A. G. Requicha, "Algorithms for computing the volume and other integral properties of solids. I and II," *Comm. ACM* 25(9):635-650, 1982. *The Divergence Theorem*, OpenStax Calculus Volume 3 (the flux-to-volume reduction, valid for regions bounded by spheres and ellipsoids). https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-3/pages/6-8-the-divergence-theorem .
> **Method / Content.** Pick a field `F` with `div F = 1` (`F = (x, 0, 0)` gives `V = integral over boundary of x dy dz`); the volume is a sum of per-face surface fluxes `integral_face F . n dA`. For the unit-axis sphere of radius `R`, center `C`, the standard parameterization is
>
> ```
> S(u, v) = C + R ( cos(v) cos(u) e1 + cos(v) sin(u) e2 + sin(v) a ),   u in [0, 2*pi),  v in [-pi/2, +pi/2]
> ```
>
> with `(e1, e2, a)` an orthonormal frame and `a` the polar axis. The partials give the surface element
>
> ```
> |S_u x S_v| = R^2 cos(v)
> ```
>
> which **vanishes at the poles** `v = +-pi/2` (`cos(+-pi/2) = 0`), the sphere analog of the cone-apex `v`-Jacobian. Integrating `R^2 cos(v)` over `v in [v0, v1]`, `u in [0, 2*pi)` gives the spherical-zone lateral area `2 pi R^2 (sin v1 - sin v0) = 2 pi R h` (section 4), the closed form. The Green-slab sends the inner u-integral to a closed form, leaving a 1D boundary integral over the trim region's boundary in the `(u, v)` plane, which `integrate_face_green` evaluates by GL8 per node, accumulating u-winding and slab samples.
> **Limitations.** Gonzalez-Ochoa-Peters is exact for polynomial patches; the sphere is trigonometric, so the inner u-integral is closed-form (Keel handles it in `local_geometry`) but the outer boundary integral over a non-iso NURBS rim is quadrature, not exact in general. The reduction assumes the per-face boundary loop is CLOSED; an open loop (a cap missing its pole closure) gives the flux of an unclosed region, wrong by the missing-pole flux.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the theory under `integrate_face_green` for the sphere. The load-bearing fact: the boundary-flux value of a spherical face equals the line integral over its CLOSED boundary loop, where "closed" means the NURBS rims plus the pole anchor whenever a pole is enclosed. **Verified** (the divergence / Green-slab reduction is standard; the `R^2 cos v` Jacobian and its pole zero are elementary).

### 1.2 The cap vs the band: which anchor keeps the flux exact

> **Citation.** The sphere pole anchor as the dual of the cone-apex anchor (dossier 60 section 1, the removable-singularity lift `lim_{v -> +-pi/2}`). Spherical-cap / spherical-segment geometry: *Spherical cap*, Wikipedia (cap with one base touching a pole; surface `2 pi R h`, volume `(1/3) pi h^2 (3R - h)`). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_cap . *Spherical segment* (zone with two bases, no pole; lateral `2 pi R h`, volume `(1/6) pi h (3 r1^2 + 3 r2^2 + h^2)`). https://www.math10.com/en/geometry/sphere.html ; https://mathalino.com/reviewer/solid-mensuration-solid-geometry/spherical-segment .
> **Method / Content.** A spherical face's trim region is a connected subset of the `(u, v)` domain. Two topologies arise from a non-coaxial wrap:
>
> - **CAP (contains a pole).** The region reaches `v = +pi/2` or `v = -pi/2`. In parameter space the pole is the degenerate `v = +-pi/2` line collapsed to a single 3D point; there is no explicit `Curve3` rim there. The boundary is therefore OPEN at the pole and is closed by the **POLE ANCHOR**: a single analytic boundary node at the pole `C +- R a`, carrying the polar u-winding turn computed as the limit `lim_{v -> +-pi/2}` (never a GL sample at the singular `v`, exactly as dossier 60 lifts the cone apex). The other side of the cap is bounded by ONE closed NURBS rim loop (the wrap intersection curve). So a cap's loop = `(NURBS rim, walked in stored orientation) + (pole anchor)`. The flux is exact because the loop is closed and the pole's `R^2 cos v -> 0` Jacobian makes the anchor's contribution the finite analytic limit.
> - **BAND (no pole).** The region is bounded on BOTH v-extremes by explicit NURBS rims (the two wrap intersection curves) and is therefore ALREADY CLOSED with no degenerate edge: there is NO pole inside, so **the pole anchor must NOT fire**. A band's loop = `(upper NURBS rim) + (lower NURBS rim)`, both walked in stored orientation, with the seam-slit u-bookkeeping (dossier 45 / 64) handling the periodic wrap. The flux is exact because both rims are explicit and the loop closes without any anchor.
>
> **The anchor rule, stated once: anchor at the pole IF AND ONLY IF a pole is enclosed (the cap case); a band encloses no pole and takes NO anchor.** Firing a pole anchor on a band closes a loop that was already closed, double-counting the polar cap flux (an over-read); omitting the anchor on a cap leaves the loop open, dropping the polar cap flux (an under-read). The `probe_wrap` 16.07-vs-17.17 / 67.58-vs-121 errors are exactly this anchor-misrouting plus the rim-vs-circle region error of section 4.
> **The per-loop sign/orientation rule.** Each loop is walked in its STORED B-rep orientation: the OUTER loop CCW about the face normal `n_out = sense x natural_normal` (dossier 46), every INNER (hole) loop CW. The CW inner loops contribute opposite-sign flux, so holes subtract automatically (dossier 66 section 2). The pole anchor carries the sign of the polar turn consistent with the outer loop's orientation, so a flipped face normal flips the rim sense and the anchor sense together, preserving the result.
> **Limitations.** The anchor assumes a TRUE sphere pole (a clean degenerate `v = +-pi/2`), not a pole removed by the wrap (a cut passing exactly through the pole is an upstream DECLINE, dossier 45 / sphere-split-integration-trap). The band assumes both rims are genuine closed encircling loops; a band that degenerates (one rim pinching to the pole) is the cap limit and is declined or re-routed upstream. Proper nesting (one outer rim, inner bores strictly inside, bores disjoint) is the dossier-64/65 precondition.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the exact cap-vs-band anchor theory, the dossier's first deliverable. The fix is a pole-enclosure test gating the existing pole anchor: enclose a pole, anchor; enclose none, do not. The NURBS rim is fed to the existing `FinCurve::Nurbs` arm either way. **Verified** that the pole Jacobian is removable and the cap/zone closed forms hold; **claimed** the pole-enclosure-gated anchor routing (the construction, dual of the cone-apex island detection of dossier 60), to validate against the `probe_wrap` and the circle-rim baseline.

### 1.3 The NURBS rim is the trivial generalisation of the circle rim

> **Citation.** Dossier 60 section 2 (the generic boundary-arc arm `FinCurve::Nurbs`, Add.271, samples any `Curve3` for point + tangent + weight). J. Spainhour, K. Weiss, "Robust Containment Queries over Collections of Trimmed NURBS Surfaces via Generalized Winding Numbers," *ACM TOG*, 2025 (the boundary line integral depends only on `(point, tangent)` along the trim curve, evaluated by adaptive quadrature to arbitrary accuracy; the boundary "may contain multiple disconnected curves" iterated uniformly). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11435 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11435v2 .
> **Method / Content.** The boundary integrand for a spherical face is `g(point(t), tangent(t))` where `g` is the slab/winding integrand `local_geometry` supplies for the sphere; it depends ONLY on the boundary point and tangent, NOT on whether the rim is a latitude circle, an ellipse, a Viviani-type sphere-cylinder intersection, or a general NURBS loop. The circle rim (the coaxial-cut baseline) is the SPECIAL case where the NURBS loop happens to be a v=const iso-curve; the integrator does not branch on it. So the generalisation from circle rims to arbitrary NURBS loops is ALREADY DONE at the arc level by the `FinCurve::Nurbs` arm: the dossier's work is the anchor routing (section 1.2) and the region witness (section 4), not the rim sampling. Spainhour-Weiss is the warrant that an arbitrary trimmed-NURBS boundary integrates by a 1D line integral over `(point, tangent)`, and that multiple disconnected boundary curves (the band's two rims, the holed cap's outer rim plus bores) are iterated uniformly with orientation-dependent sign.
> **Limitations.** Spainhour-Weiss does NOT treat periodic-surface seams or pole degeneracies (their paper extracts a parametric disk only near intersection-line cusps, not near poles); the pole anchor and the seam-slit u-bookkeeping are Keel's own (dossier 60 / 45), not theirs. A sharp NURBS rim (a near-tangent wrap producing a high-curvature seam) needs the adaptive-panel GL with a depth cap and DECLINE on exceed (dossier 60 section 2.2), preserving DECLINE-never-WRONG.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is why the NURBS-rim generalisation is cheap: the rim sampler is reused verbatim from Add.271, and the only sphere-specific additions are the anchor gate and the witness. **Verified** that the boundary integrand is `(point, tangent)`-only and curve-type-indifferent (Spainhour-Weiss; dossier 60); **claimed** that the existing `FinCurve::Nurbs` arm needs no change for the sphere rim, to validate on the NURBS-rim probe.

## 2. The winding-2 encircling band (the cylinder-barrel analog)

The second scope question: diagnose the band case where two encircling rims make the region wrap the full u-period, the sphere analog of the cylinder barrel that needed the `|net| >= 2` per-fin winding handling (dossier 66), and specify when the flip is needed versus when it breaks the cap/pole anchor.

### 2.1 Why the encircling band wraps u twice

> **Citation.** Dossier 66 (the cylinder 2-wrap barrel, the per-fin winding flip gated on `|net| >= 2`). *Spherical zone / segment* (a zone between two latitudes, no pole; the full-revolution band wraps the entire azimuth). https://www.math10.com/en/geometry/sphere.html ; MathWorld *Surface of Revolution* (zone area `2 pi R h`). https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SurfaceofRevolution.html . A. Jacobson, L. Kavan, O. Sorkine-Hornung, "Robust Inside-Outside Segmentation Using Generalized Winding Numbers," *ACM TOG* 32(4), 2013 (the integer winding of a closed boundary about a region). https://igl.ethz.ch/projects/winding-number/ .
> **Method / Content.** When a sphere is wrapped by a non-coaxial `{cyl, cone}`, the kept BAND between the two intersection rims encircles the polar axis: as `u` runs `0 to 2*pi`, the band's boundary returns to its start having enclosed the axis once on the top rim and once on the bottom rim, so the band's region wraps the FULL u-period. In the Green-slab u-winding accumulation, an encircling band that is the SHARED region of a genuine 2-wrap configuration (the sphere analog of the interpenetrating barrel) accumulates a net u-winding of magnitude 2, not 1. This is the EXACT dossier-66 barrel situation transplanted to the sphere: the per-fin winding contributions must be combined with the gated flip so the net winding is the true `+-2`, not a spuriously cancelled `0` or an under-counted `+-1`. Without the flip, the band's slab integral mis-weights and the mass reads wrong (the 16.07 / 67.58 family).
> **Limitations.** The `|net| >= 2` gate is the SAME conservative gate as dossier 66: it fires only on a genuinely doubly-wrapped region, never on an ordinary single-wrap band, so it cannot spuriously double a simple zone. The gate is keyed on the accumulated net u-winding, an integer robust to the rim's NURBS shape (Jacobson winding is shape-indifferent).
> **Kernel relevance.** The encircling band reuses the dossier-66 per-fin winding flip verbatim; the sphere adds nothing to the winding logic, only the pole-enclosure gate that keeps the anchor off the band. **Verified** that an encircling band wraps the full u-period and that winding is shape-indifferent (Jacobson); **claimed** that the dossier-66 `|net| >= 2` flip applies unchanged to the sphere band, to validate against the `probe_wrap` band fragment.

### 2.2 When the flip is needed versus when it breaks the pole anchor

> **Citation.** Dossier 60 (the pole anchor, fired only for a full-revolution pole-bearing face); dossier 66 (the flip, fired only on `|net| >= 2`); dossier 46 (the sense convention that keeps rim and anchor orientation consistent).
> **Method / Content (claimed, the diagnostic).** The flip and the anchor are MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE by topology, and conflating them is the bug:
> 1. **BAND, `|net| >= 2`: flip YES, anchor NO.** The doubly-encircling band has no pole, so the pole anchor must stay off; the per-fin winding flip supplies the correct `+-2` winding. This is the genuine barrel-analog case.
> 2. **BAND, `|net| == 1`: flip NO, anchor NO.** An ordinary single-wrap zone (one encircling rim pair from a shallow wrap) is closed by its two rims with winding `+-1`; neither the flip nor the anchor fires.
> 3. **CAP, pole enclosed: anchor YES, flip NO.** The cap reaches a pole; the pole anchor closes the loop and carries the polar turn. The flip must NOT fire: a cap is single-wrap (one rim plus the pole), and applying the `|net| >= 2` flip would double the polar cap flux.
> 4. **The failure modes.** Firing the flip on a cap doubles the cap; firing the anchor on a `|net| >= 2` band adds a phantom polar cap to a region that has no pole; both are over-reads. The decision is driven by TWO independent booleans, `pole_enclosed` (gates the anchor) and `net_winding_ge_2` (gates the flip), computed from the trim topology, never assumed from the iso-zone.
> **Limitations.** The two booleans must be computed from the ACTUAL trim region (the witness-validated NURBS rims and pole enclosure), not from a default iso-zone, else a notched or NURBS-rimmed region takes the wrong pair. This is why the area-witness (section 4) is the gate: it confirms the region is what the booleans assume before the fast path runs.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the precise rule the prompt asks for, the band-flip vs pole-anchor disambiguation. Two orthogonal gates, `pole_enclosed` and `net_winding_ge_2`, drive the anchor and the flip independently; never the same control. **Claimed** the two-boolean diagnostic, to validate against the `probe_wrap` band-plus-two-caps decomposition (the band takes flip-yes/anchor-no, each cap takes anchor-yes/flip-no).

## 3. Multi-hole caps: outer-loop flux minus each inner NURBS bore loop

The third scope question: a cap carrying inner NURBS loops (a bore through the cap), integrated as outer-loop flux MINUS each inner loop, with inner-loop orientation, the sphere analog of dossier 66's inner-hole integration.

> **Citation.** Dossier 66 section 2 (the multiply-connected inner-loop subtraction: `flux(outer) - sum_k flux(inner_k)`, the minus supplied by stored CW orientation, no special-casing). *Green's Theorem on General Regions* (Lumen, Calculus III: inner holes given negative orientation, the subtraction effect). https://courses.lumenlearning.com/calculus3/chapter/greens-theorem-on-general-regions/ . *6.4 Green's Theorem*, OpenStax (the flux form extends to a region with finitely many holes). https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-3/pages/6-4-greens-theorem . Spainhour-Weiss 2025 (boundary may contain multiple disconnected curves iterated with orientation-dependent sign). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11435 .
> **Method / Content.** A cap with `K` inner NURBS bore loops (a hole drilled through the spherical shell where another body punched out a window) is a multiply-connected spherical face. Green's theorem holds verbatim on its `(u, v)` trim region: the boundary `partial D` is the outer rim (or the pole anchor, if the cap reaches a pole) traversed CCW, plus each bore loop traversed CW, oriented so the material stays on the LEFT. The per-face flux is
>
> ```
> flux(cap) = flux(outer rim or pole-anchored outer loop)  -  sum_{k=1..K} flux(inner bore loop k)
> ```
>
> where the minus on the inner loops is the AUTOMATIC consequence of their CW storage, exactly as dossier 66 establishes for the cylinder wall. The integrator iterates ALL loops of the face through the same `node()` accumulator, sampling each inner NURBS bore arc via the `FinCurve::Nurbs` arm; the CW inner loops contribute opposite-sign flux, so the bores subtract themselves. The sphere adds nothing to the inner-loop logic over dossier 66; the only sphere-specific composition is that the OUTER loop may itself be pole-anchored (a holed cap that still reaches the pole), in which case the outer-loop flux is the pole-anchored cap flux and the bores subtract from it.
> **Limitations.** Requires the bore loops to be stored CW (the dossier-64/65 deliverable); a wrong-sense inner loop ADDS instead of subtracts (an over-read the independent oracle of section 5 must catch). The bore arcs are SSI seam curves (sphere-vs-bore intersections), integrated by the generic arm with adaptive panels and DECLINE on the depth cap (dossier 60 section 2.2). Proper nesting (bores strictly inside the cap rim, mutually disjoint, none touching the pole) is the upstream precondition; a bore through the pole is a degenerate DECLINE.
> **Kernel relevance.** The holed-cap case is the dossier-66 inner-loop subtraction with a possible pole-anchored outer loop. Iterate `face.loops`, inner bores subtract by CW orientation, compose with the pole anchor on the outer side. **Verified** the multiply-connected Green's theorem and the orientation-driven sign (Lumen / OpenStax / Spainhour-Weiss; dossier 66); **claimed** the composition with the pole anchor on a holed cap, to validate against a holed-cap probe.

## 4. The area-witness (the WRONG-risk gate) and its spherical closed forms

The fourth scope question, and the dossier's first-class soundness deliverable: the sphere dual of the cyl/cone rectangle-witness, the analytic cap/band area versus the tessellated face area, the tolerance tied to tessellation coarseness, and the route to the boundary integrator on mismatch. This witness MUST be in place before any wrap decline guard is removed, else a self-consistent WRONG could slip within the op-volume-bound slack.

### 4.1 The exact spherical cap, zone, lune, and wedge closed forms

> **Citation.** *Spherical cap*, Wikipedia (zone/cap surface `2 pi R h`, cap volume `(1/3) pi h^2 (3R - h)`). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_cap . *Spherical segment* (two-base zone: lateral `2 pi R h`, volume `(1/6) pi h (3 r1^2 + 3 r2^2 + h^2)`). https://www.math10.com/en/geometry/sphere.html ; https://mathalino.com/reviewer/solid-mensuration-solid-geometry/spherical-segment . *Spherical Lune*, MathWorld (lune surface `2 R^2 theta`). https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SphericalLune.html . *Spherical Wedge*, MathWorld / Wikipedia (wedge volume `(2/3) R^3 theta`). https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SphericalWedge.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_wedge . *Pappus's Centroid Theorem* (the surface-of-revolution area / volume by centroid travel, the unifying derivation of the zone area). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus's_centroid_theorem ; https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SurfaceofRevolution.html .
> **Method / Content (the analytic oracles, all exact to 1e-9).**
> - **Spherical zone (band, no pole)** between latitudes `v0` and `v1`: lateral area `A = 2 pi R h` with `h = R (sin v1 - sin v0)` the axial height between the two rim planes. This is the remarkable Archimedes property (zone area depends only on the axial height, not the latitude), recovered directly by integrating `R^2 cos v` (section 1.1) or by Pappus. The enclosed-solid volume contribution of the zone face to the divergence integral is the closed-form spherical-segment volume `(1/6) pi h (3 r1^2 + 3 r2^2 + h^2)` times the sense sign, with `r1, r2` the rim circle radii.
> - **Spherical cap (contains a pole)**, height `h` measured from the pole to the rim plane: surface `A = 2 pi R h` (same form, `h` the cap height), volume `V = (1/3) pi h^2 (3R - h)`.
> - **Spherical lune / wedge (partial azimuth)**, the cut that removed a `u`-wedge of angle `theta`: lune surface area `2 R^2 theta`, wedge volume `(2/3) R^3 theta`. These are the partial-azimuth dual; a full revolution is `theta = 2*pi` recovering `4 pi R^2` and `(4/3) pi R^3`.
> - **The NURBS-rim generalisation.** When the rim is NOT a latitude circle but an arbitrary closed NURBS loop, no single elementary closed form applies; the TRUE face area is the surface integral `integral integral over the NURBS-bounded region of R^2 cos v du dv`, which the boundary-flux integrator computes (Green-reducing it to the rim line integral) and the tessellation approximates. The closed forms above are the EXACT references for the ISO cases (coaxial circle rim, full or partial azimuth) and the WITNESS thresholds for the general case.
> **Limitations.** The zone/cap/lune forms are exact only for circle / great-circle rims; a NURBS rim has no elementary area, so for the general case the "analytic" side of the witness is the closed form of the candidate ISO-zone the fast path would assume, and the mismatch with the tessellated true area is precisely what flags the rim as non-circular.
> **Kernel relevance.** These are the analytic numbers the sphere witness compares against and the exact oracles the boundary-flux integrator must reproduce on the iso cases. The zone area `2 pi R h` is the sphere's `2 pi r h` (the cylinder dual of dossier 66) and the lune `2 R^2 theta` is the partial-azimuth dual. **Verified** (all closed forms are classical; the Archimedes zone property and Pappus are textbook).

### 4.2 The witness: analytic iso-zone area vs tessellated face area, and the route on mismatch

> **Citation.** Reconstruction from the landed cone/sphere rectangle-witnesses (massprops.rs ~877, ~905) and the cylinder witness of dossier 66 section 1, plus the tessellation-area cross-check practice (dossier 63; MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap, the chord-vs-arc area gap).
> **Method / Content (claimed, the deliverable, dual of the cone/sphere/cylinder witness).** Before taking the sphere iso-zone fast path:
> 1. **Analytic iso-zone area `A_iso`** for the candidate iso-zone: `2 pi R h` for a full-azimuth band/cap between the candidate `v`-bounds (`h` the axial height, or the cap height for a pole-touching candidate), scaled by `(u1 - u0) / (2 pi)` for a partial azimuth (the lune correction). This is the area the fast path implicitly assumes.
> 2. **Tessellated face area `A_tess`** = sum of the face's own triangle areas (the mesh the `mass == mesh` gate already builds). This is the TRUE face area, independent of any pcurve, and it ALREADY accounts for a NURBS (non-circular) rim, a partial-azimuth notch, AND any inner bore loops, because the tessellation triangulates the actual trimmed region.
> 3. **Compare.** If `|A_iso - A_tess| / A_iso` is within the tessellation-coarseness tolerance (section 4.3), the iso-zone IS the face: take the fast path, flux = the closed-form zone/cap value times sense, and set `pole_enclosed` / `net_winding` from the iso topology. If they disagree (the rim is a NURBS loop, the band is notched, or the cap is holed, so `A_tess` differs from the circle-rim `A_iso`), the iso-zone is NOT the face: route to the **boundary integrator over the true NURBS loop set**, computing `pole_enclosed` and `net_winding` from the actual trim topology (sections 1.2, 2.2), and iterate all loops (outer rim + pole anchor if enclosed + inner bores) through `node()`.
> 4. **The sphere witness is the exact dual of the landed cone/sphere/cylinder witnesses**, with the cone frustum area `pi (R + r) l` or the cylinder band `2 pi r h` replaced by the spherical zone/cap `2 pi R h` (and the lune `2 R^2 theta` for the partial-azimuth scale). Same control flow, same routing, same DECLINE-never-WRONG posture.
> **Exact oracle.** The `probe_wrap` sphere: the kept BAND after a non-coaxial wrap has a NURBS rim whose tessellated area differs from the latitude-circle `2 pi R h` the iso-zone path assumes; the witness fires, the integrator walks the true NURBS rims with `pole_enclosed = false`, `net_winding = 2`, and the band mass comes out the inclusion-exclusion value (section 5), not the 16.07 / 67.58 mis-read. Each CAP's witness likewise fires, routing to the pole-anchored NURBS-rim loop.
> **Limitations.** The tessellated area is itself chord-approximate (it under-reads a convex curved patch by the chord deficit), so the witness threshold must be set by the known chord error, not tighter, else a coarse tessellation triggers a false mismatch on a genuine circle-rim zone (section 4.3). The witness DETECTS that the region is non-iso; it does not by itself integrate it, which is sections 1 to 3. A NURBS rim that happens to enclose EXACTLY the same area as a latitude circle (a measure-zero coincidence) would pass the witness while being non-iso, but then the iso flux equals the true flux to the band anyway, so it is not a WRONG; the witness only needs to catch AREA-changing deviations.
> **Kernel relevance.** This closes the WRONG-risk gate: the sphere, taking the iso-zone path on a NURBS-rimmed or holed band/cap, would silently mis-read (the 16.07 / 67.58 family); with the witness it routes to the boundary integrator over the true loops with the correct anchor and winding. **Verified** that area mismatch is a sound region witness (same argument as the landed cone/sphere/cylinder cases); the sphere threshold and route are **claimed**, validated against the `probe_wrap`.

### 4.3 Threshold vs sphere tessellation coarseness

> **Citation.** Dossier 63 (adaptive curved tessellation, chord-tolerance to facet-area-error relation); MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap. MathWorld zone area `2 pi R h` (the analytic side).
> **Method / Content (claimed).** A spherical zone tessellated to chord tolerance `eps_chord` is faceted into azimuthal and meridional segments whose inscribed-polygon area under-reads the true `2 pi R h` by a relative deficit of order `eps_chord / R`, a known, bounded, sub-percent quantity for any reasonable faceting (the same order as the cylinder/cone deficits of dossier 66 section 1.3 and dossier 60 section 3.3). Set the witness threshold to a few times that bound. A real NURBS-rim deviation, a partial-azimuth notch, or a bore removes/changes a finite, percent-to-tens-of-percent fraction of the zone, FAR above the tessellation deficit, so the witness has a wide safe margin: it fires loudly on a real non-iso rim and never on tessellation noise.
> **Limitations.** A sub-tessellation-scale rim wobble or sliver bore (changing less area than the chord deficit) is below the witness floor; such a sub-tolerance feature is a dossier-64/65 upstream DECLINE (it never forms a proper distinct loop), so the witness never needs to resolve it.
> **Kernel relevance.** Sets the sphere witness tolerance honestly, tied to the faceting parameters, wide margin for real non-iso rims, sub-resolution features handled upstream by DECLINE. **Claimed**, tunable against dossier 63's faceting constants.

## 5. The validation oracle: circle-rim baseline plus the non-coaxial NURBS-wrap

The fifth scope question: per-fragment `mass == mesh == closed-form` for (a) the circle-rim three-band baseline that must NOT regress, and (b) the non-coaxial NURBS-wrap barrel-plus-two-caps, with the tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume` agreeing.

### 5.1 The circle-rim baseline (three latitude bands, must not regress)

> **Citation.** *Spherical segment* / *spherical cap* closed forms (section 4.1). The existing sphere coaxial-cut regression (the iso-zone fast path that already passes for circle rims).
> **Method / Content (the exact baseline oracle).** A sphere of radius `R` cut by two coaxial planes at latitudes `v0 < v1` produces THREE fragments: a bottom CAP (pole `-pi/2` to `v0`), a middle BAND (`v0` to `v1`), a top CAP (`v1` to `+pi/2`). Each has an exact closed-form volume:
> - bottom cap: `(1/3) pi h_b^2 (3R - h_b)`, `h_b = R (1 + sin v0)`;
> - middle band (segment): `(1/6) pi h_m (3 r0^2 + 3 r1^2 + h_m^2)`, `h_m = R (sin v1 - sin v0)`, `r_i = R cos v_i`;
> - top cap: `(1/3) pi h_t^2 (3R - h_t)`, `h_t = R (1 - sin v1)`;
> - and the three sum to `(4/3) pi R^3` exactly.
> The oracle asserts `mass(fragment) == mesh(fragment) == closed_form(fragment)` for all three, to the 2 percent curved band, with the two CAPS taking `pole_enclosed = true` (anchor on) and the BAND taking `pole_enclosed = false`, `net_winding = 1` (anchor off, no flip, an ordinary single-wrap zone). **This is the regression that must NOT break when the NURBS-rim and anchor-gating changes land**: the circle rim is the trivial NURBS loop, the iso-zone fast path stays exact, and the witness passes (circle area == `2 pi R h`).
> **Limitations.** Coaxial only; the circle rims are v=const iso-curves, so the iso-zone fast path applies and the boundary integrator is exercised only via the witness pass-through. This baseline does NOT exercise the per-fin winding flip (every fragment is single-wrap).
> **Kernel relevance.** The non-regression floor. Any change to the anchor gating or the witness must keep this three-band circle-rim oracle exact. **Verified** (the three closed forms and their sum to `(4/3) pi R^3` are elementary).

### 5.2 The non-coaxial NURBS-wrap oracle (barrel plus two caps)

> **Citation.** Sphere-sphere / sphere-cylinder intersection volume by spherical-cap summation: *Sphere-Sphere Intersection*, MathWorld (the lens volume as the sum of two spherical caps; intersection-plane offset `x0 = (d^2 + R^2 - R'^2) / (2d)`). https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Sphere-SphereIntersection.html . *Sphere-cylinder intersection*, Wikipedia (the Viviani-type intersection curve, the bore removed from a sphere). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere%E2%80%93cylinder_intersection . *Viviani's curve*, Wikipedia / MathWorld (the figure-eight sphere-cylinder intersection; Viviani's volume `(2 pi/3 - 8/9) R^3` for the tangent-cylinder case). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viviani's_curve ; https://mathworld.wolfram.com/VivianisCurve.html . *Volume of Intersection of a Cone with a Sphere*, arXiv:2203.17227 (the sphere-cone intersection volume, the wrap by a cone). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.17227 . Inclusion-exclusion identity (`V(A UNION B) = V(A) + V(B) - V(A INTERSECT B)`).
> **Method / Content (claimed, the first-class validation deliverable).** A sphere `A` wrapped by a non-coaxial `{cyl, cone} B` splits `A`'s surface into a BAND (winding-2, between the two NURBS rims, no pole) plus two CAPS (each pole-containing, NURBS-rimmed). The independent oracle is the analytic intersection volume computed by a path that shares NO machinery with either the boundary-flux mass or the tessellated mesh:
> 1. **Sphere-sphere wrap:** the lens `V(A INTERSECT B)` is the exact sum of two spherical caps (MathWorld closed form from `R`, `R'`, center distance `d`), giving `V(A UNION B) = V(A) + V(B) - V(lens)` and `V(A - B) = V(A) - V(lens)` as exact 1e-9 oracles.
> 2. **Sphere-cylinder wrap:** the removed bore volume is the sphere-cylinder intersection (the Viviani-type solid), a closed form for the tangent case (`(2 pi/3 - 8/9) R^3`) and a certified 1D quadrature for the general non-coaxial offset; the union and difference follow by inclusion-exclusion.
> 3. **Sphere-cone wrap:** the intersection volume is the arXiv:2203.17227 sphere-cone closed/quadrature form (with the apex-on-sphere spurious singularity lifted as that paper notes, the same lift dossier 60 uses for the cone apex).
> 4. **The tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume` cross-check.** Keel's tight op-volume bound for the sphere wrap is computed independently of the per-face integrator; the oracle asserts the boundary-flux mass equals BOTH the inclusion-exclusion analytic volume AND `quadric_sphere_op_volume`, all to the 2 percent band. The `probe_wrap` mis-reads (INTERSECT 16.07 vs true 17.17, UNION 67.58 vs 121) are caught because the analytic oracle and the tight bound both sit at the TRUE value, far from the mis-read.
> 5. **Per-fragment assertion.** For each of the band and the two caps, assert `mass(fragment) == mesh(fragment)` to the band, AND assert the assembled `mass(A op B) == V_analytic == quadric_sphere_op_volume`. Three-way agreement (analytic, mass-side, mesh-side) per fragment plus the assembled bound is the certificate.
> **Limitations.** The sphere-cylinder and sphere-cone general-offset intersection volumes are computed quantities (certified 1D quadrature for the non-tangent / non-coaxial case), so the oracle carries its own achieved-tolerance, which must be tighter than the 2 percent band (it is: a smooth 1D intersection integral converges to machine precision with modest GL order). The sphere-sphere lens and the Viviani tangent case are exact closed forms (1e-9 oracles).
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the independent oracle that makes the guard removal SOUND: a third, code-independent volume (inclusion-exclusion of analytic cap/lens forms, cross-checked by the tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume`) that mass==mesh self-consistency cannot fake. Remove the sphere wrap guard ONLY when mass, mesh, and this oracle all agree to the band; DECLINE on disagreement. **Verified** the inclusion-exclusion identity, the sphere-sphere cap-sum lens, and the Viviani volume; **claimed** the three-way per-fragment certificate and the `quadric_sphere_op_volume` cross-check, to validate against the `probe_wrap` before the guard is removed.

### 5.3 The guard-removal soundness argument (the soundness deliverable)

> **Method / Content.** **The sphere wrap decline guard may be removed ONLY when (a) the area-witness (section 4) plus the cap/band anchor routing (section 1.2) plus the encircling-band winding handling (section 2) plus the inner-bore subtraction (section 3), fed by correctly-oriented NURBS rims and bores from dossiers 64/65, make the boundary-flux mass equal the INDEPENDENT analytic oracle (inclusion-exclusion of the spherical cap/zone/lens closed forms, sections 4.1 and 5.2) AND the tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume`, to within the 2 percent band, AND (b) the tessellated mesh, built over the SAME NURBS-rimmed multi-loop region, also equals that oracle.** Because the analytic oracle and the tight op-volume bound share no code with either the boundary-flux mass integrator or the tessellator, a self-consistent WRONG (both mass and mesh mis-counting the NURBS-rim region in lockstep at the op-volume-bound slack edge) cannot satisfy them; passing all three certifies the result and DECLINE-never-WRONG is preserved. Any band or cap whose three numbers do not agree DECLINEs rather than emit a wrong mass. The witness is the necessary gate (it is what detects that the region is non-iso and must be boundary-integrated); the independent oracle is the sufficient certificate (it is what forbids a self-consistent WRONG within the slack).
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the load-bearing soundness deliverable. The 2 percent `mass == mesh` self-consistency gate is necessary but not sufficient (dossier 66 section 3.2): it cannot catch a co-directional mass-and-mesh miss at the op-volume-bound edge (the 16.07 / 67.58 family sits exactly within the slack). The independent analytic-plus-tight-bound oracle is the third, code-independent number that makes the removal sound. **Verified** that self-consistency cannot catch a co-directional bias; **claimed** the three-way certificate, to validate against the `probe_wrap` before the guard is removed.

## Synthesis: ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE

**ADOPT.**
1. **The pole-enclosure-gated anchor routing** (section 1.2): fire the sphere pole anchor IF AND ONLY IF a pole is enclosed (the cap case); a band encloses no pole and takes NO anchor. Two orthogonal booleans, `pole_enclosed` (gates the anchor) and `net_winding_ge_2` (gates the dossier-66 flip), drive the anchor and the flip independently, computed from the witness-validated trim topology, never from a default iso-zone.
2. **The NURBS rim as the trivial generalisation of the circle rim** (section 1.3): the existing `FinCurve::Nurbs` arm (Add.271) already samples any rim for `(point, tangent, weight)`; the circle rim is the special iso case. No rim-sampler change; the work is the anchor gate and the witness.
3. **The sphere area-witness** (section 4) as the exact dual of the landed cone/sphere/cylinder witnesses: analytic iso-zone area `2 pi R h` (band/cap) scaled by the lune factor `(u1 - u0) / (2 pi)` (partial azimuth) vs tessellated face area, threshold tied to tessellation coarseness (dossier 63), and on mismatch route off the iso-zone fast path to the boundary integrator over the true NURBS loop set with the correct anchor and winding.
4. **The encircling-band winding handling** (section 2): reuse the dossier-66 `|net| >= 2` per-fin winding flip verbatim for the doubly-wrapped sphere band; the sphere adds only the pole-enclosure gate that keeps the anchor off the band.
5. **The inner-bore subtraction** (section 3): iterate ALL loops of a holed cap through `node()`, inner NURBS bores subtract by stored CW orientation (dossier 66), composing with the pole anchor on the outer side when the cap reaches a pole.
6. **The independent inclusion-exclusion oracle** (section 5.2) cross-checked by the tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume` as the guard-removal soundness condition: sphere-sphere cap-sum lens, Viviani sphere-cylinder volume, arXiv:2203.17227 sphere-cone volume, assembled by inclusion-exclusion, a third code-independent number mass==mesh self-consistency cannot fake. Remove the wrap guard only on three-way agreement to the band; DECLINE on disagreement.
7. **The exact closed forms (zone / cap / segment / lune / wedge) as the per-fix oracle**, with the tessellation as the `mass == mesh` cross-check and the witness input, never as the fallback answer.

**AVOID.**
- **Firing the pole anchor on a band** (a region with no pole). That double-counts the polar cap flux, an over-read. Anchor only on pole enclosure.
- **Omitting the pole anchor on a cap.** That leaves the loop open, dropping the polar cap flux, an under-read.
- **Firing the dossier-66 `|net| >= 2` flip on a cap** (single-wrap). That doubles the cap. The flip is for the genuine 2-wrap encircling band only.
- **Taking the sphere iso-zone fast path without the area-witness.** That is the NURBS-rim / holed-cap silent mis-read (the 16.07 / 67.58 family), the WRONG risk. Always witness before the fast path.
- **Special-casing the inner-bore sign in code.** The subtraction must come from the stored CW orientation, not a hand-coded sign, so it stays consistent with the tessellator and the sense convention (dossier 46).
- **Removing the wrap guard on the strength of mass==mesh alone.** Self-consistency cannot catch a co-directional miss at the op-volume-bound edge; require the independent inclusion-exclusion / `quadric_sphere_op_volume` oracle first.
- **Using the tessellated NURBS-rim area/volume as the mass answer** when the boundary-flux certificate or the analytic oracle misses the band. Tessellation is chord-approximate (MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap); DECLINE instead.

**INVESTIGATE.**
- Whether the cylinder, cone, and sphere witnesses can share ONE "analytic-band-area vs tessellated-area" abstraction parameterized by `local_geometry` (the same INVESTIGATE raised in dossier 66), now with the sphere zone `2 pi R h` and lune `2 R^2 theta` as the sphere instance.
- Whether the pole anchor and the encircling-band flip compose cleanly for a HOLED cap that ALSO reaches a pole (a bore through a pole-containing cap), validated against a holed-cap-with-pole probe.
- The non-coaxial sphere-cylinder and sphere-cone general-offset intersection-volume quadrature: the right GL order and achieved-tolerance so the independent oracle is always tighter than the 2 percent band without excess work (the sphere analog of dossier 66's unequal-radius Steinmetz quadrature question).
- Whether a wrong-sense inner bore loop or a mis-gated anchor (anchor fired on a band) from a dossier-64/65 defect is reliably caught by the three-way (analytic / mass / mesh) certificate plus the tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume`, or whether an additional pole-enclosure assertion is warranted at face-build time.
- Whether the Gauss-Bonnet / signed-solid-angle formulation (the spherical region area as `2 pi` minus the boundary geodesic-curvature integral and corner turns) gives a cheaper or more robust pole-enclosure test than the parametric witness, as a cross-check on `pole_enclosed`.

**Recommended FIRST MILESTONE (narrowest honest slice with an exact oracle):**
**The pole-enclosure-gated anchor for the circle-rim three-band baseline (section 5.1), validated against the exact spherical cap `(1/3) pi h^2 (3R - h)`, segment `(1/6) pi h (3 r1^2 + 3 r2^2 + h^2)`, and their sum `(4/3) pi R^3`.** This is the smallest, highest-certainty slice: it exercises the `pole_enclosed` boolean (caps anchor, band does not) on a configuration with an EXACT closed-form oracle for every fragment, it must hold as the non-regression floor, and it proves the anchor gating before the harder NURBS-rim and winding-flip work. It does not yet need the NURBS rim (the rims are circles), the witness (the iso-zone is the face), or the encircling flip (every fragment is single-wrap), so it isolates the anchor-gating fix with a 1e-9 oracle.

**Follow-up ladder:**
1. The pole-enclosure-gated anchor on the circle-rim three-band baseline (the first milestone), exact cap/segment oracle, the non-regression floor.
2. The sphere area-witness (section 4) for a partial-azimuth (lune) sphere fragment, validated against `2 R^2 theta` / `(2/3) R^3 theta` and the iso-zone-scaled volume, routing a notched zone off the fast path.
3. The NURBS-rim cap: a single non-coaxial wrap producing one pole-containing NURBS-rimmed cap, validated against the spherical-cap / sphere-sphere-lens oracle, exercising the `FinCurve::Nurbs` rim through the pole-anchored loop.
4. The encircling NURBS-rim band with the `|net| >= 2` flip (section 2), validated against the sphere-sphere / Viviani band-fragment volume, exercising the dossier-66 flip on the sphere with `pole_enclosed = false`.
5. The full `probe_wrap` barrel-plus-two-caps decomposition (section 5.2), three-way certificate (analytic / mass / mesh) per fragment plus the tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume`, confirming INTERSECT 17.17 and UNION 121.
6. The multi-hole cap (section 3): a bore through a pole-containing cap, inner NURBS bore subtracting by CW orientation, composing with the pole anchor, validated against a holed-cap probe.
7. Remove the sphere wrap decline guard, with the three-way gate active, and confirm WRONG=0 on the sphere-wrap soak corpus.

**Configuration to DECLINE forever (justified):**
**A NURBS-rimmed sphere band or cap whose rim or bore arc cannot be certified to the 2 percent band after the adaptive-panel cap, OR whose multi-loop result disagrees with the independent inclusion-exclusion / `quadric_sphere_op_volume` oracle beyond the band.** Justification: a near-tangent wrap producing a sharp, high-curvature seam rim, or a face whose three-way certificate (analytic / mass / mesh) does not agree, cannot be integrated to the band with bounded work or cannot be certified independent of self-consistency; emitting the under-resolved or unverified number would risk a WRONG mass inside the gate. Declining the band/cap (and the boolean) is honest and rare (real CAD wraps are well inside tangency, real NURBS-rimmed faces certify three-way). It moves from permanent-decline to staged-recovery only if a closed-form rim boundary integral removes the quadrature uncertainty or the seam fit is re-certified tighter. Also DECLINE-forever (upstream, not the integrator's job): a wrap whose rim passes exactly through a pole (a degenerate pole-on-seam, dossier 45 / sphere-split-integration-trap), a sub-tessellation-scale sliver rim wobble or bore, and a degenerate inner-loop nesting, all declined before the integrator runs (dossiers 64/65).
