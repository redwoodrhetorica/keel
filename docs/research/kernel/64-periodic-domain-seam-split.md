# 64. The seam-aware periodic-domain split: partitioning a cone / cylinder / sphere parameter domain by seam curves and re-threading each fragment's closed pcurve loop

## Title and scope

This is the implementation-grade literature dossier for **the seam-aware periodic-domain split**, the single genuinely-new algorithm dossier 59 named at its last rung. Dossier 59 (curved boolean assembly) established the surrounding pipeline: a curved seam must be computed ONCE per face-pair, given a canonical `EdgeId`, shared into both operands, and fed to the UNCHANGED dossier-47 radial-splice stitcher and dossier-57 finalize. Dossier 59 deliberately deferred ONE component to here: the 2D parametric-domain arrangement that takes a periodic (cone / cylinder / sphere) parameter rectangle, a set of K seam pcurves, and emits one B-rep face per region with a CLOSED, correctly oriented pcurve loop, with the seam slit and the apex / pole as first-class domain features. This dossier does NOT repeat dossier 59's identity / stitch / finalize argument; it answers the orthogonal question that one deferred: **given the seam already lifted to its parametric preimage, how is the periodic domain partitioned and each fragment loop re-threaded so that a band wrapping the full u-period is a valid nonzero-area face.**

**Where Keel is (grounding, build on, do not re-research).** Tolerant-f64 non-manifold radial-edge B-rep; Euler operators (`split_edge`, `mev`, `mef`, `kev`, `kef`, `kemr`, `mekr`, `kfmrh`); arenas plus generational keys plus `EntityId`. Analytic surfaces plane / cylinder / cone / sphere / torus plus NURBS. Boolean pipeline: `seam_curves` (analytic SSI plus recovered-NURBS) -> `imprint_operand(a/b)` -> `classify_faces` (generalized winding number) -> `select_faces` (Requicha) -> `stitch_by_import` -> `finalize_imported_assembly` (Weiler region walk). A cone / cylinder lateral (and a sphere) is ONE periodic face: its (u, v) domain is a rectangle, u in [0, 2*pi) identified at the seam slit, v axial (the cone collapsing to the apex at one end, the sphere to poles at both v ends). The relevant imprint entry points are `imprint_closed_curve_crossing` and `imprint_crossing_pair` in `crates/keel-topo/src/imprint.rs`, with helpers `find_curve_seam_line_crossing`, `closed_curve_crosses_boundary`, `synthesize_lateral_seam`, `curve_pcurve_on` / `curve_pcurve_on_any`, and a sphere-seam latitude path `imprint_latitude_ring_crossing_sphere_seam`. **The minimal hard case that motivates the whole dossier.** The unequal-radius perpendicular cylinder / cylinder boolean: A a z-cylinder of radius r1, B an x-cylinder of radius r0 = 0.6 < r1. Their SSI is two closed NON-planar curves (the Steinmetz curve, x = +-sqrt(z^2 + 0.64) on the cross-section). On A's z-wall each is a small theta-localised HOLE; on B's x-wall each ENCIRCLES the axis, wrapping phi as z spans the tube. Mass, classify, and tessellation are DONE; the boolean DECLINES purely because of this imprint-stage gap. This is the narrowest configuration that exercises everything new here, and it has an exact analytic oracle (Q5).

This is a **design recommendation** grounded in the CGAL Arrangement_on_surface_2 framework (Berberich, Fogel, Halperin, Mehlhorn, Wein), OCCT BOPAlgo face building, ACIS periodic-face handling, and classical CAGD preimage theory, to be validated against Keel's curved-boolean regression suite and the explorer decline corpus. ## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **The periodic-domain split is NOT incremental Euler surgery on the 3D shell. It is a 2D arrangement built in the parameter rectangle of the periodic face, where the u-seam slit is an IDENTIFICATION boundary (left edge glued to right edge) and the apex / pole is a CONTRACTION boundary (a v-edge collapsed to one point). Each seam pcurve is lifted to its closed-form preimage, made x-monotone, and inserted into this arrangement; a curve that wraps the full u-period is split at the seam-slit into in-range sub-arcs whose images on the two identified boundaries are the SAME 3D point. Faces are then read off the arrangement by the standard DCEL counterclockwise half-edge walk, and EACH fragment's pcurve loop is RE-THREADED from rim sub-arcs, seam-slit sub-edges, and seam sub-arcs into a closed cycle. The incremental spur (`split-seam-at-P` + `mev-to-antipode-Q` + `mef`) collapses precisely because it treats the wrapping seam as a 3D chord across the slit instead of as a closed loop in the identified parameter domain; the band loop it builds has winding zero in (u, v) and therefore zero area. The non-collapsing construction never crosses into 3D until the closed (u, v) loop is assembled and verified to enclose nonzero signed area on the correct side.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. A periodic surface is a quotient of a parameter rectangle by gluings: the cylinder glues left edge to right edge (one identification, the seam), the cone adds a contraction of one v-edge to the apex point, the sphere contracts both v-edges to the two poles. This is exactly the topology-traits classification that CGAL's Arrangement_on_surface_2 makes first-class (identification side, contraction side, closed / open side). An encircling seam that wraps the full u-period is, in this quotient, a loop that crosses the identification boundary; in the rectangle it appears as one or more arcs that exit the right edge at v = v* and re-enter the left edge at the SAME v*, with the two endpoints being the SAME 3D point on the glued seam. The correct face is the region of the rectangle BETWEEN two such wrapping arcs (a band), and its boundary loop in (u, v) is closed by walking along one arc, down the seam slit, back along the other arc, and up the seam slit, giving a rectangle-with-curved-top-and-bottom of strictly positive area. Keel's incremental spur instead picks one crossing point P, runs an `mev` to the geometric antipode Q, and an `mef` to close: this builds a loop that goes P -> (seam arc) -> back near P and then a zero-width spur out to Q and back, which in (u, v) is a degenerate sliver, area zero, interior off-surface. The cure is to stop doing 3D Euler surgery for the encircling case and instead build the band as a region of the 2D arrangement with a re-threaded closed pcurve loop, exactly as a production arrangement package would. The single-cut planar conic case works today only because a planar circle / ellipse crosses the slit at two antipodal points and the naive P / Q spur happens to coincide with the correct two-arc band; the non-planar encircling case has no such coincidence and exposes the missing arrangement.

The one honest caveat: a small set of configurations are genuinely unrepresentable (a seam tangent to the apex, a degenerate conic through the apex, a sub-tolerance sliver band, a self-intersecting wrap). Those stay DECLINE-forever (Q4 / Q5), never WRONG. Everything else in the curved frontier flows through this one arrangement.

## Q1. The parametric arrangement: partition the periodic domain and emit one face per region with a closed loop

### Q1.0 The domain model: identification and contraction as first-class boundary conditions

The periodic face's parameter rectangle is not a plain rectangle; it carries gluings. The robust way to partition it is to model those gluings explicitly and build a 2D arrangement that respects them, then read faces off the arrangement. This is precisely the framework CGAL formalised.

#### Berberich, Fogel, Halperin, Mehlhorn, Wein, Arrangements on Parametric Surfaces (the governing framework)
- **Citation.** E. Berberich, E. Fogel, D. Halperin, K. Mehlhorn, R. Wein, "Arrangements on Parametric Surfaces I: General Framework and Infrastructure," Mathematics in Computer Science 4(1), 2010; and "Arrangements on Parametric Surfaces II: Concretizations and Applications," same issue. Earlier: E. Berberich, E. Fogel, D. Halperin, K. Mehlhorn, R. Wein, "Sweeping and Maintaining Two-Dimensional Arrangements on Surfaces: A First Step," ESA 2007, LNCS 4698. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11786-010-0042-5 ; https://people.mpi-inf.mpg.de/~mehlhorn/ftp/arr_surf_ESA07final.pdf
- **Method / content.** A set of curves on a continuous 2D parametric surface is processed by sweeping the PARAMETER SPACE. The surface is presented as an iso-rectangle (the parameter space) whose four sides each carry a boundary condition, classified into three families: **contraction** (a side collapses to a single point, for example a sphere pole or a cone apex), **identification** (two opposite sides are topologically the same locus, for example the meridian seam of a cylinder / cone / sphere, or both pairs for a torus), and **bounded / unbounded** (closed or open). Concretely: the cylinder is a rectangle with ONE pair of sides identified (the vertical seam); the sphere is a rectangle with the left / right pair identified (the seam meridian) and the top / bottom pair each CONTRACTED (north and south poles); the torus identifies both pairs. The framework lifts the planar sweep-line and zone algorithms to this setting by a small set of topology-traits predicates, so the same DCEL face-extraction runs on every surface. A curve reaching a boundary is traced to the domain edge; a curve crossing an identification boundary "reappears on the opposite side" and the DCEL maintains logical connectivity across the discontinuity; at a contraction point "multiple edges converge" and the DCEL represents it as a single vertex with multiple incident faces. Faces are constructed by tracing half-edge cycles in the embedded graph.
- **Limitations.** The CGAL realisation is exact-arithmetic and curve-traits-generic; Keel uses tolerant f64 and analytic / NURBS pcurves, so the predicates (is-on-seam, is-at-contraction, x-monotone split) must be made tolerant. The framework assumes well-defined x-monotone subcurves; a pcurve tangent to the seam or to the contraction needs special care (Q4).
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the exact structural model Keel's periodic split should adopt. The cone lateral = rectangle, left / right u-edges identified (the seam slit), top v-edge contracted to the apex, bottom v-edge the closed base rim. The cylinder lateral = rectangle, left / right identified, top and bottom both closed rims (no contraction). The sphere = rectangle, left / right identified (seam meridian), top and bottom contracted (poles). Keel does NOT need the full CGAL package; it needs the three boundary classifications as flags on the face's domain and the two derived operations: (1) split a seam pcurve at the identification boundary into in-range sub-arcs whose endpoints on left and right edges are the SAME 3D seam point; (2) treat a pcurve endpoint that reaches a contraction v-edge as the apex / pole vertex, shared by all incident fragments. **Verified** against the published framework.

### Q1.1 The cylinder: K parallel encircling loops to K+1 bands

- **Citation.** Reconstruction from the Berberich framework above plus the OCCT BuilderFace step (Q1.4) and dossier 45 (periodic seam Euler bookkeeping).
- **Method / content (the recommended Keel mechanism).** Domain: u in [0, 2*pi) identified left-to-right, v in [v_lo, v_hi] both closed rims. Given K seam pcurves each a CLOSED ENCIRCLING loop (a curve that wraps the full u-period, monotone-ish in v, for example a planar oblique cut giving an ellipse-wrap, or a non-planar quartic-wrap):
  1. For each encircling pcurve c_k(u), find its seam-slit crossing u = 0 ≡ 2*pi. An encircling loop crosses the slit an ODD number of times (once for a simple wrap). Split c_k there into in-range sub-arc(s); insert a seam-slit vertex S_k at v = v_k* on the slit edge. The two images of the split point (on the u = 0 edge and the u = 2*pi edge) are the SAME 3D point by the identification, so S_k is ONE vertex.
  2. Sort the K crossing heights v_1* < v_2* < ... < v_K* along the seam slit. This subdivides the slit edge into K+1 sub-edges between consecutive crossings (plus the two rim ends).
  3. The K encircling sub-arcs partition the rectangle into K+1 horizontal BANDS. Band j (between c_j and c_{j+1}) has the closed (u, v) loop: c_j (left-to-right across the full period) -> seam-slit sub-edge from S_j UP to S_{j+1} on the u = 2*pi side -> c_{j+1} reversed (right-to-left) -> seam-slit sub-edge from S_{j+1} DOWN to S_j on the u = 0 side. The top and bottom bands also include a rim arc. This loop has strictly positive signed area (it is a full-width strip), which is exactly what the incremental spur fails to produce.
  4. Emit one B-rep face per band, each carrying its closed pcurve loop and the cylinder surface. The seam-slit sub-edges are shared (radial-cycle-2 of the face to itself, dossier 51 discriminator); the encircling edges c_k are the shared SSI seam edges (one `EdgeId` each, dossier 59 Q2).
- **Limitations.** Assumes each encircling loop is simple and the K loops are disjoint and order-able in v. Mutually intersecting encircling loops need the full 2D arrangement (Q1.5), not this banded shortcut. A loop tangent to a rim is a Q4 boundary.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the cylinder case of the minimal hard problem. For the unequal cyl / cyl, B's x-wall sees the two Steinmetz wraps as K = 2 encircling loops, partitioning B's lateral into 3 bands: a central barrel (the part inside A, removed or kept by Requicha) and two end stubs. The closed-form preimage (Q3) makes step 1 exact. **Claimed** as the recommended algorithm, validated against the cyl / cyl repro.

### Q1.2 The cone: encircling loops plus an apex-reaching arc

- **Citation.** Reconstruction from the framework plus the cone-plane conic preimage (Q3) and dossier 59 Q1 (the cone seam-slit-plus-apex split).
- **Method / content.** Domain: u in [0, 2*pi) identified, v in [0, 1] with v = 1 CONTRACTED to the apex, v = 0 the base rim. Two seam shapes occur:
  - **Encircling loop** (a conic that wraps the full angle, an ellipse from a moderate planar cut, or a cone / cylinder SSI that wraps): handled exactly as Q1.1, splitting at the slit; a band above the loop runs UP to the apex and its loop passes THROUGH the apex vertex once (rim-free top "band" is a cap whose loop is c_k -> seam-slit-up to apex -> apex -> seam-slit-down, the apex appearing once, NOT doubled).
  - **Apex-reaching open arc** (a hyperbola branch or a cut whose conic passes near / terminates at the apex region, off-degenerate): the pcurve runs from a rim crossing up to the apex contraction. Its fragment loop uses the apex as an ordinary loop vertex; the apex is shared by every fragment whose loop reaches it, as a single contraction vertex (the CGAL "multiple edges converge at one vertex" rule).
- **Limitations.** A conic passing EXACTLY through the apex is degenerate (point / line-pair), DECLINE-forever (Q4). A conic tangent to the apex contraction is a Q4 boundary requiring a perturbed evaluation.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is dossier 59's "rim -> seam-up -> apex -> seam-down" loop made into an arrangement region rather than an Euler sequence. The apex-as-single-contraction-vertex rule is what prevents the doubled-cover apex bug (the integrate-wrong mass != mesh failure). **Claimed**, validated against cone multi-cut repros.

### Q1.3 The sphere: encircling loops plus a pole-reaching arc

- **Citation.** CGAL Arr_geodesic_arc_on_sphere_traits_2 (the canonical sphere parametrisation) plus the framework.
- **Citation detail.** CGAL, `Arr_geodesic_arc_on_sphere_traits_2`. Parameter space Phi = [alpha, 2*pi + alpha] x [-pi/2, pi/2] mapping to the sphere via phi_S(x, y) = (cos y cos x, sin y cos x, sin x); default alpha = pi gives [-pi, pi] x [-pi/2, pi/2]. Two contraction points (south pole (0,0,-1), north pole (0,0,1)); identification curve phi_S(-pi+alpha, v) = phi_S(+pi+alpha, v) for all v (the seam meridian). https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Arrangement_on_surface_2/classCGAL_1_1Arr__geodesic__arc__on__sphere__traits__2.html
- **Method / content.** Domain: u in [0, 2*pi) (longitude) identified at the seam meridian, v in [-pi/2, pi/2] (latitude) with BOTH v-edges contracted (the two poles). An encircling seam (a latitude-like circle, for example a sphere / plane cut, or a sphere / cylinder SSI that wraps) splits the sphere into a north cap, a middle band (or several), and a south cap, by the Q1.1 banding with the two end "bands" being caps whose loops pass through a pole contraction once each. A pole-reaching arc (a great-circle-like cut that runs to a pole) uses the pole as a loop vertex. Keel already has `imprint_latitude_ring_crossing_sphere_seam`, which is the latitude-circle special case; the general recommendation subsumes it.
- **Limitations.** Sphere / cylinder and sphere / cone SSI can produce non-latitude wraps (the curve dips in v as u advances), still encircling but not v-constant; the banding still applies (it only needs v-monotone order at the slit crossing), but a wrap that is non-monotone in v (turns back) must be split at its v-extrema into x-monotone (here u-monotone) sub-arcs first, exactly the CGAL x-monotone decomposition.
- **Kernel relevance.** Generalises Keel's existing latitude-ring path to arbitrary encircling sphere seams and to pole-reaching arcs, with the pole as a single contraction vertex (no doubled pole cover). **Verified** parametrisation, **claimed** banding generalisation.

### Q1.4 How production kernels realise this: OCCT and ACIS

#### OCCT BOPAlgo_BuilderFace and BOPAlgo_WireSplitter (faces from the 2D arrangement of edges, not from incremental Euler ops)
- **Citation.** Open CASCADE Technology, BOPAlgo package: `BOPAlgo_BuilderFace`, `BOPAlgo_WireSplitter` (methods `Perform`, `MakeConnexityBlocks`, `MakeWires`, `SplitBlock`, `MakeWire`); `BOPAlgo_Tools::WiresToFaces`; Boolean Operations user guide (BuildPCurves, section edges, Process Degenerated Edges). https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/occt-7.5.0/refman/html/class_b_o_p_algo___wire_splitter.html ; https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/occt-7.4.0/overview/html/occt_user_guides__boolean_operations.html
- **Method / content.** OCCT builds split faces from a 2D arrangement of edges ON the face, NOT by incremental Euler surgery. `BOPAlgo_BuilderFace` takes the original face plus the set of edges lying on it (original boundary edges plus section edges from the boolean), and rebuilds the faces. The wire-building is delegated to `BOPAlgo_WireSplitter`: it first groups edges into connexity blocks (`MakeConnexityBlocks`), then within each block walks loops by the standard angular rule (`SplitBlock` / `MakeWires`), choosing the next edge at each vertex by the smallest turning angle in the surface's 2D parameter space, which is the DCEL counterclockwise next-half-edge construction performed in (u, v). Crucially, the angular sort is done in the FACE's 2D parameter space using the edges' pcurves, so a periodic face is split correctly only when the section edges carry pcurves and the seam is present. The seam edge of a periodic surface is inserted as an edge of the wire-set (OCCT's `ShapeFix_Face` "fix of missing seam edge" exists precisely so that a face spanning the seam has the seam edge present to close its wire). Degenerate edges (cone apex, sphere pole) are handled by a dedicated "Process Degenerated Edges" step and are legal zero-length members of a wire.
- **Limitations.** OCCT relies on shape-healing (`SameParameter`, `FixMissingSeam`) AFTER the boolean to repair pcurve / seam drift, a heal-after-the-fact posture dossier 47 argues against. The angular walk needs reliable pcurves; OCCT's robustness problems on periodic faces trace to missing or inconsistent seam pcurves, the exact failure Keel must avoid by construction.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the production confirmation that the periodic split is a 2D-arrangement-of-edges face rebuild (BuilderFace over a wire-set with the seam edge present), and that the loop walk is the angular next-half-edge rule in (u, v). Keel's recommendation is the same mechanism done by-construction (seam pcurve present and consistent at imprint time) rather than healed after. The `MakeConnexityBlocks` -> `MakeWires` -> `BuilderFace` chain maps to Keel: collect the periodic face's boundary + seam-slit + apex/pole + imprinted seam pcurves, walk closed wires in (u, v), emit one face per region. **Verified** against the BOPAlgo method structure. Patent posture: OCCT is LGPL open source, no claim-reading risk; cite and follow freely.

#### ACIS periodic faces and the named seam
- **Citation.** Spatial Corp (Dassault Systemes), ACIS "Geometry > Types of Curves and Surfaces" (periodic surfaces, seams, pcurves) and "Modeling Operations > Intersectors." http://www.q-solid.com/ACIS_Docs_R17/online/SPAacisuserTechArticles/SPAacisuser_gtyp.htm
- **Method / content.** ACIS "correctly handles faces that span the seam(s) of a periodic surface." A pcurve is "a mapping of a 2D curve onto a 3D surface," and the periodic seam is a first-class NAMED edge along the identified parametric boundary so that trimming loops stay within the default parametric range and a face spanning the seam closes its loop through the seam edge. A boolean computes the SSI once; the resulting intersection edge is shared between the two trimmed faces it bounds. A periodic face forming a COMPLETE band (no seam crossing) is a documented distinct case (a closed face with no seam in its loop).
- **Limitations.** Closed source; the seam-once-and-share and the periodic-face-spanning behavior are documented but not source-visible. The complete-band case (a closed cylindrical band with no seam in the loop) is the configuration Keel must NOT accidentally create as zero-area (the Add.273 bug).
- **Kernel relevance.** Confirms the convention: the seam is a named first-class edge, faces spanning it close through it, and a complete band is a legal closed face. Keel's banding (Q1.1) must produce loops that go THROUGH the seam-slit edge (the ACIS / OCCT convention), which is exactly the re-threading that makes the band nonzero-area. **Verified** as documented behavior.

### Q1.5 Direct face-from-arrangement vs constrained Euler operators (the architectural choice)

- **Citation.** Synthesis of OCCT BuilderFace (arrangement-based), CGAL DCEL face extraction, and Keel's current Euler-surgery path; dossier 47 (retire-the-soup, identity-from-imprint).
- **Method / content.** Two implementable strategies:
  - **(A) Direct face-from-arrangement (recommended).** Build the 2D arrangement in (u, v) with the seam-slit and apex/pole as boundary features, walk DCEL faces by the angular next-half-edge rule, and CONSTRUCT each B-rep face directly from its closed (u, v) loop, only then lifting edges to 3D once. This is OCCT BuilderFace and CGAL. It never produces a degenerate intermediate state; the loop is verified closed and positive-area in (u, v) BEFORE any 3D entity is built.
  - **(B) Constrained Euler operators.** Keep doing `split_edge` / `mev` / `mef`, but drive them from the precomputed 2D arrangement (the Euler sequence becomes a deterministic playback of the arrangement's faces, not a heuristic 3D surgery). This preserves Keel's existing Euler machinery and its history / undo, but every operator must be checked against the arrangement so the spur-collapse cannot recur.
- **Recommendation.** Adopt (A) for the periodic split: compute the arrangement, emit faces from closed (u, v) loops, build 3D edges once with shared `EdgeId`s. Keep Euler operators for the genus / handle bookkeeping (dossier 45) where they are authoritative, but the FACE PARTITION of a periodic domain is an arrangement, not a sequence of spurs. The incremental spur is the source of the Add.273 collapse precisely because it is strategy (B) WITHOUT the arrangement to constrain it.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the central design decision. **Claimed**: strategy (A) eliminates the whole class of zero-area / off-surface-interior failures because the loop's area and orientation are checked in 2D before 3D construction.

## Q2. Why the incremental spur collapses, and the non-collapsing construction

### Q2.1 Diagnosis of the confirmed failure

- **Citation.** Keel Add.273 (the confirmed degeneracy) plus the quotient-topology reading of the periodic domain (Q1.0) and the DCEL signed-area definition.
- **Method / content (the root cause, verified by construction).** The spur does: `split-seam-at-P` (split the existing seam-slit edge at the single crossing point P where the encircling seam meets the slit), `mev-to-antipode-Q` (make an edge and a vertex out to the geometric 3D antipode Q on the seam loop), `mef` (make an edge / face to close). Trace the resulting loop in (u, v):
  - The encircling seam is, in (u, v), a curve from (0, v*) on the left edge to (2*pi, v*) on the right edge (one full wrap). The spur picks P = the slit crossing and Q = the antipodal point at u = pi, v = v*. The `mev` to Q draws a SPUR edge from P to Q THROUGH the interior, and the `mef` closes a loop that runs P -> seam-arc -> Q -> spur-back -> P. In (u, v) this loop encloses signed area on only one side of the spur and, because the spur retraces the seam arc's own span, the two contributions cancel: the loop has WINDING ZERO around any interior point, hence signed area ~ 0. The interior sample point the kernel then tests lands on the spur (a zero-width line in (u, v)), whose 3D image is a curve ON the seam, and a tiny perpendicular offset projects OFF the surface near the wrap. That is the "zero-area band whose interior point projects off the surface."
  - **The root is NOT the antipode choice and NOT the spur's arc-bounds per se; it is that a full-revolution encircling loop is topologically a band BETWEEN TWO seam crossings on the slit, and the spur supplies only ONE crossing plus an interior chord.** A band needs two wrapping arcs (or one wrapping arc plus a rim) and the slit sub-edge connecting their slit endpoints; the spur provides one arc and a degenerate chord, which cannot bound a positive-area region. The full-revolution (u, v) loop's winding / area degeneracy is the proximate symptom; the missing second slit endpoint (and the slit sub-edge between them) is the cause.
- **Limitations.** This diagnosis is for the simple single-wrap encircling case; a multi-wrap or self-crossing seam has additional failure modes (Q4).
- **Kernel relevance.** Names the exact defect so the fix is targeted: supply the missing slit structure (two slit endpoints and the slit sub-edge between them) and build the band as a region, not a spur. **Verified** by the quotient-topology / winding analysis.

### Q2.2 The non-collapsing re-threading construction (the deliverable)

- **Citation.** Synthesis of Q1.1 (banding), OCCT BuilderFace (loop through the seam edge), CGAL identification handling, and the DCEL signed-area orientation rule.
- **Method / content (the explicit fragment-loop re-threading).** For an encircling seam c that wraps the full u-period and crosses the slit at height v* (the minimal hard case, one wrap):
  1. **Lift c to its closed-form preimage** c(u) in (u, v) (Q3). Find its slit crossing: the point where c meets u = 0 ≡ 2*pi, giving the slit vertex S at (0, v*) ≡ (2*pi, v*) (ONE 3D point, two parameter images).
  2. **Identify the two bands** c splits the rectangle into: BELOW (v < c) and ABOVE (v > c). Each band is bounded by c, a rim (or a contraction for the cone / sphere cap), and the slit sub-edge.
  3. **Re-thread the BELOW band loop** (rim at v = v_lo present): start at S on the u = 0 side -> go DOWN the slit sub-edge to the bottom-left rim corner (0, v_lo) -> across the bottom rim to (2*pi, v_lo) -> UP the slit sub-edge on the u = 2*pi side to S -> along c REVERSED from (2*pi, v*) back to (0, v*) = S. Close. This loop is a full-width strip; its signed area in (u, v) is strictly positive (oriented counterclockwise). For the cone / sphere cap where the band reaches a contraction, replace the rim traversal with the single contraction vertex (apex / pole), so the loop is S -> slit-up to apex -> apex -> slit-down to S -> c reversed.
  4. **Re-thread the ABOVE band loop** symmetrically (rim at v = v_hi or the other contraction), with c traversed FORWARD so the two bands' shared edge c carries OPPOSITE coedge senses (the shared-edge / radial-2 condition).
  5. **Orientation / sense rule per face.** Each band's outer loop is oriented so its signed area in (u, v) is POSITIVE (counterclockwise in the standard (u, v) frame). The face's surface normal sense relative to the loop is then the surface's natural normal (Keel folds sense * natural, per the massprops resolution in MEMORY). The shared seam edge c gets ONE `EdgeId` (dossier 59 Q2); the two bands reference it with opposite coedge directions, satisfying the radial-cycle-2 pairing. The two slit sub-edges (u = 0 side and u = 2*pi side) are the SAME 3D seam-slit edge traversed by the face on both its sides (radial-cycle-2 to itself, dossier 51 free-edge discriminator distinguishes this from a true free edge).
  6. **Verify in 2D before 3D.** Assert each loop is closed (last vertex = first), simple (no self-crossing), and positive-area ABOVE a feature-relative epsilon. Only then build the 3D edges (once) and the faces. A loop failing the area / simplicity test is a DECLINE (Q4), surfaced loudly, never a silently-emitted sliver.
- **Limitations.** Written for one encircling wrap to two bands; K wraps to K+1 bands iterate the same construction along the sorted slit crossings (Q1.1). Mutually intersecting seams need the full arrangement (Q1.5 A).
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the precise replacement for the collapsing spur. The two-line core: **build the band as the region BETWEEN the encircling seam arc and the rim / contraction, closing the loop DOWN one side of the seam slit and UP the other (the slit sub-edge between the two seam-crossings is the missing piece the spur omitted); orient each band loop to positive (u, v) area, share the seam arc as one `EdgeId` with opposite coedge senses, share the slit edge as a radial-2 self-pairing.** **Claimed** as the recommended construction, validated against the Add.273 repro and the cyl / cyl oracle (Q5).

## Q3. Closed-form parametric preimages (make the split EXACT)

For Q1 / Q2 to be exact (avoid marching a seam whose preimage is closed-form, which would reintroduce the cross-operand divergence dossier 59 Q2 warns against), each analytic seam class needs its pcurve preimage in closed form. The convention: use the exact preimage where it exists; where it does not, use Keel's certified-NURBS-recovery fit with a SameParameter tolerance bound.

### Q3.1 Plane / cylinder, plane / cone, plane / sphere

- **Citation.** Classical conic-section and quadric theory; cone-plane polar form; Steinmetz / sphere-cylinder pages (Q3.2). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic_section
- **Method / content (closed-form preimages).**
  - **Plane / cylinder.** Cylinder radius R, axis z, parametrise (u = theta, v = z). A cutting plane n.x = d gives, on the cylinder x = (R cos u, R sin u, v), the relation v(u) = (d - R n_x cos u - R n_y sin u) / n_z. This is a SINUSOID in u: a single-valued v(u), an ENCIRCLING loop (wraps the full u-period) for any non-vertical cut. Its 3D image is an ellipse (or a circle if the plane is perpendicular to the axis, v = const). Exact, one wrap, crosses the slit once. This is the cylinder banding seam.
  - **Plane / cone.** Cone half-angle alpha, apex at origin, axis z; x = (v sin alpha cos u, v sin alpha sin u, v cos alpha) with v the distance from apex along axis. A plane n.x = d gives v(u) = d / (n_z cos alpha + sin alpha (n_x cos u + n_y sin u)), a polar-form conic. If the denominator never vanishes for u in [0, 2*pi) the conic is an ELLIPSE (encircling, one wrap, Q1.2 banding). If it vanishes at one u the conic is a PARABOLA (the cut parallels a ruling); at two u it is a HYPERBOLA (two open branches reaching toward the apex / infinity, an apex-reaching arc in Q1.2). The vanishing-denominator test is the exact ellipse-vs-parabola-vs-hyperbola classifier and tells the arrangement whether the seam encircles or is an apex-reaching arc.
  - **Plane / sphere.** Sphere radius rho, centre origin; a plane cut is a circle. On the sphere x = (rho cos v cos u, rho cos v sin u, rho sin v), a plane n.x = d gives a relation that for an axis-perpendicular plane is v = const (a latitude, the `imprint_latitude_ring_crossing_sphere_seam` case) and for a tilted plane is a single-valued v(u) sinusoid-like curve, ENCIRCLING (one wrap) when the circle does not enclose a pole, or a pole-reaching configuration when it does. Closed-form via substituting the parametrisation into n.x = d.
- **Limitations.** The cone parabola / hyperbola denominator-zero is a tolerance-sensitive classification (near-parabola). A plane through the apex is degenerate (Q4).
- **Kernel relevance.** Gives the EXACT preimage for every plane-cut periodic seam, so the cylinder / cone / sphere banding oracles (Q5) are analytic. **Verified** as standard quadric / conic theory.

### Q3.2 Cylinder / cylinder (the per-ruling quartic, the minimal hard case)

- **Citation.** Steinmetz curve (intersection of two perpendicular cylinders). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinmetz_curve ; Sphere-cylinder intersection. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere%E2%80%93cylinder_intersection
- **Method / content (closed-form preimage, verified).** Two perpendicular cylinders, A radius a along x-axis and B radius b along y-axis (relabel to match Keel: take the smaller-radius tube and its axis). The 3D Steinmetz intersection curve is, with parameter t in [0, 2*pi):
  - x(t) = a cos t, z(t) = a sin t, y(t) = +- sqrt(b^2 - a^2 sin^2 t).
  The +- gives two closed loops. **Preimage on the smaller cylinder A** (axis x, parametrise A by phi around x and axial coordinate along x): the curve is x = a cos t (the axial coordinate) and the angle around x is atan2(z, y-ish); as t runs 0 to 2*pi the curve wraps phi a full turn, ENCIRCLING A's axis, while the axial coordinate oscillates: this is the "encircles the axis" wrap on B's x-wall in the minimal hard case (with A and B's roles as in the prompt: on B's x-wall the curve wraps phi as z spans the tube). **Preimage on the larger cylinder B** (axis y): the curve is y = +-sqrt(...) (the axial coordinate along y) and the angle around y is atan2(x, z) = atan2(a cos t, a sin t) = a localised arc in the angle (it does NOT wrap the full turn), so on B's wall each loop is a theta-localised HOLE. Both preimages are closed-form in t (no marching). When a = b the curve DEGENERATES to two intersecting ellipses (the equal-radius Steinmetz solid); the unequal case a < b is the generic non-planar quartic wrap.
- **Limitations.** Closed-form for the axis-perpendicular intersecting case; skew or non-intersecting axes need the general quadric-quadric quartic (Levin / Wang-Goldman pencil method), which yields a parametrisable quartic but with case analysis. Keel's minimal hard case is the perpendicular intersecting case, fully closed-form.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the EXACT preimage for the minimal hard case. The encircling-wrap preimage on the smaller tube is precisely the input to Q2.2's re-threading; the closed form means the two operands compute the SAME curve (no divergence), satisfying dossier 59 Q2 by construction. **Verified** parametric equations from the Steinmetz definition.

### Q3.3 Cone / cylinder, cone / sphere, cylinder / sphere, cone / cone

- **Citation.** Quadric-quadric intersection (QSIC) theory: Levin's pencil method; Wang, Goldman, Tu, "Enhancing Levin's method for computing quadric-surface intersections," CAGD 20(7), 2003; Choi, Wang, Liu, Kim signature-sequence classification. https://i.cs.hku.hk/~ykchoi/quadrics/cagd_qsic.pdf
- **Method / content.** Two quadrics intersect in a quartic space curve (QSIC). Levin's method analyses the pencil lambda*A + mu*B of the two quadric matrices, finds a ruled quadric in the pencil, and parametrises the intersection on it; the result is a piecewise-rational parametrisation of each branch with a signature-sequence classification (number and type of branches: a closed oval, two branches, a nodal / cuspidal degenerate, etc.). For the special pairs Keel cares about (cone / cylinder, cone / cone coaxial-ish, cylinder / sphere) many cases reduce to a conic or a pair of conics with closed-form parametrisation. The preimage on each periodic surface is then obtained by inverting that surface's parametrisation (atan2 for the angle, the axial coordinate directly), giving the (u, v) pcurve.
- **Limitations.** The general QSIC parametrisation is rational but its branch structure needs the signature-sequence case analysis; a tangential intersection (cone tangent to cylinder) degenerates and needs the certified-NURBS recovery (below). Robustness of Levin's method near tangency is a known weak point.
- **Kernel relevance.** Gives a parametrisable (often piecewise-rational) preimage for the quadric-quadric seams beyond cyl / cyl, so rungs 2 to 3 of the ladder are exact or near-exact. **Verified** that QSIC is a parametrisable quartic; the per-pair closed forms are **claimed** to be derivable from Levin / Wang-Goldman.

### Q3.4 Where no exact preimage exists: certified-NURBS recovery and the SameParameter bound

- **Citation.** OCCT SameParameter (shape healing); Patrikalakis-Maekawa SSI / pcurve (dossier 59 Q1); Keel's certified evaluator-to-NURBS fit (MEMORY). https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/wiki/shape_healing
- **Method / content.** When the seam is a torus-involving curve or an analytic-vs-NURBS / NURBS-vs-NURBS intersection with no closed-form preimage, Keel fits the pcurve to a NURBS ONCE, caches it, certifies it (the certified evaluator), and reuses the SAME cached fit for both operands. The SameParameter condition bounds the fit: the 3D seam curve C(t) and each surface's pcurve image S(p(t)) must agree within a tolerance tau over the whole span, |C(t) - S(p(t))| <= tau, with tau feature-size-relative (dossier 47 / Qi-Shapiro). A fit exceeding tau returns "unknown" and the seam is DECLINEd (Q4 class), never imprinted as an uncertified curve.
- **Limitations.** The fit degree / knot placement must capture the curve's curvature; a high-curvature wrap near a pole needs dense knots. SameParameter is a necessary geometric-conformance condition, not sufficient for topological correctness (the loop must still close, Q2.2 step 6).
- **Kernel relevance.** The escape hatch for bucket-(b) seams (dossier 59), with a hard tolerance gate that keeps DECLINE-never-WRONG. **Verified** SameParameter convention; the cached-fit reuse is the dossier-59 Q2 sharing applied to recovered NURBS.

## Q4. Apex / pole / seam-slit special cases (representable vs DECLINE-forever)

- **Citation.** CGAL contraction-point handling (Q1.0 / Q1.3); degenerate-conic theory (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_conic); dossier 59 Q5; dossier 51 (free-edge discriminator).
- **Content (the case table).**
  - **Apex / pole as a legal loop member (REPRESENTABLE, re-thread).** A fragment whose loop passes through the apex / pole ONCE uses the contraction vertex as an ordinary loop vertex shared by all incident fragments (the CGAL "multiple edges converge at one vertex" rule). The loop is rim -> seam-up -> apex -> seam-down -> rim (or arc -> apex -> arc), with the apex appearing EXACTLY ONCE. The failure mode to avoid is a DOUBLED cover (the apex appearing twice, which double-counts the cap and gives mass != mesh); the arrangement guarantees single-incidence because the contraction is one vertex in the DCEL.
  - **Seam landing exactly on the existing u-seam slit (REPRESENTABLE, merge).** If an imprinted seam's slit crossing coincides (within tolerance) with the existing seam-slit edge geometry, the crossing vertex is MERGED onto the slit edge (a `split_edge` of the slit at that height), not duplicated. The slit edge is subdivided at the crossing; the two sub-edges get stable child `EdgeId`s (dossier 59 Q2 subdivision). No new degeneracy; this is the normal banding case (Q1.1 step 2 already does it).
  - **Loop tangent to the apex / pole (Q4 BOUNDARY, perturb or DECLINE).** A seam that approaches the apex tangentially (touches without crossing) has an ill-defined local structure; evaluate predicates at a point perturbed slightly down-slant from the apex (never AT the apex, where the normal is undefined). If the tangency is within tolerance of passing through the apex, escalate to the degenerate-conic test below.
  - **Degenerate conic through the apex (DECLINE-forever).** A plane (or quadric) through the cone apex yields a degenerate conic: a point, a single ruling, or a line-pair, not a proper conic. The "fragment" has no area on one side or pinches to zero width at the apex. DECLINE with a feature-relative epsilon: if apex-on-cut within tolerance, decline rather than emit an infinitesimal-parameter sliver. (A cut that merely TERMINATES at or reaches the apex off-degenerate is representable, the apex-reaching-arc case.)
  - **Self-intersecting wrap on the face (DECLINE-forever).** A seam whose preimage self-crosses on the face (a non-simple pcurve, for example certain skew quadric QSIC branches) cannot bound a single fragment; Q2.2 step 6's simplicity test catches it. DECLINE: not representable as a simple imprint edge.
  - **Sub-tolerance sliver band (DECLINE-forever).** A band whose (u, v) loop encloses area below the tolerant-edge tube has no well-defined pcurve interior and integrates ambiguously. DECLINE the fragment and the boolean rather than emit a sliver that risks mass != mesh.
- **Limitations.** The representable / decline boundary is tolerance-dependent (near-apex, near-tangent, near-self-intersection); the epsilon must be feature-size-relative, not global.
- **Kernel relevance.** Draws the exact line between the re-threadable cases (the bulk of the frontier) and the named DECLINE-forever set, keeping DECLINE-never-WRONG. **Verified** assembly; epsilons **claimed** and tunable.

## Q5. Validation oracle (mass == mesh == closed-form per fragment)

The split is validated by an EXACT analytic oracle: for each test the surface area and the enclosed volume of each band / fragment are closed-form, and the kernel must produce mass == mesh == closed-form per fragment, plus the structural count check fragment-count-out == kept-face-count-in.

### Q5.1 Cylinder cut by two parallel oblique planes (two ellipse wraps to three bands)

- **Citation.** Reconstruction from Q3.1 (plane / cylinder preimage) and elementary calculus of the oblique-cut cylinder ("cylindrical wedge / ungula").
- **Method / content (exact oracle).** Cylinder radius R, axis z, cut by two parallel oblique planes z = m x + c1 and z = m x + c2 (c1 < c2), giving two ellipse wraps (Q3.1 sinusoids v_i(u) = m R cos u + c_i). The middle band between them is a constant-vertical-thickness oblique slab of the cylinder: its lateral AREA = integral over u in [0, 2*pi) of (v_2(u) - v_1(u)) * R du = (c2 - c1) * 2*pi*R (the cos u terms integrate to zero), exactly a straight cylinder band of height (c2 - c1). The enclosed VOLUME of the solid band (cylinder of radius R between the two parallel planes) = pi*R^2*(c2 - c1) likewise (the oblique tilt cancels). The top and bottom bands' areas / volumes follow from the cylinder's total height minus the middle. **Oracle:** three bands, each mass == mesh == the closed-form value; fragment count out = 3 = kept-face count in.
- **Kernel relevance.** Directly exercises Q1.1 K = 2 banding with closed-form ellipse preimages; the cos-u cancellation is a clean exact check. **Verified** by integration.

### Q5.2 Cone cut by two coaxial circles (three frustum bands including the tip)

- **Citation.** Cone frustum lateral-area and volume formulae.
- **Method / content (exact oracle).** Cone half-angle alpha, apex at origin, cut by two axis-perpendicular planes at heights h1 < h2 (two coaxial circles, v = const wraps, the simplest encircling seams). Three bands: the tip cone (apex to h1), the middle frustum (h1 to h2), the base frustum (h2 to base). Frustum lateral area between heights ha, hb (radii ra = ha tan alpha, rb = hb tan alpha) = pi (ra + rb) * slant = pi (ra + rb) sqrt((rb - ra)^2 + (hb - ha)^2); for the axis-perpendicular cone, slant = (hb - ha)/cos alpha, so area = pi (ra + rb)(hb - ha)/cos alpha. Volume of the frustum band = (pi/3)(ra^2 + ra rb + rb^2)(hb - ha). The tip cone uses ra = 0. **Oracle:** three frustum bands, mass == mesh == closed-form; the tip band's loop passes through the apex ONCE (Q4 single-incidence check); fragment count out = 3 = kept-face count in.
- **Kernel relevance.** Exercises Q1.2 (cone banding plus the apex-reaching cap with single apex incidence), catching the doubled-apex mass != mesh bug. **Verified** by the frustum formulae.

### Q5.3 The unequal cylinder / cylinder (two non-planar quartic wraps to a barrel plus two stubs)

- **Citation.** Steinmetz / sphere-cylinder closed forms (Q3.2); the unequal-radius Steinmetz solid volume.
- **Method / content (exact oracle).** A z-cylinder A radius r1, an x-cylinder B radius r0 = 0.6 < r1. On B's x-wall the two Steinmetz wraps (Q3.2) encircle B's axis and split B's lateral into three bands: a central BARREL (the portion of B's wall inside A, the part removed by a subtraction or retained by an intersection) and two end STUBS. The exact lateral areas come from integrating the band heights of the closed-form preimage over phi; the enclosed VOLUME of the intersection solid (the part of B inside A) is the unequal-radius Steinmetz / bicylinder volume, a closed-form (elliptic-integral-free for the perpendicular equal case = 16/3 r^3; the unequal case is the standard bicylinder integral V = integral over the overlap, closed-form in r0, r1). The boolean result's mass is the operand volume minus / intersect this, exact. **Oracle:** for A subtract B, A's z-wall gets two theta-localised holes (the SSI as holes) and B's barrel band is removed; mass == mesh == closed-form bicylinder volume; on B's wall, fragment count out = 3 = kept-face count in; every seam edge carries a consistent pcurve on each incident face (SameParameter), and no band loop is zero-area (the Add.273 regression guard).
- **Limitations.** The unequal-radius bicylinder volume integral is closed-form but messier than the equal case; compute it once symbolically and pin it as the test constant. The two SSI loops must each pair across operands by `EdgeId` (dossier 59 Q2), so this oracle also validates the cross-operand identity.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is THE target oracle: it directly attacks the confirmed Add.273 decline, exercises the non-planar encircling wrap (Q2.2 re-threading), the closed-form quartic preimage (Q3.2), and the cross-operand pairing (dossier 59). Passing it converts the minimal hard case from DECLINE to PASS. **Verified** parametric preimage; the pinned volume constant is **claimed** until computed symbolically.

**ADOPT.**

This is the CGAL framework and it makes the seam slit and apex / pole first-class.

- **The non-collapsing band re-threading** (Q2.2): build each band as the region BETWEEN two seam crossings on the slit (or seam-and-rim / seam-and-contraction), closing the loop down one side of the slit and up the other; orient to positive (u, v) area; verify closed / simple / positive-area in 2D before any 3D construction.

- **The named DECLINE-forever set** (Q4): degenerate conic through the apex, self-intersecting wrap, sub-tolerance sliver band, uncertified recovered-NURBS seam, with feature-relative epsilons.

**AVOID.**
- **Incremental Euler surgery (split-seam-at-P + mev-to-antipode-Q + mef) for an encircling seam.** This IS the Add.273 collapse: it supplies one slit crossing plus an interior chord, which cannot bound a positive-area band. Build the band from the arrangement instead.
- **Treating a wrapping seam as a 3D chord across the slit.** The wrap is a loop crossing the IDENTIFICATION boundary; in (u, v) it is two arcs with the SAME 3D endpoint, bounding a strip, not a chord.
- **Marching a seam that has a closed-form preimage.** Marching reintroduces the cross-operand numeric divergence (dossier 59 Q2). Use the exact preimage.
- **Building any 3D entity before the (u, v) loop is verified closed and positive-area.** The 2D check is what makes the spur-collapse impossible.
- **Doubling the apex / pole cover, or evaluating a predicate AT the apex** (undefined normal). Perturb off the singularity; keep the contraction a single vertex.
- **Emitting a sub-tolerance sliver or degenerate-conic fragment to dodge a decline.** That risks mass != mesh (toward WRONG). DECLINE the named class.

- Whether Keel keeps Euler operators as a constrained playback of the arrangement (strategy B) for history / undo compatibility, or constructs faces directly (strategy A) and reconciles history separately.

**RECOMMENDED FIRST MILESTONE (narrowest honest slice with an exact oracle).** **A single cylinder lateral cut by ONE non-planar encircling seam, re-threaded into two valid nonzero-area bands with mass == mesh.** Concretely: take B's x-wall in the unequal cyl / cyl minimal hard case and imprint ONE of the two Steinmetz wraps, producing two bands whose loops are re-threaded by Q2.2 (down one side of the slit, up the other), verified positive-area in (u, v), then lifted to 3D. This is the smallest case that exercises everything the planar single-cut does not: the non-planar encircling wrap, the closed-form quartic preimage (Q3.2), and the band re-threading that the incremental spur fails (Q2.1 diagnosis). **Exact oracle:** the two bands' lateral areas integrate the closed-form preimage height over phi; mass == mesh == closed-form; fragment count out = 2 = kept-face count in; NEITHER band is zero-area (the direct Add.273 regression guard); the seam edge carries a consistent pcurve. It is fully analytic, attacks the exact confirmed decline, and needs no stitch / finalize change (dossier 59's machinery is untouched).

**FOLLOW-UP LADDER.**
