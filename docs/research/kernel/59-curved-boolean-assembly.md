# 59. Curved boolean assembly: stitching cross-operand CURVED seams into a valid shell

## Title and scope

This is the implementation-grade literature dossier for **curved boolean assembly**: the final stitch stage of Keel's boolean pipeline when the seams are CURVED (conic, quartic, recovered-NURBS) and the operand faces are PERIODIC or APEX-bearing (cone lateral, cylinder lateral, sphere with a pole seam, torus). It is the curved analog of dossier 47 (planar boolean result assembly) and dossier 57 (non-manifold general booleans), and it does not repeat either. Dossier 47 installed the `EdgeId` identity, the radial-splice, and the shell-closure (drop-prevention) invariant; dossier 57 generalized the stitcher to cells of mixed dimension. This dossier asks the orthogonal question those two deferred: **what changes when the shared edge is a conic on a periodic face rather than a line segment on a plane, and how does a fragment of a seam-slit-plus-apex cone lateral get split, paired across operands, and walked into a solid cell without dropping it or producing mass != mesh.**

**Where Keel is (grounding, build on, do not re-research).** Tolerant-f64 non-manifold PES / radial-edge B-rep; Euler operators (GWB); arenas + generational keys + EntityId identity. Analytic surfaces plane / cylinder / cone / sphere / torus plus NURBS. Boolean pipeline: `preimprint_coincident_overlaps` -> `seam_curves` (analytic SSI plus recovered-NURBS) -> `imprint_operand(a/b)` -> `classify_faces` (generalized winding number, 0.25 band around w = 0.5) -> `select_faces` (Requicha tables) -> `stitch_by_import` -> `finalize_imported_assembly` (Weiler face-side region walk). The polygon-soup stitcher is RETIRED (dossier 47's recommendation is adopted); ALL results route through `stitch_by_import` plus `finalize`. `stitch_by_import` already assembles planar results and single-cut curved cases (cone-oracle drilling / countersink). The explorer shows `UnassemblableSeam` (cone / cyl) and faulted / mass != mesh declines for MULTI-SEAM curved overlaps. LOG Add.250: a provably-disjoint union now bypasses to `combine_disjoint` (correct by construction); the genuinely-overlapping curved assembly is the open case. A cone lateral is ONE face whose boundary loop runs rim -> seam-up -> apex -> seam-down (seam-slit plus apex topology).

This is a **design recommendation** grounded in production-kernel (OCCT BOPAlgo, ACIS) and CAGD-intersection practice, to be validated against Keel's curved-boolean regression suite and the explorer decline corpus. House style: **no em-dashes anywhere, machine-checked; en-dashes are not used as separators either, ranges are written with "to".**

## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **Curved boolean assembly is NOT a new stitcher. It is the dossier-47 / dossier-57 `EdgeId` radial-splice stitcher plus ONE new obligation on the IMPRINT stage: a CANONICAL CURVED-SEAM IDENTITY that makes a conic / recovered-NURBS seam edge from operand A compare EQUAL to the same seam from operand B, paired with a SEAM-AWARE periodic-face split that keeps every fragment's pcurve loop closed across the parametric seam and apex. The stitch and finalize stay structurally identical to the planar path; what is curved-specific is (1) how the seam edge is NAMED canonically so cross-operand pairing is by identity not coordinate, and (2) how a periodic / apex face's boundary loop is re-closed after the cut. Everything else (group coedges by EdgeId, splice the radial cycle, assert closure, walk regions) is unchanged.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. Keel's pipeline is already correct in shape: imprint produces a local arrangement whose fragments share edges by `EdgeId`; stitch groups coedges by `EdgeId` and splices the Weiler radial cycle; finalize walks face-sides into regions. Dossier 47 proved this is drop-proof when sharing is by identity. The ONLY reason the curved multi-seam case declines today is that the two curved-specific preconditions of that proof are not yet guaranteed: (a) the seam edge must carry the SAME `EdgeId` when reached from operand A's imprint and from operand B's imprint, but a conic seam is computed twice (once per face-pair) and the two computations produce numerically distinct curves that a coordinate compare cannot reconcile, so the radial cycle never closes and the fragment is `UnassemblableSeam`; (b) a periodic or apex face's split must re-thread the parametric loop across the seam-slit and through the apex, but the analytic-SSI seam may not land cleanly on the existing parametric seam, leaving a fragment whose pcurve loop is open or doubly-covers the apex, which integrates to mass != mesh. Both are IMPRINT-stage defects surfacing at assembly. Fix them at imprint (canonical seam identity, seam-aware split) and the EXISTING stitcher assembles the result with no change, exactly as it already does for the single-cut cone-oracle case. This is the same lesson dossiers 47 and 57 reached for planar and mixed-dimension: the stitcher generalizes; the work is making sharing structural before it runs.

The one honest caveat: a small set of curved configurations are genuinely unrepresentable in a tolerant-f64 analytic B-rep (a self-intersecting seam where the SSI branch crosses itself, a sub-tolerance sliver fragment with no well-defined pcurve). Those stay in the DECLINE bucket (Q5), never WRONG. The goal is to shrink that bucket to the genuinely-ambiguous, not to widen DECLINE to cover engineering laziness.

## Q1. Curved seam imprint: splitting a periodic / apex face along an imprinted conic or quartic seam

A cone lateral is ONE face. Its boundary loop in object space runs rim -> seam-up -> apex -> seam-down, and in parameter space (u = angle around axis in [0, 2*pi), v = axial / slant) the seam-slit is the pair of parametric boundaries u = 0 and u = 2*pi identified, with the apex a degenerate v-isoparametric line collapsing to a point. Imprinting a conic seam (cone cut by a plane, or cone-cone / cone-cylinder SSI) onto this face means splitting that single periodic, apex-bearing parametric domain along a curve, then re-forming closed pcurve loops for each fragment. This is the hardest curved-specific step, and it is where the declines originate.

### Patrikalakis and Maekawa, Shape Interrogation for CAD and Manufacturing (the SSI and pcurve foundation)
- **Citation.** N. M. Patrikalakis, T. Maekawa, *Shape Interrogation for Computer Aided Design and Manufacturing*, Springer, 2002 (MIT hyperbook edition). https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/ ; Kriezis, Patrikalakis, Wolter, "Topological and differential-equation methods for surface intersections," CAD 24(1), 1992.
- **Method / content.** The canonical treatment of surface-surface intersection (SSI) for trimmed parametric / analytic surfaces. The intersection is represented BOTH as a 3D curve and as TWO pcurves, one in each surface's parameter domain. For each surface the algorithm needs the intersection's preimage as a curve in (u, v): for analytic pairs (cone-plane, cone-cylinder) the preimage is a known low-degree curve (the conic's u(v) relation on the cone is closed-form); for general parametric pairs it is traced by marching. The book is explicit that topological consistency requires the SAME 3D curve to be represented by both pcurves to tolerance (the SameParameter condition), and that singular points (the cone apex, a tangency) are isolated and handled specially because the preimage degenerates there.
- **Limitations.** Marching SSI for general NURBS pairs can miss small loops and mis-order branches near tangencies; the analytic cases Keel cares about (conics on quadrics) are the well-behaved subset.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the source of the dual-pcurve obligation Keel's imprint must honor. When `imprint_operand` splits the cone lateral along a conic seam, the seam edge must acquire a pcurve in the cone's (u, v) domain. For analytic cone cuts that pcurve is the closed-form conic preimage, not a marched approximation, which is why the single-cut cone-oracle case already works: its preimage is exact. The multi-seam decline is when two seams' preimages must coexist on one periodic domain and the splitter does not correctly partition the seam-slit-and-apex domain among the resulting fragments. **Verified** against the SSI / pcurve / SameParameter formulation.

### OCCT BOPAlgo: BuildPCurves, section edges, and the periodic-face split
- **Citation.** Open CASCADE Technology, "Boolean Operations" user guide and OCCT wiki (BOPAlgo_PaveFiller, MakePCurves, BOPAlgo_BuilderFace, BOPTools_AlgoTools::BuildPCurveForEdgeOnFace / AttachExistingPCurve). https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/wiki/boolean_operations ; https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/occt-7.4.0/overview/html/occt_user_guides__boolean_operations.html
- **Method / content.** OCCT's intersection part (`BOPAlgo_PaveFiller`) computes Face/Face interferences, creates SECTION EDGES from the intersection curves, splits them into pave blocks at shared vertices, and then runs a dedicated **BuildPCurves** step: "for each Face/Face interference build p-Curves on nFi and nFj for each section edge." Critically, `AttachExistingPCurve` "takes the p-Curve on the face from one edge and attaches this p-Curve to another edge coinciding with the first one," which is OCCT's mechanism for making the SAME section edge carry a consistent pcurve on both incident faces rather than recomputing it. The building part (`BOPAlgo_BuilderFace`) then constructs split faces from the collected edges WITH the section edges already shared. There is a separate **Process Degenerated Edges** step, which is exactly the cone-apex / sphere-pole degenerate-edge handling. Shape-healing's `SameParameter` enforces that the 3D curve and each pcurve agree to tolerance, with explicit support for periodic surfaces (cylinders, cones, tori).
- **Limitations.** OCCT's wiki does not fully document seam-edge re-threading for faces that straddle the parametric seam; in practice OCCT relies on shape-healing passes after the boolean to fix pcurve / SameParameter drift, which is a heal-after-the-fact posture dossier 47 argues against.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the production confirmation of Keel's needed mechanism. The OCCT pattern maps directly: Keel's `seam_curves` = section-edge creation; the missing piece is Keel's analog of **BuildPCurves plus AttachExistingPCurve**, namely build the conic preimage pcurve on each incident periodic face AND attach the SAME pcurve to the matching edge of the other operand's fragment, so the seam edge is shared with consistent pcurves by construction. The **Process Degenerated Edges** step is the cone-apex handling: the apex is a degenerate edge (zero-length 3D, nonzero parametric extent) and the splitter must preserve it as the loop's apex vertex, not collapse or duplicate it. **Verified** against the BOPAlgo step list. Patent posture: OCCT is LGPL open source, no claim-reading risk; cite freely.

### The cone seam-slit-plus-apex split, made concrete (reconstructed for Keel)
- **Citation.** Reconstruction from the two sources above plus dossier 45 (genus-1 solid-of-revolution Euler sequence) and the B-rep cone definition (one face, one seam edge, apex and base vertices) from CAD references (Rhino / Spatial B-rep glossaries). https://www.spatial.com/glossary/b-rep
- **Method / content (claimed, the recommended Keel mechanism).** Represent the cone lateral's parameter domain as a rectangle u in [0, 2*pi], v in [0, 1] with: the u = 0 and u = 2*pi edges identified (the SEAM SLIT, a manifold internal seam edge in 3D with a radial-cycle-2 pairing of the face to ITSELF, dossier 51 free-edge discriminator distinguishes this from a true free edge); the v = 1 edge (apex) collapsed to the apex vertex (the DEGENERATE edge). To imprint a conic seam C with closed-form preimage c(t) in (u, v):
  1. **Lift C into parameter space as a pcurve** using the analytic preimage, NOT a marched fit. A cone-plane conic has a closed-form u(v) or v(u); evaluate it to get c(t).
  2. **Detect seam-slit and apex crossings of c(t).** If c(t) crosses u = 0 / u = 2*pi (the seam), split c(t) there into sub-arcs and insert a vertex ON the seam edge (this is the curved analog of dossier 47's pave-block subdivision: the seam edge is subdivided where the new seam meets it). If c(t) passes through or terminates at the apex (v = 1), the apex vertex is a node of c(t).
  3. **Re-thread the fragment loops.** Each resulting fragment's boundary is assembled from: portions of c(t); portions of the original rim, seam-up, seam-down sub-arcs; and the apex vertex where present. The loop must be a CLOSED pcurve cycle in (u, v) for the fragment to tessellate and integrate. A fragment that contains the apex keeps the apex as a single vertex its loop passes through (rim -> c(t) -> apex -> c(t) -> rim), NOT a doubled cover.
  4. **Build the 3D seam edge once, attach both pcurves.** The seam edge gets a stable `EdgeId` (Q2), its 3D conic, and the cone-side pcurve; the OTHER operand's incident face gets the SAME edge with its own pcurve attached (OCCT AttachExistingPCurve pattern).
- **Limitations.** Closed-form preimages exist for the analytic pairs (quadric-quadric conics, cone-plane); for recovered-NURBS seams the preimage is a fitted pcurve and SameParameter must be enforced to tolerance. The apex is a true singularity: a seam passing exactly through the apex is a degenerate-conic case (Q5 boundary).
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the precise mechanism the multi-seam decline needs. The single-cut cone-oracle case works because one conic, cleanly off the apex, produces two fragments with trivially-closed loops. The decline is multi-seam: two conics partition the periodic domain into fragments whose loops must each be re-threaded across the seam-slit and possibly the apex, and the current splitter does not do step 3 robustly. **Claimed** as the recommended algorithm, to be validated against the cone / cylinder `UnassemblableSeam` repros.

## Q2. Cross-operand seam pairing: canonical curved-seam identity (the centerpiece)

After importing fragments from both operands, the shared seam edge must pair 1:1 (radial-2 for a manifold curved cut). The planar path solves this with `subdivide_seam_ring`: the seam ring is subdivided into pave blocks with stable child ids, so operand A's coedge and operand B's coedge on the same sub-segment carry the SAME `EdgeId` and pair by identity. The curved question is: **what canonical subdivision and identity makes a conic or recovered-NURBS seam from operand A compare EQUAL to the same seam from operand B, when each operand computed the seam independently and the two numeric curves differ in the last bits?**

### The problem, precisely
A boolean computes the A-face/B-face intersection. In a naive pipeline `imprint_operand(a)` intersects A's faces against B and `imprint_operand(b)` intersects B's faces against A; the SAME geometric conic is computed TWICE, once per direction, and the two results differ by tolerance (different parametrization, different marching seeds, opposite traversal sense). A coordinate compare at stitch time cannot reliably decide they are the same edge, so the radial cycle does not close: `UnassemblableSeam`. This is the curved manifestation of the exact failure dossier 47 diagnosed for the planar oblique edge, and the cure is the same in spirit (identity, not coordinate) but needs a curved-specific canonicalization because there is no shared lattice of integer coordinates to round to.

### The recommendation: compute each seam ONCE, canonicalize it, share the EdgeId
> **A curved seam must be computed exactly once per face-pair, assigned a canonical orientation and parametrization, given a stable `EdgeId`, and that SAME edge (with its `EdgeId`) must be inserted into BOTH operands' imprints. Cross-operand pairing is then by `EdgeId`, identical to the planar path; no curved coordinate compare ever runs at stitch time.**

This is the OCCT design: `BOPAlgo_PaveFiller` computes Face/Face interferences ONCE in a shared Data Structure, creates ONE section edge per interference, and `AttachExistingPCurve` shares it to both faces. Keel's `seam_curves` should likewise produce one canonical seam edge per face-pair, not one per imprint direction.

### ACIS section-edge and SSI sharing
- **Citation.** Spatial Corp (Dassault), ACIS "Modeling Operations > Intersectors" and "Types of Curves and Surfaces" technical articles (q-solid.com ACIS R17 mirror). http://www.q-solid.com/ACIS_Docs_R17/online/SPAacisuserTechArticles/SPAacisuser_mointrintr.htm ; http://www.q-solid.com/ACIS_Docs_R17/online/SPAacisuserTechArticles/SPAacisuser_gtyp.htm
- **Method / content.** ACIS represents cones, cylinders, planes, spheres, tori explicitly and NURBS implicitly; its intersector layer provides CCI / CSI / SSI. ACIS "correctly handles faces that span the seam(s) of a periodic surface," and a pcurve is "a mapping of a 2D curve onto a 3D surface." A boolean computes the SSI once and the resulting intersection edge is shared between the two trimmed faces it bounds; the seam of the periodic surface is a named edge along the identified parametric boundary so that trimming loops do not run outside the default parametric range.
- **Limitations.** ACIS is closed source; the SSI-once-and-share behavior is inferred from the documented intersector / pcurve model, not from source. Periodic faces forming a COMPLETE band (no seam crossing) are noted as a handled-but-distinct case.
- **Kernel relevance.** Confirms the production convention: the seam / intersection edge is computed once and shared, and the periodic seam is a first-class named edge so loops stay in-range. Keel's canonical-seam-identity recommendation matches ACIS exactly. **Verified** as documented behavior; SSI-once is **claimed** from the intersector model.

### Canonical orientation and parametrization for a conic / recovered-NURBS seam
- **Citation.** Reconstruction from Hoffmann (below), Patrikalakis-Maekawa (Q1), and dossier 11 (curved exact predicates).
- **Method / content (claimed, the recommended canonicalization).** Make the seam edge canonical by deterministic rules independent of which operand is processed first:
  1. **Canonical curve type and parametrization.** For analytic pairs, the seam is a typed conic (circle / ellipse / parabola / hyperbola branch) with a canonical parametrization derived from the two surfaces' axes (e.g., a cone-plane ellipse parametrized by the plane's intersection-with-axis frame). The same two surfaces always yield the same canonical curve regardless of order. For recovered-NURBS seams, fit once and cache (Keel already caches the certified evaluator-to-NURBS fit), and reuse the cached fit for both directions.
  2. **Canonical orientation.** Orient the seam by a deterministic key (e.g., increasing along a canonical frame axis, ties broken by a lexicographic tuple of the surface EntityIds), so both operands traverse it the same way; sense relative to each face is then derived from that face's natural normal, not from traversal order.
  3. **Canonical subdivision (the curved subdivide_seam_ring).** Where the seam meets other seams or pre-existing edges (including the periodic seam-slit and apex), subdivide at those nodes into sub-edges, each with a stable child `EdgeId` keyed by the canonical curve plus the ordered node parameters. Operand A's and operand B's fragments on the same sub-edge then reference the same child `EdgeId`.
- **Limitations.** Canonicalization must be stable under tolerant perturbation: two seam computations that agree geometrically must hash to the same `EdgeId`. This needs a tolerant node-snapping at the canonicalization step (done ONCE, with full geometric context, dossier 47's allowed use of tolerance), not a coordinate compare at stitch time.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the curved analog of `subdivide_seam_ring`. It is the single most important new piece. With it, cross-operand pairing reduces exactly to the planar mechanism (group by `EdgeId`, splice radial cycle), and `UnassemblableSeam` cannot occur because there is no second curve to fail to match. **Claimed**, validated against the cone / cylinder multi-seam repros.

### Hoffmann, geometric and topological consistency
- **Citation.** C. M. Hoffmann, *Geometric and Solid Modeling: An Introduction*, Morgan Kaufmann, 1989; C. M. Hoffmann, "How Solid Is Solid Modeling?" Workshop on Applied Computational Geometry, 1996, https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Hoffmann-Workshop_on_App_Comp_geo-1996-How_SolidIs_Solid_Modeling.pdf ; C. Hoffmann, V. Shapiro, "Solid Modeling," Handbook of Discrete and Computational Geometry, ch. 57. https://www.csun.edu/~ctoth/Handbook/chap57.pdf
- **Method / content.** Hoffmann's central thesis: robustness failures come from the DIVORCE of the geometric model (the numeric curves and surfaces) from the topological model (the incidence graph), and the cure is to keep topology authoritative and force geometry to conform, never the reverse. For booleans specifically, the shared intersection edge between two trimmed surfaces must be ONE topological entity even though each surface sees it through its own pcurve; representing it as two independently-computed curves is the root cause of gaps and non-watertight results.
- **Limitations.** A philosophy and a survey, not a turnkey algorithm; the consistency it demands must be engineered per kernel.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the theoretical backing for the canonical-identity recommendation and for dossier 47's identity-from-imprint. The seam edge is ONE topological entity (one `EdgeId`) carrying multiple pcurves; the two operands must not each own a private copy of the curve. Keel's `UnassemblableSeam` is precisely the Hoffmann divorce: two numeric seams, one intended topological edge, no shared identity to bind them. **Verified** against Hoffmann's consistency thesis. Pre-1995, no patent risk.

## Q3. Disconnected and cellular curved finalize: connected components and the Weiler region walk over curved fragments

After stitching, `finalize_imported_assembly` walks face-sides into regions (Weiler face-side region walk). For curved fragments with periodic / apex faces, the question is deriving one solid cell per connected component, plus enclosed-void cavities, robustly. Add.250: clean disjoint import resolves via `combine_disjoint` (correct by construction); the open case is OVERLAPPING curved cells.

### Connected components and the region walk on curved B-rep
- **Citation.** Weiler radial-edge structure (dossier 02 / 47); the B-rep shell / region definition (a shell is a connected component of a lump's boundary; regions are the connected parts of R3 minus the boundary; a void is an inner shell). Stallings, "Reduced Topology B-Reps," and the Spatial / general B-rep references. https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1532602 ; https://www.spatial.com/glossary/b-rep
- **Method / content.** Connected face components are found by union-find / graph traversal over the face-edge adjacency: two faces are in the same component if they share an edge (radial cycle), and the apex vertex and seam-slit edge are ordinary adjacencies that keep a cone lateral connected to itself across the slit. The region walk is Weiler's: each coedge (face-side) belongs to exactly one region; walk from a face-side across the radial cycle around each edge, ordered by dihedral angle, to enumerate the region on each side of each face. A component encloses a void if its outward shell contains an inner shell with reversed orientation; nesting is resolved by a ray-cast / containment test between shells.
- **Limitations.** The dihedral-angle ordering of the radial cycle needs a well-defined surface normal at the edge; at the cone apex and at a tangency the normal is undefined or ambiguous, so the angular sort degenerates there and must fall back to a perturbed-off-the-singularity evaluation.
- **Kernel relevance.** Keel already has this walk (`finalize_imported_assembly`) and it works for single-cut curved cases. The curved-specific hazards for the OVERLAPPING multi-cell case are: (1) the radial-cycle angular sort at an edge that terminates at the apex (sort using a point perturbed slightly down the slant from the apex, never at the apex); (2) two periodic faces sharing the seam-slit must not be mistaken for a region boundary (the slit is an internal manifold seam of one face, radial-cycle-2 to itself, dossier 51 discriminator). With the canonical seam identity (Q2) ensuring all coedges are correctly grouped, the region walk itself is unchanged from the planar / single-cut case. **Verified** that the walk machinery is reusable; the apex / slit perturbation is the **claimed** robustness fix.

### Mesh-arrangement cell labeling (the completeness lesson, curved restatement)
- **Citation.** Q. Zhou, E. Grinspun, D. Zorin, A. Jacobson, "Mesh Arrangements for Solid Geometry," ACM TOG 35(4), 2016, https://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/ ; G. Cherchi, M. Livesu, R. Scateni, M. Attene, "Fast and Robust Mesh Arrangements using Floating-point Arithmetic," ACM TOG 39(6), 2020.
- **Method / content.** Resolve all intersections into ONE complete arrangement, assign a winding-number vector to every cell, extract output cells by a per-cell predicate. No cell is ever welded away; the partition is complete BEFORE labeling, so a combinatorially-required cell cannot be dropped by numeric error.
- **Limitations.** Mesh-based and exact-arithmetic; the exact predicates do not transfer to curved analytic B-rep (no general exact SSI predicate). The completeness-then-labeling PRINCIPLE transfers fully.
- **Kernel relevance.** Already established in dossiers 47 and 57; restated here only to anchor the curved finalize: the imprint must produce a COMPLETE curved arrangement (every fragment shares its seam edges by identity) so that `finalize`'s region walk is a labeling over a complete partition, never a re-derivation that can drop a curved cell. The disjoint bypass (Add.250) is the trivial complete-partition case; the overlapping case becomes equally drop-proof once Q1 / Q2 guarantee completeness. **Verified** principle, curved predicates excepted.

## Q4. The drop-proof invariant for curved fragments

Dossier 47's invariant: every coedge of every kept face has exactly one radial partner (or a complete consistent radial cycle) before the shell is declared closed; an unmatched coedge is an ERROR to surface, never a face to silently drop. The curved analog must additionally account for periodic seam-slits, apex degenerate edges, and the multi-pcurve seam edge.

### The curved drop-proof invariant (the deliverable)
> **Before the curved shell is finalized: (1) every coedge of every selected curved fragment lands in exactly one consistent radial cycle keyed by `EdgeId`, including the seam-slit coedges (radial-cycle-2 of a periodic face to itself) and the apex (a degenerate edge whose radial cycle is the loop's apex vertex incidences); (2) every seam edge carries a consistent pcurve on EACH incident face (SameParameter to tolerance), so no fragment has an open or doubly-covered parametric loop; (3) the count of selected curved fragments out equals the count of selected KeptFaces in. Any unmatched coedge, any seam edge missing a pcurve on an incident face, or any count mismatch is a HARD ERROR with a diagnostic (which `EdgeId`, which face, which side), never a silent drop or a DECLINE-disguised-as-success.**

Clause (1) is dossier 47 unchanged, with the periodic-seam-slit and apex made explicit as legal radial-cycle members rather than special cases. Clause (2) is the curved addition: a planar fragment cannot have an inconsistent pcurve (a plane's pcurve is the identity), but a curved fragment can, and an open / doubled parametric loop is exactly the mass != mesh decline (the loop integrates wrong even though the topology closed). Clause (3) is the count check dossier 47 recommends, which would have caught `UnassemblableSeam` immediately as "fragments out < KeptFaces in."

### Why this is the right invariant, and what it catches
- **Citation.** Dossier 47 (closure invariant, count check); Sugihara-Iri topology-oriented implementation (Algorithmica 27, 2000) for "topology decided combinatorially, geometry conforms"; OCCT SameParameter (shape healing) for the pcurve-consistency clause.
- **Method / content.** The Sugihara-Iri principle says the combinatorial fact "this curved fragment's coedges share these named seam edges with these neighbors" is decided at imprint and is true regardless of f64 error in the conic; finalize must honor it and force geometry to conform. The SameParameter condition (OCCT) is the geometric conformance: the 3D seam curve and both pcurves agree to tolerance, which is the curved precondition for the fragment to integrate correctly.
- **Kernel relevance.** The invariant makes the two curved decline classes (`UnassemblableSeam` and mass != mesh) into LOUD ERRORS at the exact stage they arise, not silent corruptions. `UnassemblableSeam` violates clause (1) (a coedge with no `EdgeId` partner); mass != mesh violates clause (2) (a fragment with an inconsistent / open pcurve loop). Surfacing them as the named clause they break is what turns a mysterious decline into a fixable imprint bug. **Verified** assembly of established principles into the curved invariant.

## Q5. Decline policy for the genuinely unrepresentable

Keep DECLINE-never-WRONG. Maximize the representable set; reserve DECLINE for the genuinely ambiguous or unrepresentable, and make every decline a NAMED class with a justification, never a catch-all.

### What is genuinely unrepresentable (the curved DECLINE-forever set)
- **Citation.** Degenerate-conic theory (a plane through the cone apex yields a point / line / line-pair, not a proper conic; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_conic ; the practical note that the apex-on-plane test must use an epsilon, else the conic has an infinitesimal parameter); Qi-Shapiro epsilon-solidity (dossier 47); Patrikalakis-Maekawa on SSI singularities.
- **Content (the DECLINE classes, each justified).**
  1. **Self-intersecting seam.** When the SSI branch crosses itself (a curve that is not a simple arc on the face), the seam cannot be a single manifold edge; splitting along it would produce a fragment with no well-defined interior. DECLINE: the seam is not representable as a simple imprint edge. (Distinguish from a seam that crosses the parametric seam-slit, which is representable by subdivision, Q1.)
  2. **Seam through the apex (degenerate conic).** A cut plane through the cone apex gives a degenerate conic (point or line-pair). The "fragment" boundary has no area on one side or pinches at the apex. DECLINE with the degenerate-conic epsilon test: if apex-on-cut within tolerance, decline rather than emit an infinitesimal-parameter sliver. (A cut that merely TERMINATES at the apex, off-degenerate, is representable, Q1 step 3.)
  3. **Sub-tolerance sliver fragment.** A fragment whose parametric loop encloses an area below the tolerant-edge tube (dossier 42) has no well-defined pcurve and integrates ambiguously. DECLINE the fragment (and the boolean), do not emit a sliver that would make mass != mesh.
  4. **Uncertified recovered-NURBS seam.** When the recovered-NURBS seam fit's certification (Keel's certified evaluator) returns "unknown" (the fit error exceeds tolerance), DECLINE rather than imprint an uncertified curve. (Consistent with dossier 57's uncertified-curved-coincidence DECLINE and the MEMORY three-bucket oracle.)
- **Limitations.** The boundaries are tolerance-dependent: a near-apex cut and a near-self-intersecting seam are decline-or-represent depending on epsilon, and the epsilon must be feature-size-relative (dossier 47 / Qi-Shapiro), not global.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the honest floor. Each class is a NAMED decline with a geometric reason, so the explorer's decline corpus is a worklist (MEMORY: drive declines toward zero by SOLVING classes), not a pile. The bulk of the current `UnassemblableSeam` and mass != mesh declines are NOT in this set: they are Q1 / Q2 imprint defects (no canonical identity, bad seam-slit re-threading) that become PASS once fixed. Only classes 1 to 4 are DECLINE-forever. **Verified** assembly; the epsilon boundaries are **claimed** and tunable.

### Maximizing the representable set
> **Every decline must first be triaged against Q1 / Q2: is this an imprint-identity / seam-split defect (FIXABLE, becomes PASS) or a genuine class 1 to 4 (DECLINE-forever)? Only after the canonical-seam-identity and seam-aware-split work is in place is a residual decline trustworthy as genuine. Until then, an `UnassemblableSeam` is presumed a fixable imprint bug, not an unrepresentable input.**

This is the discipline that shrinks DECLINE toward the true floor without ever risking WRONG.

## Cross-cutting three-bucket catalog (required deliverable)

Which curved-assembly configurations (a) reduce to the EXISTING planar imprint / stitch machinery with only a curved-seam swap; (b) need a certified-evaluator / recovered-NURBS treatment Keel already caches; (c) genuinely need new general machinery.

**(a) REDUCE TO EXISTING MACHINERY with a curved-seam swap.** The seam is a closed-form analytic curve with a known parametric preimage; once it carries a canonical `EdgeId` and a closed-form pcurve, the stitch and finalize are byte-for-byte the planar path.
- Single conic cut of a cone / cylinder / sphere lateral, off the apex and not crossing the seam-slit (the cone-oracle case, ALREADY working).
- Cone / cylinder / sphere cut by a plane producing one conic seam: closed-form preimage, two fragments, trivially-closed loops.
- Multi-seam cuts of a single periodic face where each seam is a closed-form conic and seams do not mutually intersect on the face: needs only the canonical identity (Q2) and the seam-slit / apex re-threading (Q1 step 3); the stitch is unchanged. **This is the bulk of the current decline set and the first-milestone target.**
- Quadric-quadric conics (cone-cylinder, cylinder-cylinder, cone-cone) where the SSI is a true conic / quartic with a parametrizable preimage.

**(b) CERTIFIED-EVALUATOR / RECOVERED-NURBS treatment Keel already caches.** The seam has no closed-form preimage but Keel's certified evaluator-to-NURBS fit produces a cached pcurve; SameParameter must be enforced.
- Torus-involving seams (torus-plane, torus-cylinder) whose SSI is a higher-degree curve fit to NURBS.
- Any analytic-vs-NURBS or NURBS-vs-NURBS seam where the recovered curve is fit once, cached, certified, and reused for both operands (Q2 point 1). The two-sided neighborhood test (dossier 39) classifies fragments where the 0.5 band fires on a curved coincident face.
- Tangential curved seams (cone tangent to cylinder) where the SSI degenerates to a tangency curve: certified-evaluator classification, decline if certification is "unknown" (Q5 class 4).

**(c) GENUINELY NEW GENERAL MACHINERY.** No closed-form preimage and no clean certified fit; irreducibly hard.
- Seams that self-intersect on the face or pass through the apex as a degenerate conic (Q5 classes 1 to 2): DECLINE-forever, not new machinery, listed here to mark the boundary of (c).
- Mixed-dimension curved general bodies (a curved sheet imprinting a curved solid as an interior partition wall): the dossier-57 cell-complex selection layer with curved seams, which is (a)/(b) seam handling under a dimension-keyed keep predicate. New code is the selection layer (dossier 57), not the curved stitch.
- Many mutually-intersecting curved seams on one periodic face forming a non-trivial 2D arrangement in the parameter domain (a curved face cut by several other curved faces at once): needs a robust 2D arrangement in (u, v) of the periodic domain, the curved analog of the planar arrangement, with seam-slit and apex as domain features. This is the only genuinely-new algorithm, and it is the LAST rung.

The payoff: buckets (a) and (b) cover the overwhelming majority of the open curved declines, and both reuse Keel's existing stitch / finalize with the Q1 / Q2 imprint work as the only new code. Only the multi-seam 2D-arrangement case (c) needs a genuinely new algorithm, and it is the final rung, not the first milestone.

## Patent awareness

- **Requicha-Voelcker boundary evaluation, Weiler radial edge, Hoffmann consistency, Patrikalakis-Maekawa SSI / pcurve methods (1985 to 2002, mostly pre-1995)** are old prior art, any patents expired. These are the foundation to cite and build on. Prefer them.
- **OCCT BOPAlgo (BuildPCurves, AttachExistingPCurve, SameParameter, Process Degenerated Edges)** is LGPL open source; the techniques are published and freely usable, no claim-reading risk. Keel may follow the BOPAlgo step structure (compute section edges once, build / attach pcurves, share by identity) directly.
- **ACIS periodic-face and intersector behavior** is documented in public technical articles; the underlying methods (SSI, pcurve mapping, named periodic seam) are standard CAGD prior art predating ACIS, not novel claims. The specific SSI-once-and-share is a convention, not a patented mechanism. Building Keel's canonical-seam identity on the published SSI / pcurve methods (Patrikalakis-Maekawa) is the clean independent path.
- **Post-2006 curved-boolean / pcurve-repair patents** may exist around specific shape-healing and intersection-repair heuristics (the B-rep-boolean-repair-by-inference line, arXiv 2310.10351, and any vendor healing patents). Flag for claim-reading: Keel's posture (consistency-by-construction at imprint via canonical `EdgeId`, NOT heal-after-the-fact repair) is a deliberate design-around. Keel does not repair an inconsistent result; it never produces one, because the seam is shared by identity. That posture reads on Requicha / Hoffmann prior art, not on any post-2006 repair patent. Verify any specific healing heuristic against live patents before copying it; the recommendation copies none.
- **GWN over trimmed NURBS (Spainhour-Weiss 2025)** for curved classification: recent academic, no patent flagged, but patent-check before shipping a verbatim implementation; the underlying GWN (Jacobson 2013) is clear prior art.

Net: **build on the pre-1995 SSI / pcurve / consistency foundation and the LGPL OCCT step structure; achieve consistency by construction (canonical `EdgeId` at imprint), which is the design-around of any post-2006 repair patent; the path is clear.**

## Synthesis: ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE

**ADOPT.**
- **Canonical curved-seam identity** (Q2): compute each seam ONCE per face-pair, canonicalize orientation / parametrization / subdivision, assign a stable `EdgeId`, insert the SAME edge into both operands. This is the curved `subdivide_seam_ring` and the single most important new piece.
- **Seam-aware periodic / apex face split** (Q1): lift the conic into the parameter domain via its closed-form (or cached-NURBS) preimage, detect and subdivide at seam-slit and apex crossings, re-thread each fragment's loop closed, build the 3D seam edge once and attach a consistent pcurve to each incident face (OCCT BuildPCurves / AttachExistingPCurve pattern).
- The **dossier-47 radial-splice stitcher and dossier-57 finalize, UNCHANGED**, with the curved drop-proof invariant (Q4): periodic seam-slit and apex are legal radial-cycle members; every seam edge carries a consistent pcurve on each face; fragment count out equals KeptFace count in.
- **SameParameter enforcement** (OCCT) as the geometric-conformance clause: 3D seam and both pcurves agree to tolerance, the precondition for correct integration (kills mass != mesh).
- The **named DECLINE-forever set** (Q5 classes 1 to 4) with feature-size-relative epsilons; triage every residual decline against Q1 / Q2 first.

**AVOID.**
- **Computing the seam twice** (once per imprint direction) and reconciling by coordinate compare at stitch time. This IS `UnassemblableSeam` (the Hoffmann divorce). Compute once, share by identity.
- **Heal-after-the-fact** pcurve / SameParameter repair as the primary strategy. Achieve consistency by construction at imprint; reserve healing for nothing in the new path (it also design-arounds post-2006 repair patents).
- **Marching / fitting a seam that has a closed-form preimage** (cone-plane, quadric-quadric conics). Use the exact preimage; marching introduces the very numeric divergence that breaks cross-operand pairing.
- **Evaluating the radial-cycle angular sort or winding number AT the apex / a tangency.** Perturb off the singularity; the apex normal is undefined.
- **Emitting a sub-tolerance sliver or infinitesimal-parameter degenerate-conic fragment** to avoid a decline. That risks mass != mesh (toward WRONG). DECLINE the named class instead.

**INVESTIGATE.**
- The exact closed-form parametric preimage for each analytic seam class Keel supports (cone-plane, cone-cylinder, cylinder-cylinder, cone-cone, sphere-plane, torus-plane), so step-1 of the seam-aware split is exact for every bucket-(a) case.
- The tolerant canonicalization hash for `EdgeId` assignment: how to guarantee two geometrically-equal seam computations hash to the same id under perturbation (the stability condition of Q2).
- The 2D periodic-domain arrangement (bucket c) for many mutually-intersecting curved seams on one face: the only genuinely-new algorithm, needed at the last rung.

**RECOMMENDED FIRST MILESTONE (narrowest honest slice with an exact oracle).** **A single periodic face (cone or cylinder lateral) cut by TWO non-mutually-intersecting closed-form conic seams, assembled into a valid shell with mass == mesh.** This is the smallest case that exercises the curved-specific work the single-cut case does not: two seams partitioning the seam-slit-and-apex domain, requiring canonical `EdgeId` per seam (Q2) and per-fragment loop re-threading (Q1 step 3), but NO new stitch or finalize code. **Exact oracle:** a cone (or cylinder) drilled by two parallel planar cuts (two circular / elliptical seams) producing three bands; analytic surface area and enclosed volume of each band are closed-form; mass == mesh == the closed-form value; fragment count out == KeptFace count in; every seam edge has a consistent pcurve on each incident face. Fully analytic, bucket (a), and directly attacks the `UnassemblableSeam` decline.

**FOLLOW-UP LADDER.**
- Rung 1 (first milestone): two-conic cut of one periodic lateral, mass == mesh.
- Rung 2: cone / cylinder / sphere cut by ANOTHER quadric (cone-cylinder, cylinder-cylinder) producing a non-planar conic / quartic seam, single seam, mass == mesh.
- Rung 3: multi-seam quadric-quadric where seams cross the parametric seam-slit and one terminates at the apex (off-degenerate), exercising full Q1 step 3.
- Rung 4 (bucket b): torus-involving and recovered-NURBS seams, certified-evaluator pcurve, SameParameter enforced, two-sided neighborhood classification on curved coincident faces.
- Rung 5 (bucket c): many mutually-intersecting curved seams on one periodic face (the 2D periodic-domain arrangement), and curved mixed-dimension general bodies (dossier 57 selection layer with curved seams).

**DECLINE FOREVER.** Self-intersecting seams (Q5 class 1); seams through the apex as a degenerate conic (class 2); sub-tolerance sliver fragments (class 3); uncertified recovered-NURBS seams (class 4). Each a named class with a geometric justification and a feature-size-relative epsilon; never WRONG, and triaged against Q1 / Q2 before being trusted as genuine.

**The one-line verdict.** Curved boolean assembly is delivered as **canonical curved-seam identity plus seam-aware periodic / apex face split at the IMPRINT stage, feeding the UNCHANGED dossier-47 radial-splice stitcher and dossier-57 finalize**, which makes the bulk of the `UnassemblableSeam` and mass != mesh declines FIXABLE imprint bugs (buckets a and b) rather than unrepresentable inputs, leaving only the multi-seam 2D-arrangement case as genuinely-new machinery (bucket c, the last rung) and a small named DECLINE-forever set.
