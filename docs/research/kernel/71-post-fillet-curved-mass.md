## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **The fillet-trimmed support cylinder is a geometrically-correct, SIMPLY-connected, NON-WRAPPING partial face whose true boundary-flux value is the closed-form partial-cylinder slab `flux = sense * integral over the trimmed (u, v) region of (the cylinder slab integrand)`, and the green-slab reads it wrong for ONE reason: the post-fillet loop does not satisfy the precondition the `winding-0 v_base = v_min` slab anchor assumes. The anchor assumes the boundary loop, projected to the parameter plane, is a CLOSED loop whose signed area in `v` is taken from a single `v_min` datum with a consistent u-winding; but the spring-line imprint leaves a loop that is closed in 3D yet, in the cylinder's periodic `(u, v)` parameter plane, has (i) an AXIS-PARALLEL slit edge (the spring line, a `u = const` segment running the full `v`-range of the blend) and (ii) end arcs whose pcurves the blend imprint may not have attached to the SUPPORT face (the engine notes blend-face pcurves are follow-ups), so the slab integrator walks an open or mis-pcurved loop and integrates the WRONG planar region. The fix is NOT to edit the green-slab core (forbidden, it is the most delicately tuned code in the kernel). The fix is, in order of preference: (1) RECOVER a reduced ISO path. A spring-line trim that removes a single connected azimuthal wedge (the common fillet-on-a-boss case) leaves a face that is STILL an iso-rectangle in a REDUCED u-range `[u0, u1]`, exactly integrable by the cheap exact path over the partial azimuth, so the face should never reach the fragile green-slab at all; the cylinder rectangle-witness of dossier 66 is the router that detects this. (2) Where the trim is genuinely non-iso (the spring line plus curved end arcs do not reduce to a u-interval), supply the spring edge and the end arcs with their EXACT support-face pcurves (the spring line is an axis-parallel segment `u = u_s, v in [v0, v1]`; each end arc is the blend's contact conic projected to the cylinder `(theta, z)` plane, a closed-form curve), so the green-slab walks the CORRECT closed loop with the region on the left, and the axis-parallel slit contributes its true (generally zero-area-in-u but nonzero-flux) term. The slit is the cylinder analog of the dossier-66 inner-loop CW edge: it carries sign by orientation, not by a hand-coded rule. Either way the per-face value matches the closed-form partial-cylinder oracle. And independent of WHICH fix, a per-face area/volume WITNESS, face-local analytic flux vs face-local tessellated flux in the SAME reference (avoiding the confounded cross-face origin compare the prompt forbids), guards the trimmed support face so a self-consistent WRONG cannot slip, and lets the floor gate relax from the `>25%` whole-body band to a tight per-face PASS.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. The divergence theorem reduces the solid's volume to a sum of per-face surface fluxes; the Green-slab reduction sends each per-face cylinder term to a 1D line integral over the face's trim boundary in the `(theta, z)` parameter plane (dossier 60). That line integral equals the enclosed flux ONLY when the integrator walks the face's TRUE, CLOSED parameter-plane boundary with the kept region consistently on one side. A full-wrap cylinder has a trivial boundary (two `z = const` circles, the u-winding is a clean `+-1`), which the iso-rectangle path integrates exactly. The fillet spring-line imprint replaces part of that trivial boundary with a partial loop: an axis-parallel slit (the spring line, a `theta = const` segment) plus two end arcs. In the parameter plane this is a non-wrapping loop, and the `winding-0 v_base = v_min` anchor (which picks a single `v_min` datum and assumes the u-coordinate sweeps a clean closed circuit) is reading the WRONG datum or the WRONG winding for this loop, OR it never sees the correct end-arc pcurves because the blend imprint attached them only to the blend face, not the support, leaving the support loop effectively open in parameter space. The mesh is correct because tessellation triangulates the actual 3D trimmed region directly and never relies on the parameter-plane winding the slab anchor needs. The cure is to keep correct cases off the fragile path entirely (the witness routes a reduced-iso trim back to the exact path) and, for the genuinely non-iso remainder, to feed the green-slab the exact support-face pcurves for the spring line and end arcs so it walks the right closed loop. The closed-form partial-cylinder slab is the exact oracle, and a face-local flux-vs-tessellation witness in a shared reference is the WRONG-risk gate that lets the floor relax.

## 1. Diagnose the green-slab on a fillet-trimmed cylinder

The first scope question: state the correct boundary-flux value for a cylinder lateral face trimmed by a spring-line imprint (a straight spring edge along the axis plus the blend's end arcs, a non-wrapping partial loop), and identify the precise failure mode of the current `winding-0 v_base = v_min` slab anchor: anchor choice, pcurve attachment, loop orientation, or iso-vs-green path-selection threshold.

### 1.1 The correct boundary-flux value of a partial-cylinder trimmed face

> **Citation.** C. Gonzalez-Ochoa, S. McCammon, J. Peters, "Computing Moments of Objects Enclosed by Piecewise Polynomial Surfaces," *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 17(3):143-157, 1998 (the divergence theorem reduces volume / moments to a sum of per-face surface-flux integrals; the per-face term is reduced to its trimmed-patch boundary). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2459122_Computing_Moments_of_Piecewise_Polynomial_Surfaces ; Purdue TR: https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cstech/1348/ . A. Krishnamurthy, S. McMains, "Accurate GPU-accelerated surface integrals for moment computation," *Computer-Aided Design* 43(10):1284-1295, 2011 (surface moments of a TRIMMED parametric patch by Gauss / Green reduction directly over the trimming-curve boundary, the trimmed region honored exactly). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S001044851100162X ; SPM2010 preprint: https://mcmains.me.berkeley.edu/pubs/SPM2010KrishnamurthyMcMains.pdf .
> **Method / Content.** The cylinder of radius `R`, axis `a`, frame `(e1, e2, a)`, center `C` has the ruling parameterization
>
> ```
> P(theta, z) = C + R (cos(theta) e1 + sin(theta) e2) + z a,   theta in [0, 2*pi),  z in [z0, z1]
> ```
>
> with surface element `|P_theta x P_z| = R` (constant, no Jacobian singularity, no anchor needed). Pick `F` with `div F = 1`; the per-face volume contribution is `integral over the trimmed (theta, z) region of F(P) . (P_theta x P_z) d theta dz`. The Green-slab reduces the inner integral to a 1D line integral over the trim region boundary in the `(theta, z)` plane: walking the boundary with the kept region on the left, `flux = sense * oint_{partial D} g(theta, z) d(path)`, where `g` is the cylinder slab integrand `local_geometry` supplies and `sense = sense_sign * outer_sign` (dossier 46). For a fillet-trimmed support cylinder whose kept region is the rectangle MINUS a single azimuthal wedge of angle `(2*pi - (u1 - u0))` (the wedge the rolling ball ate, replaced by the blend), the TRUE region is `(theta in [u0, u1]) x (z in [z0, z1])`, a SMALLER rectangle, and the exact flux is the closed-form partial-cylinder slab
>
> ```
> flux_true = sense * pi R^2 (z1 - z0) * (u1 - u0) / (2*pi)        [partial-azimuth wedge case]
> ```
>
> the full-cylinder slab `pi R^2 (z1 - z0)` scaled by the kept azimuth fraction. When the spring line and end arcs do NOT reduce to a clean `theta`-interval (a fillet whose blend runs obliquely, leaving curved end arcs that are not `z = const`), the TRUE region is the rectangle minus a curved-boundary notch, and the exact flux is the boundary integral over the actual loop (spring slit + end arcs + the surviving rim segments), evaluable to the band by the generic arm.
> **Limitations.** Gonzalez-Ochoa-Peters is exact for polynomial patches; the cylinder is trigonometric, so the inner integral is closed-form (Keel's `local_geometry`) but the outer boundary integral over a genuinely curved end-arc trim is quadrature, exact only for the iso (`z = const` or `theta = const`) boundary segments. Krishnamurthy-McMains is GPU-targeted but the reduction (trimmed surface moment = boundary integral over the trim curves, the un-trimmed region contributing zero) is exactly the per-face value Keel needs.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the EXACT number the green-slab must reproduce for the trimmed support cylinder. The load-bearing fact: the correct value is the flux over the SMALLER kept region, and the bug is that the green-slab is integrating a LARGER (or mis-bounded) region, over-counting, which is exactly the seed-11400715918834829014 symptom (analytic 8405 over mesh 6170, an over-read of about 36%). **Verified** (the partial-cylinder slab closed form and the trimmed-patch boundary reduction are standard; Krishnamurthy-McMains is the canonical trimmed-patch surface-moment instance).

### 1.2 The failure mode: anchor datum, pcurve attachment, loop orientation, or path-selection threshold

> **Citation.** *Green's theorem* on a region traversed with a slit / branch cut (the slit's two sides cancel, leaving the true boundary), MIT 18.02SC and LibreTexts. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/3.-double-integrals-and-line-integrals-in-the-plane/part-c-greens-theorem/ ; https://math.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Calculus/Vector_Calculus_(Corral)/04:_Line_and_Surface_Integrals/4.03:_Greens_Theorem . Signed area as the integral of the winding number over the plane (a boundary loop's signed planar area is reference-independent only when the loop is CLOSED and consistently oriented). https://jeffe.cs.illinois.edu/teaching/comptop/2023/notes/02-winding-number.pdf . Dossier 40 section 3.3 (the blend-face pcurve is isoparametric and closed-form; the SUPPORT-face spring-line pcurve is the foot-of-perpendicular image, also closed-form, but must be attached to the support face at imprint time).
> **Method / Content (the diagnosis, claimed, ranked by likelihood given the constraints).** The prompt fixes three facts that sharply constrain the failure mode: the MESH is correct on the same loops (so the 3D geometry and winding are sound); the BLEND face integrates correctly (so the green-slab core is not generically broken); and the over-count is specifically the support cylinder. These exclude a green-slab-core bug and exclude a bad 3D winding. The remaining candidates, in order:
> 1. **PCURVE ATTACHMENT (most likely).** The green-slab needs each boundary edge's pcurve in the SUPPORT cylinder's `(theta, z)` plane to walk the loop. `fillet_edge` attaches the blend cylinder surface (blend.rs ~898), and dossier 40 section 3.3 notes the blend-face pcurves are isoparametric and cheap, but the SUPPORT-face pcurves for the spring line and the end arcs are the foot-of-perpendicular images, a separate construction; the engine's own note that "blend-face pcurves are follow-ups" is the tell. If the spring edge or the end arcs lack a support-face pcurve, the green-slab cannot close the loop in the `(theta, z)` plane: it either walks an OPEN loop (integrating the wrong, larger region, the over-read) or falls back to a default `theta`-range (the full `2*pi`, the full-wrap flux, exactly the over-count). This is the cylinder manifestation of the dossier-59 BuildPCurves gap and the dossier-66 pcurve-less cut edge, now on the fillet support.
> 2. **PATH-SELECTION THRESHOLD (joint cause).** The face fell to the green-slab when it should have taken a REDUCED iso-rectangle (section 3). The iso-vs-green selector saw a non-iso edge (the spring line and end arcs) and routed to the fragile path; but a spring-line trim that removes one connected azimuthal wedge is STILL exactly an iso-rectangle in `[u0, u1]`. The selector lacks the cylinder rectangle-witness (dossier 66) that would recover the reduced-iso route, so a correct, cheap path was available and not taken.
> 3. **ANCHOR DATUM (consequence, not root).** Given a mis-pcurved or open loop, the `winding-0 v_base = v_min` anchor reads its `v_min` datum and u-winding from the wrong loop, so the slab is anchored to the wrong baseline; but this is the SYMPTOM of (1), not an independent bug. Editing the anchor blind to "fix" it is forbidden and would not address the root (the loop it anchors is wrong).
> 4. **LOOP ORIENTATION (excluded).** A wrong loop orientation would flip the flux sign or corrupt the mesh; the mesh is correct and the magnitude (not sign) is wrong, so orientation is not the fault.
> **The precise condition under which a trimmed cylinder must take the green-slab vs a recoverable reduced iso-rectangle.** A trimmed cylinder face is EXACTLY a reduced iso-rectangle (recoverable to the exact path) IF AND ONLY IF its kept region in the `(theta, z)` plane is an axis-aligned rectangle `[u0, u1] x [z0, z1]`, equivalently: every trim edge is either a `z = const` segment (a rim) or a `theta = const` segment (an axis-parallel spring slit), and the kept region is their single rectangular intersection. The spring-line fillet on a boss (spine a circle or line giving a torus or cylinder blend whose contact curve on the support is an axis-parallel line, dossier 40 cases A/B) satisfies this when the blend's end arcs are `z = const`. It must take the green-slab ONLY when an end arc is genuinely curved in the `(theta, z)` plane (an oblique or variable-radius fillet whose contact end arc is a conic, not an iso-line), so the kept region is a rectangle minus a curved notch. The seed repro is, per the prompt, the wedge case (a straight spring edge plus end arcs), so it is RECOVERABLE to the reduced iso path; the bug is that it was not recovered.
> **Limitations.** The pcurve diagnosis is claimed from the engine's own follow-up note plus the dossier-40 construction; confirming it is a one-line check (does the support face's spring edge carry a `(theta, z)` pcurve after `imprint_spring_line`). The reduced-iso recovery assumes the end arcs are iso; an oblique fillet needs the section-2 pcurve fix.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the diagnosis the prompt asks for: the failure is PCURVE ATTACHMENT (the support-face spring-line and end-arc pcurves are incomplete after imprint) compounded by a MISSING PATH-SELECTION witness (the reduced-iso route was not recovered), NOT the anchor choice (a symptom) and NOT loop orientation (excluded by the correct mesh). **Verified** that an open / mis-pcurved parameter-plane loop integrates the wrong region (Green-with-slit, signed-area reference-dependence); **claimed** the specific attribution to the support-face pcurve follow-up plus the missing cylinder witness, to confirm by the one-line pcurve check and validate against the seed.

### 1.3 Why the mesh stays correct while the analytic mass is wrong

> **Citation.** MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap (the mesh triangulates the actual trimmed region; a chord-vs-arc gap is bounded and NOT a geometry bug). Dossier 66 section 3.2 (mass==mesh is necessary, not sufficient; a co-directional bias can agree). MIT 18.02SC slit / branch-cut Green's theorem (the parameter-plane subtlety the mesh never touches).
> **Method / Content.** The tessellation builds the `mesh` side by triangulating the face's actual 3D trimmed region from its loops directly; it never reduces the per-face term to a parameter-plane boundary integral, so it never depends on the `(theta, z)` winding or the `v_min` anchor the green-slab needs. A loop that is correct in 3D but mis-pcurved in the support cylinder's parameter plane tessellates correctly (the triangles cover the right region) while the green-slab, which DOES depend on the parameter-plane loop, mis-integrates. This is why the prompt's constraint holds: the surgery geometry is sound (correct mesh), the bug is purely the analytic parameter-plane integration. The corollary is that a naive per-face "analytic dv vs mesh dv" localizer is CONFOUNDED (the prompt's dead end): per-face analytic flux uses a face-specific origin reference and the analytic path SKIPS interior partition walls that the naive mesh sum does NOT, so the two do not match per-face even when both are correct. The witness must therefore be face-LOCAL in a SHARED reference (section 4), not a cross-face origin compare.
> **Limitations.** The mesh being correct is specific to THIS bug class (a parameter-plane pcurve / winding fault); a genuine 3D geometry bug WOULD corrupt the mesh, which is exactly why the correct mesh is the diagnostic that rules a geometry bug out here.
> **Kernel relevance.** Establishes that the fix targets the analytic parameter-plane path only (pcurve attachment + path-selection), never the surgery, and that the witness must avoid the confounded cross-face origin compare. **Verified** (tessellation independence of the parameter-plane winding is structural; the confound is the prompt's stated dead end).

## 2. The pcurve question: exact support-face pcurves for the spring line and end arcs

The second scope question: does the green-slab require an exact `(theta, z)` pcurve for the spring edge and the end arcs on the trimmed cylinder, and what is the correct pcurve for each (the spring line is an axis-parallel segment; the end arc is the blend's conic on the support)? State the construction.

### 2.1 The green-slab requires an exact support-face pcurve for every boundary edge

> **Citation.** Demetri Terzopoulos course notes, "Trimming Curves and Surfaces" (the projection of a trimmed surface's boundary curves onto the parameter domain is the p-curve; the trimmed region is defined in parameter space). http://web.cs.ucla.edu/~dt/papers/tog94/node24.html . "Fast and Robust Point Containment Queries on Trimmed Surfaces" (p-curves are essential B-rep components; a trim loop is walked in parameter space). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25159 . Dossier 60 section 2 (the boundary integrand is `(point, tangent)`-only, but on a PARAMETRIC surface the green-slab walks the parameter-plane loop, so it needs each edge's `(theta, z)` image). Dossier 40 section 3.3 (support-face spring-line pcurve is the foot-of-perpendicular image, closed-form; blend-face pcurve is isoparametric).
> **Method / Content.** The green-slab is a Green's-theorem line integral over the trim region in the cylinder's `(theta, z)` plane; to walk it the integrator needs each boundary edge's PCURVE (its image in `(theta, z)`). An edge represented only by a 3D `Curve3`, with no support-face pcurve, cannot be walked in the parameter plane: the slab either skips it (open loop, wrong region) or substitutes a default (full wrap). So YES, the green-slab requires an exact `(theta, z)` pcurve for the spring edge and for each end arc ON THE SUPPORT FACE, distinct from the pcurve those same edges carry on the BLEND face. The blend face gets its pcurves cheaply (the spring curve is an isoparametric boundary of the canal loft, dossier 40 section 3.3, a `v = const` line in the blend's own parameters); the SUPPORT face needs its own, and that is the follow-up the engine flagged.
> **Limitations.** A pcurve obtained by 3D-point-projection-and-fit (sample, invert, fit) is approximate and can introduce a sliver or a tolerance gap; for the cylinder support the pcurves are closed-form (section 2.2), so projection-fit is unnecessary and should be avoided (it is the usual source of pcurve gaps, dossier 40 section 3.3).
> **Kernel relevance.** Confirms the diagnosis of section 1.2: the missing support-face pcurves are why the green-slab walks the wrong loop. The fix is to ATTACH the closed-form support pcurves at imprint time, not to touch the slab core. **Verified** that a parameter-plane trim integral needs pcurves; **claimed** that the support-face spring-line/end-arc pcurves are the specific missing data, to confirm by the one-line check.

### 2.2 The exact pcurve construction for the spring line and the end arcs on the support cylinder

> **Citation.** Dossier 40 section 1.3 (the spring curve on a cylinder is the radial projection of the spine onto the cylinder; in cylinder `(theta = u, z = axial)` parameters it is closed-form) and section 3.3 (the support-face pcurve is exact and analytic, never a fitted projection). *Cylinder* parameterization and the foot-of-perpendicular projection (radial projection onto a cylinder). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder .
> **Method / Content (the construction, the deliverable).** Let the support cylinder be `P(theta, z) = C + R (cos theta e1 + sin theta e2) + z a`. The two trim-edge families a constant-radius fillet leaves on the support cylinder are:
> 1. **The spring line (axis-parallel slit).** For the common boss / plane-cylinder-parallel-axis fillet (dossier 40 case A, spine a line, blend a cylinder), the rolling-ball contact curve on the support cylinder is a STRAIGHT line parallel to the axis. Its support-face pcurve is the AXIS-PARALLEL SEGMENT
>
>    ```
>    spring pcurve:  theta = theta_s (const),  z in [z0, z1]
>    ```
>
>    where `theta_s` is the azimuth of the radial direction from the axis to the spring contact (the foot of perpendicular from the spine line, projected radially: `theta_s = atan2((g_s - C) . e2, (g_s - C) . e1)` for a contact point `g_s`). This is the cylinder analog of the dossier-66 inner-loop edge: a `theta = const` slit, contributing its flux by orientation. For a fillet whose blend is a torus (spine a circle, plane-cylinder-perpendicular or plane-sphere), the contact curve on the cylinder is a CIRCLE (a `z = const` rim) when the spine circle is coaxial, giving a `z = const` rim pcurve, the trivial iso case.
> 2. **The end arcs (the blend's conic on the support).** Each end of the blend terminates on the support cylinder along the blend's end cross-section, whose image on the support is the contact point's locus as the cross-section is traced. For an iso (`z = const`) blend end the end arc is a `z = const` rim segment (iso, recoverable). For an oblique or variable-radius blend the end arc is the blend cross-section circle's contact image, a CONIC in the support cylinder's `(theta, z)` plane (the radial projection of the end cross-section circle onto the cylinder, a sinusoid-like closed-form curve `z = z(theta)` along the contact). Its pcurve is the closed-form radial-projection image, computed analytically from the blend cross-section circle and the cylinder, never fitted.
> **The full support-cylinder trim loop** is then: surviving rim segment(s) (`z = z0` and `z = z1`, the parts not eaten), the two end arcs (iso `z = const` or closed-form conic), and the spring slit (`theta = theta_s`, axis-parallel), walked with the kept region on the left. In the pure wedge case this loop bounds the reduced rectangle `[u0, u1] x [z0, z1]` and is recoverable to the exact path (section 3); in the oblique case it bounds a curved-notch region the green-slab integrates with these exact pcurves.
> **Limitations.** The closed-form pcurve holds for analytic support cylinders and analytic blends (dossier 40 cases A to C); a NURBS-canal blend (case D) has a fitted end arc whose support pcurve is the certified-NURBS contact image, integrated by the generic arm with adaptive panels and DECLINE on the cap (dossier 60 section 2.2). The spring slit's `theta_s` must be the SUPPORT cylinder azimuth, not the blend's parameter, so it is computed in the support frame.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the pcurve construction the imprint must attach to the support face so the green-slab walks the correct loop: the spring line is an axis-parallel `theta = const` segment, each iso end arc is a `z = const` rim, each oblique end arc is the closed-form radial-projection conic. With these attached, the slit carries its flux by orientation (the dossier-66 CW-edge mechanism) and the loop closes correctly. **Verified** that the cylinder support pcurves are closed-form (dossier 40); **claimed** the specific spring-slit and end-arc constructions, to validate against the seed and the closed-form partial-cylinder oracle.

## 3. Path-selection robustness: recover the cheap exact iso path for correct cases

The third scope question: the iso-rectangle path is EXACT and cheap, the green-slab is general but fragile here; give the rule for when a fillet-trimmed cylinder can still be integrated by a (clipped) iso path versus must use the slab, so correct cases avoid the fragile path entirely.

### 3.1 The reduced-iso recovery rule (the cylinder rectangle-witness applied to the fillet trim)

> **Citation.** Dossier 66 section 1 (the cylinder rectangle-witness: analytic full-band area `2 pi R h` vs tessellated face area, route off the iso-rectangle fast path on mismatch). Dossier 60 section 3 (the cone/sphere rectangle-witness, the partial-azimuth region check). Lee-Requicha framing (the modeling scheme dominates the integration-algorithm design; a fast specialization assumes a region that may not be the face). Y. T. Lee, A. A. G. Requicha, "Algorithms for computing the volume and other integral properties of solids. I and II," *Comm. ACM* 25(9):635-650, 1982.
> **Method / Content (the rule, claimed, the deliverable).** A fillet-trimmed cylinder face takes the CHEAP EXACT path (no green-slab) IF its kept `(theta, z)` region is an axis-aligned rectangle, detected by:

### 4.1 The confound: why a naive per-face analytic-vs-mesh compare fails

> **Citation.** The prompt's stated dead end (per-face flux uses different origin references; interior partition walls are skipped by the analytic path but not the naive mesh sum, so they do not match per-face even when both are correct). UAF CS482 lecture notes (the divergence-theorem flux `F = (x, 0, 0)` is origin-dependent per face; only the CLOSED-body sum is origin-independent). https://www.cs.uaf.edu/2015/spring/cs482/lecture/02_20_boundary.html .
> **Method / Content.** The per-face flux for `F = (x, 0, 0)` depends on the chosen origin: `integral_face x dy dz` shifts by `x0 * (projected area)` under an origin shift `x -> x + x0`. The whole-body sum is origin-independent (the projected areas cancel over a closed shell), but any per-face compare must use the SAME origin on both the analytic and the mesh side, AND must account for interior partition walls (shared faces between cells) that the analytic per-face path may skip while a naive triangle-sum over the mesh includes them. A naive "analytic dv on this face vs mesh dv on this face" therefore mismatches even when both are correct, which is the confound. This is why the floor gate is a whole-body `>25%` band: the whole-body sum is the only origin-clean, partition-clean comparison the naive approach supports, and `>25%` is a loose tolerance to avoid false DECLINEs on that coarse check.
> **Limitations.** The confound is specific to the origin-dependent volume field; an origin-INDEPENDENT per-face quantity (the face AREA, or the face's flux computed in a face-LOCAL frame) sidesteps it.
> **Kernel relevance.** Explains why the current gate is whole-body and loose, and why a tighter per-face gate needs a confound-free witness. **Verified** (per-face flux origin-dependence and the partition-wall skip are standard divergence-theorem facts).
