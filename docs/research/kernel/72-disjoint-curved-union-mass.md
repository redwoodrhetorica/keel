# 72. Disjoint curved-union mass: why analytic mass_properties collapses on a multi-lump combine_disjoint result, and the orientation fix

## Title and scope

This dossier is the implementation-grade literature foundation for the **multi-lump mass accumulation defect on a disconnected curved body**: why Keel's divergence-theorem `mass_properties` returns a collapsed volume (238 against the true 14441 = mesh) on a `combine_disjoint` result built from a sphere mirror plus union of two disjoint curved lumps, and the narrowest fix that converts that floored DECLINE into a PASS without touching the planar disjoint-union case (exact today).

It builds on two prior dossiers and does not repeat them. Dossier 60 covered the **single** curved lump: completing the per-face Green-slab boundary integrator (apex anchor, generic boundary arc, partial-fragment witness) so one correctly-assembled curved face integrates to the band. Dossier 61 covered the **disjointness certificate**: a sound lower-bound proof that two AABB-overlapping operands do not touch, which licenses the `combine_disjoint` short-circuit. This dossier is the third piece: given that two lumps are correctly imported into one disconnected (multi-shell, multi-region) body, why does the **accumulation across lumps** lose one lump's flux, and how is the per-region / per-shell boundary-flux sum made correct.

The defect is confirmed (repro seed 11400715918834829910). The grounding facts, verified against the source:

- The union of two AABB-separated (or proven-disjoint, dossier 61) bodies short-circuits to `combine_disjoint` (boolean.rs:4096), which imports both operands verbatim into one body with one shared solid region, producing a valid disconnected multi-shell body. The arm calls `import_face(..., reversed=false, ...)` for **both** operands (boolean.rs:2407 to 2412).
- Mass is the divergence-theorem boundary flux, accumulated per face over **all** faces of the body in one global loop (massprops.rs:122), with an interior-wall skip on `(front_solid, back_solid) == (true, true)` (massprops.rs:137), a single-solid-side validity check (massprops.rs:142), and the outward-normal authority `n_out = sense * natural_normal` folded in as `sense_sign` (massprops.rs:163, dossier 46).
- `import_face` sets the imported face's surface sense to `ssense ^ reversed` (boolean.rs:2226). With `reversed=false`, the imported sense equals the source sense bit verbatim.
- The floor gate (commit 3d49fee, boolean.rs:4108 to 4118) cross-checks analytic `mass_properties().volume` against `mesh_volume()`; a `>25%` relative gap declines the disjoint union. This converts the present WRONG (238 vs 14441) to a DECLINE. The ask is to make the analytic mass correct so the gate passes.

**The load-bearing conclusion, stated once.** The accumulation loop is structurally correct for multiple regions: a global per-face flux sum with the interior-wall cancellation IS the per-region volume sum, because each disjoint region's outward-oriented boundary faces contribute that region's volume and there are no shared walls between disjoint lumps. The collapse is **not** in the loop and **not** a shared pole/seam anchor and **not** the interior-wall skip firing across lumps. The collapse is an **orientation defect on the mirrored lump**: a mirror is an improper isometry (determinant -1) that reflects each surface's natural normal, but `combine_disjoint` imports the reflected lump's faces with `reversed=false`, so their stored `sense` bit is unchanged while their natural normal has flipped. The outward-normal rule `n_out = sense * natural_normal` then points **inward** for every face of the mirrored lump, and that lump's boundary flux **subtracts** instead of adds. The body volume reads `V_unmirrored - V_mirrored` (a near-cancellation, 238) instead of `V_unmirrored + V_mirrored` (14441). The fix is to make the mirror carry a sense reversal (the determinant-sign / reverse-sense rule, ISO 10303-42), so the imported reflected face's outward normal points outward again, exactly as the STEP standard requires of a face transformed by a negative-determinant operator.

House style: **no em-dashes anywhere, machine-checked; en-dashes are not used as separators either, ranges are written with "to".** Structure mirrors files 01 to 69: annotated source blocks (Citation / Method / Limitations / Kernel relevance), explicit verified-vs-claimed labels, patent flags, and a closing ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE with a first milestone, a follow-up ladder, and a DECLINE-forever set.

## 1. Per-region flux on a disconnected curved body, and the four candidate collapse modes

The first scope question: how must the divergence boundary flux accumulate over a body with multiple disjoint solid regions each bounded by its own shell, and where in the `mass_properties` face loop can the sphere-pair case collapse. This section states the correct accumulation, then analyzes each candidate collapse mode against the source, eliminating three and confirming the fourth (the mirror orientation, section 2).

### 1.1 The per-region flux sum is exactly the global per-face flux sum (no per-region loop needed)

> **Citation.** Divergence theorem and its additivity over a partitioned / disconnected region: *Divergence Theorem*, Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergence_theorem); *6.8 The Divergence Theorem*, OpenStax Calculus Volume 3 (https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-3/pages/6-8-the-divergence-theorem); MIT OCW 18.013A *Measures on Volumes and the Divergence Theorem* (https://ocw.mit.edu/ans7870/18/18.013a/textbook/HTML/chapter22/section03.html). Per-face signed-volume accumulation: M. E. Mortenson, *Geometric Modeling*, Wiley (mass properties by per-face parametric integration); the polyhedral signed-tetra sum (each facet contributes a signed determinant term).
> **Method / Content.** For the volume field `F = (x, 0, 0)`, `div F = 1`, so `V = integral over the boundary of x dy dz = integral over the boundary of F . n dA`, evaluated face by face with the **outward** normal `n`. The decisive additivity fact (verified, all sources): if a region is partitioned, the flux out of the whole equals the **sum** of the fluxes out of the parts, because any wall shared by two sub-regions is traversed with opposite outward normals from the two sides and **cancels**. For a **disconnected** body (the sphere pair) the two lumps share **no** wall at all, so the cancellation set is empty and the body flux is simply the sum of the two lumps' fluxes, each computed over its own outward-oriented shell. Concretely: `V_body = sum over regions r of (sum over faces f of region r of integral_f F . n_out dA)`. Because every face belongs to exactly one solid region on its solid side, **a single global loop over all faces, each integrated with its own outward normal, computes the same sum** with no explicit per-region grouping. The region structure matters only through the per-face `(front_solid, back_solid)` test that decides which faces are boundary faces and the sense that decides the outward direction.
> **Limitations.** The equality holds only if every face's `n_out` truly points out of its own lump. If one lump's faces are all inward-oriented, that lump's flux enters the sum with a global sign flip and **subtracts**: `V_body = V_a - V_b`. This is the failure mode of section 2. The additivity itself never fails; the per-face orientation can.
> **Kernel relevance.** This justifies Keel's design: `mass_properties` (massprops.rs:111 to 172) is **right** to use one global face loop (massprops.rs:122) with no per-region inner loop. The multi-region body needs no new accumulation structure. What it needs is for every imported lump's faces to carry the correct outward orientation, which is a per-face property set at import time, not an accumulation property. **Verified** that the global per-face sum equals the per-region volume sum for a disconnected body; **verified** that an inward-oriented lump subtracts.

### 1.2 Candidate A: is a second shell's (front_solid, back_solid) solidity read wrong?

> **Method / Content.** The per-face solidity test (massprops.rs:137 to 146) reads `face.front_region` and `face.back_region`, maps each to its region's `solid` flag, skips `(true, true)` interior walls, and requires exactly one solid side otherwise. In `combine_disjoint` (boolean.rs:2400 to 2416) **both** lumps' faces are imported into the **same** `solid` region (`dst.new_region(..., true, ...)` allocated once at boolean.rs:2403, passed as the `solid` argument to every `import_face`) with the shared infinite `inf` region on the outside. So every boundary face of every lump has `(front=inf=false, back=solid=true)` or its reverse, identically for lump A and lump B. The solidity test therefore reads `(false, true)` (a genuine boundary face) for both lumps and never sees a spurious `(true, true)` between them (there is no face between disjoint lumps). **The solidity read is correct for the second shell.**
> **Limitations.** The test would mis-read only if the two lumps were placed in **different** solid regions with a face somehow shared, or if a lump's boundary face were assigned `(solid, solid)`. Neither happens: disjoint lumps share one solid region and no faces. A single shared solid region for multiple disjoint shells is valid (a region is a point set bounded by possibly several shells; entity.rs Region holds `shells: Vec<ShellKey>`).
> **Kernel relevance.** Candidate A is **eliminated**. The solidity test is orientation-blind (it only asks which side is solid), and it reads correctly for both lumps. The collapse is not here. **Verified** against boolean.rs:2403 to 2416 and massprops.rs:137 to 146.

### 1.3 Candidate B: does a pole / seam anchor get shared or duplicated across lumps?

> **Method / Content.** The curved integrator `integrate_curved_face` (massprops.rs:167, the dossier-60 Green-slab path) anchors a full-revolution sphere face at a POLE and accumulates u-winding per face. The anchor is a **per-face** construct: it is recomputed inside the integration of each face from that face's own surface and loop, with no cross-face or cross-region state (the loop at massprops.rs:122 carries only the `Moments` accumulator `m`, which is a sum of scalars and vectors, not a geometric anchor). Importing a second sphere lump creates **new** faces, loops, and a new surface (boolean.rs:2224 `dst.add_surface(sg)` clones the surface per imported face), so the second lump's pole anchor is computed from the second lump's own geometry. There is no shared pole, no shared seam vertex, no shared anchor across lumps.
> **Limitations.** A shared anchor could only arise if the two lumps shared a surface or vertex by identity. `import_face` clones the surface (boolean.rs:2224) and maps vertices through a per-operand `vmap` keyed by `(Operand, id)` (boolean.rs:2139, 2404), so operand A's and operand B's entities never alias. The anchor is per-face and per-lump by construction.
> **Kernel relevance.** Candidate B is **eliminated**. The anchor is per-face local state, recomputed for each lump's faces; nothing is shared or double-counted across lumps. The collapse magnitude (a near-cancellation to 238, not a doubling or a halving) is also inconsistent with a duplicated anchor and consistent with a sign flip. **Verified** that the integrator carries only a scalar/vector accumulator across faces (massprops.rs:114, 164) and clones surfaces per import (boolean.rs:2224).

### 1.4 Candidate C: does the interior-wall skip ((true,true) continue) wrongly fire across lumps?

> **Method / Content.** The interior-wall skip (massprops.rs:137) fires only when a face's **own** two incident regions are both solid. Between two disjoint lumps there is **no face** (they do not touch, that is the disjointness premise, dossier 61), so there is no candidate face for the skip to mis-fire on. Within each lump, every boundary face has the infinite region on one side (`inf`, solid=false), so `(false, true)` and the skip never fires. The skip is correct: it suppresses only genuine internal partition walls (item 29, a face with solid material on both sides), which a disjoint union does not contain.
> **Limitations.** The skip could wrongly fire only if a boundary face were assigned solid on both sides, which would be an import bug placing the infinite region wrong. boolean.rs:2150 assigns `(inf, solid)` to every imported face front/back, so the outside is always the non-solid infinite region. No across-lump mis-fire is possible.
> **Kernel relevance.** Candidate C is **eliminated**. The skip is a per-face local test on the face's own two regions; with no shared face between lumps and the infinite region always on the outside, it cannot fire across lumps. **Verified** against massprops.rs:137 and boolean.rs:2150.

### 1.5 Candidate D (confirmed): the mirrored lump's faces are inward-oriented

> **Method / Content.** With A, B, C eliminated, the collapse is the orientation of the **mirrored** lump's faces. The repro is a sphere **mirror** plus union: operand B is a reflected copy. `combine_disjoint` imports both operands with `reversed=false` (boolean.rs:2407 to 2412), so the imported face sense is `ssense ^ false = ssense`, the source sense bit unchanged (boolean.rs:2226). But the mirror reflected the **geometry** upstream: the surface's natural normal is computed from reflected coefficients and now points the **opposite** way relative to the solid. The outward-normal authority `n_out = sense * natural_normal` (massprops.rs:163, dossier 46) therefore points **inward** for every face of the mirrored lump. By section 1.1, that lump's flux **subtracts**: `V_body = V_a - V_b`. For two near-equal sphere lumps this is a near-cancellation, matching the observed 238 against the true `V_a + V_b = 14441` (each lump near 7100, the small residue being the geometric difference between the original and mirrored placements plus integrator rounding). The mesh path is correct (14441) because the tessellator orients facets from the actual face geometry, independent of the stored sense bit, so mesh and mass disagree exactly when the sense bit is stale relative to the reflected geometry.
> **Limitations.** This is confirmed by elimination plus the sign-and-magnitude signature (near-cancellation, not doubling), the mirror in the repro, and the verbatim `reversed=false` import. The exact residue (238) depends on the specific reflected placement and is not load-bearing; the load-bearing fact is the sign flip on the whole mirrored lump.
> **Kernel relevance.** Candidate D is **confirmed** and is the target of the fix (section 2). The collapse is a per-face orientation defect introduced at import for an improperly-isometric (mirrored) operand, not an accumulation, anchor, or skip defect. **Verified** (the verbatim import path, the outward-normal rule, the mesh-vs-mass disagreement signature); the residue value is **claimed** (placement-dependent).

## 2. The mirror-produced geometry and the reflected-shell orientation rule

The second scope question: does the reflection invert a handedness the mass path assumes, so the second lump's flux subtracts, and what is the orientation rule for a reflected curved shell.

### 2.1 A reflection is an improper isometry: determinant -1 reverses orientation and flips the normal

> **Citation.** Improper isometry / orientation reversal under determinant -1: *Reflection (mathematics)* and *Improper rotation* (orientation-reversing, determinant -1); *Geometry of Linear Transformations* (V. Naik, https://files.vipulnaik.com/math-196/geometryoflineartransformations.pdf). Mesh consequence (negative determinant inverts triangle winding and the normal, producing a negative volume): "Negative Jacobians in mesh" (GiD, https://forum.gidsimulation.com/viewtopic.php?t=2635); "Inspecting mesh models" (US patent 11,513,494, the MUST-3 negative-volume rule under a negative-determinant transform). Standard divergence-theorem orientation requirement (boundary oriented outward): OpenStax 6.8, MIT OCW 18.013A (section 1.1 sources).
> **Method / Content.** A mirror `M` (reflection across a plane) is an **improper isometry**: it preserves distances but has `det(M) = -1` and **reverses orientation** (verified). Its effect on a surface's geometry: for a parametric face `S(u, v)`, the reflected face is `M . S(u, v)`, with partials `M . S_u`, `M . S_v` and surface-element vector `(M . S_u) x (M . S_v) = det(M) . M . (S_u x S_v) = - M . (S_u x S_v)`. The cross product picks up the **determinant sign** (the cross product is a pseudovector), so the natural normal of the reflected face points in the direction **opposite** to the mirror-image of the original normal. Equivalently: the reflected face's natural normal, computed afresh from the reflected coefficients in the standard parametrization convention, points **into** the solid where the original pointed out. To keep the boundary oriented outward (the divergence-theorem requirement), the reflected face must **flip its sense**. In mesh terms (verified): a negative-determinant transform inverts triangle winding, flips the normal, and yields a negative signed volume unless the winding is corrected.
> **Limitations.** The sign comes from the cross product's pseudovector behavior under improper isometries; it is exact and basis-independent (it is `det(M)`). A **proper** isometry (a rotation, translation, `det = +1`) does **not** flip the normal, so a rotated-and-translated (non-mirrored) disjoint lump needs **no** sense flip. The fix must therefore be conditioned on the determinant sign of the transform that produced the operand, not applied to every disjoint import.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the orientation rule, stated precisely for Keel below. The mass path assumes `n_out = sense * natural_normal` points outward (massprops.rs:163, dossier 46); a reflection flips `natural_normal` (the `det(M) = -1` pseudovector flip) while the imported `sense` is unchanged, so `n_out` points inward and the lump subtracts. **Verified** (the determinant-sign normal flip is standard linear algebra; the mesh negative-volume consequence is documented).

### 2.2 The reflected-shell orientation rule (the deliverable), and the STEP precedent

> **Citation.** ISO 10303-42:2021 (STEP geometric and topological representation), the negative-determinant face-sense rule: when the determinant of a transformation operator is -1, the transformed face's interior lies on the opposite side of its bounding loops, so the face sense must be reversed (https://www.steptools.com/stds/smrl/data/resource_docs/geometric_and_topological_representation/sys/4_schema.htm). Keel dossier 46 (face orientation / sense convention, `n_out = sense x natural_normal`). Keel dossier 47 (boolean result assembly, the import / sense-folding path).
> **Method / Content (the rule, claimed for Keel, with verified mathematical and STEP backing).** Let `T` be the rigid-or-improper isometry that produced an operand from a base body, and let a base face have stored sense `s` and a surface whose natural normal is `N(u, v)`. After transforming by `T`:
> - If `det(T) = +1` (proper: rotation / translation): the natural normal transforms as `T . N` and still points outward for sense `s`. **Keep the sense: `s' = s`.** No flip.
> - If `det(T) = -1` (improper: a mirror, or any reflection / improper rotation): the natural normal of the transformed surface points inward for sense `s`. **Flip the sense: `s' = NOT s`** (equivalently `s' = s XOR (det T < 0)`). This is exactly the STEP rule: a `det = -1` transform reverses the face sense so the interior stays correctly bounded.
> The mass path then computes `n_out = s' * natural_normal'`, which points outward again, and the reflected lump's flux **adds**. The same flip keeps the mesh and mass consistent (the mesh already orients from geometry; the flip makes the stored sense agree).
> **Where Keel applies it.** `combine_disjoint` (boolean.rs:2407 to 2412) currently calls `import_face(..., reversed=false, ...)` unconditionally. `import_face` already has the full machinery: `reversed=true` flips the surface sense (`ssense ^ reversed`, boolean.rs:2226), reverses fin senses (`sfin.forward ^ reversed`, boolean.rs:2178), and reverses loop order (boolean.rs:2193 to 2195). **So the fix is to pass `reversed = (det of the operand's accumulated transform < 0)` instead of the hard-coded `false`.** The mechanism to flip a whole imported lump's orientation **already exists and is exercised** (the difference path uses `reversed` to flip subtracted walls inward, boolean.rs:719); the disjoint-union arm simply never sets it for a mirrored operand. The transform determinant must be tracked on the operand (or recovered: if the operand's accumulated modeling transform is not stored, the sign can be detected geometrically per shell, section 2.3).
> **Limitations.** The rule needs the **determinant sign of the transform that produced the operand**. If Keel applies mirrors by baking reflected coefficients into the geometry without recording the transform, the sign must be recovered (section 2.3). The flip is per **operand** (per imported body) when the whole operand is a single improper image; if an operand itself contains mixed-handedness sub-shells (rare, a pre-mirrored sub-assembly), the sign is per **shell**, detected geometrically. A double mirror (`det = +1` net) needs no flip; the sign rule handles this automatically (`(-1)(-1) = +1`).
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the fix. It is minimal (set one boolean argument by a determinant-sign test instead of hard-coding `false`), it reuses the fully-built `reversed` path in `import_face`, and it matches the STEP standard's own negative-determinant sense-reversal rule. It converts the inward-oriented mirrored lump to outward, so `V_body = V_a + V_b` and the floor gate passes. **Verified** (the determinant-sign rule and STEP precedent; the `reversed` machinery exists in `import_face`); **claimed** (that passing `reversed = det < 0` is the correct and sufficient wiring, to validate against the seed-11400715918834829910 oracle).

### 2.3 Recovering the sign when the transform is not recorded: per-shell geometric handedness

> **Citation.** Per-shell signed volume as a handedness witness (the polyhedral signed-tetra sum, section 1.1 Mortenson / OpenStax sources; the GiD / US-11,513,494 negative-volume-under-reflection rule, section 2.1). Keel dossier 02 (non-manifold cellular topology, the shell / region structure the per-shell test walks).
> **Method / Content (claimed).** If the operand's modeling transform determinant is not available at `combine_disjoint` time, recover the orientation per shell with a **cheap signed-volume witness** that does not depend on the (suspect) stored sense: for each shell, compute the **mesh** signed volume of that shell's faces (the tessellator's geometry-only orientation, which is correct), and separately the **sense-based** signed volume of the same shell (using the stored sense). If the two **agree in sign**, the shell's sense is consistent (no flip). If they **disagree in sign** (the sense-based volume is negative while the mesh volume is positive), the shell is sense-inverted: import that shell with `reversed=true`. This is a per-shell, geometry-grounded determinant-sign recovery, immune to a missing transform record, and it reuses the same mesh volume the floor gate already computes (boolean.rs:4108 `mesh_volume()`).
> **Limitations.** The per-shell witness needs the shell decomposition of each operand (available: a region holds `shells: Vec<ShellKey>`) and one mesh-volume pass per shell (cheap, the mesh is already built). It is robust because the sign comparison is a coarse `>0` vs `<0` decision with a wide margin (a full lump's volume is far from zero), not a tolerance-sensitive equality. It fails only for a degenerate zero-volume shell (a sheet, not a solid lump), which is out of scope for the solid disjoint-union arm.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the fallback when the transform sign is not plumbed through to the boolean: detect the inverted shell by comparing its sense-based signed volume to its (correct) mesh signed volume, and flip exactly the inverted shells. It is the more robust of the two realizations because it grounds the flip in geometry rather than trusting a recorded transform, and it generalizes to any improperly-oriented imported shell, not just a known mirror. **Claimed**, validated against the mirror repro and the planar baseline (which must show **no** flip).

## 3. Interaction with the floor gate, and preserving the exact planar case

The third scope question: state the fix so the analytic mass is correct and the floor gate passes, and confirm the planar disjoint-union case (exact today) is untouched.

> **Citation.** Keel commit 3d49fee (the floor gate, boolean.rs:4096 to 4119); dossier 61 (the disjointness certificate that licenses `combine_disjoint`); MEMORY: minimize-declines (DECLINE-never-WRONG is the floor, the goal is to convert DECLINE to PASS by solving the class).
> **Method / Content.** The floor gate (boolean.rs:4108 to 4118) computes `render_mesh = body.mesh_volume()` and declines if `|mass - render_mesh| / max(|mass|, |render_mesh|) > 0.25`. Today, for the mirror repro, `mass = 238` and `render_mesh = 14441`, the relative gap is near 1.0, the gate declines. **The fix (section 2.2 or 2.3) makes `mass = V_a + V_b ~ 14441 = render_mesh`, the relative gap drops to integrator-rounding (well under the 2% curved band, far under 25%), and the gate passes.** The gate itself is unchanged; it remains a sound cross-check (it would still decline any residual curved single-lump integrator gap, dossier 60) and a DECLINE-never-WRONG backstop. Once the orientation is correct, the gate is satisfied by construction for the multi-lump curved union, so the class moves from DECLINE to PASS.
> **The planar case stays exact and untouched.** A planar two-block disjoint union built without a mirror has `det(T) = +1` for both operands (translations only), so the determinant-sign rule (section 2.2) sets `reversed = false`, identical to today's behavior: the import is byte-for-byte unchanged, the planar mass stays exact, the gate stays satisfied. A planar union built **with** a mirror would today **also** collapse (the same inward-lump bug applies to a reflected box), so the fix **extends** correctness to the mirrored-planar case without regressing the unmirrored-planar case. The per-shell witness realization (section 2.3) is even safer: for an unmirrored planar lump the sense-based and mesh signed volumes agree in sign, so no flip is applied, and the exact planar path is provably untouched. Either realization leaves the `det = +1` path bit-identical.
> **Limitations.** The fix changes behavior **only** for operands whose producing transform has `det < 0` (mirrored) or whose per-shell witness detects inversion. All proper-isometry disjoint unions (the entire planar baseline and all rotated/translated curved unions) are unchanged. The 25% gate threshold is left as-is; tightening it to the 2% curved band is a separate hardening (INVESTIGATE), not required to pass the mirror case.
> **Kernel relevance.** The fix is **inside** `combine_disjoint`'s import (set `reversed` correctly), not in the gate and not in `mass_properties`. The gate remains the honest backstop; the orientation fix makes the analytic mass agree with the mesh so the backstop passes the now-correct body. The planar exact case is preserved because the determinant-sign rule reduces to `reversed=false` for proper isometries. **Verified** (the gate arithmetic, the `det=+1` no-op); **claimed** (that the corrected mass equals the mesh to the band, validated by the oracle of section 4).

## 4. Validation oracle

The fourth scope question: `mass == mesh == closed-form` (sum of the two lumps) for the named cases, with `quadric_sphere_op_volume` agreeing where applicable.

> **Citation.** Closed-form lump volumes: sphere `(4/3) pi r^3`, cylinder `pi r^2 h` (Wolfram MathWorld *Surface of Revolution*, https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SurfaceofRevolution.html; AMSI *Cones, Pyramids and Spheres*). Additivity of disjoint-component volume (section 1.1 divergence-theorem sources). Keel `mesh_volume` (boolean.rs:4108) and `mass_properties` (massprops.rs:111); the tight `quadric_sphere_op_volume` exact sphere-op reference (dossier 60 exact-oracle tier).
> **Method / Content (the oracle battery).** For each case, assert the triple `mass == mesh == closed_form` within the 2% curved band (planar to 1e-9), where `closed_form` is the **sum** of the two lumps' exact volumes (disjoint additivity, section 1.1):
> 1. **Two disjoint spheres** (radii `r_1`, `r_2`, AABB-separated, no mirror): `closed_form = (4/3) pi (r_1^3 + r_2^3)`. Asserts the baseline multi-lump curved accumulation is additive with **no** mirror (the `det=+1` path), so it must already pass once the loop is confirmed correct (section 1); a regression here would mean the accumulation itself is broken, which section 1 rules out. `quadric_sphere_op_volume` agrees per sphere.
> 2. **Two disjoint cylinders** (`pi r^2 h` each, AABB-separated, no mirror): `closed_form = pi (r_1^2 h_1 + r_2^2 h_2)`. Second curved primitive, proper isometry, confirms additivity is not sphere-specific.
> 3. **Sphere mirror plus union (seed 11400715918834829910)**: the **confirmed-bug** case. One lump is a reflected copy (`det = -1`). Before the fix: `mass = 238`, `mesh = 14441`, gate declines. After the fix (section 2.2 or 2.3): `mass ~ 14441 = mesh = closed_form` (the sum of the original and mirror-image sphere volumes, equal since a reflection preserves volume), gate passes. This is the **load-bearing** oracle: it must move from DECLINE to PASS, and the mass must equal the mesh to the band. `quadric_sphere_op_volume` agrees on each sphere lump.
> 4. **Planar two-block disjoint union (the baseline)**: `closed_form = V_block1 + V_block2`, exact. Must stay **exact** (1e-9) and **bit-unchanged** after the fix (the `det=+1` path, section 3). This is the no-regression oracle: the fix must not perturb the planar exact case.
> **The soundness pair.** Cases 1, 2, 4 (proper isometry) assert the fix is a **no-op** there (exactness preserved). Case 3 (improper isometry) asserts the fix **corrects** the inward lump. Together they pin the determinant-sign rule: flip iff `det < 0`, never otherwise.
> **Limitations.** The mesh side is chord-approximate (MEMORY: tessellation-tolerance-trap), so case 3's `mass == mesh` is asserted to the 2% curved band, not to 1e-9; the **closed-form** sum is the tight oracle the mass is checked against, with the mesh as the independent cross-check. The exact planar cases stay at 1e-9. A double-mirror case (`det = +1` net) is a useful extra oracle: it must show **no** flip and remain exact, confirming the sign rule composes.

## Patent awareness

- **The divergence-theorem / per-face signed-volume mass-property method** and its additivity over disconnected regions are classical (Mortenson; Lee-Requicha 1982, dossier 60) and long expired. The per-region-sum-equals-global-face-sum fact is textbook vector calculus. Build on it freely.
- **The determinant-sign orientation rule** (an improper isometry, `det = -1`, reverses orientation and flips the normal) is elementary linear algebra, no patent.
- **The STEP / ISO 10303-42 negative-determinant face-sense reversal rule** is a published international standard (2021 edition cited), used here only as **precedent** that a kernel must reverse face sense under a `det = -1` transform, not as a copied algorithm. Implementing the standard's stated semantics is not a patent risk; the rule is mathematical necessity.
- **The per-shell signed-volume handedness witness** (section 2.3) is derived from the classical signed-tetra polyhedral volume sum; the GiD forum note and US 11,513,494 ("Inspecting mesh models," the MUST-3 negative-volume-under-reflection check) are cited as **corroboration** that a negative-determinant transform yields a negative signed volume, a general fact, not as a method to copy. US 11,513,494 is post-2006; flag for claim-reading only if Keel were to ship a mesh-inspection product feature, which it is not. Keel's witness computes a signed volume sign, a pre-2006 classical operation, and design-arounds (use the existing `mesh_volume` per shell) avoid any inspection-product claim entirely.
- **Post-2006 CAD mass-property / moment-engine patents** (a "Systems and methods for determining mass properties of a modeled object," US 10,783,708, surfaced in dossier 60) are flagged for claim-reading; Keel's posture (classical divergence theorem plus the determinant-sign rule, both pre-2006) is the design-around.

Net: **the fix stands on classical vector calculus (divergence-theorem additivity), elementary linear algebra (the determinant-sign normal flip), and a published standard's stated semantics (ISO 10303-42 negative-determinant sense reversal); the per-shell witness is a classical signed-volume sign test. ## Synthesis: ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE

**ADOPT.**
1. **The determinant-sign sense-reversal rule** (section 2.2): in `combine_disjoint`, import an operand with `reversed = (det of its producing transform < 0)`, flipping the mirrored lump's faces to outward orientation. 2. **The per-shell signed-volume handedness witness** (section 2.3) as the robust realization when the transform sign is not plumbed to the boolean: flip exactly the shells whose sense-based signed volume disagrees in sign with their mesh signed volume. Geometry-grounded, immune to a missing transform record, reuses the existing per-shell mesh volume.
3. **The global per-face flux loop unchanged** (section 1): the multi-region accumulation needs no per-region inner loop; the divergence-theorem additivity makes the global face sum equal the per-region volume sum for a disconnected body. The fix is per-face orientation at import, not accumulation.
4. **The floor gate unchanged as the backstop** (section 3): once the orientation is correct, `mass == mesh` is satisfied by construction and the gate passes; it remains the DECLINE-never-WRONG cross-check for any residual single-lump integrator gap (dossier 60).

**AVOID.**
- **Importing a mirrored operand with `reversed=false`** (the current bug, boolean.rs:2407): it leaves the reflected lump inward-oriented and its flux subtracts. Always condition `reversed` on the determinant sign.
- **Adding a per-region inner loop to `mass_properties`** to "fix" the accumulation: the accumulation is already correct (section 1); a per-region loop would be redundant and would not address the orientation defect, which is the real cause.
- **Trusting the stored sense bit over geometry for a transformed operand**: a mirror invalidates the sense relative to the reflected natural normal. Either flip the sense by the determinant rule, or ground the orientation in the geometry-correct mesh signed volume (the per-shell witness).
- **Tightening or removing the floor gate as part of this fix**: the gate is the backstop; leave it. Tighten it to the 2% band separately (INVESTIGATE), only after the orientation fix lands.
- **Perturbing the `det=+1` import path**: the planar and proper-isometry curved cases are exact today and must stay bit-unchanged. The determinant rule reduces to `reversed=false` there; do not add any flip on that path.

**INVESTIGATE.**
- Whether the operand's modeling transform determinant is reliably available at `combine_disjoint` time, or whether the per-shell signed-volume witness (section 2.3) should be the **primary** realization for robustness (it is immune to a missing or stale transform record and grounds the flip in geometry).

- A double-mirror (`det = +1` net) and a mixed-handedness sub-assembly oracle, confirming the sign rule composes (`(-1)(-1) = +1`, no flip) and the per-shell witness handles per-shell mixed handedness.

**Recommended FIRST MILESTONE (narrowest honest slice with an exact oracle).**
**Pass `reversed = (det of the operand transform < 0)` in `combine_disjoint`, validated against the sphere mirror+union (seed 11400715918834829910) and the two-disjoint-spheres baseline.** This is the smallest, highest-certainty fix: it sets one boolean argument by a determinant-sign test, reuses the already-exercised `reversed` machinery in `import_face`, and has an exact closed-form oracle (the sum of the two sphere volumes, equal under reflection). Either way the milestone is one orientation decision with a tight exact oracle and a no-regression baseline.
