# 67. Frame-robust sphere NURBS-cut classify: a frame-agnostic strictly-interior point for a spherical region bounded by arbitrary closed NURBS loops (the band-and-cap discriminator that gates select_faces and the tessellation anchor)

## Title and scope

This is the implementation-grade literature dossier for **the frame-agnostic interior-point construction on a trimmed sphere face**: given one periodic sphere domain (u in [0, 2*pi) identified at the seam slit, v in [-pi/2, +pi/2] contracting to a pole at each end) and K closed seam pcurves (each an encircling NURBS loop that wraps u as v varies, or a non-wrapping loop), return ONE strictly-interior (u, v) per trimmed region, with maximal margin from every bounding loop AND from the u=0 seam slit AND from both poles. That point is consumed in two places: `classify_faces` uses it as the sample for the generalized-winding inside/outside test, and `tessellate_sphere` uses it as the triangle-fan anchor. Both fail in lockstep when the point is degenerate or exterior, which is the confirmed bug.

This dossier builds on dossiers 64 (periodic-domain seam split: the identification / contraction domain model, the cylinder analog) and 66 (curved multi-loop face mass: the outer-minus-inner loop convention), and does NOT repeat them. Dossier 64 established that a periodic face's parameter rectangle carries gluings (left/right u-edges identified at the seam; for the sphere BOTH v-edges contracted to poles) and that a region is read off a 2D arrangement, not 3D Euler surgery. Dossier 66 established that loop orientation (outer CCW, inner CW about the face normal) carries the band-vs-hole sign automatically. THIS dossier answers the orthogonal question those two deferred: **once the region's loop set exists on the periodic sphere, how is a strictly-interior representative point chosen so that the choice is invariant to where the sphere's parametric frame happens to put its seam meridian and its two poles.** That frame-invariance is the entire content of the confirmed bug, and it is the headline deliverable.

## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **The interior point must be chosen in 3D first and reduced to (u, v) only to verify, never chosen in (u, v) first. For a CAP (a region bounded by exactly one encircling loop and containing one pole), the frame-agnostic anchor is the loop's spherical mean direction (the normalized sum of the rim sample unit-directions from the sphere centre) pushed to the pole-side of the rim, that is, the surface point in the direction of the rim's mean normal on the side the loop's orientation marks as interior. For a BAND (a region bounded by two encircling loops and containing NO pole), the anchor is the surface point in the direction of the AVERAGE of the two rims' mean directions (the bisector great circle between the two rim mean-directions), which lies on the band by construction and never coincides with a pole or the seam of ANY frame. The region is classified as cap-vs-band by a frame-independent POLE-INSIDE test: a pole P is inside a loop iff the loop's signed spherical area (equivalently its solid angle subtended at the sphere centre, by Van Oosterom-Strackee / Gauss-Bonnet) enclosing P is nonzero; with two encircling loops, the region between them that contains neither pole is the band. The candidate 3D point is then mapped to (u, v) and verified by `point_in_face_uv`; only if the analytic anchor fails verification (a highly eccentric loop) does the construction fall back to a (u, v) grid search, and that grid search EXCLUDES a tolerance tube around the seam slit and around both poles so it can never return a degenerate sample. Every quantity in this construction is a direction in 3D space or a solid angle, both invariant to the sphere's parametric frame; the (u, v) coordinates appear only as the final verification and the fallback, both seam-and-pole-excluded. The frame that put the band ring through the poles (the X-pole frame) is defeated because the band anchor is a 3D bisector direction that knows nothing about where v=+-pi/2 sits.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. The sphere face is the unit-sphere quotient: the trimmed region is a spherical polygon-like region whose edges are the imprinted seam loops, and the "interior point" question is exactly the spherical point-in-region question the geoscience literature solved (Bevis-Chatelain, Ketzner). The single robustness lesson from that literature is that the answer must not be computed in a fixed lat/long frame, because any fixed frame has a seam (the antimeridian) and two poles where the lat/long test is degenerate; the fix (Ketzner, the antipode-excluding reduction) is to compute crossings and containment in **Cartesian / solid-angle space**, which is frame-free, and to choose the query point so its antipode is outside the region. Keel's bug is the mirror image: it picks the candidate INTERIOR point in the sphere's lat/long frame and then is surprised when the frame's seam or pole lands in the region. The cure is the same frame-free move: choose the candidate as a 3D direction (a mean direction for a cap, a bisector direction for a band, both rotation-covariant), and decide cap-vs-band by a solid-angle / signed-spherical-area pole-inside test (Van Oosterom-Strackee / Gauss-Bonnet), both of which are invariant under any rotation of the parametric frame. The (u, v) coordinates re-enter only to VERIFY the candidate against the actual trimmed domain (`point_in_face_uv`) and, if the analytic anchor somehow fails, as a last-resort grid search that is itself seam-excluded and pole-excluded so it cannot reproduce the degeneracy. Because every primary quantity is a frame-covariant 3D direction or a frame-invariant solid angle, rotating the sphere's frame rotates the answer point with it and never changes which region it lands in: that is frame-invariance, and it is provable, not merely tested.

The one honest caveat: a rim that passes exactly through a pole (a great-circle-like cut tangent to or through v=+-pi/2) and a sub-tolerance sliver region are genuinely degenerate; those stay DECLINE-forever (Q3 / Q5), never WRONG. Everything else in the sphere NURBS-cut frontier flows through this one frame-agnostic construction.

### Q1.1 The point-in-region test on the periodic domain (wrap-aware winding), and why it must be frame-free

> **Citation.** M. Bevis, J.-L. Chatelain, "Locating a point on a spherical surface relative to a spherical polygon of arbitrary shape," Mathematical Geology 21(8):811-828, 1989. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00894449 . R. Ketzner, V. Ravindra, M. Bramble, "A robust, fast, and accurate algorithm for point in spherical polygon classification with applications in geoscience and remote sensing," Computers and Geosciences 167:105185, 2022. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0098300422001388 ; https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e2abf5c44786a6cbcd2b2dd145c16197d7f6e97 . "On the Reduction of the Spherical Point-in-Polygon Problem for Antipode-Excluding Spherical Polygons," arXiv:2309.03822, 2023. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03822
> **Method / Content.** The spherical point-in-polygon (SPIP) problem: classify a query point Q as inside / outside / on a region bounded by closed curves on the sphere. Bevis-Chatelain extend the even-odd (ray-crossing) rule to the sphere: pick a reference point R, form the geodesic (great-circle arc) from Q to R, count how many region edges it crosses; odd = inside. The crossing test is done in **Cartesian space** (great-circle arc intersection via cross products and sign tests), NOT in lat/long, which is the source of its speed AND its frame-independence: no trigonometry, no antimeridian special case. Ketzner-Ravindra-Bramble (2022) harden this: they choose R so the arc Q-to-R avoids degenerate configurations, handle the on-boundary and edge-through-special-point cases explicitly, and report robustness improvements over Bevis-Chatelain's edge cases. The antipode-excluding reduction (arXiv:2309.03822) formalizes the key precondition: if the polygon is contained in an open hemisphere (boundary antipode-excluding, BAE), the SPIP reduces cleanly to a planar test after a single rotation that sends Q to a pole. The headline, **verified** across all three sources: the robust test is a Cartesian crossing / winding count with a deliberately-chosen reference, NOT a fixed-frame lat/long sweep.
> **Limitations.** Bevis-Chatelain's original has documented edge-case fragility (a query exactly on an edge, an edge through the chosen reference's antipode), which Ketzner repairs. The BAE reduction assumes the region fits in an open hemisphere; a band that wraps a full great circle is not BAE and needs the general crossing test.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the warrant for the whole dossier: the point-in-region test on the periodic sphere must be the Cartesian crossing / winding test (which Keel already approximates with its generalized winding number in `classify_faces`), evaluated at a candidate chosen by a FRAME-FREE rule, and the (u, v) `point_in_face_uv` is used only as the final in-domain confirmation. Keel's wrap-aware winding on the (u, v) domain is the periodic analog: a loop that wraps u contributes a full turn, so the winding of the loop's pcurve around a candidate (u, v) is computed mod the u-period (dossier 64's identification). **Verified** that the robust SPIP is Cartesian / frame-free; **claimed** that Keel's GWN sample at the frame-free candidate inherits that robustness.

### Q1.2 The maximal-margin interior point: max over loops AND seam AND poles, in the right (frame-free) metric

> **Citation.** Reconstruction from the spherical centroid / mean-direction theory (Q2), the solid-angle pole-inside test (Q3), and Keel's live `sphere_face_interior_point` (boolean.rs ~1323, the band path's `margin = to_seam.min(to_pole)` that this dossier replaces).
> **Method / Content (the recommended Keel construction).** The interior point is chosen to maximize a margin that is the MINIMUM over three frame-free distances: (a) the great-circle distance from the candidate direction to every bounding loop (the rim mean-directions and, for safety, the nearest sampled rim point), (b) the great-circle distance to the seam meridian of the CURRENT frame, and (c) the great-circle distance to each pole. The crucial correction over the live code: (a) is computed as a 3D great-circle distance (an angle between unit directions), not a (u, v) coordinate gap, so it is frame-free; (b) and (c) are kept as soft penalties only to break ties and to keep the FINAL (u, v) away from the degenerate strip, NOT as the primary objective. For a cap, the analytic anchor (Q2) already maximizes (a) approximately (the mean direction is the "centre" of the cap) and the verify step (Q1.1) confirms it; for a band, the bisector direction (Q2) maximizes (a) between the two rims. The seam / pole penalties (b, c) only nudge the azimuth of the final point so the returned (u, v) is not ON the slit or AT a pole, which matters solely because downstream code (tessellate's fan, classify's atan2) is itself degenerate there, not because the point is geometrically wrong.
> **Limitations.** The "min over three distances" is a heuristic objective; for a pathological loop the true max-margin interior point may not be the mean / bisector direction (Q2 limitation), in which case the grid fallback (Q2.4) finds it. The seam / pole penalties must be weighted SMALL relative to the loop distance so an adverse frame cannot pull the point off the geometric centre toward an exterior azimuth (the live-code failure: it let the seam / pole term dominate).
> **Kernel relevance.** Restates the prompt's "max margin from every bounding loop AND from the u=0 seam slit AND from both poles" with the correct primary metric (frame-free great-circle distance to the loops) and the seam / pole terms demoted to tie-breakers, which is exactly the fix the live code needs. **Claimed**, validated against the X-pole-frame oracle (Q5).

## Q2. Frame-agnostic construction: the mean-direction cap anchor and the bisector band anchor

This is the core deliverable. The construction must be invariant to the sphere's parametric frame; the live bug is that an X-pole frame put the band ring through the poles because the anchor was chosen in the frame. The cure is a 3D-geometric anchor that is rotation-covariant.

### Q2.1 The spherical mean direction (the cap anchor) and its one failure mode

> **Citation.** "Centroid of Points on the Surface of a Sphere," skeptric (the normalize-the-sum method and its antipodal failure). https://skeptric.com/calculate-centroid-on-sphere/ ; "Centroid Spherical Polygon," skeptric. https://skeptric.com/centroid-spherical-polygon/ . Directional-statistics mean resultant direction (Fisher / Mardia-Jupp), standard. PBRT 4e section 3.8 Spherical Geometry (spherical centroids in rendering). https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Geometry_and_Transformations/Spherical_Geometry
> **Method / Content.** The spherical mean direction of a set of unit directions {d_i} (here, the rim's sampled points expressed as unit vectors from the sphere centre) is `dbar = normalize(sum_i d_i)`. It is rotation-covariant by construction: rotating every d_i by R rotates dbar by R, so it is FRAME-FREE. For a CAP region bounded by one encircling loop, the rim's mean direction points (approximately) at the rim's "centre"; the cap interior is on the POLE side of the rim, so the cap anchor is the surface point in the direction `sign * dbar_rim_normal`, where `dbar_rim_normal` is the rim's mean NORMAL direction (the normalized sum of the rim's local outward normals, equivalently the area-weighted normal of the spherical cap the rim bounds) and `sign` is set by the loop's orientation (outer loop => pole on the interior side; inner ring => opposite side), exactly the orientation rule dossier 66 Q2.2 uses for the inner-loop sign. Concretely: a cap's rim is a closed loop on the sphere; the direction to the pole it contains is the rim's mean direction `dbar`, and the cap anchor is `center + radius * dbar` (pushed slightly toward the pole if the rim is far from a great circle, to stay strictly interior). This is the frame-free replacement for the live code's "return a pole in the sphere frame."
> **Limitations.** The normalize-the-sum mean direction FAILS when the rim points are antipodally spread (the sum is near zero): this happens precisely when the rim is near a GREAT CIRCLE (the cap is a near-hemisphere). For a near-hemisphere cap, `dbar` is ill-conditioned; the fix is to use the rim's mean NORMAL (the Van Oosterom-Strackee / Gauss-Bonnet signed-area normal of Q3, which is well-conditioned even for a great-circle rim because it is the loop's enclosed-solid-angle axis, not the centroid of its points), and only fall back to the grid search (Q2.4) if even that is degenerate (a rim that is exactly a great circle, which is the pole-through-rim DECLINE of Q3 / Q5).
> **Kernel relevance.** The cap anchor is `center + radius * (rim mean normal direction) * orientation_sign`, frame-free, well-conditioned except at the exact great-circle rim (a DECLINE). This replaces the live pole-in-frame return with a rotation-covariant direction. **Verified** that normalize-the-sum is rotation-covariant and fails only near antipodal spread; **claimed** the mean-normal hardening, validated against the tilted / X-pole oracle (Q5).

### Q2.2 The bisector band anchor (the headline frame-agnostic construction)

> **Citation.** Reconstruction from Q2.1 (mean direction), Q3 (solid-angle rim axis), and the great-circle bisector (the normalized sum of two unit directions is the direction bisecting them, standard spherical trigonometry).
> **Method / Content (the deliverable).** A BAND is bounded by TWO encircling loops and contains NO pole. Let `n1` and `n2` be the two rims' mean NORMAL directions (Q2.1 / Q3), each the axis of the solid angle its loop subtends at the sphere centre, oriented OUTWARD from the band (n1 points toward the north cap's pole, n2 toward the south cap's pole, by each loop's orientation relative to the band face normal, dossier 66 Q2.2). The band lies BETWEEN the two rims; the direction at its "middle" is the bisector of the two rims' positions, which is `bisect = normalize(m1 + m2)` where `m1, m2` are the two rims' mean directions (Q2.1) on the band side, OR equivalently `bisect = normalize(n1 - n2)` rotated into the band (the band's centre direction is orthogonal-ish to both pole axes). The band anchor is `center + radius * bisect`. Because `bisect` is a normalized sum of frame-free directions, it is rotation-covariant: rotating the sphere frame rotates `bisect` with it. It lies ON the band (between the two rims) by construction and, critically, it NEVER coincides with a pole or with any frame's seam meridian, because it is the geometric middle of the band, which the X-pole frame cannot move there. This is the exact construction that defeats the confirmed bug: the X-pole frame puts v=+-pi/2 inside the band, but `bisect` is a 3D direction that does not know or care where v=+-pi/2 is.
> **Limitations.** If the two rims are very close (a thin band), `m1 + m2` is well-defined but the band is thin and the anchor's margin is small; a sub-tolerance band is a DECLINE (Q5). If the band wraps such that the two rim mean-directions are near-antipodal (a band spanning more than a hemisphere in v), `normalize(m1 + m2)` is ill-conditioned; use the cross-product form `bisect = normalize((n1 - n2) cross seam-free-reference)` or fall back to the grid (Q2.4). For a band whose two rims are non-parallel and tilted (the general non-coaxial wrap), the mean directions handle the tilt automatically because they are computed from the actual rim samples, not from a frame axis (the live-code `parallel-rims` gate is dropped entirely).
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the two-line headline construction. **The cap anchor is the surface point in the rim's mean-normal direction (pole side, orientation-signed); the band anchor is the surface point in the direction `normalize(m1 + m2)` of the two rim mean-directions (the bisector great circle between them).** Both are rotation-covariant 3D directions, so the answer rotates with the frame and never lands on a pole or seam of any frame. The live code's `Curve3::Circle`-only and `parallel-rims` gates are removed; the construction works for arbitrary closed NURBS rims because it consumes only sampled rim directions. **Claimed** as the recommended construction, validated against the multi-frame oracle (Q5).

### Q2.3 Why this is provably frame-invariant (not merely tested)

> **Citation.** Group-covariance of the normalized vector sum under SO(3) (elementary); the rotation-invariance of solid angle (Van Oosterom-Strackee, Q3).
> **Method / Content.** Let `phi` be the candidate-point construction (cap mean-normal or band bisector) as a function of the rim sample points {p_i} expressed in 3D (frame-independent, they are points on the sphere in world space). The sphere's parametric frame is a rotation R applied to the (u, v) -> 3D map; changing the frame to R' replaces the map but does NOT move the rim points in world space (the rim is the same 3D curve regardless of how we parametrize the sphere). Therefore `phi({p_i})` is literally unchanged by a frame change: it is a function of world-space directions only. The (u, v) of the candidate DOES change with the frame (it is the inverse map of a fixed 3D point), but that is correct and harmless: the candidate is the same 3D point, mapped to whatever (u, v) the current frame assigns, and `point_in_face_uv` verifies it in that frame. The ONLY frame-dependent quantities in the whole construction are the final verification (which is frame-correct by definition) and the seam / pole tie-breaker penalties (Q1.2, demoted to negligible weight). Hence the returned 3D point is frame-INVARIANT and the returned (u, v) is the frame-correct image of that invariant point. This is the proof the live code lacked.
> **Limitations.** The proof assumes the rim sampling is itself frame-free (sample by 3D arc length or by the rim's own parameter, NOT by uniform u-steps, since uniform-u sampling is frame-dependent and would slightly bias the mean direction). Sample the rim in 3D.
> **Kernel relevance.** Establishes that the fix is correct by construction, not by passing a test suite: the answer is a function of world-space rim directions, invariant under SO(3). **Verified** (SO(3) covariance of the normalized sum and rotation-invariance of solid angle are elementary).

### Q2.4 The fallback grid search (seam-excluded and pole-excluded)

> **Citation.** Reconstruction from the trimmed-patch seed-point literature (robust tessellation of trimmed NURBS, grid / Delaunay seeding). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010448598000645 ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3758550_Robust_tessellation_of_trimmed_rational_B-spline_surface_patches . Keel's live `point_in_face_uv` (the in-domain test the grid candidates are filtered by).
> **Method / Content.** Only if the analytic anchor (Q2.1 / Q2.2) fails `point_in_face_uv` (a highly eccentric loop, a near-degenerate band) does the construction fall back to a (u, v) grid search. The grid is over u in (0, 2*pi) and v in (-pi/2 + eps, +pi/2 - eps), EXCLUDING a tolerance tube around the seam slit (|u| < eps_u and |u - 2*pi| < eps_u) and around both poles (|v ± pi/2| < eps_v), so a grid candidate can NEVER be on the slit or at a pole. For each grid (u, v) passing `point_in_face_uv == In`, compute the frame-free margin (Q1.2) and keep the max. This is the only frame-DEPENDENT step, and it is made safe by the explicit seam / pole exclusion, so even here the degenerate strip is never sampled. The grid is the last resort, not the primary path: the analytic anchor handles the overwhelming majority (a real CAD cap or band is well inside the great-circle / sub-tolerance limits).
> **Limitations.** A grid is O(N^2) in the resolution and can miss a thin region between grid lines; adaptively refine near a found `In` cell, or seed the grid from the (failed) analytic anchor's (u, v) neighborhood. The exclusion tubes mean a genuinely thin region hugging the seam or a pole is not found by the grid and is DECLINEd (Q5), which is correct (it is sub-tolerance or pole-through).
> **Kernel relevance.** The safety net that preserves DECLINE-never-WRONG: it never returns a seam / pole-degenerate point, and if it finds nothing it DECLINEs rather than emit a degenerate sample. **Claimed**, validated against the eccentric-loop oracle.

## Q3. Band vs cap discrimination and the pole question (the frame-free pole-inside test)

This decides, from the loop set alone, whether a region is a cap (contains a pole) or a band (does not), and handles a band split by the seam slit and the degenerate rim-through-pole case.

### Q3.1 The pole-inside test by solid angle / signed spherical area (frame-free)

> **Citation.** A. Van Oosterom, J. Strackee, "The Solid Angle of a Plane Triangle," IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering BME-30(2):125-126, 1983. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c166f350d7f594ef07aa28936dfc54c8226e0ba . "Area formula for spherical polygons via prequantization," arXiv:2303.14555, 2023 (signed spherical area mimicking Green's theorem, robust on degenerate loops). https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14555 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2303.14555v2 . Gauss-Bonnet theorem (spherical excess = enclosed area). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss%E2%80%93Bonnet_theorem . Solid Angle, Wolfram MathWorld. https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SolidAngle.html
> **Method / Content.** A pole P (a unit direction, +z or -z in the sphere frame, but the test works for ANY direction so it is frame-free) is INSIDE a closed loop on the sphere iff the loop's generalized winding number around P is nonzero, which on the sphere equals the loop's signed spherical area (enclosed solid angle at the sphere centre) being a full 4*pi-relative turn around P rather than zero. Concretely, the **signed solid angle** the loop subtends at the centre, accumulated by Van Oosterom-Strackee per triangle fan (or by the prequantization signed-area formula, which is robust on degenerate / near-great-circle loops because it mimics Green's theorem and needs no angle measurement), is `~ ±2*pi` (loop encircles P) vs `~0` (loop does not). For a region bounded by ONE encircling loop, compute the signed solid angle of the loop around each pole: if it encircles a pole, the region containing that pole is a CAP; the other side is the rest. For TWO encircling loops splitting the sphere into north cap / band / south cap, each loop encircles exactly one pole (its own), and the BAND is the region between them encircling NEITHER pole (both poles' winding around the band's boundary is zero net, since the band's two loops cancel). This test uses only solid angles, which are rotation-invariant (a solid angle is a frame-free measure), so cap-vs-band is decided frame-freely.
> **Limitations.** A loop that passes EXACTLY through a pole has an ill-defined winding around that pole (the solid angle is on the discontinuity); that is the rim-through-pole degeneracy (Q3.3 / Q5 DECLINE). A loop tangent to the great circle through a pole is near-degenerate; use the prequantization signed-area formula (robust there) and a tolerance band, declining only the exact-through-pole case.
> **Kernel relevance.** The frame-free cap-vs-band discriminator. Keel already has a generalized winding number in `classify_faces`; the pole-inside test is the SAME GWN evaluated at the pole direction, so no new machinery is needed, only a per-pole evaluation. **Verified** (solid angle is rotation-invariant; Van Oosterom-Strackee and Gauss-Bonnet are classical; prequantization is the robust modern form); **claimed** the per-region wiring.

### Q3.2 A band split by the seam slit

> **Citation.** Dossier 64 Q1.1 (the seam-slit sub-edges close the band loop) and Q1.3 (sphere banding); dossier 66 Q2 (multi-loop, region on the left).
> **Method / Content.** The sphere's u=0 seam meridian slits every full-revolution band: in the (u, v) domain the band is a rectangle-with-curved-top-and-bottom whose left (u=0) and right (u=2*pi) edges are the SAME 3D seam meridian (identification, dossier 64). The band is ONE region (its two slit edges are glued), so it gets ONE interior point. The frame-free band anchor (Q2.2) is a 3D direction; mapping it to (u, v) puts it at some interior u that is NOT on the slit (the bisector direction is at the band's geometric middle, generically far from any frame's seam). The seam tie-breaker (Q1.2) ensures the returned u is comfortably inside (0, 2*pi). So a seam-split band is handled with no special case beyond the dossier-64 loop closure: the interior-point construction is indifferent to the slit because it works in 3D. The ONLY interaction is the final (u, v) verification, which must use the wrap-aware winding (Q1.1) so the band's full-u-wrap loop is recognized as enclosing the candidate.
> **Limitations.** If the band is so thin that its geometric middle is within the seam exclusion tube of EVERY frame (impossible for a real band, possible for a sub-tolerance sliver), it is DECLINEd (Q5).
> **Kernel relevance.** Confirms the construction needs no seam-split special case: 3D anchor, frame-correct (u, v) image, wrap-aware verify. **Claimed**, inherits dossier 64's loop closure.

### Q3.3 The degenerate sub-case: a rim passing through a pole

> **Citation.** The contraction-point handling of dossier 64 Q4 (apex / pole as a single contraction vertex, but a CUT through the contraction is degenerate); the solid-angle discontinuity at the pole (Q3.1 limitation); degenerate-conic / great-circle theory.
> **Method / Content.** A rim that passes through a pole (a great-circle-like cut reaching v=+-pi/2, or a sphere / cylinder graze whose SSI touches the pole) is the boundary case. Two regimes:
> - **Rim merely REACHES a pole as a loop vertex (REPRESENTABLE).** If the cut is a meridian-like arc that runs TO a pole and the pole is a loop vertex shared by incident fragments (dossier 64 Q4 single-contraction rule), the region is well-defined and the interior point is the mean / bisector anchor pushed off the pole; the pole-inside test (Q3.1) is evaluated at a perturbed direction slightly off the pole (never AT it). Representable.
> - **Rim is a GREAT CIRCLE through a pole, splitting the sphere into two half-orange lunes (DECLINE-forever).** A rim that is exactly a great circle through both poles gives a winding / solid angle on the discontinuity (Q3.1), the mean-direction anchor is ill-conditioned (Q2.1), and the cap-vs-band test is ambiguous (each lune contains BOTH poles on its boundary). This is the sphere analog of dossier 64's "degenerate conic through the apex." DECLINE with a feature-relative epsilon: if a rim is within tolerance of a pole-through great circle, decline rather than emit an ambiguous interior point.
> **Limitations.** The representable / decline boundary is tolerance-dependent (near-pole, near-great-circle); the epsilon must be feature-size-relative, not global, consistent with dossier 64 Q4.
> **Kernel relevance.** Draws the line: a rim reaching a pole as a vertex is representable (anchor perturbed off-pole); a great-circle-through-pole rim is DECLINE-forever. **Verified** assembly; epsilon **claimed** and tunable.

## Q4. Production-kernel realisation: OCCT and ACIS point-in-face on a periodic sphere with NURBS pcurve trims

### Q4.1 OCCT BRepTopAdaptor_FClass2d / IntTools_FClass2d (2D classification in the face's parameter space, with seam and degenerate-edge handling)

> **Citation.** Open CASCADE Technology, `BRepTopAdaptor_FClass2d` (`Load`, `Perform`, `State` returning `TopAbs_State`), `IntTools_FClass2d`. https://old.opencascade.com/doc/occt-7.4.0/refman/html/ ; XbimGeometry mirror of `IntTools_FClass2d.hxx`. https://github.com/xBimTeam/XbimGeometry/blob/master/Xbim.Geometry.Engine/OCC/src/IntTools/IntTools_FClass2d.hxx . OCCT Technical Overview (periodic surfaces, seam, degenerate edges). https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/overview/html/
> **Method / Content.** OCCT classifies a point relative to a face by a 2D point-in-wire test in the face's PARAMETER (u, v) space: `BRepTopAdaptor_FClass2d` loads the face's wires as 2D pcurve polygons and runs a ray-crossing classification, returning `TopAbs_IN / OUT / ON`. For a PERIODIC surface (cylinder, sphere), the face's wires include the SEAM edge so the wire is closed in (u, v) (dossier 64 Q1.4 / ACIS Q4.2), and the classifier respects the periodic range. A DEGENERATE edge (sphere pole, cone apex, a zero-length pcurve segment that maps to one 3D point) is a legal wire member handled by a dedicated path, so a wire reaching a pole closes through the degenerate edge rather than leaving a gap. The crucial point for Keel: OCCT does NOT pick an interior point in a fixed frame and hope; it tests an EXPLICIT query (u, v) against the wire polygon with the seam present, and the seam / pole are first-class wire members. To pick a representative interior point (for example for a face-on-face test), OCCT samples candidate (u, v) and classifies each with `FClass2d`, taking the first `IN`, which is the same "candidate then verify" pattern Keel should use, with the candidate chosen frame-freely (this dossier Q2) rather than at a frame corner.
> **Limitations.** OCCT's classification needs reliable seam pcurves; its periodic-face robustness problems trace to missing / inconsistent seam pcurves (dossier 64 Q1.4), and it relies on shape-healing after the fact. The classifier itself is sound; the upstream pcurve quality is the risk.
> **Kernel relevance.** Confirms the production pattern: point-in-face is a 2D wire-crossing classification in (u, v) WITH the seam edge present and the pole as a degenerate wire member; an interior point is found by classifying candidates. Keel's `point_in_face_uv` is the analog; the contribution of this dossier is to supply the candidate frame-freely. **Verified** against the FClass2d method structure. Patent posture: OCCT is LGPL, no claim-reading risk.

### Q4.2 ACIS periodic faces, the named seam, and parametric-singularity avoidance

> **Citation.** Spatial Corp (Dassault Systemes), ACIS "Geometry > Types of Curves and Surfaces" (periodic surfaces, seams, pcurves, parametric singularities). http://www.q-solid.com/ACIS_Docs_R17/online/SPAacisuserTechArticles/SPAacisuser_gtyp.htm ; ACIS "Model Topology" (faces, loops, periodic-face handling). http://www-isl.ece.arizona.edu/ACIS-docs/PDF/FCG/06TOPO.PDF
> **Method / Content.** ACIS "correctly handles faces that span the seam(s) of a periodic surface." The seam is the point at the ends of the primary parametric interval, a first-class NAMED edge; a face spanning it closes its loop through the seam edge. The ACIS documentation states the design rule directly relevant to the bug: "parametric singularities should be avoided, since they are likely to cause problems," where a parametric singularity is precisely "one side of the rectangle [mapping] into a single point in object space," that is, the sphere pole. So ACIS's own guidance is to keep interior points and queries AWAY from the pole singularity, which is exactly this dossier's seam / pole exclusion (Q1.2 / Q2.4). A boolean computes the SSI once and shares the intersection edge between the two trimmed faces; a periodic face forming a complete band (no seam crossing) is a documented distinct case.
> **Limitations.** Closed source; the seam-once-and-share and singularity-avoidance behaviors are documented but not source-visible. The complete-band case (a closed band) is the configuration Keel must not emit as degenerate.
> **Kernel relevance.** ACIS's documented rule "avoid the parametric singularity (the pole)" is the production confirmation of this dossier's central move: never choose the interior point at or near the pole; choose it frame-freely and keep it off the singularity. **Verified** as documented behavior.

### Q4.3 The generalized-winding-number view (the modern unifier Keel already uses)

> **Citation.** A. Jacobson, L. Kavan, O. Sorkine-Hornung, "Robust Inside-Outside Segmentation Using Generalized Winding Numbers," ACM TOG 32(4), 2013. https://igl.ethz.ch/projects/winding-number/ . J. Spainhour, K. Weiss, "Robust Containment Queries over Collections of Trimmed NURBS Surfaces via Generalized Winding Numbers," ACM TOG, 2025/2026 (boundary formulation via solid angle, adaptive quadrature, robust near the surface). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11435 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11435v2 ; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3797957
> **Method / Content.** The GWN of a point with respect to a set of boundary curves / surfaces is the solid angle they subtend, divided by the full angle; it is robust to gaps and indifferent to the arrangement, and it is frame-free (a solid angle is rotation-invariant). Spainhour-Weiss (2025/2026) compute it for trimmed NURBS via a boundary solid-angle integral with rapidly-converging adaptive quadrature, avoiding discretization error near the surface. This is the SAME solid-angle object as the pole-inside test (Q3.1) and the SAME GWN Keel's `classify_faces` uses. The unification: the interior-point question, the cap-vs-band question, and the classify inside / outside question are ALL solid-angle / GWN evaluations, all frame-free; the only thing that was frame-DEPENDENT (and wrong) was the live code's CHOICE of where to sample, which this dossier corrects.
> **Limitations.** GWN near the boundary is delicate (Spainhour-Weiss's contribution is exactly the robust near-surface evaluation); Keel's 0.25 band around w=0.5 is the tolerant analog and must keep the sample (the interior point) WELL inside the region (max margin, Q1.2) so the GWN is unambiguous.
> **Kernel relevance.** Confirms Keel's GWN classify is the right substrate and that the fix is purely the sample location. The max-margin frame-free interior point (Q1.2 / Q2) keeps the GWN sample far from the boundary, which is also what Spainhour-Weiss requires for robustness. **Verified** that GWN is frame-free and is the production trimmed-surface containment tool; **claimed** that the frame-free sample inherits its robustness.

## Q5. Validation oracle: a sphere cut by two non-coaxial NURBS wrap loops (band plus two caps) under several frames, frame-invariance the headline assertion

The construction is validated by an EXACT, frame-INVARIANT oracle: the same physical cut, expressed under several sphere frames, must return THE SAME 3D interior point per region (up to the construction's tolerance), each verified on the surface, in the correct region by an independent solid-angle containment test, and margin-positive.

### Q5.1 The core battery: one cut, several frames, same answer

> **Citation.** Reconstruction from Q2 (the construction), Q3.1 (the independent solid-angle containment oracle), and the prompt's frame list (axis-aligned, tilted, X-pole).
> **Method / Content (exact, frame-invariant oracle).** Take a unit sphere and a fixed physical cut: two non-coaxial NURBS wrap loops (the solved sphere-vs-{cylinder, cone} SSI, ssi.rs `quadratic_branch_field`), splitting the sphere into a north cap, a band, and a south cap. Now express the SAME sphere under THREE parametric frames: (1) axis-aligned (z = the natural pole axis), (2) tilted (frame rotated by a generic R, so the seam meridian and poles fall at generic places), (3) X-pole (frame's pole axis = world x, the specific adverse frame that put the band ring through the poles in the live bug). For EACH frame, run `sphere_face_interior_point` on each of the three regions. Assert:
> - **On-surface.** `|returned_point - center| == radius` to 1e-9 (the anchor is `center + radius * dir`, exact by construction).
> - **Correct region (independent oracle).** The returned point's GWN / signed solid angle (Q3.1, Van Oosterom-Strackee, computed by a path that shares NO code with the anchor construction) places it INSIDE the intended region and OUTSIDE the other two. The band point encircles NEITHER pole; each cap point's region encircles its pole.
> - **Margin-positive.** The frame-free great-circle distance (Q1.2) from the returned point to every bounding loop is positive and above a feature-relative epsilon.
> - **THE HEADLINE: frame-invariance.** The THREE frames' returned 3D points for the SAME region are EQUAL to within the construction tolerance (they are the same world-space direction by Q2.3). This is the assertion the live code fails: under the X-pole frame it returns a degenerate / exterior point, under the axis-aligned frame it returns a good one. The fixed construction returns the SAME 3D point under all three.
> **Limitations.** "Equal to within tolerance" must account for the rim-sampling discretization (Q2.3 limitation): sample the rim in 3D arc length so the mean direction is frame-free; a small frame-dependent residual from finite sampling must be below the assertion epsilon.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is THE target oracle and it directly attacks the confirmed Add.283 decline: the same cut under the X-pole frame must now return the same valid interior point it returns axis-aligned. Passing it converts the non-coaxial sphere-vs-{cylinder, cone} wrap from DECLINE to PASS. **Claimed** as the recommended oracle; the solid-angle containment cross-check is **verified** classical.

### Q5.2 The sub-oracles that pin each piece

> **Citation.** Q2.1 (cap mean-normal), Q2.2 (band bisector), Q3.1 (pole-inside), Q3.3 (rim-through-pole DECLINE).
> **Method / Content.**
> - **Latitude-circle regression (no regression to the working class).** A sphere cut by two PARALLEL latitude circles (the live working case, Add.262) must still pass under the new construction byte-for-result-unchanged: the band bisector reduces to the mid-latitude point, the cap mean-normal reduces to the pole-side point. This guards the Add.262/267/268 working classes.
> - **Single non-planar wrap cap.** A sphere cut by ONE non-coaxial wrap loop (a cap plus a rest): the cap anchor is the mean-normal direction; verify on-surface, in-region, margin-positive under all three frames.
> - **Eccentric loop -> grid fallback.** A highly eccentric (near-great-circle) cap rim: the mean-direction anchor is ill-conditioned (Q2.1), the construction falls to the mean-NORMAL (Q3.1) or the grid (Q2.4), and the grid's seam / pole exclusion is exercised. Assert the returned point is NOT on the seam or at a pole under any frame.
> - **Rim-through-pole DECLINE.** A great-circle-through-pole rim (Q3.3) must DECLINE, not emit an ambiguous point, under all three frames identically (the DECLINE is itself frame-invariant).
> **Kernel relevance.** Each sub-oracle pins one branch of the construction and one DECLINE, all asserted frame-invariant. **Claimed**, the latitude regression is the load-bearing no-regression guard.

## Patent awareness

- **Spherical point-in-polygon (Bevis-Chatelain 1989; Ketzner-Ravindra-Bramble 2022)** is published academic / geoscience work; the Cartesian crossing and antipode-excluding reduction are published prior art, freely usable. Ketzner (2022) is post-2006 but is an academic paper with no patent flagged; implement from the paper.
- **Van Oosterom-Strackee solid-angle formula (1983)** and **Gauss-Bonnet** are classical, public domain. The **prequantization signed-area formula (arXiv:2303.14555, 2023)** is recent academic; no patent flagged, but patent-check before shipping a verbatim implementation. Keel uses it only as the robust signed-area warrant (a general fact), not a copied algorithm; the underlying Gauss-Bonnet area is clear prior art.
- **Generalized winding number (Jacobson 2013)** is clear prior art; **Spainhour-Weiss (2025/2026)** is recent academic, same flag as dossiers 59 / 60 / 66: used as the warrant that solid-angle containment over trimmed boundaries is robust and frame-free (a general fact), not as a copied algorithm.
- **Spherical mean direction / Fisher mean resultant** is classical directional statistics, no patent.
- **OCCT BRepTopAdaptor_FClass2d / IntTools_FClass2d** is LGPL open source; the 2D wire-crossing classification with seam and degenerate-edge handling is published and freely followable. **ACIS** is closed source but its documented rules (named seam, avoid the parametric singularity) are conventions, not patentable methods.
- **Post-2006 periodic-face / point-in-face / pole-handling patents** may exist around specific commercial classification heuristics. Keel's posture (choose the interior point as a frame-free 3D direction, decide cap-vs-band by a frame-free solid angle, verify in (u, v) with seam / pole exclusion, DECLINE the rim-through-pole and sub-tolerance cases) is a deliberate design-around: it reads on the published SPIP / solid-angle / GWN prior art and on classical directional statistics, not on any post-2006 commercial heuristic. The recommendation adopts none.

Net: **build on the published spherical point-in-polygon (Bevis-Chatelain / Ketzner), the classical Van Oosterom-Strackee / Gauss-Bonnet solid angle, the clear-prior-art GWN (Jacobson), the spherical mean direction, and the LGPL OCCT FClass2d pattern; choose the interior point as a frame-free 3D direction and verify it in (u, v) with seam / pole exclusion. The path is clear of post-2006 claim-reading risk.**

## Synthesis: ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE

**ADOPT.**
- **The frame-free 3D anchor** (Q2): cap = surface point in the rim's mean-NORMAL direction (pole side, orientation-signed, dossier 66 sign rule); band = surface point in the bisector direction `normalize(m1 + m2)` of the two rim mean-directions. Both are rotation-covariant world-space directions, provably frame-invariant (Q2.3). Drop the live `Curve3::Circle`-only and `parallel-rims` gates; consume sampled rim directions so arbitrary closed NURBS rims work.
- **The frame-free pole-inside test** (Q3.1): decide cap-vs-band by the loop's signed solid angle (Van Oosterom-Strackee / Gauss-Bonnet / prequantization) around each pole, reusing Keel's GWN. Rotation-invariant, so cap-vs-band is frame-free.
- **The candidate-then-verify pattern** (Q1.1 / Q4.1): map the 3D anchor to the current frame's (u, v) and confirm with `point_in_face_uv` and the wrap-aware winding; the (u, v) appears only as verification, never as the primary choice.
- **The seam-and-pole-excluded grid fallback** (Q2.4): only if the anchor fails verification, grid-search (u, v) with explicit tolerance tubes around the seam slit and both poles, so a fallback can never return a degenerate sample; DECLINE if the grid finds nothing.
- **The demoted seam / pole tie-breakers** (Q1.2): keep the seam / pole margins as SMALL tie-breakers that nudge the final (u, v) off the degenerate strip (for downstream atan2 / fan safety), never as the primary objective (the live-code error was letting them dominate).
- **The named DECLINE-forever set** (Q3.3 / Q5): great-circle-through-pole rim, sub-tolerance sliver band, with feature-relative epsilons; the DECLINE is itself frame-invariant.

**AVOID.**
- **Choosing the interior point in the sphere's parametric frame** (the live bug): an adverse frame (X-pole) puts the seam or a pole inside the region and a frame-fixed metric picks a degenerate / exterior point. Choose the point as a 3D direction.
- **Keying the band path on `Curve3::Circle` and parallel rims.** A non-coaxial wrap rim is a non-planar NURBS loop, not a circle, and the rims are not parallel; the circle / parallel gates make the band path return `None` on exactly the case it must handle. Consume sampled rim directions.
- **Returning a pole as a cap interior point in the frame** (the live circle-cap path): the pole is the parametric singularity ACIS explicitly says to avoid; push the cap point off the pole in the mean-normal direction.
- **Letting the seam / pole margin term dominate the objective.** That pulls the point toward an exterior azimuth under an adverse frame. Keep it a small tie-breaker.
- **Sampling the rim in uniform u-steps** (frame-dependent): sample in 3D arc length so the mean direction is frame-free (Q2.3 limitation).
- **Emitting an ambiguous interior point for a rim-through-pole or a sub-tolerance band** (risks a wrong classify / fan): DECLINE the named class.

**INVESTIGATE.**
- The exact rim-sampling density and the frame-invariance assertion epsilon (Q5.1): how few 3D arc-length samples keep the mean direction frame-invariant below the assertion tolerance.
- Whether the cap mean-NORMAL (the solid-angle axis, Q3.1) should ALWAYS be preferred over the mean-direction (Q2.1) since it is well-conditioned even near a great circle, collapsing two branches into one.
- Whether the pole-inside solid-angle test can be fused with `classify_faces`'s existing GWN evaluation so cap-vs-band costs one extra pole-direction sample, not a separate pass.
- The feature-relative epsilon for the rim-through-pole DECLINE (Q3.3) and the sub-tolerance band DECLINE, tied to the tolerant-edge tube (dossier 64 Q4 epsilons).

**RECOMMENDED FIRST MILESTONE (narrowest honest slice with an exact oracle).** **A unit sphere cut by ONE non-coaxial NURBS wrap loop (a cap plus a rest), returning the cap's interior point as the rim mean-normal direction, asserted frame-INVARIANT across the axis-aligned and the X-pole frames.** Concretely: take one of the solved sphere-vs-cylinder SSI wrap loops, build the cap face, and call `sphere_face_interior_point`; the new construction returns `center + radius * (rim mean-normal) * orientation_sign`. Assert (1) on-surface to 1e-9, (2) inside the cap by an independent Van Oosterom-Strackee solid-angle test, (3) margin-positive, and (4) THE HEADLINE: the returned 3D point is the SAME under the axis-aligned frame and the X-pole frame (the exact frame that breaks the live code). This is the smallest case that exercises everything new: the frame-free 3D anchor (Q2.1), the solid-angle containment oracle (Q3.1), and the frame-invariance assertion (Q2.3 / Q5.1) that is the entire content of the bug. It is fully analytic, attacks the confirmed Add.283 decline directly, and needs no change to SSI, stitch, or finalize.

**FOLLOW-UP LADDER.**
- Rung 1 (first milestone): one non-coaxial wrap to a cap plus rest, cap interior point frame-invariant across axis-aligned and X-pole frames, independent solid-angle oracle.
- Rung 2: TWO non-coaxial wraps to a band plus two caps (the full sphere-vs-{cylinder, cone} wrap), the BAND interior point as the bisector direction, frame-invariant across axis-aligned, tilted, and X-pole frames; the cap-vs-band discrimination by the frame-free pole-inside test (Q3.1).
- Rung 3: the latitude-circle no-regression guard (Q5.2): the new construction reduces to the live mid-latitude / pole-side point on parallel circle rims, Add.262/267/268 classes unchanged.
- Rung 4: the eccentric-loop grid fallback (Q2.4) with seam / pole exclusion exercised; assert no seam / pole-degenerate sample under any frame.
- Rung 5: wire the cap-vs-band pole-inside test into `classify_faces`'s GWN (one extra pole-direction sample), and confirm the non-coaxial sphere wrap boolean goes DECLINE-to-PASS on the soak corpus, WRONG=0.

**DECLINE-forever set (justified).**
- **A rim that is a great circle through a pole** (Q3.3): ambiguous winding / ill-conditioned mean direction / each lune contains both poles on its boundary; emitting an interior point risks a wrong classify. DECLINE with a feature-relative epsilon; rare in real CAD (a real cut is generically off the pole-through great circle).
- **A sub-tolerance sliver band** (Q3.2 / Q5.2): a band whose bisector point is within the seam / pole exclusion tube of every frame has no well-defined interior; DECLINE rather than emit a degenerate sample.
- **A self-intersecting wrap rim on the sphere** (inherited from dossier 64 Q4): not a simple loop, cannot bound a single region; DECLINE upstream.

These move from permanent-decline to staged-recovery only if a closed-form pole-perturbed anchor removes the ambiguity (rim-through-pole) or a tighter feature-size model resolves the sliver; the recommendation declines them now to keep DECLINE-never-WRONG.
