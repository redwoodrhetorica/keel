# 40. Blend / Fillet Surface Generation (Exact-Analytic and NURBS)

**Scope.** This dossier is about the *surface generation* step of constant- and variable-radius edge fillets in an analytic + NURBS B-rep kernel, and specifically about the **exact-analytic vs NURBS decision** with the geometry written out. It prioritizes making the plane-plane and plane-cylinder cases exact-analytic, and gives a clear ladder from those exact cases up to a certified NURBS canal-surface approximation for the general case.

It deliberately does **not** repeat three adjacent dossiers, and cross-references them throughout:

- **`docs/research/nurbs/05-offsets-blends-sweeps.md`** covers canal surfaces, rotation-minimizing frames (RMF), the Vida-Martin-Varady blend *taxonomy*, the offset-approximation methods, and setback vertex blends at the *survey* level. This dossier reuses that machinery and points back to it for the RMF and offset-approximation primitives.
- **`docs/research/kernel/28-fillet-chamfer-engine.md`** covers the *engine architecture*: the spine + spring-curve + polymorphic cross-section recipe, the chamfer family, overflow/runout, hold-line, blend networks and mitring, and recognition/unblend. This dossier is the *geometry* under that engine: it tells the engine which exact surface type to emit and when to fall to NURBS.
- **`docs/research/kernel/26-transfinite-nsided-surfacing.md`** covers n-sided patch construction (central-split, Gregory/GB patches, the vertex-enclosure parity constraint). Area 2 below ties the vertex-blend hole directly to that machinery.

Other cross-references: SSI and offset surfaces are existing Keel capabilities; canonical recovery is `kernel/24`; local boolean / imprint is `kernel/03` and `kernel/39` (coincident/tangent faces); persistent naming is `kernel/07`; medial-axis / offset self-intersection is `kernel/10`; the numerical substrate is `kernel/37`.

The centerpiece is the **exact-analytic catalog table** in Area 1: a face-pair-plus-configuration to blend-surface-type lookup (cylinder / torus / cyclide / NURBS canal). The closing synthesis gives the recommended build order from plane-plane-exact to general NURBS.

## Area 1. The rolling-ball blend surface as the envelope of a rolling sphere

### 1.1 The construction, stated precisely

Let two support faces carry surfaces `S_a` and `S_b` meeting at a convex (or concave) sharp edge. A **rolling-ball blend of radius r** is generated by a sphere `B(c, r)` of fixed radius `r` that rolls along the edge while maintaining **tangency to both** `S_a` and `S_b` simultaneously. As the ball rolls, three loci are traced:

- The **spine** is the locus of the ball center `c(t)`.
- The two **spring curves** (also: contact curves, tangency curves, rail curves) are the loci of the two points where the ball touches `S_a` and `S_b` respectively. Call them `g_a(t)` on `S_a` and `g_b(t)` on `S_b`.
- The **blend surface** is the **envelope** of the one-parameter sphere family `{ B(c(t), r) }`. Only the strip of that envelope lying between the two spring curves is kept; that strip is the blend face.

Because the radius is constant, the envelope of a one-parameter family of equal spheres centered on a curve is by definition a **canal surface** (a *pipe surface*, the constant-radius special case of a canal surface). This single fact drives the entire exact-analytic catalog: the blend surface type is determined by the *shape of the spine curve*.

The blend is **tangent to each support face along its spring curve** by construction: at the contact point the sphere, the support face, and the blend envelope share a common tangent plane (the sphere is tangent to both). This gives G1 continuity for free and is the basis of the certification test in Area 5.

### 1.2 The spine is the intersection of the two offset surfaces (the key procedure)

The ball center is the unique point at distance exactly `r` from **both** support faces, on the material side. The set of points at distance `r` from `S_a` (on the correct side) is the **offset surface** `S_a^{+r}`; likewise `S_b^{+r}`. Therefore:

> **Spine = SSI( offset(S_a, r), offset(S_b, r) ).**

This is the single most important procedural fact in the dossier and it is exactly what production kernels do (ACIS calls it "the offset-intersection algorithm", Parasolid states it as a blendability precondition; see Area 5.3). For **analytic** support faces, Keel offsets the carriers analytically and then runs its existing analytic SSI, so the spine comes out as an analytic curve with no approximation. The offset of each of Keel's analytic surface types stays in the same family:

| Support surface | Offset by `r` toward material | Spine input is analytic? |
|---|---|---|
| **Plane** `n . x = d` | Parallel plane `n . x = d - r` (sign per material side) | yes |
| **Cylinder** radius `R`, axis `L` | Coaxial cylinder radius `R - r` (convex/external) or `R + r` (concave/internal), same axis `L` | yes |
| **Sphere** radius `R`, center `p` | Concentric sphere radius `R - r` or `R + r`, same center `p` | yes |
| **Cone** half-angle `alpha`, apex `A` | Coaxial cone, same half-angle `alpha`, apex shifted along axis by `r / sin(alpha)` (radial offset `r`) | yes |
| **Torus** `(R_major, R_minor)` | Coaxial torus `(R_major, R_minor - r)` or `(R_major, R_minor + r)`, same center/axis | yes |
| **NURBS** | Approximate offset (NURBS), see `nurbs/05` | no (NURBS spine) |

So for any pair of analytic faces, the spine is the **analytic SSI of two offset analytic surfaces**, which Keel already computes. Worked spines for the important pairs:

- **plane / plane** (dihedral edge). Two offset planes; their intersection is a **straight line** parallel to the original sharp edge, offset into the dihedral by `r / sin(theta/2)` along the angle bisector where `theta` is the dihedral angle. *Spine is a line.*
- **plane / cylinder** (axis parallel to the plane, the classic round on the side of a boss). Offset plane intersected with coaxial offset cylinder. If the cylinder axis is parallel to the plane the SSI is a pair of **lines** (spine is a line). If the cylinder axis is perpendicular to the plane (a fillet around the base of a circular boss) the SSI is a **circle** coaxial with the cylinder, of radius `(R -/+ r)` projected, lying in the offset plane. *Spine is a circle.*
- **plane / sphere.** Offset plane intersected with concentric offset sphere: a **circle** (the intersection of a plane and a sphere is always a circle). *Spine is a circle.*
- **cylinder / cylinder, coaxial.** Two coaxial offset cylinders; SSI is a **circle** (or empty). *Spine is a circle.*
- **cylinder / cylinder, parallel axes.** Two offset cylinders with parallel axes; SSI is a curve that projects to a conic in the cross-plane: in general a **higher-degree space curve**, not analytic-primitive. *Spine is non-primitive.*
- **cylinder / cylinder, skew (non-intersecting, non-parallel) axes.** SSI of two offset cylinders is a **quartic** space curve. *Spine is a quartic, non-primitive.*
- **cone / plane**, **cone / sphere**, **cone / cylinder coaxial.** Offsets stay coaxial cones/spheres/cylinders; SSI is a **circle** when an axis of symmetry is shared, otherwise a conic or higher-degree curve. *Spine is a circle in the coaxial cases.*

### 1.3 Spring curves

The spring curve on a face is the **foot of the perpendicular from the spine onto that face** (equivalently, the point where the rolling ball of center `c(t)` touches the face). Concretely:

- **Plane.** `g_a(t)` is the orthogonal projection of the spine point `c(t)` onto the plane: `g_a = c - ((c - x0) . n) n`. Because the spine is at constant distance `r`, this is the spine translated by `-r n`. The spring curve has the same shape as the spine (a line projects to a line, a circle to a circle).
- **Cylinder.** `g_a(t)` is the **radial projection** of `c(t)` onto the cylinder: drop a perpendicular from `c` to the axis `L`, then the contact point is at distance `R` from the axis along that radial direction. `g_a = axis_point + R * unit(c - axis_point)`.
- **Sphere.** `g_a(t)` is the radial projection toward the sphere center: `g_a = p + R * unit(c - p)`.
- **Cone.** project `c(t)` onto the cone along the line through `c` perpendicular to a generator (the contact lies on the generator nearest `c`).
- **NURBS.** `g_a(t)` is the closest-point foot computed by point-surface projection (`nurbs/04`).

The blend face is bounded by `g_a` and `g_b`; the support faces are trimmed back to remove the strip between the original sharp edge and the spring curve (Area 3).

### 1.4 The exact-analytic catalog (the canal-surface specialization), the centerpiece

The blend is a canal surface, and **the canal surface type is fixed by the spine shape and radius**:

#### Case A: spine is a straight line to a CYLINDER (exact)

If the spine is a straight line `L_s` (plane-plane dihedral; plane-cylinder with parallel axis), the envelope of equal spheres of radius `r` centered on a line is a **circular cylinder** of radius `r` whose axis is `L_s`.

- **Blend surface:** `Cylinder(axis = L_s, radius = r)`, trimmed between the two spring lines.
- **Exactness:** exact, native Keel cylinder. No NURBS.
- This is the highest-value case: it is the plane-plane fillet, the single most common fillet in mechanical parts. Build it first.

#### Case B: spine is a circle to a TORUS (exact)

If the spine is a circle `C_s` of radius `rho` lying in plane `Pi_s` with center `m`, the envelope of equal spheres of radius `r` centered on `C_s` is a **torus** with major radius `R_major = rho`, minor radius `R_minor = r`, center `m`, axis normal to `Pi_s`.

- **Blend surface:** `Torus(center = m, axis = normal(Pi_s), R_major = rho, R_minor = r)`, trimmed between the two spring circles.
- **Exactness:** exact, native Keel torus. No NURBS.
- **Coverage:** plane-sphere (always, spine is a circle); plane-cylinder with perpendicular axis (boss base fillet); cylinder-cylinder coaxial; cone-plane / cone-sphere / cone-cylinder in the coaxial / shared-axis configurations. Degenerate sub-cases: if `rho = 0` the torus degenerates to a sphere (the blend is a spherical cap, e.g. a fillet at a sphere-plane tangency apex); if `rho` is infinite the torus degenerates to the cylinder of Case A.

#### Case C: natural-quadric pairs in general to a DUPIN CYCLIDE (exact, rational)

For pairs of **natural quadrics** (plane, sphere, cylinder, cone) in general relative position, the constant-radius rolling-ball blend is a **Dupin cyclide**: the unique surface all of whose curvature lines are circles, which specializes exactly to torus, cylinder, cone, and sphere. The cyclide is the natural completion of the torus catalog: where the torus requires the spine to be a *circle*, the cyclide covers the cases where the spine is a more general curve but the two support faces are still natural quadrics.

The Dupin cyclide has a known **rational biquadratic parameterization** (Pratt; Foufou-Garnier; Boehm; Krasauskas), i.e. it is representable **exactly as a rational biquadratic NURBS surface** (degree 2x2, with weights), and it has the *parallel-offset* property (offsetting a cyclide gives another cyclide), which is exactly what a blend surface needs to interact cleanly with downstream offset/shell operations.

**Important caveat (the existence condition).** Not every natural-quadric pair admits a *fixed-radius* cyclide blend. The classical result (Shene; Zhou-Straub-... summarized via the rational-rolling-ball-blend literature) is that only specific pairs and touching-point configurations yield a rational *fixed-radius* rolling-ball blend, e.g. cone/plane, cone/cone (one or two touching points), cone/sphere (one touching point). For other natural-quadric pairs the *constant-radius* blend is **not** a cyclide; the rational blend then requires a **variable-radius** rolling ball (a canal surface with rational spine and rational radius, which is always rational by Peternell-Pottmann; Area 4 and source [12]). This is the subtle point that separates "looks like it should be a cyclide" from "is exactly a fixed-radius cyclide".

**The design question for Keel (recommendation).** Three options:

1. Add a first-class **Dupin-cyclide surface type** (store center/axes and the four cyclide parameters; specialize to torus/cylinder/cone/sphere on recovery).
2. Represent cyclide blends as **exact rational biquadratic NURBS** (degree 2x2 with weights), reusing the existing NURBS surface type.
3. Specialize to **torus / cylinder** wherever the configuration permits and fall to NURBS otherwise.

**Recommendation: option 2 as the production path, with option 3 as the fast path.** Adding a new analytic primitive (option 1) is a large, invasive change: every algorithm in the kernel (SSI, offset, recovery, boolean, tessellation, interrogation, STEP I/O) must learn the new type, and the payoff is narrow because the cyclide *already has* an exact rational NURBS form. Instead: (a) take Case A (line to cylinder) and Case B (circle to torus) as exact native primitives, which already cover the overwhelming majority of real fillets, because in practice plane-plane and plane-cylinder-perpendicular and plane-sphere dominate; (b) for the remaining true natural-quadric cyclide cases, emit the **exact rational biquadratic NURBS** for the cyclide. This keeps the surface set small (plane/cyl/cone/sphere/torus/NURBS, exactly what Keel has today) while remaining geometrically exact, since the rational NURBS *is* the cyclide. Keel's canonical-recovery pass (`kernel/24`) can still recognize a cyclide-NURBS and report it as a cyclide if downstream code wants that label. STEP AP242 (`kernel/38`) has no Dupin-cyclide entity anyway, so a new primitive would have to be exported as NURBS on the way out regardless; emitting NURBS natively avoids a lossy primitive-to-NURBS conversion at export time.

#### Case D: general spine to NURBS CANAL APPROXIMATION

If the spine is a higher-degree curve (cylinder-cylinder skew axes giving a quartic spine; cone-cone in general position; or any case where at least one support face is already NURBS), the canal surface has **no analytic primitive form** and must be **approximated as a NURBS surface to a tolerance**. The construction (ties to `nurbs/05` canal-surface and offset-approximation, and to Farouki-Sverrisson source [3]):

1. Compute the spine as `SSI(offset(S_a, r), offset(S_b, r))` (NURBS SSI if inputs are NURBS).
2. **Sample** the spine adaptively (denser where spine curvature is high; step size driven by the deviation bound, source [3]).
3. At each sample `c(t_i)`, build the **cross-section circle** of radius `r` in the plane normal to the spine, oriented by a **rotation-minimizing frame** (RMF, `nurbs/05`) to avoid twist. Each circle is a rational quadratic (NURBS conic).
4. **Skin / loft** the circles into a NURBS surface (one direction is the exact rational-circle cross-section, the other is a fitted spline through the circle frames).
5. Certify the deviation: the true blend at parameter `t` is at distance exactly `r` from both spring feet, so a **certified deviation bound** is obtained by densifying sampling until the max deviation of sampled spheres from the lofted surface is below tolerance (source [3] gives the accuracy measures that govern the arc-length step). Tie the bound to `kernel/37` numeric substrate tolerances.

Note that even Case C (cyclide) is a *special case* of this canal construction in which the loft closes into exact rational form; and Cases A/B are the further specializations where the loft is a primitive. This is why the catalog is a clean ladder, not a set of disjoint algorithms.

### 1.5 The clean ladder (state it once)

> **line spine to CYLINDER (exact)  ->  circle spine to TORUS (exact)  ->  natural-quadric pair to DUPIN CYCLIDE (exact rational NURBS)  ->  else NURBS CANAL APPROXIMATION (certified to tolerance).**

The engine (file 28) decides position on the ladder by **classifying the spine curve** after computing the offset SSI: line, circle, conic-on-quadric-pair, or general. This classification is exactly Keel's canonical-recovery step (`kernel/24`) applied to the spine.

## Area 2. Setback / vertex blends (3+ filleted edges at a corner)

### 2.1 Why the edge blends do not close at a vertex

When three or more blended edges meet at a vertex, each edge contributes its own canal-surface blend face. These canal faces are tangent to their support faces along their spring curves, but **they do not meet each other cleanly at the vertex**: the spring curves of the incident blends end at different points and at different "heights" up each support face, and the three (or more) canal faces leave an **n-sided curvilinear hole** around the corner that no single canal surface fills. Forcing the canal surfaces to extend to the vertex produces self-intersection and tangent discontinuities. This is the classic corner problem.

### 2.2 Setback

The **setback** treatment (Vida-Rockwood, source [13]; survey in `nurbs/05` and file 28) **retracts each edge blend away from the vertex by a setback distance**, so each blend ends on a *clean cross-section* short of the corner rather than running into it. Setting back leaves a well-defined **n-sided opening** bounded by:

- the **setback cross-section curves** (one per incident edge blend, where each blend was truncated), and
- the **support-face arcs** between consecutive setback cross-sections.

The opening is then filled by a single **vertex (corner) patch**. Setbacks turn an ill-defined corner into a clean transfinite-interpolation problem.

### 2.3 The corner-patch construction (tie to file 26)

The n-sided opening is filled with the **transfinite n-sided patch machinery of `kernel/26`**: central-split or Gregory/GB patches, subject to the **vertex-enclosure parity constraint** (file 26). Concretely, the vertex-blend setup is:

- **Boundary curves of the n-sided patch:** the spring-curve ends (where the edge blends were set back) and the setback cross-sections, interleaved around the corner. For three set-back edge blends the patch is typically **6-sided** (Vida-Rockwood: vertex blends are `2n`-sided patches, two sides per incident edge: one setback cross-section side and one support-face side), with odd-sided special cases.
- **Continuity ribbons (cross-boundary tangents):** taken from the incident surfaces. Along a setback cross-section the ribbon comes from the **adjacent edge-blend canal surface** (so the corner patch is G1 with the canal blend). Along a support-face arc the ribbon comes from the **support face** (so the corner patch is G1 with the support face). This makes the corner patch G1 to *everything* it touches. G2 corner patches use second-order ribbons (curvature data) and tie to Pegna-Wolter (Area 5.1, source [5]) for the linkage-curve condition.
- **Setback split / control frame:** the interior is built by the "repeated chamfering" / setback-split control frame of Vida-Rockwood, which provides free interior shape parameters.

### 2.4 The rolling-ball vertex blend and its limits

The simplest vertex blend places a **sphere of the blend radius at the corner** (the natural limit of all three rolling balls converging). When all incident edges have the **same radius `r`** and meet in a sufficiently symmetric configuration, this spherical corner is exact and tangent to all three incident toroidal/cylindrical blends. ACIS/Parasolid expose this as the default vertex blend. Its **limits**: it only works cleanly for equal radii and roughly symmetric corners; with unequal radii or skew geometry the single sphere is not tangent to all incident blends and the general setback patch (2.3) is required.

### 2.5 The hard cases

- **Unequal radii on incident edges.** The setback distances differ per edge (larger radius wants a larger setback), the incident blend cross-sections sit at different heights, and the n-sided opening is asymmetric. The single-sphere vertex blend fails; a general setback patch with per-edge setback is needed. Parasolid encodes special rules here (its three-edge-vertex and four-edge-vertex rules, source [9]; e.g. for a facet/mixed three-edge vertex of differing convexity it requires the two same-convexity edges to share a radius).
- **More than three edges.** The opening is `2n`-sided for `n` incident edges; the vertex-enclosure parity constraint (file 26) becomes binding and central-split is typically required to guarantee a valid patch.
- **Mixed convex/concave edges.** Convex and concave blends approach the vertex from opposite material sides; the setback cross-sections face opposite ways and the corner patch must reconcile them. This is the least robust case; production kernels restrict it (Parasolid forbids mixed-convexity chains in several configurations, source [9]).

## Area 3. Trimming the support faces to the spring curves and stitching in (the local boolean / imprint)

### 3.1 The local operation

Inserting a blend is a **local boolean / imprint operation** (tie to `kernel/03` local operations and `kernel/39` coincident/tangent handling, since the blend is *tangent* to its supports along the spring curves, the most delicate imprint case). For each blended edge:

1. **Imprint** each spring curve `g_a`, `g_b` onto its support face as a new boundary loop edge.
2. **Trim** each support face to **remove the strip** between the original sharp edge and the spring curve (the material the ball "ate").
3. **Delete** the original sharp edge and its two coedges.
4. **Stitch** the new blend face in, sewing its two boundary edges to the two spring-curve edges on the support faces, sharing them (so the model stays manifold and watertight).

Framing it as a local boolean matters because it reuses the same coincident/tangent-aware sewing already researched in `kernel/39`: the blend-to-support junction is a **tangent** edge (G1), which is exactly the configuration `kernel/39` flags as needing special tolerance handling so the kernel does not misclassify the smooth junction as a sharp edge or drop it.

### 3.2 Topology surgery: entities created and destroyed

- **Destroyed:** the original sharp edge `E` (and its coedges); at a non-blended vertex the original vertex may survive, at a blended vertex it is replaced (Area 2).
- **Created:** one **blend face** `F_blend`; two **spring-curve edges** `E_a`, `E_b` (the new shared boundaries); at the ends of `E`, the original end vertices migrate to the **setback cross-section** ends (the blend face's end edges) or to new vertices where the blend terminates / caps (ACIS end-cap vs side-cap, source [2]).
- **Lineage / persistent naming (tie `kernel/07`).** `F_blend`, `E_a`, `E_b` are genuinely new entities and need **lineage records** that trace them back to the parent sharp edge `E` and the two parent support faces, so that downstream feature edits (radius change, parent-face edit, rollback) can re-target the blend. The blend's identity is "the fillet of edge E", not a raw face id, so the naming record should key on the parent edge and the two support faces plus the radius/law, mirroring how file 28's recipe records the blend intent declaratively.

### 3.3 Pcurves (parameter-space curves)

Each spring curve needs a **pcurve on both faces it bounds**: on the support face and on the blend face. Crucially these should come from the **construction, not from projection**, wherever possible:

- **On the support face:** the spring curve is the foot-of-perpendicular locus (1.3). For a plane it is the spine projected along `-r n` (a closed-form pcurve: a line or circle in the plane's `(u,v)`). For a cylinder it is the radial-projection image, which in cylinder `(u = theta, v = axial)` parameters is again closed form. So the support-face pcurve is **exact and analytic**, never a fitted projection.
- **On the blend face:** the blend is tangent to the support along the spring curve, which means the spring curve is an **isoparametric curve of the canal surface** (one of the two cross-section circle endpoints, i.e. a `v = const` boundary of the loft). Hence the blend-face pcurve of a spring curve is literally a **parameter-space boundary line** `v = 0` or `v = 1`, exact by construction. This is the single biggest robustness win of generating the blend as a canal loft whose isolines are the cross-section circles: both pcurves of both spring curves are closed-form, so the kernel never has to recover them by point-projection (which is the usual source of pcurve gaps and sliver edges). Only the *cap*/setback ends, which cut across the canal isolines, may need projected pcurves.

## Area 4. Variable-radius, chordal, and asymmetric blends

### 4.1 Variable radius `r(t)` along the edge

When the radius varies along the edge, the rolling ball changes size as it rolls, and **the spine is no longer a simple offset-surface intersection**: the ball center is at *varying* distance from each face, so it does not lie on a single fixed-offset surface. ACIS states this explicitly (source [1]): "The offset-intersection algorithm used to find the spine and spring curves works only for constant-radius blends, because a variable offset is ill-defined on a surface. A figurative rolling ball must be set up at the desired position... and a marching algorithm is required." Concretely:

- **Calibration of `r` along the edge.** The radius law `r(v)` is parameterized by the **edge-curve parameter** (ACIS: "the parameter of the radius function is the parameter of the edge curve"). At parameter `v`, a ball of radius `r(v)` is placed with its center in the plane **perpendicular to the edge curve** at `v` (Parasolid's "cross-section plane", source [8]).
- **Variable-radius spine.** In each such cross-section plane, solve for the center at distance `r(v)` from both faces (a local 2-constraint solve), then march `v` along the edge. The spine and spring curves are computed by this predictor-corrector march; they are **no longer perpendicular to the cross-section plane** (ACIS [1]).
- **Variable-radius canal surface.** The blend is the envelope of spheres of radius `r(v)` centered on the spine: a **general canal surface** (varying-radius), source [3] (Farouki-Sverrisson) and source [12] (Peternell-Pottmann). By Peternell-Pottmann (source [12]): *any canal surface with a rational spine and a rational radius function is rational*, so a variable-radius blend over rational supports is exactly a rational (NURBS) surface, though of higher degree than the fixed-radius cyclide. In practice these are **never analytic primitives** and are emitted as **NURBS** (skin the radius-varying cross-section circles, as in Case D but with `r = r(v)`).
- **Snapshot vs envelope cross-section (ACIS [1]).** A subtle but important production detail: the naive "rolling-ball snapshot" surface (the circle is just the ball cross-section at each `v`) has a **crease** when the ball rolls across a smooth-but-not-curvature-continuous edge while the radius varies, which corrupts later offsets. ACIS introduced the **"rolling-ball envelope"** surface whose cross-sections are subtly reshaped so the sequence is smooth across transitions and offsets cleanly. Keel should generate the **envelope** form (cross-section in the true normal plane of the envelope, not the snapshot plane) for variable radius, for the same offset-robustness reason.
- **Radius law forms.** Linear (the common default; Parasolid's `vary` linear law, source [7]/[9]); by discrete control values with a fitted law curve; or by an arbitrary user **law curve**. Continuity of `r(v)` propagates to the surface: a `C^1` radius law gives a `G^1` canal, a `C^2` law gives `G^2` (Lukacs, source [6], works out the differential geometry of `G^1` variable-radius rolling-ball blends, the conditions on the radius law and spine so the resulting canal is genuinely `G^1` to the supports). Parasolid's default makes the radius have a **constant extension** at the chain ends so a variable blend meets neighboring blends tangentially at vertices (source [7]).

### 4.2 Chordal and asymmetric blends

- **Chordal blends** hold a constant **chord width** (distance between the two spring curves) rather than constant radius. The radius then *varies* to keep the chord constant as the dihedral angle changes along the edge, so chordal is a variable-radius construction with the radius law defined implicitly by the chord constraint. Same NURBS canal machinery, with the per-cross-section solve targeting chord width instead of radius.
- **Asymmetric blends** use **different setback/offset on each face** (the offset-and-angle analog of an asymmetric chamfer). The modified construction offsets `S_a` by `r_a` and `S_b` by `r_b` (`r_a != r_b`), so the spine is `SSI(offset(S_a, r_a), offset(S_b, r_b))` and the cross-section is no longer a circular arc centered on the spine but a conic/biarc meeting each face at its own contact point. Parasolid exposes this as `range_1`/`range_2` face-offset control (source [9]); it is the same as the face-offset chamfer construction but with a rounded (conic) cross-section.

### 4.3 G2 / curvature-continuous cross-sections (brief, tie file 28)

For aesthetic / class-A surfaces the circular cross-section is replaced by a **conic** (rational quadratic with a shape/rho parameter) or a **curvature-continuous spline** cross-section, giving G2 contact with the supports instead of G1. Parasolid (source [9]) and ACIS both expose conic and curvature-continuous cross-sections; the cross-section curve is swept the same way along the spine. The continuity certification (Area 5.1) then targets G2 via the Pegna-Wolter linkage-curve condition (source [5]). Full treatment of the polymorphic cross-section is in file 28; here the point is only that the *surface-generation* loft is unchanged, only the swept profile differs.

## Area 5. Robustness

### 5.1 G1 (and G2) continuity certification

By construction the rolling-ball blend is tangent to both supports along the spring curves (1.1). The kernel should nonetheless **certify** it rather than trust the construction, because variable-radius marching and NURBS-canal approximation can degrade tangency.

- **G1 test.** Along each spring curve, sample points and check that the **blend-surface unit normal equals the support-face unit normal** (to a tolerance keyed to `kernel/37`). Because the spring curve is an isoparametric boundary of the canal loft (3.3), the blend normal there is cheap to evaluate. Failure tolerance ties to the angular tolerance used in `kernel/39` tangent-face handling.
- **G2 test (Pegna-Wolter, source [5]).** For curvature-continuous blends, the **Linkage Curve Theorem** reduces the G2 check to a 1D condition: given the patches are already G1-joined along the linkage (spring) curve, it is **sufficient that the normal curvatures agree in one direction other than the tangent to the linkage curve**. So certifying G2 is a curve-fairing-dimensional check along the spring curve, not a full surface test. This is the practical criterion to implement for class-A blends.
- **What breaks it.** Variable radius (the snapshot crease, 4.1); an under-sampled NURBS canal approximation (insufficient cross-sections to hold the deviation/tangent bound, source [3]); and degenerate spines (next).

### 5.2 Self-intersection and radius-too-large rejection

The blend is invalid when the rolling ball is too big for the local geometry. Three failure modes and their tests:

1. **Radius exceeds local concave curvature radius.** On a **concave** support face, the offset surface `S^{+r}` self-intersects (develops a cusp/swallowtail) when `r` exceeds the **minimum radius of curvature** of that face (the offset's medial-axis collapse; tie to `kernel/10` offset self-intersection and the medial axis, and `nurbs/05`). When the offset self-intersects, the spine SSI is corrupted and the blend self-intersects. **Test:** before computing the blend, check `r < min_concave_radius_of_curvature(S_a)` and `< (S_b)` over the blend region. For an internal cylinder fillet this is `r < R`; for a concave region of a NURBS face it is the min principal curvature radius over the spring-curve footprint. **Pegna-Wolter** (source [5]) and the existence-criterion literature give the geometric condition for a valid (existing, curvature-continuous) blend; the practical reduction is the min-radius-of-curvature limit.
2. **Blend overflows the face extent.** The spring curve runs off the edge of the support face (the strip to be trimmed is wider than the face). **Test:** check each spring curve stays within its face's boundary; if not, either reduce `r` locally, roll onto the neighbor face (ACIS "roll on", source [11]), or cap/runout (file 28 overflow/runout; ACIS end-cap/side-cap, source [2]).
3. **Opposing spring curves cross.** On a narrow support face the two spring curves (from this blend, or from this blend and a neighbor) cross, meaning the ball touches "through" the face. **Test:** check the spring curves do not intersect within the face; crossing means `r` is too large for the face width and must be reduced or the blend rejected.

**Detect-and-reject policy.** All three tests run on the **offset/spine** stage *before* generating the surface (Parasolid states the precondition directly, source [9]: "the offsets... must not self-intersect and must intersect to define a spine curve for the blend"). On failure: either reject with a precise diagnostic (which test, where), or **locally reduce the radius** (variable-radius fallback) to the largest admissible `r(v)`. This is far cheaper and more robust than generating an invalid surface and trying to repair it downstream.

### 5.3 How production kernels structure the blend recipe pipeline

The shared pattern across all three production kernels is the **declarative blend recipe / blend graph**: record the blend *intent* (which edges, what radius/law, what cross-section) as attributes on the topology, then **evaluate to faces later** in a single batched pass. This decouples intent from geometry, lets the kernel sequence and reconcile interacting blends (networks, mitring, vertex order) before committing any surface, and makes the operation re-evaluable (radius edit, rollback).

- **OCCT (`BRepFilletAPI_MakeFillet` -> `ChFi3d` / `ChFiDS`).** The recipe lives in **`ChFiDS_Spine`** (the guideline: a unified sequence of edges + tangent curves + extremity `ChFiDS_State` telling whether ends are free-border / on-section / closed, plus arc-length parameterization and offset edge sets) and **`ChFiDS_SurfData`** (per-segment surface data). **`ChFi3d_Builder`** is the root that computes the fillet surfaces and reconstructs the shape; `ChFi3d_FilBuilder` for fillets, `ChFi3d_ChBuilder` for chamfers. Circle cross-sections are emitted in three forms via `ChFi3d_FilletShape`: **`ChFi3d_Rational`** (classical NURBS circle, the default), **`ChFi3d_QuasiAngular`** (NURBS circle whose parameterization matches the true circle angle), and **`ChFi3d_Polynomial`** (polynomial approximation of the circle). The radius law supports a linear evolution between the first and last spine vertices (`SetRadius` with an evolution law). **Known fragilities:** OCCT fillets are notoriously brittle at vertices where multiple fillets meet, on tangent/near-tangent support faces, and when radii approach the local curvature limit; failures often surface as self-intersecting or unstitched faces. OCCT emits the blend as **NURBS even for plane-plane**, i.e. it does *not* exploit the exact-cylinder/torus specialization, which is precisely the gap Keel's exact-analytic ladder closes.
- **ACIS (Blending Component BLND + Advanced Blending Component ABL).** Constant-radius blends are the **rolling-ball surface type `rb_blend_spl_sur`**, evaluated as a spline (canal) surface; the spine and spring curves come from the **offset-intersection algorithm** (source [1], exactly Keel's 1.2). Blend intent is stored as **attributes on entities** (the recipe), then fixed to faces. **Variable radius** uses a marching algorithm and offers **snapshot vs envelope** surface types (4.1), plus a **sliding-disc** type in ABL. **ABL entity-entity blends** carry no edge sequence; the blend **propagates as the ball rolls**, reading **blend instructions** ("roll on" vs "cap", source [11]) from whatever entity the contact point runs into, switching among face-face / edge-face / edge-edge / vertex-face blends on the fly (source [10]). Termination is **end-cap vs side-cap** decided by geometry, or the blend meeting itself (source [2]). This entity-entity propagating model is the most general "blend graph" of the three.
- **Parasolid (PK edge / face-face / three-face blending).** **Unfixed** blends are attributes set by `PK_EDGE_set_blend_constant` / `_chain` / `_chamfer` (the recipe), then **`PK_BODY_fix_blends`** evaluates them all to faces in one pass; `PK_EDGE_remove_blend` and rollback unwind. Constant rolling-ball, chamfer (face-offset and apex-range), and variable rolling-ball (circular / conic / curvature-continuous cross-section) are the edge-blend types. **Face-face blending** (`PK_FACE_make_blend`) generalizes to non-adjacent / disjoint walls with explicit control of **cross-section plane** (rolling-ball orthogonal-to-walls, or disc orthogonal-to-spine, or isoparameter), **contact points** (size or boundary curve; symmetric or asymmetric), and **cross-section shape** (conic / chamfer / curvature-continuous). Parasolid states the **spine-as-offset-intersection precondition explicitly** (source [9]) and exposes a **blend-ribs** fallback: when it cannot make a valid surface (e.g. a wall with smaller curvature than the blend), it returns curve/point ribs for an external surfacer rather than failing outright, a graceful-degradation pattern worth mirroring (tie `kernel/29`). Parasolid encodes detailed **vertex rules** (one/two/three edges at three- and four-edge vertices; same-radius / smoothness / convexity preconditions, source [9]) and `PK_BLENDSF_ask` returns the **standard (analytic) form** of a rolling-ball blend surface, evidence that Parasolid *does* recover the exact primitive where one exists.

**Why the recipe/graph pattern matters for Keel.** Recording blend intent declaratively on the topology (parent edges + radius/law + cross-section) and evaluating in a single batched `fix_blends`-style pass lets Keel (a) sequence interacting blends and resolve vertices *before* committing geometry, (b) run all the Area-5.2 rejection tests on the offset/spine stage and locally reduce radius or fall back to ribs, (c) pick the right rung of the exact-analytic ladder per segment, and (d) keep a lineage record (`kernel/07`) keyed on the parent edge so radius edits and rollback re-evaluate cleanly. This is the same recipe architecture as file 28; this dossier supplies the *geometry* each recipe step evaluates to.

## Blend surface generation for Keel (synthesis)

1. **Spine as offset-intersection (the spine procedure).** For every constant-radius edge blend, compute `Spine = SSI(offset(S_a, r), offset(S_b, r))` using Keel's existing analytic offsets and analytic SSI. Offsets of plane/cylinder/cone/sphere/torus stay in-family (table in 1.2), so for analytic supports the spine is an exact analytic curve. Then **classify the spine** with the canonical-recovery pass (`kernel/24`): line, circle, conic-on-quadric-pair, or general.

2. **Exact-analytic ladder and the cyclide decision.** Emit the surface by spine class: **line -> Cylinder(axis = spine line, radius = r)** (exact); **circle -> Torus(R_major = spine radius, R_minor = r)** (exact); **natural-quadric pair in a cyclide-admissible configuration -> exact rational biquadratic NURBS for the Dupin cyclide** (do *not* add a new primitive type; the rational NURBS is exact and STEP-exportable, and recovery can still label it a cyclide); **everything else -> certified NURBS canal approximation** (sample spine, RMF cross-section circles of radius `r`, skin, certify deviation per source [3]). Cylinder and torus cover the dominant plane-plane / plane-cylinder / plane-sphere fillets exactly; the cyclide rung is exact-but-rational; the NURBS rung is the universal fallback.

3. **Trim-and-stitch (local boolean).** Insert each blend by imprinting the two spring curves on the supports, trimming away the strip to the original sharp edge, deleting that edge, and sewing the blend face to the spring-curve edges (`kernel/03`, with `kernel/39` tangent-aware sewing). Get **both pcurves of each spring curve in closed form**: analytic on the support (foot-of-perpendicular image), isoparametric on the blend (the spring curve *is* a `v = const` boundary of the canal loft). Record lineage keyed on parent edge + supports (`kernel/07`).

4. **Vertex blends.** At 3+ blended-edge corners, **set back** each incident blend to a clean cross-section, then fill the resulting `2n`-sided opening with a `kernel/26` transfinite patch (central-split / Gregory-GB, vertex-enclosure parity), taking G1 ribbons from the incident canal blends (along setback sides) and the support faces (along support sides). Use the single corner **sphere** only for equal-radius symmetric corners.

5. **Variable-radius NURBS path.** For `r(t)` (and chordal, asymmetric), drop the offset-intersection shortcut: place a ball of radius `r(v)` in the edge-normal plane at each `v`, march to get spine + spring curves, build the **envelope** (not snapshot) canal, emit NURBS. By Peternell-Pottmann (source [12]) this is exactly rational over rational supports; in practice always NURBS. Make `C^1`/`C^2` radius laws to get `G^1`/`G^2` blends (Lukacs, source [6]).

6. **Radius-too-large rejection (the test).** On the offset/spine stage, *before* generating any surface, reject (or locally reduce `r`) if: (a) `r >= min concave radius of curvature` of either support over the footprint (offset self-intersects, `kernel/10`); (b) a spring curve overflows its face extent; or (c) the two spring curves cross. This mirrors Parasolid's explicit precondition (source [9]) and is far cheaper than repairing invalid geometry; on overflow, prefer roll-on / cap / ribs fallback (`kernel/29`).

7. **Recommended build order.**
   1. **plane-plane to exact cylinder** (highest value, simplest; validates the spine-offset-SSI procedure and trim-and-stitch end to end).
   2. **plane-cylinder / plane-sphere to exact torus** (circle-spine class; covers boss-base and sphere fillets).
   3. **Trim-and-stitch + closed-form pcurves + lineage** hardened on these exact cases.
   4. **General NURBS canal approximation** (RMF loft, certified deviation) for non-primitive spines and any NURBS support, with the radius-too-large rejection tests.
   5. **Variable-radius NURBS** (marching spine, envelope cross-section, radius law).
   6. **Dupin-cyclide exact rational NURBS** for the remaining natural-quadric cyclide cases (optional optimization over rung 4).
   7. **Setback vertex blends** (reuse `kernel/26`), starting equal-radius three-edge corners, then unequal radii and 4+ edges.
   8. **G2 / conic / curvature-continuous cross-sections and chamfers** (file 28 polymorphic profile) last.

This makes plane-plane and plane-cylinder **exact-analytic** with a single clean ladder up to certified NURBS, exactly as required, while keeping Keel's surface set unchanged (plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, NURBS).
