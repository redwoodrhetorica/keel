## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **The malformed `Ok` is produced because the carved B-cap wall is kept with the WRONG sense: it is given an OUTWARD-pointing (convex) normal when the difference operation requires an INWARD-pointing (concave) normal. The result is a shell that is not consistently oriented: the A-remainder face points outward and the B-cap wall ALSO points outward, so the two faces fight across their shared seam circle and the shell does not bound a positive region. The divergence integral over this inconsistently-oriented shell sums to a non-positive number (`mass_properties` correctly declines), and the tessellation, which inherits the same per-face winding, produces front and back triangles that cancel to roughly zero signed volume (`mesh ~ 0`). The fix is NOT a new integrator and NOT new geometry: the seam is an exact circle and the remainder is an exact spherical zone with a closed-form oracle. The fix is (i) at `select_faces`, REVERSE the kept B-cap wall's sense for the difference operation, exactly as dossier 39's keep/drop/orient table prescribes, so the wall's `n_out` points into the cavity (away from the A-material), (ii) make `finalize_imported_assembly` re-run the dossier-46 canonicalizer so the kept wall's fins, sense cache, and pole anchor agree, and (iii) interpose a SHELL-VALIDITY PRECONDITION before the mass-vs-mesh gate (closed, orientable, consistent radial sense, positive total area) so a near-zero mesh can NEVER pass as a wrong-positive. With (i) and (ii) the body PASSES (mass == mesh == the exact spherical-zone-minus-cap oracle); if any residual defect remains, (iii) forces a DECLINE. Either way the malformed `Ok` is gone.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. Difference `A - B` keeps the part of A's surface outside B (the A-remainder, a spherical zone, outward normal unchanged) and the part of B's surface inside A (the carved wall, a spherical cap), but the carved wall now bounds a CAVITY in A, so its outward-from-the-result-solid normal points INTO what was B's interior, i.e. it is the REVERSED-sense case (dossier 39, dossier 46 section 4). A divergence-theorem volume integral is correct only over a consistently-oriented closed shell (every face's normal points away from the single solid region); if one face keeps the donor body's outward normal instead of being reversed, the shell is inconsistently oriented and the signed volume is meaningless, which is exactly the "non-positive volume, orientation conventions violated" decline. The mesh inherits the per-face winding, so the same inconsistency makes the signed tetrahedron sum collapse toward zero rather than to the true volume. The seam between the two faces is the exact SSI circle (plane perpendicular to the line of centers), and the kept A-remainder is an exact spherical zone, so the corrected body has a closed-form oracle to within `1e-9`. The whole trap is a sense-bookkeeping error at one face plus a missing validity gate, not a geometry or integration failure.

## 1. The correct cap-trim topology of sphere A minus sphere B

The first defect is the spherical-cap trim's pcurve/seam handling. This section fixes the exact loop / Euler structure of the result, the seam circle, the two faces, their radial-2 pairing, and the sense of each.

### 1.1 The seam circle is the exact sphere-sphere intersection circle

> **Citation.** "Sphere-Sphere Intersection," Wolfram MathWorld, https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Sphere-SphereIntersection.html . "Circle of a sphere," Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_a_sphere . Keel dossier 58 (curved SSI) for the in-kernel sphere-sphere case.
> **Method / Content.** Two spheres, radii `R` (A) and `r` (B), centers distance `d` apart along the line of centers, intersect (when `|R - r| < d < R + r`) in a single CIRCLE lying in the plane PERPENDICULAR to the line of centers, at signed distance `x = (d^2 - r^2 + R^2) / (2 d)` from A's center along that line. The seam-circle radius is `a = sqrt(R^2 - x^2)`, an exact closed form. The plane is perpendicular to the centers' axis; the circle is a latitude circle of BOTH spheres simultaneously. **Verified** (standard analytic geometry; the SSI plane and radius are exact).
> **Limitations.** When `d <= |R - r|` one sphere is wholly inside the other (no seam; `A - B` is either A unchanged, A with an interior void, or empty). When `d >= R + r` the spheres are disjoint (no seam; `A - B = A`). The single-circle result assumes transverse intersection; the tangent case `d = R + r` or `d = |R - r|` gives a single point (degenerate, DECLINE class, section 4).
> **Kernel relevance.** Keel's `seam_curves` already returns this exact circle for the sphere-sphere case, so the SEAM geometry is not the defect: defect (1) is downstream, in how the circle is attached as a trim boundary (pcurve / loop) to each kept face. The seam circle is the SINGLE shared edge between the two result faces (section 1.3), and being a latitude circle of both spheres it has an exact pcurve on each (a constant-`v` iso-line in each sphere's `(u, v)` parameterization), which is the key that lets both kept faces carry a proper iso-boundary rather than a pcurve-less 3D edge.

### 1.2 What A minus B keeps: a spherical zone of A plus a concave spherical cap of B

> **Citation.** Spherical zone / segment closed forms: https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Zone.html , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_segment , https://www.math10.com/en/geometry/sphere.html . Spherical-cap volume: V_cap = (pi h^2 / 3)(3 R - h). A. A. G. Requicha, H. B. Voelcker, "Boolean operations in solid modelling: boundary evaluation and merging algorithms," Proc. IEEE 73(1):30-44, 1985, https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3205703b19600586ec5c400893977b968ba84ec1 .
> **Method / Content.** Set-membership classification (Requicha-Voelcker) partitions each operand's boundary into the part INSIDE the other solid, the part OUTSIDE, and the part ON (coincident). For `A - B = A intersect complement(B)`:
> - From sphere A's surface, KEEP the part OUTSIDE B (it survives the difference). This is a spherical ZONE of A: the sphere A surface minus the cap that B cut away. Its outward normal is UNCHANGED (still points away from A's center, out of the result solid).
> - From sphere B's surface, KEEP the part INSIDE A (it becomes the new boundary of the carved cavity). This is a spherical CAP of B. But its sense is REVERSED: in B's own body the cap normal pointed away from B's center (out of B); in the result, the same surface now bounds a CAVITY carved into A, so the result-outward normal must point AWAY FROM A's MATERIAL, which is INTO what was B's interior, i.e. the cap is kept CONCAVE / inward-facing.
> The result of `A - B` (when B carves a cap into A, B not wholly inside A and not disjoint) is therefore a body bounded by exactly TWO faces: (the remainder spherical zone of A, convex, outward normal) plus (a concave spherical patch of B's surface, inward-facing), meeting at the single seam circle. **Verified** (Requicha-Voelcker classification and the zone/cap decomposition are standard; the reversed cap sense is the dossier-39 difference rule).
> **Limitations.** This is the canonical "B cuts a cap into A" configuration. Other configs (B wholly inside A leaving a void shell; B containing A's near side; both centers coincident) have different topology and are catalogued in the cross-cutting section. The two-face result assumes each sphere contributes one connected trimmed face; a tangential or grazing cut can split a face, handled by the same per-face classification.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the exact result topology Keel must produce. The remainder zone has the closed-form volume oracle `V_zone_solid = V_sphere_A - V_capA_removed`, and the carved-cavity contribution is the cap of B; the net solid volume is `V(A) - V(lens overlap)` where the lens overlap is two spherical caps (cross-cutting bucket (a)). The reversed cap sense (defect 2) is the heart of the trap and is detailed in section 2.

### 1.3 The exact loop / Euler structure and the radial-2 pairing

> **Citation.** Keel dossier 45 (genus-1 Euler sequence, seams, poles), dossier 46 (face orientation), dossier 47 (boolean result assembly). Mantyla, "An Introduction to Solid Modeling," 1988 (Euler-Poincare, loop = face). Kevin Weiler, "The Radial Edge Structure," 1988 (radial ordering of face-uses around an edge).
> **Method / Content (claimed, the deliverable topology).** The corrected `A - B` body has:
> - **Faces: 2.** Face F_A = the kept spherical zone of A (surface = sphere A, sense = FORWARD, `n_out` = A's outward normal). Face F_B = the kept spherical cap of B (surface = sphere B, sense = REVERSED, `n_out` = into the cavity, away from A-material).
> - **Edges: 1.** The single seam circle E_seam, shared by F_A and F_B. It is a latitude circle of both spheres, so it carries an exact pcurve on each face (constant-`v` iso in each sphere parameterization). On a sphere a single seam circle plus the pole structure means each face is a full-azimuth `(u: 0 to 2*pi)` patch bounded in `v` by the seam on one side and reaching a POLE on the other (the far pole of A for F_A, the near pole of B for F_B). So each face also has the usual sphere SEAM meridian (the `u = 0` periodic seam edge of the sphere parameterization) and a POLE anchor (full-revolution face, dossier 45 / 60).
> - **Vertices.** The seam circle, being a full circle, can be a closed edge with a single seam vertex (dossier 45 per-band seam) where the sphere's `u = 0` meridian crosses it; plus the two pole vertices (A's far pole on F_A, B's near pole on F_B).
> - **Radial-2 pairing.** Around the seam edge E_seam exactly TWO face-uses meet: the use from F_A and the use from F_B. This is an ordinary manifold edge (radial valence 2), and the two coedges traverse the seam circle in OPPOSITE directions (half-edge consistency, dossier 46 validator clause C). This opposite traversal is the topological signature that the two faces' normals are consistently oriented across the seam: F_A's outward zone normal and F_B's inward cap normal both keep the SOLID on the same side of the seam, so the loop closes the boundary of one consistent region.
> - **Euler check.** Two full-revolution sphere faces joined at one circle, each reaching one pole: this is topologically a sphere (the result solid is a ball with a dimple, genus 0). V - E + F = 2 must hold after counting the seam vertex, pole vertices, seam edge, two meridian seam edges, and the two faces; the exact count depends on the seam/pole modeling (dossier 45), but the GENUS is 0 and the shell is a single closed orientable 2-sphere.
> **Limitations.** The pole-and-seam bookkeeping is the dossier-45 machinery; getting it wrong is defect (3) (the Green-slab anchor for the remainder face). The two-face / one-edge count is for the clean cap-cut; a cut that grazes a pole or the sphere seam needs the dossier-45 degenerate branches.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the topology `finalize_imported_assembly` must emit. The decisive structural facts: ONE shared seam edge with radial valence 2 and OPPOSITE coedge traversal; F_B reversed-sense; each face full-revolution with a pole anchor. If `imprint_operand` attaches the seam as a pcurve-less 3D edge to F_B (defect 1), the half-edge pairing breaks and the cap face cannot be integrated or oriented; if `select_faces` keeps F_B FORWARD instead of REVERSED (defect 2), the coedges traverse the seam in the SAME direction and the shell is inconsistently oriented (section 2). **Claimed** (the exact Euler count, to validate against the dossier-45 sphere machinery); **verified** that the result is genus-0, two-face, one-seam, with F_B reversed.

## 2. Why the volume integrates to a non-positive number

The second defect is the region/sense of the carved cavity wall. This section explains why `mass_properties` correctly declines with "non-positive volume, orientation conventions violated," why the planar analog is clean, and exactly where the pipeline gets the sense wrong.

### 2.1 The divergence integral is meaningless on an inconsistently-oriented shell

> **Citation.** Divergence theorem and orientation: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergence_theorem ; "using inward-facing normals on a closed surface inverts the sign of the volume integral," Oregon State / Math Insight, https://sites.science.oregonstate.edu/math/home/programs/undergrad/CalculusQuestStudyGuides/vcalc/diverg/diverg.html , https://mathinsight.org/divergence_theorem_idea . Keel dossier 46 (the `n_out = sense * n_nat` invariant; reversed-sense cavity walls).
> **Method / Content.** The divergence theorem `V = integral over closed boundary of x dy dz` (field `F = (x,0,0)`, `div F = 1`) holds only when the boundary is a CLOSED, CONSISTENTLY-ORIENTED surface with every face normal pointing away from the single enclosed solid. If ONE face's normal is flipped relative to the rest, that face contributes its flux with the WRONG sign, so the sum is `V_correct - 2 * (flux of the flipped face)`, which can be negative or near zero. For sphere-minus-sphere kept with F_B FORWARD (not reversed): F_A contributes `+V_zone` (correct outward), but F_B contributes its cap flux with the donor-body sign, which after the difference is the WRONG sign for the result solid, so the two fight and the sum is non-positive. `mass_properties`, which derives `n_out` from the sense folded with the loop winding (dossier 46), sees the two faces disagree on which side is material and reports "orientation conventions violated, non-positive volume." It DECLINES, which is correct behavior given the malformed input. **Verified** (the sign flip from an inward / wrongly-oriented face is elementary divergence-theorem; the decline is the integrator correctly refusing an inconsistent shell).
> **Limitations.** The decline is only as good as the orientation check; an integrator that did NOT check consistency would return the wrong non-positive number silently. `mass_properties` does check, which is why it declines rather than returning garbage; the silent-malformed escape is therefore NOT in `mass_properties` (it declines honestly), it is in the MESH path and the GATE (section 3).
> **Kernel relevance.** This pins defect (2): the carved B-cap is kept with the wrong sense, producing an inconsistently-oriented shell, and `mass_properties` correctly declines it. The fix is to keep F_B REVERSED so all normals point away from the single result solid, after which the divergence integral is the honest positive `V(A) - V(lens)`.

### 2.2 Why the carved B-cap needs reversed sense, and where the pipeline gets it wrong

> **Citation.** Keel dossier 39 (coincident/tangent boolean keep/drop/orient tables: "difference keeps B's inner wall WITH REVERSED normals; the cavity's new outward boundary"). Dossier 46 section 4 (reversed-sense faces: cavity wall, inner-void shell, `n_out = -n_nat`, normal points into the void away from the solid). Parasolid functional description: "for an inner shell (a void inside a solid) the face normals point inwards, that is, still away from the solid."
> **Method / Content.** In B's own body, the cap surface's outward normal points away from B's center (out of B). After `A - B`, that surface is the wall of a CAVITY carved into A. The result solid is A-material; the wall's "away from material" direction is away from the A-material, which is INTO the carved hollow, which is toward B's center, i.e. the OPPOSITE of B's original outward normal. So the kept cap must have its sense REVERSED relative to sphere B's natural normal: `n_out(F_B) = -n_nat(sphere B)`. This is exactly dossier 39's difference rule (B's inner wall kept reversed) and dossier 46 section 4's canonical reversed-sense case (the cavity wall). The pipeline gets it wrong at one of two stages:
> - **At `select_faces` (Requicha table application).** The difference table must mark the kept B-face as REVERSED. If `select_faces` keeps it with B's original (forward) sense, the wall points the wrong way. This is the most likely locus: the sphere-sphere difference is a CURVED reversed-cap case that the planar tables may not cover, so the curved branch keeps the donor sense unflipped.
> - **At `finalize_imported_assembly` (canonicalization).** Even if `select_faces` marks the reversal, the dossier-46 canonicalizer must re-establish the invariant on the stitched face so the FINS, the `sense` cache, and the pole anchor all agree with `n_out = -n_nat`. If finalize does not re-canonicalize the imported B-cap (because it was imported from B, not Euler-built in place), the sense cache and the fin winding split (the dossier-46 two-convention bug) and the wall is malformed even if its sense bit is nominally reversed.
> **Verified** that the carved cap needs reversed sense (dossier 39 / 46 / Parasolid inner-shell rule). **Claimed** (the precise locus: `select_faces` curved-difference table plus `finalize` canonicalization, to confirm against the code).
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the direct fix target. Reverse the kept B-cap sense in the curved-difference branch of `select_faces`, then force `finalize_imported_assembly` to call `canonicalize_face_orientation` (dossier 46 section 3.2) on the imported cap with the cavity-outward hint, so fins, sense cache, and the section-3 pole anchor agree.

### 2.3 Why the planar analog is clean

> **Citation.** Keel MEMORY tilted-cut-boolean-bug (resolved: planar difference assembles to true volume, mass == mesh) and massprops-sense/region-inconsistency (resolved). Dossier 47 (boolean result assembly).
> **Method / Content.** A planar difference (box minus box, the tilted-cut case, now resolved) is clean because the kept cut FACE is PLANAR: its natural normal is constant, the reversed-sense bookkeeping was the single resolved fix, and a plane has NO pole / seam / full-revolution structure, so there is no anchor to mis-handle and no parametric singularity. The sphere case stacks two extra hazards on top of the same reversed-sense issue: (i) the kept cap is a FULL-REVOLUTION face with a pole, so it needs the Green-slab pole anchor (defect 3), and a wrongly-anchored full-revolution face declines "unsupported boundary winding" or integrates the wrong region even when the sense is right; (ii) the seam is a curved circle whose pcurve attachment (defect 1) must be exact on both faces or the half-edge pairing breaks. So the planar case exposed ONLY the sense bug (one fix); the sphere case exposes the sense bug PLUS the pcurve attachment PLUS the pole anchor, three stacked defects that must all be fixed for a PASS. **Verified** that the planar difference is now clean and that the difference is the curved pole/seam machinery, not the set-operation logic.
> **Kernel relevance.** The planar resolution is the template: the sphere fix is the same reversed-sense correction (section 2.2) plus the two curved-only pieces (the exact pcurve on the seam, section 1.1, and the pole anchor on the remainder, section 3). It confirms the trap is not a new class of bug but the curved generalization of an already-solved planar one.

## 3. The minimal fix: PASS or DECLINE, never the malformed Ok

This section gives the narrowest fix that makes sphere-minus-sphere either PASS (mass == mesh == truth) or DECLINE honestly, and closes the `mesh ~ 0` wrong-positive escape as a first-class deliverable.

### 3.1 The three-part minimal fix

> **Method / Content (claimed, the deliverable).**
> - **Fix A (sense, the root cause).** In the CURVED-difference branch of `select_faces`, mark the kept B-cap REVERSED (dossier 39 rule). Then in `finalize_imported_assembly`, call `canonicalize_face_orientation(F_B, p, cavity_outward_hint)` (dossier 46 section 3.2) so the cap's fins, `sense` cache, and `n_out = -n_nat` agree. After Fix A the shell is consistently oriented and `mass_properties` integrates to the honest positive `V(A) - V(lens)`.
> - **Fix B (seam pcurve).** In `imprint_operand`, attach the seam circle as an exact constant-`v` ISO pcurve on BOTH F_A and F_B (it is a latitude circle of both spheres, section 1.1), not a pcurve-less 3D edge, so the radial-2 half-edge pairing is exact and the Green-slab integrator sees a closed iso-bounded loop on each face.
> - **Fix C (remainder anchor).** Ensure each kept full-revolution sphere face carries the POLE anchor `integrate_face_green` already implements for full-revolution sphere faces (dossier 60 section 1 is the cone dual; the sphere pole anchor already exists), so the remainder zone F_A and the cap F_B each close their boundary loop and integrate to the exact spherical-zone / spherical-cap closed form.
> **Exact oracle.** With Fixes A to C, `V(A - B) = V_sphere(A) - V_lens`, where `V_lens` is the two-cap lens overlap `V = pi h_1^2 (3R - h_1)/3 + pi h_2^2 (3r - h_2)/3` (the partial-overlap formula, cross-cutting bucket (a)), with `h_1 = R - x` and `h_2 = r - (d - x)`, `x = (d^2 - r^2 + R^2)/(2d)`. The integrator must reproduce this to `1e-9`, far inside the 2% curved band. `mass == mesh == oracle` -> PASS.
> **Kernel relevance.** Fix A is the root cause and the single most important change; B and C are the curved-only enablers that turn a now-consistently-oriented shell into an integrable one. All three reuse existing machinery (the dossier-39 table, the dossier-46 canonicalizer, the existing sphere pole anchor); none is a new integration theory. **Claimed** (the wiring; validate against the existing curved-band regression and the lens oracle).

### 3.2 Closing the mesh ~ 0 wrong-positive: a shell-validity precondition before the gate

> **Citation.** B-rep validity / closed-orientable-shell checks: Euler-Poincare one-sided test, https://pages.mtu.edu/~shene/COURSES/cs3621/NOTES/model/euler.html ; "a B-rep is a solid if each edge is shared by exactly two faces, faces around each vertex form a closed fan," geometric-modelling course notes, https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/CVonline/LOCAL_COPIES/BOWYER1/c2.htm . Watertight / orientable mesh criteria (edge-manifold, vertex-manifold, no self-intersection, no flipped triangles), https://academic.oup.com/jcde/article/13/1/239/8383411 , Open3D mesh manifold definitions, https://open3d.org/docs/latest/tutorial/Basic/mesh.html . OCCT `ShapeAnalysis_Shell::HasBadEdges`, BRepClass3d consistent-outward-normal contract (dossier 46 S12).
> **Method / Content (claimed, the first-class deliverable).** The escape hatch is that `mesh_volume ~ 0` passes the `mass == mesh` gate when BOTH are near zero (and here `mass` declined, but a future variant could return a near-zero number). The fix is a VALIDITY PRECONDITION that runs on the assembled shell BEFORE the mass-vs-mesh gate, and is itself a DECLINE trigger:
> 1. **Closed.** Every edge has radial valence exactly 2 (for a manifold solid shell); no boundary edges (radial valence 1). The seam circle and the two meridian seams must each pair up. A malformed sphere-difference with a pcurve-less unpaired seam edge FAILS this and DECLINES.
> 2. **Orientable and consistently oriented.** Each manifold edge's two coedges traverse it in OPPOSITE directions (dossier 46 clause C); equivalently, adjacent face normals agree on the material side across every shared edge. The wrong-sense F_B (un-reversed cap) FAILS this: across the seam the two coedges traverse in the SAME direction, signaling the fight that produces `mesh ~ 0`. This single check catches the exact malformed body.
> 3. **Consistent radial sense.** Every face's `n_out` points away from the SAME solid region (Parasolid's defining law). A shell where F_A points out and F_B points out (both away from their own donor centers, not from the common result solid) FAILS: a ray-test or a `dot(n_out, region_void_dir) > 0` check per face (dossier 46 validator clause B) is inconsistent.
> 4. **Positive total area and non-degenerate.** The summed face area must exceed a tolerance; a body whose tessellation collapses to near-zero area (the thin-lens degenerate, the wrongly-cancelled shell) FAILS the positive-area precondition. This directly blocks the `mesh ~ 0` body: a near-zero-area or near-zero-signed-volume mesh is a precondition failure, not a gate pass.
> If ALL four preconditions hold, run the mass-vs-mesh gate (now a near-zero mesh is impossible for a valid positive-volume shell, so a near-zero result can only arise from a genuinely tiny body, which the positive-area / volume floor separates from the malformed case). If ANY precondition fails, DECLINE with a specific reason (open shell / inconsistent orientation / inconsistent radial sense / degenerate area). The boolean NEVER returns the malformed `Ok`: it either passes a valid shell or declines.
> **Verified** that closed-orientable-consistent-shell checks are the standard B-rep validity battery (OCCT, the solid-model conditions, watertight-mesh criteria); **claimed** that interposing them as a pre-gate DECLINE trigger closes Keel's specific escape hatch.
> **Kernel relevance.** This is the structural guarantee that DECLINE-never-WRONG holds even if a future geometry bug reintroduces a malformed shell: the precondition is a NECESSARY condition for a positive-volume solid, it is cheap (edge-pairing, coedge-direction, per-face void-side dot, area sum), and it runs before any number is trusted. It converts the silent-malformed `Ok` into either an honest PASS (valid shell) or an honest DECLINE (precondition failure). It is the first-class deliverable: even before Fixes A to C land, this precondition alone removes the WRONG outcome by forcing the malformed body to DECLINE.

### 3.3 Why mesh_volume is approximately zero

> **Citation.** Signed-tetrahedron / signed-volume mesh formula (divergence theorem on triangles); flipped-triangle cancellation, Open3D / watertight-mesh references above. Keel MEMORY tessellation-tolerance-trap (chord-vs-arc), tessellation-tolerance-trap.
> **Method / Content.** `mesh_volume` sums signed tetrahedra `(1/6) v_i . (v_j x v_k)` over triangles, with sign from triangle winding (which inherits face sense). When F_B is kept with the wrong (un-reversed) sense, its triangles wind OPPOSITE to what a consistent shell needs, so the cap's positive contribution is entered with the wrong sign and partially or wholly CANCELS F_A's contribution. For a near-symmetric cap cut the cancellation is near-total, so `mesh_volume ~ 0`. It is NOT that the geometry has zero volume; it is that the signed sum of a wrongly-oriented shell cancels. This is exactly why `mesh ~ 0` is a WRONG-POSITIVE: the mesh is "valid" triangle-by-triangle but globally mis-oriented, so its signed volume is meaningless, and a gate that only compares two meaningless-but-equal near-zero numbers passes it. The positive-area precondition (3.2 item 4) and the consistent-orientation precondition (3.2 item 2) both catch this before the gate. **Verified** (signed-volume cancellation under mis-orientation is elementary; the near-zero result is the symmetric-cap cancellation).
> **Kernel relevance.** Confirms the escape is an ORIENTATION cancellation, not a geometry zero, so the orientation-consistency and positive-area preconditions are the correct and sufficient catches. It also confirms Fix A (reverse F_B) makes `mesh_volume` recover the true positive value, so after Fix A the gate compares two correct positive numbers and PASSES.

**Bucket (a): EXACT, closed-form oracle (the PASS target).** The seam is an exact circle (transverse intersection, `|R - r| < d < R + r`, not near-tangent), the kept A-remainder is an exact spherical ZONE, the carved cavity is an exact spherical CAP, and the result volume is `V(A) - V_lens` with `V_lens` the two-cap closed form. These configs MUST pass `mass == mesh == oracle` once Fixes A to C land. Includes: a small B carving a cap into a large A; two comparable spheres overlapping by a clean cap; B's center outside A. The oracle is exact to `1e-9`.

**Bucket (b): needs the certified boundary-flux integrator with the right anchor.** Configs where the kept face is full-revolution and reaches a pole (every clean cap-cut does, since the remainder zone spans from the seam to the far pole and the cap spans from the seam to the near pole), so each face needs the Green-slab POLE anchor (dossier 60). These are still EXACT in principle (the integrand is a sphere, closed-form), but they go through the certified quadrature plus pole anchor rather than a single algebraic formula; the anchor must be present and correct (defect 3). If the cap or zone is partial-azimuth (a second cut also present), it needs the partial-fragment area-witness (dossier 60 section 3) in addition. PASS via certified integrator, oracle cross-check from bucket (a).

**Bucket (c): DECLINE honestly.** Near-tangent thin lens (`d -> R + r`, the seam circle radius `a -> 0`): the cap height and the lens volume go to zero, the seam circle shrinks below tolerance, and the trimmed cap is a sub-tolerance sliver whose pcurve and pole anchor are numerically unreliable. Also: exact tangency (`d = R + r` or `d = |R - r|`, seam is a single point, not a circle), and the sub-tolerance coincident-center / equal-radius degenerate. These DECLINE: the seam is below the geometry tolerance, so no certified result is possible, and DECLINE-never-WRONG requires declining rather than emitting an unreliable sliver volume. The positive-area precondition (3.2 item 4) is the catch that routes a sub-tolerance lens to DECLINE rather than to a wrong-positive PASS.
