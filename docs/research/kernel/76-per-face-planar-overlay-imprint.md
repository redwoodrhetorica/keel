# 76. The per-face planar-overlay imprint: arranging {outer loop, hole loops, all seam chains} on one face and re-forming the sub-faces in one pass

## Title and scope

This is the implementation-grade literature dossier for **the per-face planar-overlay imprint**, the engine that clears the bulk of the compound-operand decline class (request batch 0007 #76). The problem: on a COMPOUND boolean operand (post-extrude, post-boolean, post-fillet) a single planar face arrives at the imprint carrying an outer loop, zero or more inner HOLE loops, AND several seam chains (the tool's intersection curves) that may cross each other, the holes, and the outer loop at shared vertices. The landed per-chain rungs (`imprint_open_chain` / `split_face` in `boolean.rs`) resolve ONE chain against ONE loop at a time and provably cannot compose the stacked case (FINDINGS 73c section 5). The fix is to compute the FULL planar arrangement of all these curves on the face's 2D surface-parameter domain and re-form the correct sub-faces in ONE pass.

This dossier is the **planar analog of dossier 64** (the periodic-domain seam split, which arranges curves on a cylinder/cone/sphere parameter rectangle with identification and contraction boundaries). Here the carrier is a plane: no identification, no contraction, but with K inner HOLE loops, which the periodic case does not stress. It builds on dossier 04 (Part 2: CGAL 2D Arrangements, the DCEL, Jiang-Bunke face extraction, winding rules, snap rounding, exact predicates) and does NOT repeat that machinery; it builds on dossier 47 (the import-and-glue / shell-closure invariant) and dossiers 73 / 73b / 73c (the attribution chain that isolated this class and landed its two simple rungs). It is a **design recommendation** grounded in the DCEL map-overlay literature (de Berg et al.), OCCT BOPAlgo_BuilderFace / BOPAlgo_WireSplitter, ACIS / SpaceClaim imprint-and-split, and classical planar-subdivision theory, to be validated against Keel's curved-boolean regression suite and the explorer decline corpus.

**Where Keel is (grounding, build on, do not re-research).** Tolerant-f64 non-manifold radial-edge B-rep; GWB Euler operators (`split_face`, `mef`, `mev`, `kev`, `kef`, `mekr`, `kemr`, `kfmrh`, `split_edge`). Analytic surfaces plane / cylinder / cone / sphere / torus plus NURBS. Boolean pipeline: `seam_curves` -> `imprint_operand` -> `classify_faces` (generalized winding number) -> `select_faces` (Requicha) -> `stitch_by_import` -> `finalize_imported_assembly` (Weiler region walk). The wall (LOG Add 296-297): on a compound operand a face's imprint stacks an outer loop, K hole loops, and M seam chains that cross each other, the holes, and the outer loop at shared vertices; `imprint_open_chain` / `split_face` resolve one shape at a time and yield absent / radial-1 fragments, so the (correct) shell-closure check declines. Two rungs already LANDED and the overlay must SUBSUME them without regression: the **T-junction imprint** (73b: split the boundary edge at the mid-edge junction so a sibling seam attaches vertex-to-vertex) and the **isolated seam-crosses-a-hole** (73c: `mekr`-bridge the hole into the outer loop, then `split_face`). The residual (73c section 5, seed 11400715918834826715) STACKS a hole-crossing AND a sibling-seam T-junction on ONE face; the one-shape rungs cannot compose. The all-planar instance (straight loops, straight seams) has an EXACT closed-form result volume, the verifiable beachhead.

## The load-bearing conclusion, stated once

> **The per-face imprint must stop being a sequence of one-shape Euler surgeries and become a single planar-arrangement OVERLAY. Project the face's outer loop, its K hole loops, and the M seam polylines into the face's 2D surface-parameter domain (an exact affine map for a planar carrier); insert all of them, with the crossing vertices that Phase 1 already imprinted, into ONE doubly-connected edge list (DCEL); the arrangement's bounded faces ARE the correct sub-faces, each read back as one outer boundary cycle plus zero or more hole cycles by the standard leftmost-vertex / signed-area classification. The whole stack of crossings (seam-on-loop T-junctions, seam-on-seam junctions, seam-through-hole dips) is resolved at once because in a DCEL every crossing is just a degree-bumped vertex with its incident half-edges re-linked in angular order; there is no "first chain then second chain" ordering to get wrong. The B-rep is then re-formed from this arrangement by a deterministic Euler sequence: pre-split boundary edges at every crossing vertex (`split_edge`), bridge each hole loop into the connected component that will own it (`mekr`, one bridge per hole), then issue one `split_face` (`mef`) per arrangement edge that separates two distinct sub-faces, in any order, with V minus E plus F bookkeeping conserved at every step. This SUBSUMES 73b (a lone seam-on-loop crossing is the K equals 0, one-component overlay, whose single `split_face` is exactly the landed T-junction split) and 73c (a lone seam-through-one-hole is the K equals 1 overlay whose single `mekr`-bridge plus `split_face` is exactly the landed hole-dip sequence); the overlay reproduces both on their inputs and additionally resolves their COMPOSITION, which the hand-coded rungs cannot. Genuinely intractable inputs (a self-touching seam, a tangential / non-transversal crossing, a sub-tolerance sliver sub-face) are detected as arrangement degeneracies and DECLINE, never WRONG.**

The defensible reason, in one paragraph. The reason the two landed rungs cannot compose is structural, not a coding gap: `mef` (the engine under `split_face`) moves the half-edges of ONE loop between the two cut fins and leaves every OTHER loop with the old face (FINDINGS 73c section 1). So each one-shape rung is correct only when there is exactly one shape to move; the moment a face carries a hole-crossing AND a sibling T-junction, the order in which you apply the two rungs determines which loop ends up stranded, and no order is correct (73c proved a permissive composition MANGLED seed 715, 4 danglers to 12+). The arrangement removes the ordering entirely: it computes the final subdivision combinatorially, in 2D, where a hole is just a bounded inner cycle and a T-junction is just a vertex of degree 3, then re-forms the B-rep from that finished picture. This is precisely how every production kernel splits a face by multiple imprint curves (OCCT's BOPAlgo_BuilderFace builds ALL split faces of a face from the pooled set of original-boundary edges plus all section edges in one wire-splitter pass; ACIS / SpaceClaim imprint all section curves then re-make faces), and it is the planar instance of the same arrangement Keel already adopted for the periodic case in dossier 64. The all-planar instance is exact: straight loops and straight seams give a polygonal arrangement whose sub-face areas, and hence the assembled body's volume, are closed-form, so the beachhead is verifiable to the last bit.

The one honest caveat: a seam that is tangent to a hole rather than transversally crossing it, a seam that self-touches, or a sub-tolerance sliver sub-face, are genuinely ambiguous in tolerant f64 and stay DECLINE-forever, never WRONG.

## Q1. The arrangement: subdivide the 2D parameter domain of the face

### Q1.0 The domain model: a plain bounded plane, holes as inner cycles

The face's carrier is analytic plane (a curved carrier falls through to dossier 64's periodic machinery or declines). Pick the plane's orthonormal frame (origin plus u, v basis already stored on the surface); the projection 3D point -> (u, v) is an exact affine map (no distortion, unlike the curved preimages of dossier 64 Q3). The outer loop, the K hole loops, and the M seam polylines all map to straight 2D segments (the all-planar beachhead) or to 2D arcs (a general planar face, handled by the same arrangement with arc traits, dossier 04 Q2.1). Unlike the periodic domain of dossier 64, this rectangle carries NO identification and NO contraction boundary; the only structure is that K of the input cycles are HOLES (bounded cycles whose interior is NOT part of the face). The arrangement subdivides the (u, v) plane; the face's material region is the set of arrangement faces inside the outer loop and outside all holes, and the seam curves cut THAT region into the sub-faces.

#### de Berg, Cheong, van Kreveld, Overmars: the DCEL and map overlay (the governing planar framework)
- **Citation.** M. de Berg, O. Cheong, M. van Kreveld, M. Overmars, "Computational Geometry: Algorithms and Applications," 3rd ed., Springer, 2008, ch. 2 (DCEL and map overlay). Lecture treatments: D. Mount, CMSC 754 Lecture 10 "The Doubly-Connected Edge List," University of Maryland; M. Sellares, "Doubly-connected edge list, Map overlay," Univ. de Girona. https://www.cs.umd.edu/class/spring2020/cmsc754/Lects/lect10-dcel.pdf ; https://ima.udg.edu/~sellares/ComGeo/DCEL4.pdf
- **Method / content.** A planar subdivision is stored as a DCEL with three record types: a **vertex** (coordinates plus one incident half-edge), a **half-edge** (origin vertex, twin, next, prev, incident face to its left), and a **face** (one OUTER-component half-edge plus a list of INNER-component half-edges, one per hole). Every undirected edge is two oppositely-directed twin half-edges. A face's outer boundary is the cycle reached by following `next` from its outer half-edge; each hole is a separate `next`-cycle reached from an inner-component pointer. The **overlay** of two subdivisions S1 and S2 is computed by a Bentley-Ottmann plane sweep: copy both DCELs, then at each event re-stitch. When a half-edge of S1 crosses a half-edge of S2 at an interior point, both are split into two half-edges at a new vertex, and the four (or more) half-edges incident to that vertex are re-linked so that, going around the vertex, each half-edge's `next` is the next half-edge in clockwise angular order on the other side (the local "reconnect by angular order" rule). After the sweep, **faces are reconstructed**: trace every `next`-cycle to get all boundary cycles; for each cycle compute its leftmost vertex and the turn there (or equivalently its signed area), which determines whether the cycle is an OUTER boundary (counterclockwise, positive area, locally convex at the leftmost vertex) or a HOLE boundary (clockwise, negative area); build a graph linking each hole cycle to the cycle immediately containing it (found by a sweep that shoots a ray left from each hole's leftmost vertex to the first edge it hits) and read off, per connected component, one outer cycle plus its holes as one face. The sweep runs in O((n plus k) log n) for n edges and k intersections.
- **Limitations.** The textbook algorithm assumes exact predicates; tolerant f64 must snap near-coincident vertices and treat a crossing-at-an-existing-vertex as a vertex event, not an interior split (dossier 04 Q2.5 snap rounding). Overlapping collinear edges (two seams along the same line, or a seam along a hole edge) are a degeneracy needing the labeled-overlap handling of the degeneracy literature (dossier 04 Q2.3).
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the exact data structure and algorithm Keel's per-face imprint should build, with ONE simplification: Keel is not overlaying two arbitrary maps; it is inserting M seam polylines (and the K holes, already present as inner loops of the face) into the SINGLE map that is the face's own boundary. So it is an incremental INSERTION (zone) construction, not a two-map overlay, which is cheaper and matches CGAL's `insert` rather than `overlay`. The crossing vertices are mostly already imprinted by Phase 1 (FINDINGS 73c section 2: a tool corner landing on a hole becomes a hole vertex), so the sweep mainly needs to confirm them and link half-edges, not discover them. **Verified** against the de Berg framework; the insertion specialization is **claimed** as the right Keel realization.

### Q1.1 Build the half-edge overlay with the kernel's tolerant predicates

- **Citation.** Reconstruction from de Berg ch. 2 (above), CGAL 2D Arrangements incremental insertion (dossier 04 Q2.1: Wein, Fogel, Zukerman, Halperin), and Jiang-Bunke region extraction (dossier 04 Q2.2).
- **Method / content (the recommended Keel mechanism).** Seed the DCEL from the face's existing loops: the outer loop becomes one CCW outer cycle, each hole becomes one CW inner cycle (Keel already stores loops with this orientation, dossier 04 Q2.2 winding convention). Then, for each of the M seam polylines, insert its segments one at a time by the zone algorithm:
  1. **Locate the start vertex.** The seam's endpoints were placed by Phase 1; snap each to the nearest existing vertex within `etol` (a loop vertex, a hole vertex, or a previously-inserted seam vertex). If a seam endpoint lands in the interior of an existing boundary edge (the 73b T-junction: a sibling seam ended mid-span of a fresh edge), split that edge there (`split_edge` analog in 2D: replace the half-edge pair by two pairs sharing the new vertex). This is the arrangement-level statement of the 73b fix, now applied uniformly to every endpoint rather than as a special case.
  2. **Insert the segment.** Walk the segment across the current arrangement (the "zone"); at each crossing with an existing half-edge, create an intersection vertex (or reuse the snapped existing one), split both crossed half-edges, and re-link the incident half-edges in angular order. A seam-on-seam crossing and a seam-on-hole-edge crossing are the SAME operation: a vertex of degree 4 (or degree 3 at a T) whose half-edges are sorted by angle. This is where the stacked case is resolved with no per-shape ordering.
  3. **Shared vertices.** A crossing that lands exactly on an existing vertex (seam-on-loop at a corner, seam-on-seam at a shared imprinted point, FINDINGS 73c step 3: crossings are already hole VERTICES) is handled by the SAME angular re-link, just without creating a new vertex: insert the new half-edge pair into the existing vertex's rotational order. Tolerant equality (within `etol`) decides "same vertex"; this is the single most safety-critical predicate and the place snap rounding (dossier 04 Q2.5) earns its keep.
  4. **Read back the sub-faces.** After all M seams are inserted, trace every `next`-cycle. Classify each cycle outer-vs-hole by signed area (shoelace; exact for straight segments, the all-planar oracle). Build the containment tree (ray-left from each hole's leftmost vertex) and emit one sub-face per outer cycle, carrying its hole cycles. Discard any cycle whose interior lies OUTSIDE the face's original outer loop or INSIDE an original hole (these are the "void" cells the seam cut through, FINDINGS 73c: the middle leg of a chain that dips into a hole's void bounds nothing and must be dropped, which the arrangement does automatically because that cell is classified non-material).
- **Limitations.** Assumes transversal crossings; a seam tangent to a hole (touching without crossing) produces a degree-2 "vertex" that is not a real subdivision and must be detected and declined (Q3). Sub-tolerance sub-faces (a sliver between two near-coincident seams) are detected by an area-below-`etol`-squared test and declined.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the planar counterpart of dossier 64 Q1.1 to Q1.5 (the periodic banding) with holes added and the seam slit / apex removed. The "read back sub-faces by signed-area and containment tree" step is exactly dossier 04 Q2.2 Jiang-Bunke, reused. The material-vs-void classification by the original outer loop and original holes is what makes the seam's in-void legs vanish, resolving the 73c residual mechanism. **Claimed** as the recommended algorithm, validated against the stacked repro (Q5).

## Q2. Re-form the B-rep faces via Euler operators

### Q2.0 The bookkeeping target

The face arrives as ONE B-rep face F with V0 boundary vertices, E0 boundary edges, and 1 plus K loops (outer plus K holes). The arrangement adds the M seams' interior vertices and edges and partitions F into P sub-faces. The re-form must reach a B-rep with the same V, E (now including seam edges) and P faces, with every coedge radial-paired (the shell-closure invariant of dossier 47 that the stitch later checks). The arrangement gives the TARGET; the Euler sequence is how to reach it from F while keeping a valid B-rep at every step (so lineage and validity hold by construction, FINDINGS 73c section 1).

### Q2.1 The deterministic re-form sequence
- **Citation.** Mantyla, "An Introduction to Solid Modeling," 1988 (the GWB Euler-operator set `mev` / `mef` / `mekr` / `kemr` and the V minus E plus F minus L plus 2H equals 2C identity); Weiler, "Edge-Based Data Structures for Solid Modeling in Curved-Surface Environments," IEEE CG&A 5(1), 1985 (the radial-edge structure Keel uses); FINDINGS 73b section 3.1 (the landed `split_edge` T-junction repair) and 73c section 3.1 (the landed `mekr`-bridge plus `split_face`).
- **Method / content (the recommended Keel mechanism).** Drive the Euler ops directly off the arrangement, in three phases:
  1. **Vertex injection.** For every arrangement vertex that lies on F's existing boundary but is not yet a B-rep vertex (a T-junction point, a seam-on-hole-edge crossing), `split_edge` the carrying boundary edge there. Each `split_edge` adds 1 vertex and 1 edge (V plus 1, E plus 1, F unchanged, identity preserved). This generalizes 73b's single mid-edge split to ALL crossing vertices, applied once up front.
  2. **Hole bridging.** F has K holes (K inner loops). For each hole, the arrangement says which connected component of the material region will own it. Issue one `mekr` (make-edge-kill-ring) per hole: it adds a bridge edge from the outer loop (or the owning sub-region's boundary) to the hole loop, MERGING that inner loop into the outer loop and reducing the loop count by 1 (E plus 1, L minus 1; F unchanged). After K bridges the face is simply connected with one combined boundary, exactly as 73c does for its single hole (73c section 3.1: "`mekr(outer-end, P1)` MERGES the hole into the outer loop"). The bridge edge is chosen along a seam segment that the arrangement already routes from the outer region to the hole (so the bridge is a real material seam edge, not an arbitrary chord), which is why the seam-through-hole case needs no separate chord: the seam IS the bridge.
  3. **Face cutting.** Now issue one `split_face` (`mef`, make-edge-face) per arrangement edge that separates two DISTINCT sub-faces and whose two endpoints lie on the (now single, merged) boundary of the current face being cut. Each `mef` adds 1 edge and 1 face (E plus 1, F plus 1; identity preserved). Process the cuts in the order the arrangement's dual graph gives (any spanning order works because each cut's endpoints are guaranteed on a single loop after the bridges); after P minus 1 cuts the face is partitioned into P sub-faces. A lone seam-on-loop crossing (no hole, K equals 0) needs zero bridges and exactly one `mef`, which is bit-identical to the 73b landed `split_face` (subsumption, Q3). A lone seam-through-one-hole (K equals 1) needs one `mekr` then one `mef`, bit-identical to the 73c landed sequence (subsumption, Q3).
  - **Running V minus E plus F check.** Phase 1: each split_edge is (plus1 V, plus1 E), net 0. Phase 2: each mekr is (plus1 E, minus1 L), the loop-count term absorbing it. Phase 3: each mef is (plus1 E, plus1 F), net 0 in V minus E plus F minus L. The identity V minus E plus F minus L plus 2H equals 2C holds throughout (H, the through-holes / genus, is untouched: bridging an inner LOOP is not bridging a handle), so every intermediate state is a valid B-rep. This is the by-construction validity that the FINDINGS rely on.
- **Limitations.** Choosing the bridge segment and the cut order is a graph problem on the arrangement's dual; a pathological arrangement (a hole reachable from the outer region only THROUGH another hole) needs a careful bridge order, and a hole with no incident seam needs an arbitrary bridge chord (acceptable: it is a real edge that the stitch will radial-pair, but it adds a non-seam edge the later glue must reconcile, so prefer to leave un-crossed holes as inner loops and only bridge holes the seams actually cross). A sub-face whose boundary the arrangement leaves with an unpaired coedge is a detected failure -> decline, not a shipped body (dossier 47 invariant intact).
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the exact generalization the FINDINGS asked for: 73b's "split the boundary edge at the mid-edge junction" becomes Phase 1 applied to every crossing; 73c's "`mekr`-bridge the hole then `split_face`" becomes Phases 2 and 3 applied to K holes and the full cut set. The Euler ops are unchanged (no new operator), so the 2.24B-fuzz correctness program's coverage of `split_edge` / `mef` / `mekr` carries over. **Claimed** as the recommended sequence, validated by the V minus E plus F invariant and the Q5 oracle.

## Q3. Subsumption of the landed rungs, and the genuine-DECLINE boundary

### Q3.1 The overlay reproduces 73b and 73c exactly (non-regression)
- **Citation.** FINDINGS 73b (T-junction repair, regression tests `compound_operand_difference_assembles_at_exact_oracle`, oracle 920) and 73c (seam-crosses-hole, tests `seam_crosses_hole_assembles_at_exact_oracle`, oracles 989 and 1020).
- **Method / content (the subsumption argument, stated as two reductions).**
  - **73b reduction (K equals 0, one component, one seam-on-loop crossing).** A tool wall cut by two body faces gives one seam chain whose interior endpoint lands mid-span of a fresh boundary edge. In the overlay: the face has no hole (K equals 0); inserting the seam splits the carrying boundary edge at the T-point (Q1.1 step 1) and the seam becomes one edge with both endpoints on the single outer loop; reading back gives exactly two sub-faces. The re-form is Phase 1 (one `split_edge` at the T-point) plus zero bridges plus one `mef`. That `split_edge` plus `mef` pair is the SAME two operations 73b lands by hand. The overlay therefore produces the 73b result on the 73b input, bit-for-bit (the all-planar oracle 920 is unchanged).
  - **73c reduction (K equals 1, one component, seam dips through one hole).** A seam enters a hole on its first segment and exits on its last (73c's gated structure). In the overlay: the face has K equals 1; inserting the seam crosses the hole boundary at P1 and P2 (Q1.1 step 2), and the cell BETWEEN P1 and P2 is classified non-material (inside the original hole, Q1.1 step 4) so the seam's middle leg is dropped; the two material sub-chains are outer-to-P1 and P2-to-outer. The re-form is Phase 2 (one `mekr` bridging the hole into the outer loop along the outer-to-P1 sub-chain) plus Phase 3 (one `mef` along the P2-to-outer sub-chain). That `mekr` plus `mef` pair is the SAME sequence 73c lands by hand (73c section 3.1). The overlay produces the 73c result on the 73c input (oracles 989 and 1020 unchanged).
  - **The composition the rungs CANNOT do, the overlay CAN.** Seed 715's residual stacks BOTH: a seam that crosses a hole AND T-junctions a sibling seam on the same face (73c section 5). In the overlay this is K equals 1 with TWO seams inserted; their mutual crossing is one more degree-3 vertex, their hole crossings two more, and the read-back gives the correct sub-faces with no ordering choice. The hand-coded rungs decline it (73c's conservative first/last-segment gate excludes it, correctly, to stay WRONG-safe); the overlay resolves it. This is the whole point of the dossier.
- **Limitations.** Subsumption is an argument, not yet a proof; it must be discharged by RUNNING the 73b and 73c regression tests against the overlay implementation and asserting identical oracles (Q5). If the overlay's edge/vertex IDs differ from the hand-coded path, downstream lineage tests may need the overlay to be the sole path (retire the rungs) rather than a parallel one, to avoid two code paths producing different IDs for the same input.
- **Kernel relevance.** The overlay must be landed as a STRICT GENERALIZATION: same oracles on the simple inputs, plus the composition. The cleanest landing retires the 73b/73c special cases once the overlay passes their tests, leaving one imprint path. **Claimed**; the validation is the existing 73b/73c regression suite re-pointed at the overlay.

### Q3.2 Where the overlay genuinely declines (DECLINE-never-WRONG floor)
- **Citation.** Synthesis of dossier 04 Q2.3 (degeneracy-robust clipping), Q2.5 (snap rounding), and dossier 64 Q4 (the periodic DECLINE-forever set), specialized to the planar-with-holes case.
- **Method / content.** The overlay must DECLINE, never ship a wrong body, on:
  1. **Tangential / non-transversal crossing.** A seam that TOUCHES a hole or the outer loop without crossing (a common point, same tangent) does not subdivide; in tolerant f64 it is ambiguous whether material is on one side or both. Detect (the crossing vertex has even local degree with no alternation of inside/outside) and decline.
  2. **Self-touching or self-intersecting seam.** A single seam polyline that revisits a vertex creates a non-simple cycle whose material side is ambiguous; detect (a seam vertex of degree greater than 2 within the same seam) and decline (or, if cleanly splittable into simple sub-chains, split and proceed; conservative first cut: decline).
  3. **Sub-tolerance sliver sub-face.** An arrangement face of area below `etol`-squared cannot be reliably oriented or integrated; detect and decline the whole imprint rather than emit a degenerate face.
  4. **Coincident-collinear overlap.** A seam lying ALONG a hole edge or another seam (not crossing, overlapping) is the labeled-overlap degeneracy; conservative first cut: decline (the degeneracy-robust handling of dossier 04 Q2.3 is the eventual upgrade).
  These are exactly the planar analogs of dossier 64 Q4's apex-tangent / self-intersecting-wrap DECLINE-forever set. The floor: every input is either a watertight body whose all-planar mass equals its mesh exactly, or a clean decline. Never a wrong-positive.
- **Limitations.** The line between "cleanly splittable self-touch" and "genuine ambiguity" is itself a research item; the conservative first cut declines all self-touches, costing some recoverable cases to guarantee WRONG equals 0.
- **Kernel relevance.** This is the sacred-floor guard for the class, mirroring the 73b/73c battery tests (`*_battery_is_decline_never_wrong`). The overlay's new battery must assert the same: every result a clean decline or an exact-watertight body. **Claimed.**

## Q4. Production realization (OCCT, ACIS, SpaceClaim)

### Q4.1 OCCT: BOPAlgo_BuilderFace plus BOPAlgo_WireSplitter (the direct production analog)
- **Citation.** Open CASCADE Technology, "Boolean Operations" user guide and `BOPAlgo_BuilderFace` / `BOPAlgo_WireSplitter` / `BOPAlgo_Tools::WiresToFaces` reference; OCCT GitHub wiki "boolean_operations." https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/wiki/boolean_operations ; https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__modeling_algos.html
- **Method / content.** OCCT's boolean builds the split faces of an argument face F in ONE pass, exactly the overlay this dossier recommends, expressed in OCCT's vocabulary: (1) collect all edges (images) of F's original boundary; (2) collect all SECTION edges SEk for F (the intersection curves, Keel's seams); (3) hand the pooled edge set to `BOPAlgo_BuilderFace`, which builds ALL split faces Fi1..FiN of F at once. Inside, `BOPAlgo_WireSplitter` "implements the algorithm to build loops from a set of edges ES and face F, tracing paths in 2D along the edges using conditions of connectivity through vertices and minimal clockwise angle between adjacent edges" (the angular-order half-edge walk of Q1, in the face's 2D parametric space). Holes are classified afterward: `BOPTools_AlgoTools::IsHole` classifies a wire relative to a face, `IntTools_Tools::ClassifyPointByFace` classifies a point relative to a face, and `BOPAlgo_Tools::WiresToFaces` builds planar faces from planar wires where "wires located in the same plane and completely included into other wires will create holes in the faces built from outer wires" (the containment-tree hole attachment of Q1.1 step 4). So OCCT's split-by-multiple-section-edges IS a 2D arrangement (wire splitter) plus a hole-classification (IsHole / point-in-face) pass, built on the pooled boundary-plus-section edge set, with NO per-section-edge ordering. This is the production precedent for the recommended Keel design, point for point.
- **Limitations.** OCCT operates in exact-ish ParaCurve space with its own fuzzy tolerance (the `Fuzzy Value`); its wire splitter has documented failures on near-tangent and sub-tolerance section edges (the same DECLINE class as Q3.2). It is C++ and not directly reusable from Rust, but the ALGORITHM (pooled edges -> wire splitter by minimal angle -> hole classification) is exactly what to port.
- **Kernel relevance.** The single strongest "this is how production does it" confirmation: do NOT imprint section edges one at a time; pool them with the boundary edges and run one wire-splitter (arrangement) plus hole-classification pass. Keel's `imprint_open_chain` per-chain loop is the anti-pattern OCCT explicitly avoids. **Verified** against the OCCT sources.

### Q4.2 ShapeUpgrade and the imprint precedent
- **Citation.** OCCT `ShapeUpgrade_UnifySameDomain` reference; OCCT `BRepFeat` (feature operations); ACIS / SpaceClaim imprint (US8330775B2, below). https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/refman/html/class_shape_upgrade___unify_same_domain.html
- **Method / content.** OCCT's imprint of one shape onto another (used by BRepFeat and by general fuse-then-split workflows) runs the same section-edge computation then the same BuilderFace re-make; `ShapeUpgrade_UnifySameDomain` is the INVERSE cleanup (merge faces / edges that lie on coincident geometry after an over-split), which is relevant to Keel as the post-pass that would remove a spurious seam edge if the overlay over-subdivides. ACIS exposes `api_imprint` (imprint one body's faces onto another), which internally intersects, imprints the section curves, and re-makes faces, the same three-step shape.
- **Limitations.** UnifySameDomain has documented bugs producing invalid shapes on some inputs (OCCT tracker 0029504), a reminder that the merge-back pass is itself non-trivial; Keel should prefer to NOT over-split (emit only seam edges that separate distinct sub-faces, Q2.1 phase 3) rather than over-split then merge.

### Q4.3 ACIS / SpaceClaim: imprint-and-split as a claimed method

### Q4.4 The arrangement primitive they share, and its classical roots
- **Citation.** Guibas, Stolfi, "Primitives for the manipulation of general subdivisions and the computation of Voronoi diagrams," ACM TOG 4(2), 1985 (the quad-edge, the dual-aware subdivision primitive); Weiler, "Polygon comparison using a graph representation," SIGGRAPH 1980 (the radial-edge boolean ancestor, dossier 04 Q2.3); Jiang, Bunke, "An optimal algorithm for extracting the regions of a plane graph," Pattern Recognition Letters 14(7), 1993 (dossier 04 Q2.2). https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/282918.282923
- **Method / content.** Every production imprint-and-split rests on the same classical primitive: a planar-subdivision data structure (DCEL / quad-edge) into which curves are inserted, with faces extracted by an angular half-edge walk. Guibas-Stolfi's quad-edge is the dual-aware variant (it stores a subdivision and its dual together) and supplies the algebra (`splice`) that re-links half-edges at a vertex, which is the formal version of Q1's "re-link in angular order." Weiler-1980 is the boolean-specific ancestor that became radial-edge B-rep traversal (Keel's own representation). These are all pre-2000, no live patent.
- **Limitations.** Quad-edge is for MANIFOLD subdivisions; a self-intersecting seam wanting non-manifold local structure needs the radial-edge generalization Keel already has, not bare quad-edge. The classical algorithms assume exact predicates; tolerant f64 needs the snapping of dossier 04 Q2.5.
- **Kernel relevance.** Establishes the prior-art floor (the primitive is free) and names the `splice` operation as the formal model for Keel's angular re-link. **Verified.**

## ADOPT / AVOID / INVESTIGATE

**ADOPT.**
1. **One DCEL overlay per face, not per chain.** Pool the face's outer loop, K hole loops, and M seam polylines; insert the seams into the face's own DCEL by zone insertion (de Berg ch. 2; CGAL `insert`); read sub-faces back by signed-area plus containment tree (Jiang-Bunke, dossier 04 Q2.2). **AVOID.**
1. **Per-chain `imprint_open_chain` extended by hand to compose** (the dead end 73c proved: the rungs do not compose, a permissive composition MANGLED seed 715).
