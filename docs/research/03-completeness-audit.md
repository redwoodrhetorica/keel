# Keel Research Corpus: Completeness and Reliability Audit

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Auditor role:** adversarial reviewer. The job here is to find what is wrong, thin, contradictory, unvalidated, or missing, not to praise. Where the corpus is genuinely strong it is noted briefly, but problems lead.
**Scope read:** both index files in full; all four syntheses (`00-synthesis`, `00-synthesis-v2`, `01-synthesis-wave2`, `02-synthesis-wave3`); the architecture spec; and roughly 14 dossiers deep-read or grep-verified (kernel 01, 02, 06, 08, 09, 11, 13, 14, 18, 23, 25, 29, 35, 36, 37; nurbs 04; wave-1 tracks a, d, e). Citations below use file numbers.

## 1. Internal contradictions

**1.1 The D3 manifold-first reversal is reconciled in the spec and syntheses but left contradictory inside the wave-1 dossier, which was never amended.** The spec D3 and `01-synthesis-wave2` V1 adopt non-manifold/PES/region-first from the first commit. But `e-kernel-architecture.md` (the wave-1 track-E dossier) still reads, verbatim and unretracted:
- line 84: arena/handle coedge hierarchy is "the mainstream, validated choice for a **manifold-first** kernel";
- line 101 (INVESTIGATE verdict): "the chosen arena/handle + coedge hierarchy + Euler operators **is correct for Keel's stated manifold-first** ... roadmap ... build the manifold coedge core now."

So a reader who opens track E is told manifold-first is the verdict, while the spec and three later syntheses say the opposite. The reconciliation lives only in the *forward-pointing* documents (synthesis v2 delta 11 explicitly flags that "the stronger form governs"). The *source* dossier was never annotated with a superseded-by note. This is the exact failure mode the audit was asked to check: the decision is consistent in the binding spec, but the corpus still contains an unmarked contradictory recommendation. Cost is low (track E is wave-1 and self-describes as superseded depth) but a future reader mining track E for the topology rationale will get the wrong verdict. **Fix: add a one-line superseded-by header to `e-kernel-architecture.md` ADOPT/INVESTIGATE topology bullets pointing at D3/V1.**

**1.2 "One solver behind every query" (D6) is contradicted by the SSI architecture it must coexist with.** D6 and synthesis delta 3 state a single Bernstein-basis Projected-Polyhedron (PP) subdivision solver "backs every polynomial-system query ... curve-curve and curve-surface intersection, surface-surface start points, closest-point projection, interrogation." But `nurbs/04` line 265 states plainly that PP is *slow* for the underconstrained system (m < n), "the SSI/marching case ... parametric tracing is preferred," and the spec's own M5 (line 162) routes SSI through predictor-corrector marching with Krawczyk steps, **not** the central solver. So the central solver backs *start-point finding* for SSI, not SSI itself. The headline "one solver behind every query" overstates: the single most important and hardest query type (surface-surface intersection, the M5/M7 critical path) is served by a *separate* marching engine that merely consumes seeds from the central solver. This is fine architecturally but the framing is misleading and could drive a designer to under-resource the marching engine because they believe the central solver covers it.

**1.3 Two different "one engine" claims share a slogan but are unrelated engines, and the corpus never says so.** Finding nurbs/03 and kernel/32 both assert "one linear-system engine covers fitting, fairing, smoothing" (the penalized least-squares `(N^T W N + lambda K)` engine), while finding B / D6 asserts "one Bernstein subdivision solver behind every query." These are two distinct solvers (a sparse linear/KKT factorization vs a polynomial-root subdivision search). The corpus repeatedly uses the rhetorical shape "subsystem X collapses to one engine," which is persuasive but creates an impression of more consolidation than exists: there are at least **three** "one engine" claims (the subdivision solver, the penalized-LS surfacing/fitting solver, the 2D arrangement+winding engine), plus the tweak/reintersect core, plus the envelope/MAT core. That is five "one engine" cores, not one. No document tallies them or shows they compose. The consolidation thesis is real but oversold by repetition of the slogan.

**1.4 Tessellation tolerance formula is stated two ways.** Synthesis v2 delta 15 writes the sag bound as `L <= sqrt(8 R delta)`; the wave-2 A4 entry writes the same; the spec D-tess paraphrases. This is internally consistent but is one of the formulas the corpus itself flags as reconstructed (see section 6); it should be primary-source checked because it is a load-bearing per-edge discretization constant, not because two dossiers disagree.

No other hard contradictions were found between dossiers. The D9/lineage, determinism, patent, and predicate-tier decisions are stated consistently across all four syntheses and the spec.

## 2. Load-bearing but unvalidated assumptions

**2.1 Per-entity tolerance propagation is the day-one primary model, yet the corpus admits the propagation algorithms do not exist in the open literature.** This is the single most serious unvalidated dependency. Spec D2 makes "per-entity local tolerances **with propagation** ... the primary tolerance model from day one." But:
- `a-robust-numerics.md` finding 6: "Tolerance-propagation theory is underspecified in the open literature. Commercial kernels implement local tolerance propagation but the algorithms are proprietary; the public literature gives the concept but not a complete, citable recipe, **so Keel will be doing original engineering here**."
- `d-booleans-tolerant.md` finding 2: "Tolerance propagation under chained booleans is **poorly characterized** ... there is **no widely accepted theory** bounding that growth or specifying when re-intersection/healing is required."

So the spec leans hard on a mechanism the research says is unsolved and proprietary, while presenting it as a settled day-one decision. The *concept* (tolerant edges/vertices carrying a stored tolerance) is well sourced; the *propagation algorithm* (how tolerances grow and when they force re-intersection) is original research Keel has not done and cannot cite. This is correctly flagged inside two wave-1 dossiers but the flag did not propagate to the spec's confidence level: D2 reads as decided, not as "decided in principle, algorithm to be invented." This is the highest-risk item in the corpus because the tolerance model is explicitly called "unfixable late" in the risk register, and it rests on an admitted literature gap.

**2.2 The "exact-topology-plus-tolerant-geometry hybrid that no kernel ships" is asserted achievable at interactive speed but never quantified.** This is the mission's differentiation thesis. The supporting dossier (kernel/11) is honest about the wall: separation bounds for degree-8 quadric-intersection comparisons over 53-bit inputs "means a fallback at **hundreds of bits**" (line 49-50), and "CAD is degeneracy-dense BY DESIGN ... so the 100-1000x fallback fires on purpose-built configurations" (wave-2 addendum line 94). The corpus states the fallback is hot, then asserts the tier routing "keeps those fallbacks low-degree" so it stays affordable. That mitigation is asserted, never measured. There is no benchmark, no worked latency budget, no demonstration that a degeneracy-dense real CAD model (the exact case the kernel must handle) stays interactive when the exact fallback fires on every intentional tangency. The reason "no kernel ships this" may be that the incumbents measured it and chose tolerant modeling on purpose. The corpus treats incumbent tolerant modeling as a *failure of nerve*; it is at least as likely to be a *measured performance decision*. This thesis needs a prototype microbenchmark before it is load-bearing in code.

**2.3 The degree-tiered predicate affordability claim rests on a literature the corpus itself calls "an order of magnitude thinner."** kernel/11 line 9 and the wave-2 addendum say the curved-exact-predicate literature is concentrated in "a small community ... over roughly 1995 to 2015," is order-of-magnitude thinner than the linear case, and that several of its formulas (the BFMSS recursion, the QI singular-case rationality) were reconstructed from abstracts. The tier table in D2 (Tier 1 exact conics, Tier 2 exact-when-cheap quadrics) is built on EXACUS/QI/CGAL-AK papers that the agents could not always fetch in full. "Conics are exactness-affordable" is plausible and probably true, but it is a design-load-bearing claim resting on thin, partly-reconstructed sourcing. It has not been validated by implementing even one conic-conic exact comparison and timing it.

**2.4 "The corpus is the robustness moat an OSS project can out-build" is an unvalidated organizational bet, not a technical finding.** kernel/29 (lines 332, 412) is candid that robustness "is **not primarily an algorithm, it is an organizational asset**" and the moat depends on "frictionless public contribution of failing models ... **given users and discipline**." The whole testing strategy (pass-rate over ABC, golden corpus, fTetWild methodology) inherits this bet. The unvalidated assumption: that an OSS B-rep kernel will attract enough users submitting enough dirty models to rival C3D's 500k. That is a community-growth assumption with no evidence behind it; most OSS geometry projects never reach that scale. If the moat is "users contributing models" and the users do not come, the differentiation thesis (robustness on dirty data) has no engine. This is the project's biggest non-technical risk and it is stated as a strength.

**2.5 "Determinism bitwise-identical across platforms" is asserted reachable but the corpus flags WASM and libm as holes it has not closed.** D9 promises cross-platform bit-identity. kernel/37 line (interval section) and kernel/14 admit: Rust rounding-mode control is "awkward (LLVM assumes round-to-nearest, **WASM lacks the instruction**)," and "system libm is the **largest cross-platform hole**." The proposed fix (pure-Rust libm, ULP-widened intervals) is a plan, not a validated result. Bit-identity across x86/ARM/WASM for a full NURBS evaluation pipeline has not been demonstrated anywhere in the corpus; it is promised. Given D9 is called "the single most load-bearing non-geometric decision," the gap between promise and demonstration matters.

## 3. Thin or shallow coverage

**3.1 STEP/AP242 import is named as the must-implement interchange but covered only as a healing-pipeline afterthought.** D9 says "STEP AP242 is the must-implement neutral interchange." The actual STEP *parsing/mapping* problem (the AP242 EXPRESS schema is enormous; entity coverage decisions, the geometry-vs-PMI-vs-assembly split, the well-known ambiguities in trimmed-curve and pcurve representation on import) gets essentially no dedicated dossier. kernel/13 covers *healing* the geometry after import; the STEP schema-mapping layer itself, which is months of work and a notorious source of interop bugs, is name-dropped. For a kernel whose interop value depends on STEP, this is under-researched relative to its importance.

**3.2 Midsurfacing is honestly flagged as unsolved, but it is simultaneously load-bearing for the non-manifold justification.** The single most concrete commercial argument for non-manifold-from-day-one (kernel/02 line 227, repeated in wave-2 V1) is "CAE midsurface-in-solid idealization." Yet the midsurface *operation* is repeatedly called "commercially unsolved, ship with explicit failure reporting" (finding 15, watch list). So the headline justification for the corpus's biggest architecture reversal (non-manifold core) is a feature the corpus admits cannot be reliably produced. The non-manifold decision may still be right (zone modeling, sheet-in-solid are real), but leaning the justification on midsurfacing is leaning on an admitted non-deliverable.

**3.3 The 2D constraint solver boundary is decided ("above the kernel") but the in-kernel 2D arrangement engine that must replace its geometric half is thin on robustness specifics.** kernel/04 cleanly draws the boundary, but the in-kernel arrangement+winding engine (A3) that does region extraction, curved planar booleans, and offset cleanup is the foundation of M4 and gets one synthesis paragraph. Its hardest part (robust curved-segment arrangement under tolerance, where kernel/06 line 279 warns "a single inconsistent decision can break QI propagation globally") is acknowledged but not researched to implementation depth. This is M4 critical-path and thinner than M5/M6.

**3.4 Attribute-propagation policy (copy/split/merge/delete per attribute type) is mentioned in five places but specified nowhere.** kernel/08 (lines 339, 349, 417) and kernel/20 repeatedly say the attribute system needs split/merge/delete callbacks and a per-type propagation policy, and D9 lists "typed versioned attribute system." But no dossier defines *what the propagation rules actually are* for the common attribute kinds (a color, a PMI tolerance, a user tag, a persistent name). This is the same gap as 2.1 (propagation) in a different subsystem: the hook is specified, the policy is not.

Adequately covered for design (not thin): topology hierarchy (01), NURBS evaluation/algorithms (nurbs 01-02), SSI architecture (nurbs/04, c), determinism mechanics (14), patent landscape (18), fitting (23), tessellation contract (05), performance/parallelism (36), numerical substrate (37). These are genuinely implementation-ready.

## 4. Genuine blind spots / missing topics

This is the highest-value section. The two capability audits (Parasolid file 25, XNURBS file 31) catalog *modeling operations*. They are blind to everything that is not a modeling operation. The whole lifecycle outside "what the kernel computes" is under-addressed.

**4.1 Corpus licensing and redistribution rights for ABC / Thingi10K / Fusion 360 Gallery.** The entire testing and robustness strategy depends on running over ABC (1M B-reps) and Thingi10K and publishing pass rates. No dossier checks whether those datasets' licenses permit redistribution, derivative golden cases, or commercial-adjacent use. Thingi10K aggregates Thingiverse models under heterogeneous and sometimes non-commercial licenses; ABC has its own terms. The robustness moat (2.4) could be legally constrained at its foundation and nobody looked. This is a concrete, checkable gap the patent dossier (18) did not cover because it is data licensing, not algorithm patents.

**4.2 Public API evolution policy: SemVer, deprecation, breaking-change discipline for a published crate.** The spec promises `cargo add keel`, a stable facade, and "decades of file compatibility." File *format* compatibility is researched (14). The *API* compatibility story (Rust SemVer hazards, how an opaque-handle facade evolves without breaking downstream crates, deprecation cadence, the 1.0 commitment) is absent. For an OSS library whose adoption thesis is `cargo add`, API stability policy is as load-bearing as file format and is unaddressed.

**4.3 Project governance, contribution, and maintainer-bus-factor.** The robustness moat is "frictionless public contribution of failing models." There is no research on *how* contribution works: CLA vs DCO (which interacts with the patent-grant licensing in D10), triage workflow for submitted models, who has commit rights, how a single-developer (here an AI across sessions) project sustains a community. The corpus treats "users will contribute" as mechanism; the mechanism is unbuilt and unresearched.

**4.4 Numerical-precision security / denial-of-service via crafted input.** Importers are flagged as an adversarial boundary for *memory* safety (validate counts before allocating). But a crafted NURBS surface that forces the exact predicate fallback to hundreds of bits on every query (2.2) is a *compute* DoS: a malicious or pathological STEP file could make a boolean run for minutes. The degeneracy-dense-by-design observation is treated as a correctness concern; its weaponization as a resource-exhaustion vector is not considered. A kernel embedded in a server (the WASM/cloud demo path) needs a compute budget / timeout / escalation cap, which nothing specifies.

**4.5 Floating-point exception, signal, and panic-at-the-FFI-boundary behavior.** The spec says library code never panics and returns `Result`. But the future C ABI and PyO3 layers must convert Rust panics (which *will* occur in dependencies or arithmetic edge cases despite best efforts) into something safe across an FFI boundary; an unwinding panic across `extern "C"` is undefined behavior. No dossier addresses unwind-safety at the boundary, NaN/Inf trapping policy, or what happens when a `f64` operation produces a NaN mid-boolean. "Panics are bugs" is a policy, not a containment mechanism.

**4.6 Versioned regression-result storage and bisection at scale.** The golden corpus grows to (aspirationally) hundreds of thousands of models. Running the full ABC corpus per commit is computationally infeasible; nothing addresses tiered CI (smoke vs nightly vs release), how to bisect a robustness regression across a 1M-model corpus, or storage/cost of per-release pass-rate artifacts. The methodology (fTetWild pass-rate) is cited; the engineering of running it continuously at OSS-budget scale is not.

**4.7 Internationalization / units / orientation convention as an explicit kernel contract.** kernel/37 normalizes to a unit box internally (good) and kernel/16 cites a units-precision interop source, but the kernel's *external* contract (is the API unitless? right-handed? what is the model-box normalization's interaction with very-large assembly coordinates and the "never bake world offsets" rule?) is scattered and never stated as a single contract. For a kernel meant to interoperate, the unit/coordinate contract is a first-class API decision that no dossier owns.

**4.8 Support and diagnostics surface: error-message quality, repro-bundle export, telemetry.** Call journaling (record/replay) is specified as a debugging tool. But the user-facing support story (when a boolean fails, what does the caller get beyond a `KernelError` enum; can they export a minimal repro bundle; is there a structured fault report a downstream app can show) is only partially covered by the "partial-success fault tags" idea (wave-2 A8). For a kernel whose differentiation is graceful degradation, the *reporting* surface of that degradation deserves more than it got.

## 5. Over-research / diminishing returns

**5.1 The post-M7 long tail is researched to implementation depth while M4-M7 (the actual roadmap) has thinner spots.** Fillet/chamfer (28), free-form deformation (27), n-sided transfinite surfacing (26), variational surfacing (32), constrained surfacing continuity (33), sheet metal (20), kinematics (21), GD&T/PMI (17), feature recognition (15), metrology fitting (23), canonical recovery (24), model diff (22) are all full dossiers with 20-34 sources each. Every one of these is post-M7, i.e. *after* the proof milestone. Meanwhile the M4 2D arrangement robustness (3.3) and the STEP import schema mapping (3.1), both on the path to a shippable kernel, are thinner. The corpus has front-loaded research on the long tail. For a project at M7b whose proof milestone is "booleans that survive fuzzing," dossiers 26-28 and 31-33 (surfacing) and 20-21 (sheet metal/kinematics) are effort the project cannot use for one to two years. This is the clearest over-research signal: roughly a third of the kernel dossiers describe features beyond the proof milestone.

**5.2 Duplicated coverage of the penalized-least-squares "one engine" across four dossiers.** nurbs/03, kernel/32, kernel/33, and kernel/23 all independently arrive at and re-derive the `(N^T W N + lambda K)` fitting/fairing/variational unification. The convergence is reassuring once; four times is duplication. Synthesis v2 delta 16 and finding 36/37 restate it again. The point was made; the repetition is diminishing returns.

**5.3 The dirty-geometry / imperfect-B-rep pair (29, 30) and the test-corpus / verification pair (34, 35) overlap heavily.** Virtual topology, winding-number floors, fTetWild, epsilon-solidity, and corpus-as-moat appear in both members of each pair and again in the syntheses. The doctrine is sound and important, but it is now stated four-plus times. Further research into dirty-geometry doctrine is unlikely to add anything the corpus does not already have.

**Verdict for section 5:** the corpus has clearly passed the point of diminishing returns on (a) the consolidation/one-engine thesis, (b) dirty-geometry doctrine, and (c) post-M7 surfacing and adjacent-subsystem features. Further dossiers in these areas would be wasteful.

## 6. Citation and reliability concerns

Both index files carry explicit verification caveats (kernel index lines 126-128; nurbs index lines 30-37), which is good practice. The honest self-assessment is itself a quality signal. But it localizes the risk loosely. The high-stakes claims that most need primary-source verification *before code* are:

**6.1 The curved-predicate separation-bound and QI rationality claims (kernel/11).** The BFMSS recursion rules (line 47-48) and the claim that QI yields a rational parameterization "whenever singular, one square root in the smooth case" (D2 Tier 2) are reconstructed from a thin, partly-paywalled literature. These directly drive the predicate-tier routing that *is the differentiation thesis*. If the QI singular-case rationality claim is wrong or narrower than stated, Tier 2 collapses. **Verify against Dupont-Lazard-Lazard-Petitjean primary before implementing M5 quadric SSI.**

**6.2 Parasolid/ACIS session and tolerance mechanics (kernel/07, and the propagation claims in a/d).** The index explicitly flags file 07 session mechanics as reconstructed. Since tolerance *propagation* is admitted to be proprietary and uncited (2.1), and the session/rollback/partition design (D9) is built partly on reconstructed Parasolid behavior, the entire D9 + tolerance edifice rests on secondary sourcing about how Parasolid actually works. This is unavoidable (the real algorithms are trade secrets) but it means D9's "this is how Parasolid does it, so we will too" confidence is partly inferred, not verified.

**6.3 Reconstructed formulas the corpus itself lists:** the tessellation sag bound (1.4), Piegl-Tiller algorithm numbers (nurbs index line 35), and "formulas in nurbs/01 should be checked against The NURBS Book before implementation" (nurbs index line 37). These are low-stakes individually (well-known results, easy to verify) but they are in the M1-M3 critical path and should be checked first because they are cheap to check and early to need.

**Overall reliability posture:** the citation *metadata* (author/title/venue/year) is verified throughout; the *mechanism detail* is the soft spot, concentrated exactly where the literature is paywalled (curved predicates, commercial kernel internals). The corpus is honest about this. The residual risk is that the *differentiation thesis itself* (exact curved topology) rests on the thinnest, most-reconstructed dossier (11). That is an uncomfortable concentration of risk on the load-bearing claim.

## 7. Prioritized verdict

Ranked by importance (impact x likelihood-of-being-wrong), most urgent first:

1. **Tolerance propagation is the primary day-one model but the algorithm is admitted-proprietary and uncited (2.1).** This is unfixable-late by the spec's own risk register. Before M3 freezes the tolerant data model, do the *original engineering* the corpus says is required: a written, tested propagation specification with worked growth bounds under chained booleans, even a conservative one. Do not let M3 freeze on a TODO.

2. **The differentiation thesis (exact curved topology at interactive speed) is unvalidated and rests on the thinnest dossier (2.2, 2.3, 6.1).** Prototype one conic-conic and one quadric-quadric exact predicate, run it on a deliberately degeneracy-dense input, and measure the fallback cost. If it is not interactive, the mission statement needs revision *now*, not at M5.

3. **Verify the QI rationality and BFMSS bounds against primary sources before M5 (6.1).** Cheap relative to the cost of building M5 on a wrong claim.

4. **Close the lifecycle blind spots that gate shipping, not just computing (4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 4.5):** dataset redistribution rights, API SemVer policy, compute-DoS budget/timeout, and FFI unwind-safety. None require new geometry research; all are checkable in days and all can sink the project if discovered late.

5. **Annotate the stale manifold-first contradiction in track E (1.1)** and add a "superseded-by" note. Trivial; removes a live trap for future readers.

6. **Reframe the "one solver / one engine" claims honestly (1.2, 1.3):** there are ~5 cores, SSI marching is separate from the central solver, and that is fine. Misframing risks under-resourcing the marching engine.

7. **Acknowledge the robustness-moat community bet as a risk, not a strength (2.4, 4.3).** Add governance/contribution mechanics to the plan.

### Stop or continue?

**Stop the broad research phase. Do not commission more dossiers.** The corpus is past diminishing returns on consolidation doctrine, dirty-geometry doctrine, and post-M7 features (section 5: roughly a third of the kernel dossiers cover beyond-proof-milestone work). The remaining gaps are not *literature-review* gaps; they are *original-engineering and validation* gaps (tolerance propagation, the curved-exactness performance prototype, lifecycle/legal checks). Those are closed by writing specifications and running benchmarks, not by reading more papers. The one narrow exception worth a *targeted* (not broad) effort is STEP/AP242 import schema mapping (3.1), which is a real literature-and-engineering gap on the shippable path and is currently thin.

The corpus is, for what it covers, genuinely strong and unusually honest about its own limits. Its weakness is not depth but *calibration*: several load-bearing claims are presented at higher confidence than their sourcing supports, and the effort was distributed toward the long tail rather than the proof-milestone critical path and the non-geometry lifecycle. The next move is validation and original engineering on the top three items, not more reading.
