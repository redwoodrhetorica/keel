# Track C: Surface-Surface Intersection (SSI) — Literature Review and Design Impact for Keel

Status: research deliverable, 2026-06-07. Scope: surface-surface intersection for a Parasolid-class B-rep kernel on tolerant f64, with a roadmap toward robust booleans on NURBS-bounded solids.

## 1. Executive Summary

Surface-surface intersection is the correctly identified long pole of a B-rep kernel. Every boolean, every imprint, every trim, and every shell operation ultimately reduces to computing the intersection curve(s) between two faces and getting both the geometry and the topology right. The geometry problem (locate points on the intersection to tolerance) is largely solved by marching with predictor-corrector and validated refinement. The topology problem (how many curve components exist, whether any are small closed loops, where curves are singular or tangent, and whether two faces overlap) is the hard, schedule-dominating part, and it is where most production failures and almost all of the research effort live.

The literature converges on a layered architecture. First, special-case the analytic-analytic pairs with closed forms or near-closed forms: plane/plane, plane/quadric, and quadric/quadric all have exact or near-exact parameterizations (Levin's pencil method, made robust and exact by Dupont, Lazard, Lazard, and Petitjean). Second, for any pair involving a freeform (NURBS) surface, use a topology-first pipeline: detect the global structure (loops, turning points, singular points) using subdivision plus algebraic or Gauss-map criteria before any tracing, then march each component with step control and validated correctors, then certify. The canonical reference architecture is Grandine and Klein (Boeing, 1997): resolve topology via a Projected-Polyhedron-style subdivision, then trace with a differential-algebraic ODE integrator. The 2023 to 2026 work from Yang, Jia, and Yan (ICT-CAS) is the current state of the art and is the single most important body of work for Keel to track: it provides a practical topology-guaranteed B-spline SSI that beats SISL, OCCT, and ACIS on near-critical configurations, with follow-on papers specifically on error-controlled tracing, small-loop and tangent handling, and overlap-region extraction.

The honest unsolved core is the same one Patrikalakis and Maekawa flagged in the 1990s: tangential and near-tangential intersections, coincident or partially overlapping surfaces, and tiny loops near critical relative positions. These are exactly the cases booleans on touching faces produce, and no method is simultaneously fast, fully general, and provably complete on f64. Keel should adopt a topology-first marching kernel, special-case analytics, certify segments with the Krawczyk operator, and explicitly engineer the tangential/overlap path as a first-class subsystem rather than an afterthought.

## 2. Annotated Key References

**Patrikalakis, Maekawa, Cho. _Shape Interrogation for Computer Aided Design and Manufacturing._ Springer, 2002; free hyperbook at web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/.** The foundational text. Chapter 5 lays out the four-way taxonomy of SSI methods: lattice/grid evaluation (sample one surface, intersect contours, cheap but misses small features), analytic (closed form, only for low-degree special cases), subdivision (recursive bounding, robust isolation but slow and weak on tangencies), and marching (predictor-corrector tracing, accurate and fast given correct starting points but prone to branch jumping and loop misses). It also formalizes the polynomial-system formulations and the Interval Projected Polyhedron solver. Takeaway: this is the shared vocabulary and the default mental model; Keel's design language should map directly onto it.

**Sherbrooke, Patrikalakis. "Computation of the solutions of nonlinear polynomial systems." CAGD 10(5), 1993.** Introduces the Interval Projected Polyhedron (IPP) algorithm: recast shape-interrogation root finding as repeated convex-hull computation on Bernstein coefficients, with rounded interval arithmetic for validated bounds. URL: web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node42.html. Takeaway: IPP is the workhorse for starting-point and characteristic-point computation; it is simple to implement (subdivide Bernstein form, take 2D convex hulls) and gives validated enclosures, which is exactly what a tolerant kernel needs.

**Grandine, Klein. "A new approach to the surface intersection problem." CAGD 14(2), 1997, 111-134.** The production-grade reference architecture, developed at Boeing. Two phases: (1) determine the topology of all intersection contours, including closed loops, by deciding for every boundary and turning point whether it begins or ends a contour, using a PP-style subdivision; (2) trace each contour by numerically integrating a differential-algebraic equation (DAE) system. Takeaway: topology-first then march is the design Keel should copy. Its known weakness (some critical-case decision criteria are inapplicable) was later patched.

**Mukundan, Patrikalakis et al.; and "Classification and resolution of critical cases in Grandine and Klein's topology determination using a perturbation method." CAGD 25(7), 2008.** Catalogs the critical cases where Grandine-Klein's turning-point logic fails and resolves them by controlled perturbation. URL: sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167839608000162. Takeaway: even the best topology-resolution scheme has a residual set of degenerate cases; budget for a perturbation/fallback layer.

**Sederberg, Meyers. "Loop detection in surface patch intersections." CAGD 5(2), 1988.** The original loop-detection criterion: if the normal cones (Gauss-map bounds) of two patches are separable, the patches cannot intersect in a closed loop, so subdivision can stop. Also the collinear-normal criterion: a loop implies a line perpendicular to both surfaces. Takeaway: loop detection is the gate that makes marching safe (a marcher that starts only on boundaries will silently miss an interior loop). Implement a normal-cone test.

**Hohmeyer. "A surface intersection algorithm based on loop detection." Int. J. Comput. Geom. Appl., 1991; and UC Berkeley TR "Robust and Efficient Surface Intersection for Solid Modeling," 1992.** Strengthens Sederberg-Meyers by bounding the Gauss maps with pseudo-normal patches and reducing the separability test to linear programming. URL: www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1992/6140.html. Takeaway: the LP-based Gauss-map separation is tighter than cones and is the practical loop-detection method; note the documented caveat that it works well mainly when surfaces are relatively flat, so deep subdivision is still needed for high-curvature faces.

**Krishnan, Manocha. "An efficient surface intersection algorithm based on lower-dimensional formulation." ACM TOG 16(1), 1997, 74-106 (system: BOOLE).** Combines marching with an algebraic (resultant/matrix) formulation: represents the intersection curve by a matrix, computes start points on every component including closed loops, detects singularities, and finds all branches at a singularity. Reports an order-of-magnitude speedup over prior robust algorithms. URL: dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/237748.237751. Takeaway: the hybrid symbolic-numeric approach is the most reliable way to get all start points and to resolve branches at singular points, but it pulls in algebraic machinery (resultants, eigenvalue methods) that is heavy to build and maintain.

**Ye, Maekawa. "Differential geometry of intersection curves of two surfaces." CAGD 16(8), 1999.** Derives tangent, curvature, binormal, torsion, and higher derivatives of the intersection curve for both transversal and tangential intersections. URL: sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167839699000187. Takeaway: this is the math the marcher's predictor needs; crucially it gives the tangential-case formulas where the naive cross-product-of-normals tangent degenerates to zero.

**Maekawa, Patrikalakis et al. "Tracing of self-intersection curves" and singularity classification (hyperbook ch. 11; CAGD papers on offset self-intersection).** Validated-ODE tracing of intersection and self-intersection curves using Runge-Kutta-Gill plus interval correction, with a classification of singular points (isolated points, cusps, self-intersections). Takeaway: self-intersection and offset singularity handling reuse the same tracer; build one validated tracer and apply it to SSI, self-intersection, and offset trimming.

**Levin. "A parametric algorithm for drawing pictures of solid objects composed of quadric surfaces." CACM 19(10), 1976; and Levin, CAGD 1979.** The classical quadric-quadric intersection via the pencil of quadrics, reducing QSIC to finding a ruled quadric in the pencil. Takeaway: conceptually the right idea for analytics, but numerically fragile in floating point (can give topologically wrong results or fail), so use the modern exact successor below rather than Levin directly.

**Dupont, Lazard, Lazard, Petitjean. "Near-optimal parameterization of the intersection of quadrics" (parts I-III), J. Symbolic Computation 43, 2008; and "Intersecting quadrics: an efficient and exact implementation," 2003.** The first exact, complete, near-optimal rational parameterization of the intersection of two arbitrary quadrics with rational coefficients, with a robust C++ implementation (QI library). Minimizes the number of square roots in the output and is topologically correct. URLs: inria.hal.science/inria-00186089 and inria.hal.science/inria-00104003. Takeaway: this is the analytic-analytic gold standard for quadrics; for cones, cylinders, spheres, planes, and tori-as-needed it gives exact curves with no marching and no missed components. Strongly worth adopting for the analytic subset.

**Sherbrooke, Patrikalakis, Brisson. "Computation of the medial axis..." and the general IPP/interval-Newton line; plus Stahl et al. and recent "Robust Algebraic Curve Intersections with Tolerance Control," CAD 145, 2022.** Demonstrates the modern pattern: subdivision to exclude empty regions, then the Krawczyk operator to certify existence and uniqueness of a root in a box with guaranteed tolerance. URL: dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cad.2022.103236. Takeaway: Krawczyk is the certification primitive Keel should standardize on; it certifies that a refined intersection point exists and is unique inside a small box, which is what tolerant booleans need to trust the topology.

**Jia, Wang et al. "Topology-driven approximation to rational surface-surface intersection via interval algebraic topology analysis (IATA)." ACM TOG 42(4), 2023.** A hybrid symbolic-numeric framework that configures the SSI topology inside a 4D interval box, maps topology to the solutions of an algebraic system, enumerates all cases (cusps, tangent points, tiny loops, self-intersections, mixtures) as combinations of four fundamental cases, then realizes it with subdivision plus marching. URL: dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3592452. Takeaway: the most rigorous recent treatment of rational (analytic-class and rational-NURBS) SSI topology; strong on completeness of case enumeration.

**Yang, Jia, Yan. "Topology Guaranteed B-Spline Surface/Surface Intersection." ACM TOG 42(6) / SIGGRAPH Asia 2023.** The current state of the art for freeform-freeform SSI. A practical algorithm that guarantees correct intersection topology for two B-spline surfaces even under near-critical and complicated relative positions, benchmarked against SISL, OCCT, and commercial ACIS and shown to be more topology-correct and competitive in speed. URL: dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3618349. Takeaway: this is the single most relevant paper for Keel; its pipeline (topology guarantee first, then trace) is the target architecture, and its benchmark set is the bar to clear.

**Yang, Jia, Yan. "Topology guaranteed and error controlled curve tracing for parametric SSI," CAGD 2025; "Overlap Region Extraction of Two NURBS Surfaces," SIGGRAPH Asia 2025; "A Robust and Efficient Intersection Algorithm for NURBS Surfaces: Handling Small Loops and Tangent Intersections," ACM TOG 2026.** The follow-on trilogy: error-controlled tracing with topology guarantee, dedicated overlap/coincidence extraction (the exact primitive booleans on touching faces require), and a robustness paper aimed squarely at small loops and tangent intersections. URL: yangjieyin.github.io/homepage/. Takeaway: this group is actively closing the historically unsolved cases; Keel should treat these four papers as its implementation reference and watch the homepage for code or supplementary material.

**OpenCASCADE (OCCT) Boolean Operations and Modeling Algorithms documentation; Spatial/ACIS "Tolerant Modeling" (Kernel R10 ch. 6).** Production-kernel practice: per-entity geometric tolerances rather than a single global epsilon, sphere-of-tolerance point representations, a Pave/PaveFiller intersection stage, and explicit handling of near-coincident faces via a prepare/snap phase. URLs: dev.opencascade.org/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__boolean_operations.html and the ACIS tolerant-modeling PDF. Takeaway: real kernels survive on tolerant modeling, not exact arithmetic; the intersection stage feeds tolerances forward into the boolean, and coincident-face handling is a named, separate workflow.

## 3. State-of-the-Art Assessment

For analytic-analytic pairs the problem is effectively solved: plane/plane and plane/quadric are trivial, and quadric/quadric has an exact, complete, near-optimal rational parameterization (Dupont-Lazard-Lazard-Petitjean, QI). Tori introduce degree-4 implicit surfaces where closed forms exist for some pairings but marching is the practical fallback.

For freeform-freeform, geometry (point accuracy along a known curve component) is solved by validated marching plus Newton/Krawczyk refinement. The frontier is topology guarantee. As of 2023 to 2026, Yang-Jia-Yan demonstrate provable topology correctness for B-spline pairs that defeats SISL, OCCT, and ACIS on near-critical configurations, and the IATA framework (Jia et al. 2023) gives a complete case enumeration for rational surfaces. So "provably complete SSI topology" now exists in the literature for the rational/B-spline setting, which is a genuine advance over the 1990s state.

What remains genuinely unsolved or unsatisfying: (1) Tangential and near-tangential intersections, where the intersection curve has higher multiplicity, the marching tangent degenerates, and tiny loops appear and disappear under perturbation. (2) Coincidence and partial overlap between surfaces, which is not a curve problem at all but a region problem, and which booleans on touching faces produce constantly; dedicated methods are only now appearing (Yang et al. 2025 overlap extraction). (3) Robustness on f64 without exact arithmetic: every guarantee in the literature assumes rational coefficients or interval arithmetic, and bridging to a fast f64 tolerant kernel reintroduces the snapping and case-classification fragility that production kernels manage but do not eliminate. (4) Performance of the fully certified path: the most rigorous methods (IATA, algebraic BOOLE) are markedly slower than naive marching, so kernels still lean on fast-but-fallible marching and pay for it in boolean failures. No published method is simultaneously general, fast, and provably complete on floating point.

## 4. Design Impact for Keel

Recommended SSI architecture, layered from fast/special to slow/general:

1. **Type dispatch.** Classify the surface pair. Route plane/plane, plane/quadric, and quadric/quadric to closed forms. Route everything else to the freeform pipeline. Keep this dispatch a single chokepoint so analytics never silently fall through to marching.
2. **Analytic layer (closed forms).** Implement exact plane/plane and plane/quadric; adopt the QI-style exact quadric/quadric parameterization (Dupont-Lazard-Lazard-Petitjean). This eliminates an entire class of marching failures for the most common engineering surfaces (planes, cylinders, cones, spheres).
3. **Topology resolution first (freeform).** Before any tracing, compute global structure: starting points on every component via Interval Projected Polyhedron (Sherbrooke-Patrikalakis), loop detection via Gauss-map/normal-cone separation (Sederberg-Meyers, Hohmeyer LP), and turning/singular-point identification in the Grandine-Klein style. Target the Yang-Jia-Yan 2023 topology-guarantee algorithm as the reference.
4. **Validated marching.** Trace each component with a predictor-corrector tracer: differential-geometry predictor (Ye-Maekawa formulas, including the tangential-case tangents), Newton corrector back onto both surfaces, adaptive step control from curvature, and a per-step Krawczyk certification that the corrected point is the unique intersection in its box. This single tracer also serves self-intersection and offset trimming.
5. **Certification and tolerance hand-off.** Standardize on the Krawczyk operator for existence/uniqueness certification of segments and points, and emit per-edge tolerances (sphere-of-tolerance style) that the boolean layer consumes. Adopt tolerant modeling (per-entity tolerance), not global epsilon.
6. **Tangential/overlap subsystem as a first-class citizen.** Explicitly detect coincidence and partial overlap (Yang et al. 2025 overlap extraction) and near-tangency; these feed the boolean's same-surface and on-boundary classification. Do not bolt this on later.

ADOPT:
- Topology-first-then-march pipeline (Grandine-Klein structure, Yang-Jia-Yan 2023 guarantee).
- Interval Projected Polyhedron for starting/characteristic points; rounded interval arithmetic for enclosures.
- Gauss-map/normal-cone loop detection (Sederberg-Meyers plus Hohmeyer LP) as the gate before marching.
- Exact quadric/quadric (QI / Dupont-Lazard-Lazard-Petitjean) and trivial plane analytics.
- Krawczyk operator as the universal certification primitive.
- Ye-Maekawa differential geometry for predictor steps, including tangential formulas.
- Tolerant modeling with per-entity tolerances, mirroring Parasolid/ACIS/OCCT.
- A dedicated overlap/coincidence extraction path (Yang et al. 2025) wired into booleans.

AVOID:
- Pure naive marching with boundary-only start points and no loop detection (silently drops interior loops; this is the classic production failure).
- Levin's quadric method as implemented in floating point (topologically unreliable); use the exact successor instead.
- A single global epsilon tolerance model.
- Relying on subdivision alone for high-curvature or tangential faces (Hohmeyer's flat-surface caveat; subdivision blows up and still misses tangencies).
- Branch-jumping-prone tracers without per-step Krawczyk validation and without explicit singular-branch resolution.
- Deferring tangential/overlap handling; it is the dominant boolean failure mode.

INVESTIGATE:
- The Yang-Jia-Yan 2025/2026 trilogy (error-controlled tracing, overlap extraction, small-loop/tangent robustness) for available code, licensing, and direct portability to Rust.
- IATA (Jia et al. 2023) as the completeness backstop for the analytic/rational-NURBS topology enumeration.
- Algebraic lower-dimensional formulation (Krishnan-Manocha BOOLE) for robust all-component start-point finding and singular-branch resolution, weighed against its heavy resultant/eigenvalue machinery.
- Cost and integration of exact/interval arithmetic crates in Rust (rational, MPFR-backed intervals) for the certified path, versus an f64-with-Krawczyk compromise.
- Whether GPU/parallel acceleration is worth it; current evidence (OCCT, research) shows only modest CPU parallelism in the intersection stage and no mature GPU SSI, so this is low priority.

## 5. Gaps and Open Problems

1. **Tangential and near-tangential intersections on f64.** Higher-multiplicity contact, degenerate marching tangents, and perturbation-sensitive tiny loops. The literature solves these with rational/interval arithmetic; doing so at f64 kernel speed is unsolved and is Keel's chief schedule risk.
2. **Coincidence and partial overlap.** A region problem, not a curve problem, central to booleans on touching faces. Dedicated methods are brand new (2025) and unproven at production scale.
3. **f64 to certified bridge.** All completeness guarantees assume exact or interval inputs; the snap/classify logic that ports them to a fast tolerant kernel is where production robustness is actually won or lost, and it is under-published.
4. **Performance of certified topology.** The rigorous methods are slow; there is no published method that is general, fast, and provably complete simultaneously.
5. **Singular-branch enumeration robustness.** Finding all branches at a singular point reliably still leans on algebraic machinery (resultants) that is fragile and heavy.
6. **No mature GPU or learning-based SSI.** Recent ML-assisted and GPU work exists at the margins but nothing production-credible; not a near-term lever for Keel.
